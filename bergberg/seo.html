SEO

SEO means showing up when people search. It still works, and AI search runs on the same things. You can do all of it yourself, for free.

Start here: You don’t need to learn SEO. Do the checklist a little at a time. When an SEO email shows up, read “Should I Hire Someone?” on this page.

What SEO Means

Think of Google as a giant library. Robots walk through your website and read it. Then, when a couple types “Evergreen Lake House wedding photographer,” Google and the AI tools pick the pages that best answer that question.

Here is the catch. The robots can’t see your photos. They only read words. So the words on your site have to say where you were, what happened and who you are. Right now a lot of your photos have no words at all, just file names.

Is the Old Way Dead?

No. People emailing you say the “old way” of SEO doesn’t work anymore. Google disagrees. In its 2026 guide to AI search, Google asks whether SEO still matters for AI and answers, “In short, yes!” It also says the new names people are selling (AEO, GEO) are just new names for the same work.

So the truth is in the middle. The basics didn’t die. A few new places got more important. Nothing on this page costs money.

How AI Picks a Photographer

When a couple asks ChatGPT or Google “Who is a good photographer for Evergreen Lake House?”, the AI looks for pages that say so in plain words. Studies from 2026 show where it looks:

  • Google Business Profile. It was the most cited source for local businesses in a 2026 study of 1,355 businesses.
  • Yelp. Yelp showed up in most of ChatGPT’s local answers in that same study.
  • Your own website. When four different AI tools agreed on a local business, they almost always cited that business’s own site.
  • Wedding sites. The Knot and Zola come up a lot in AI answers about weddings.
  • Venue vendor lists. A venue’s list that names you is proof from someone else.

Your posts don’t clearly name the venue in their search titles, descriptions or photo text yet. That makes them hard for any robot to match. The checklist below fixes that.

Where to Click in Squarespace

Show the click-by-click steps

Squarespace moves buttons around now and then. If a button isn’t where this says, search the Squarespace help center for the task name.

Alt text on a photo

  1. Open the post and click Edit.
  2. Click the photo, then the pencil icon.
  3. In the Content tab, type in the box called “Image alt text.”
  4. For a gallery, the photo’s title is used as its alt text, so type the sentence in the title box.
  5. Click Save.

SEO title and description on a post

  1. In the blog list, hover over the post and click the three dots, then Settings.
  2. Click the SEO tab.
  3. Type the SEO title and the SEO description.
  4. Click Save.

Changing a post’s web address

Only do this if the address is a jumble. If you change it, add a redirect so old links still work: Settings, then Developer tools, then URL mappings. Type the old address, then the new one.

Google Business Profile

This is the box with the map that shows up when someone searches “Golden wedding photographer.” Google says three things decide who shows up: how well you match the search, how close you are, and how well known you are. Reviews help a lot. You can’t pay Google to rank higher.

  • Make your main category “Wedding photographer.”
  • Use your real business name only. Don’t stuff extra words into it.
  • Fill in every service: wedding photography, engagement sessions, elopements, film photography, albums.
  • Add new photos every week from your newest wedding. New photos help with AI answers too.
  • Post each new blog to your profile as an Update with a “Learn more” button. Posts don’t move your ranking much, but they show you’re busy. Leave phone numbers out of the post text, since Google can reject it.
  • Reply to every review within a week, in your own words.

Reviews

Reviews are the biggest thing you control. Most people care about reviews from the last three months, so a steady trickle beats a pile from years ago.

  • Google first. It feeds the map and Google’s AI answers.
  • Then Zola or The Knot. They come up in AI answers about weddings, and Zola needs three or more reviews a year for its “Best of” badge.
  • Yelp, once you claim it. It feeds ChatGPT.

Ask every couple, not just the happy ones you pick, and never offer a discount for a review. Google’s rules forbid both. The Clients tab has the exact email and the right moment to send it.

Red flags in any SEO pitch

  • They emailed you out of the blue. Google’s own guide warns about this.
  • They promise a number one ranking. No one can promise that.
  • They send a scary report with “87 errors.” Their tools can’t see inside Google; it’s an opinion.
  • They sell “backlink packages.” Buying links breaks Google’s rules and can hurt you.
  • They want to pump out lots of AI-written pages. Google counts that as spam.
  • They want a monthly fee but won’t write down exactly what they’ll change.

If you ever hire someone

Pay for one job with a fixed price and a written list of every change, like “fix the titles, descriptions and alt text on 40 posts.” That is a few hours to a couple of days of work, not a year-long contract. In one survey of SEO providers, the most common rate was $75 to $100 an hour. Ask them to show past results, explain what they’ll change and why, and say how they’ll measure it.

Check Your Progress Each Month

On the first of each month, spend 20 minutes on this.

  1. Read the Search Console email. Look for venue names in the searches, like “evergreen lake house photographer.” Are clicks going up?
  2. In Bing Webmaster Tools, open AI Performance. It shows when Copilot names your pages.
  3. Ask the same five questions in ChatGPT, Google, Gemini, Perplexity and Copilot. Write down whether you’re named and which sites the answer used.
  4. Count how many inquiries said how they found you. Ask every couple, “How did you hear about us?”
1. Who are the best wedding photographers in Golden, Colorado? 2. Who is a good photographer for an Evergreen Lake House wedding? 3. Recommend a wedding photographer for Boettcher Mansion. 4. Who photographs weddings at Headwaters Center in Winter Park? 5. Husband and wife wedding photographers near Denver with a photojournalistic style.

AI answers change from day to day. Watch the trend over a few months, not one answer.
