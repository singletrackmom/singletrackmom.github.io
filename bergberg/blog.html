Blog

Every blog post is one more door Google and the AI tools can send a couple through. Two posts a month is enough. Steady beats a burst.

Start here: When it’s blog week, follow “How to Write a Post” top to bottom. That’s the only part you need that day.

Why the Blog Matters

  • Each post is its own page on Google. Your home page can only show up for a few searches. Each post adds a venue, a town and a season you can be found for.
  • Couples who read a full wedding at their venue already trust you before they write.
  • Venues and planners share posts that make them look good. Each share is a link back to you, and links count as votes.
  • A blog that stops in July looks like a business that slowed down. Your last post went up 13 July 2026. Since then you’ve shot weddings that never got a post, including a new one at Evergreen Lake House.

How to Write a Post

About one hour. Do it on a Thursday, every other week.

  1. Pick 25 to 40 photos. Mix getting ready, the ceremony, portraits on the property, details, and the party.
  2. Rename the files before you upload. Venue, town, a word or two, a number: “evergreen-lake-house-wedding-dock-01.jpg.”
  3. Write the title. Names, venue, town: “[Names]’ Evergreen Lake House Wedding in Evergreen, Colorado.”
  4. Write the first paragraph. Say the venue and town in the first two sentences.
  5. Tell the day in about 500 words. Use short headings: Getting Ready, The Ceremony, Portraits, The Reception. Mention the light, the weather, and one tip for couples at that venue.
  6. Add one question and answer. For example: “Where are the best photo spots at Evergreen Lake House? The dock at sunset, the trail along the lake, and the stone fireplace inside.” AI tools love a plain answer to a plain question.
  7. List every vendor with a link. Venue, planner, florist, caterer, DJ, cake, hair and makeup. Vendors share posts that link to them.
  8. Link to your venue page for that venue, and to one other post on your site.
  9. Add alt text to every photo. One plain sentence: who, what, where.
  10. Fill in the SEO title and SEO description (the SEO tab in the post settings). Never leave the default description.
  11. Publish, then share it four ways the same day (table below).

Venue Guides

A venue guide is one strong page about one venue. It links to every wedding you’ve shot there, and every one of those posts links back to it. It is the page couples land on when they search the venue name, and it stops your many Evergreen Lake House posts from competing with each other.

Write these in order: Evergreen Lake House, Boettcher Mansion, Headwaters Center, Mount Vernon Canyon Club, The Vista at Applewood. About 1,000 words each.

  • What the venue is like, in every season, with your photos.
  • The best photo spots on the property, and the best time of day for each.
  • Where to get ready, where guests can stay, and a rehearsal dinner idea nearby.
  • A rain or snow plan for photos.
  • Planners who know the venue well (with links).
  • Three short questions and answers, like “How much time do we need for portraits at Boettcher Mansion?”
  • Links to every wedding you’ve photographed there.

Your old business plan had venue notes for Boettcher, Evergreen Lake House and Applewood (getting ready, staying over, rehearsal dinners, transportation, light). Use those as your starting notes.

The posts that make an argument

  • For the Couple Who Found Each Other Later. You two met later in life. Couples planning a second marriage or a vow renewal want to know you get it.
  • What to Ask Before You Book a Wedding Photographer. Will the photographer whose work I liked be the one who shows up? How many weddings have they led? Never name a competitor. Let the questions do the work.
  • Why We’ve Never Sent a Substitute Photographer. The personal version: your names are on every photo.
