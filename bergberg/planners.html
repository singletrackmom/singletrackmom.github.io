Planners

Planners send couples to the photographers they trust and heard from last. You’ve already worked with more than a dozen of them. The job now is to stay in touch.

Start here: Most weeks you only need the planner list and one of the two messages below it.

Why Planners Matter

  • A couple who hires a planner asks the planner, “Who should we get for photos?” The planner’s answer is usually the one they book.
  • Planners pick photographers who make their job easier: on time, calm, and quick with photos afterward.
  • Planners forget. If you haven’t talked in a year, someone else is on their short list now.
  • A collab post on Instagram puts you in front of every one of the planner’s followers, who are mostly engaged couples.

The Collab Plan

For every planner on the list, you already have photos of their work. Turn each one into a collab post on Instagram. That gives you a post every week for months, and each one puts you in front of a planner’s followers.

  1. Pick the planner for the week (top of the list first).
  2. Open your blog post from the wedding you did together. Pick 8 to 15 photos that show their work: the setup, the flowers, the timeline running smoothly.
  3. Send the message below a day or two before you post.
  4. Post the carousel and invite them as a collaborator. Add the venue too.
  5. Send them the full-size photos with a credit line, even if they don’t accept.
  6. Write down the date and what they said.
Hi [Name]! I was looking back at [couple]’s wedding at [venue] and your work looked so good. We’d love to post a carousel from it this week and add you as a collaborator so it shows on your page too. I’ll send the full-size photos either way, free to use with credit. We’d also love to work with you again in 2027. Thanks! [Your name], Berg Berg Photography

The Planner Note

Once a month, send each planner you’ve worked with recently a short personal note. Not a sales email. Tuesday or Thursday morning is a good time.

Hi [Name], I was thinking about the [venue] wedding we did together. Here’s the gallery: [link]. You’re welcome to use any of these with credit. We have a few 2027 dates open, and if a couple ever asks you for a photographer, we’d love to work with you again. Hope your season wrapped up well. [Your name], Berg Berg Photography

Ask one clear thing

If a planner seems warm, ask one simple question: “Do you keep a list of photographers you recommend? We’d love to be on it.”

New Planners to Meet

Every venue on the Venues tab has planners who work there often. When you visit a venue, ask the coordinator, “Which planners do you see here the most?” Then send each one a short hello with a link to your post from that venue. Planners who specialize in small weddings, second marriages and vow renewals are a great fit for you.

Your Vendor Circle

Planners aren’t the only ones couples ask. Caterers, florists and DJs get asked “Who do you like?” too.

  • After every wedding. Within a week, send each vendor 10 to 20 photos of their work, free to use with credit. This is the single best way to get them talking about you.
  • Caterers. Couples often book catering early. Photograph the setup before guests arrive, and tag the caterer on Instagram. Start with caterers you’ve worked with at Evergreen Lake House and Boettcher: Rocky Mountain Catering, Hippie Chicks Catering, Biscuits and Berries, Relish, Three Tomatoes and Italic.
  • A partner perk. Offer caterers this: couples who book them and you get a free extra hour with you. Ask what small thing they would add for couples who come from you, like a late-night snack. A flat add-on works better than a percent off.
  • DJs and videographers. Pick two or three you like and ask their starting prices once, so you have an answer when couples ask.
  • Other photographers. A Golden photography studio already wants to collaborate. A shared guest post for Visit Golden, like “Where to Take Wedding Photos in Golden,” helps you both.
