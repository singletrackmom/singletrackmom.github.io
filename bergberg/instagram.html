Instagram

Instagram won’t book weddings by itself. It’s where couples, planners and venues go to check you out after they hear your name. One good post a week is enough.

Start here: Each week you only need the Feed Rules, “Your Weekly Hour” and the caption formula. The rest is background.

Should You Do Instagram?

Yes, but small. Here’s what it does for you:

  • A trust check. A venue or friend mentions you. The couple looks you up. Your feed tells them you’re real, busy and their style.
  • A vendor check. Planners and venues look at your feed before they put your name on a list.
  • A little bit of Google. Since July 2025, public posts from business accounts can show up in Google search. Good captions now help on Google too.
  • New followers, through collabs. A collab post shows up on the planner’s or venue’s page too, in front of all their followers.

The minimum: one carousel a week from a real wedding, with the vendors added as collaborators, and replies to messages within a day. That’s about an hour a week.

How It Works Now

These come from Instagram’s own leaders and help pages.

  • Sends matter most for new people. When someone sends your post to a friend in a message, Instagram shows it to more people. Brides send posts to their partner, their mom and their planner. Ask for it: “Send this to a friend planning a lake wedding.”
  • Carousels are strong right now. A carousel is a post with several photos people swipe through. You can use up to 20. If someone scrolls past, Instagram may show it again starting at photo two.
  • Reels reach the most strangers. Short videos. Try one every other week if you have time.
  • Trial Reels are free practice. Flip the “Trial” switch before you share a Reel. It shows to non-followers first and stays off your grid unless it does well.
  • Be original. Instagram stopped recommending accounts that mostly repost other people’s photos. Your photos are yours, so you’re fine. Share vendor posts in Stories, not on your grid.
  • Hashtags barely matter. Instagram now allows only a few per post. Use three to five specific ones.
  • Location tags and vendor tags help. Always tag the venue as the location, and tag every vendor on the photos.

Feed Rules

Couples look at your grid as a whole, in a few seconds. It has to say “wedding photographer” right away.

  • Weddings first. At least three of every four posts are weddings.
  • Never two engagement posts in a row. One engagement post now and then is fine. Too many in a row makes you look like an engagement photographer.
  • Your photo is always the first slide. Never a venue’s or vendor’s graphic with their text on it. It makes your whole grid look like an ad.
  • Check the contract first. Some couples say no blog or no social media in their contract. If it says no, don’t post them.
  • Pick a strong first photo. It’s the only one people see on your grid: the couple, the venue, real emotion.

Fix the Baldoria post

The Baldoria photos are good, but the cover with their text on it makes your feed look bad. Instagram won’t let you change a post’s cover after it’s up, so take it down and post it again:

  1. Save the photos you want from that post (you already have them in your files).
  2. On the post, tap the three dots, then Delete. (Or Archive, if you want to keep the likes and comments hidden but saved.)
  3. Post the photos again as a new carousel, with your best photo first and no text graphic.
  4. Tag Baldoria on the Water as the location, and invite them as a collaborator.

Lake House First

Evergreen Lake House is your most popular venue now, and your feed doesn’t show it. Your recent posts are Boettcher Mansion, Baldoria, Denver Botanic Gardens and The Pines. The fix:

  1. Post your newest Evergreen Lake House wedding this week as a carousel, with the planner and the venue as collaborators.
  2. Post one Evergreen Lake House wedding every month after that. You have many on the blog to pull from.
  3. Make the “Evergreen Lake House” highlight.
  4. Always use the venue as the location tag and #evergreenlakehousewedding in the caption.

Caption Formula

Place, moment, credits, ask. The first line matters most, because that’s what Instagram search and Google read.

Winter wedding at Evergreen Lake House in Evergreen, Colorado. [Two or three sentences about one real moment from the day.] Planner @[planner] · Florals @[florist] · Catering @[caterer] · Venue @[venue] Send this to a friend planning a mountain wedding. 2027 dates are open, link in bio. #evergreenlakehousewedding #evergreencolorado #coloradowedding

Alt text on Instagram

Before you post, tap Advanced settings, then write alt text. Already posted? Tap the three dots, Edit, then Accessibility. Write one plain sentence: “Couple kissing on the dock at Evergreen Lake House in the snow.”

Collab Posts

A collab post shows on your page and on each collaborator’s page at the same time. It reaches all their followers. This is the fastest free way to grow and to get on planners’ radar. The Planners tab lists every planner from your blog, with their Instagram.

How to send a collab invite

  1. Start a new post and add your photos.
  2. Write the caption.
  3. Tap Tag people, then Invite collaborator.
  4. Pick the planner and the venue (you can add several accounts, but check the limit in the app).
  5. Share. They get an invite in their messages. When they accept, the post shows on their page too.
  6. Forgot someone? Open the post, tap the three dots, Edit, Tag people, and invite them.

Good manners

  • Message them a day or two before, so the invite isn’t a surprise.
  • Put their work in the post: the flowers, the tables, the view.
  • Credit every vendor in the caption, even the ones you didn’t invite.
  • Send them the full-size photos too, so they can post on their own.
  • If they say no or don’t answer, tag them anyway and move on.
Hi [Name], we loved working with you on [couple]’s wedding at [venue]. We’re posting a carousel this week with your beautiful work in it. Would you like us to add you as a collaborator so it shows on your page too? We’re happy to send you the full-size photos either way. [Your name], Berg Berg Photography

Pinterest, Lightly

Pinterest is a search engine for pictures, and pins keep working for months. January through March is the busiest wedding-planning season there. Keep it simple:

  • Make boards named the way people search: “Evergreen Lake House Weddings,” “Boettcher Mansion Weddings,” “Colorado Mountain Wedding Photos,” “Golden Colorado Wedding Venues,” “Colorado Winter Wedding Ideas.”
  • Pin three tall photos from every new blog post, each linking to that post.
  • Write the title with the venue first: “Evergreen Lake House Wedding Photos | Colorado Mountain Wedding.”
  • Schedule a week of pins in one sitting with Pinterest’s free scheduler or Hootsuite.
