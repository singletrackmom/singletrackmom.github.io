Instagram

Instagram won’t book weddings by itself. It’s where couples, planners and venues go to check you out after they hear your name. One good post a week is enough.

Start here: Each week you only need the Feed Rules, “Your Weekly Hour” and the caption formula. The rest is background.

Should You Do Instagram?

Yes, but small. Here’s what it does for you:

  • A trust check. A venue or friend mentions you. The couple looks you up. Your feed tells them you’re real, busy and their style.
  • A vendor check. Planners and venues look at your feed before they put your name on a list.
  • A little bit of Google. Since July 2025, public posts from business accounts can show up in Google search. Good captions now help on Google too.
  • New followers, through collabs. A collab post shows up on the planner’s or venue’s page too, in front of all their followers.

The minimum: one carousel a week from a real wedding, with the vendors added as collaborators, and replies to messages within a day. That’s about an hour a week.

How It Works Now

These come from Instagram’s own leaders and help pages.

  • Sends matter most for new people. When someone sends your post to a friend in a message, Instagram shows it to more people. Brides send posts to their partner, their mom and their planner. Ask for it: “Send this to a friend planning a lake wedding.”
  • Carousels are strong right now. A carousel is a post with several photos people swipe through. You can use up to 20. If someone scrolls past, Instagram may show it again starting at photo two.
  • Reels reach the most strangers. Short videos. Try one every other week if you have time.
  • Trial Reels are free practice. Flip the “Trial” switch before you share a Reel. It shows to non-followers first and stays off your grid unless it does well.
  • Be original. Instagram stopped recommending accounts that mostly repost other people’s photos. Your photos are yours, so you’re fine. Share vendor posts in Stories, not on your grid.
  • Hashtags barely matter. Instagram now allows only a few per post. Use three to five specific ones.
  • Location tags and vendor tags help. Always tag the venue as the location, and tag every vendor on the photos.

Feed Rules

Couples look at your grid as a whole, in a few seconds. It has to say “wedding photographer” right away.

  • Weddings first. At least three of every four posts are weddings.
  • Never two engagement posts in a row. One engagement post now and then is fine. Too many in a row makes you look like an engagement photographer.
  • Your photo is always the first slide. Never a venue’s or vendor’s graphic with their text on it. It makes your whole grid look like an ad.
  • Check the contract first. Some couples say no blog or no social media in their contract. If it says no, don’t post them.
  • Pick a strong first photo. It’s the only one people see on your grid: the couple, the venue, real emotion.

Fix the Baldoria post

The Baldoria photos are good, but the cover with their text on it makes your feed look bad. Instagram won’t let you change a post’s cover after it’s up, so take it down and post it again:

  1. Save the photos you want from that post (you already have them in your files).
  2. On the post, tap the three dots, then Delete. (Or Archive, if you want to keep the likes and comments hidden but saved.)
  3. Post the photos again as a new carousel, with your best photo first and no text graphic.
  4. Tag Baldoria on the Water as the location, and invite them as a collaborator.

Rotate Your Three Venues

Boettcher Mansion, Evergreen Lake House and Headwaters Center are your three main venues. Give each one equal time on your grid.

  1. Post one wedding a month from each venue, in turn, as a carousel with the planner and the venue as collaborators. Start with your newest Evergreen Lake House wedding, since nothing from it is up yet.
  2. Make a highlight for each of the three venues.
  3. Always use the venue as the location tag, plus its hashtag in the caption: #boettchermansionwedding, #evergreenlakehousewedding or #headwaterscenterwedding.

Check the Grid Before You Post

Your profile grid crops every post to a tall 3:4 rectangle, whatever shape you upload. The first photo of a carousel becomes the thumbnail, so a tight crop of a couple can lose heads, hands or the dress on the grid, even when it looks great full size. Pick the first photo for how it reads small, next to the posts around it.

  • Pick a wider photo first. The whole couple, some breathing room, the venue showing. Save the close-ups for slides 2 and up.
  • Check before you share. On the last screen before posting, tap the preview and drag to adjust the crop.
  • Fix it after, too. On any post, tap the three dots, then Adjust Preview, and drag the photo so nobody is cut off.
  • Look at the whole grid once a month. Open your profile and scroll the top 12. If a thumbnail looks cut off, Adjust Preview fixes it in a few seconds.

Caption Formula

Place, moment, credits, ask. The first line matters most, because that’s what Instagram search and Google read.

See the caption formula
Winter wedding at Evergreen Lake House in Evergreen, Colorado. [Two or three sentences about one real moment from the day.] Planner @[planner] · Florals @[florist] · Catering @[caterer] · Venue @[venue] Send this to a friend planning a mountain wedding. 2027 dates are open, link in bio. #evergreenlakehousewedding #evergreencolorado #coloradowedding

Alt text on Instagram

Before you post, tap Advanced settings, then write alt text. Already posted? Tap the three dots, Edit, then Accessibility. Write one plain sentence: “Couple kissing on the dock at Evergreen Lake House in the snow.”

Collab Posts

A collab post shows on your page and on each collaborator’s page at the same time. It reaches all their followers. This is the fastest free way to grow and to get on planners’ radar. The Planners tab lists every planner from your blog, with their Instagram.

How to send a collab invite

  1. Start a new post and add your photos.
  2. Write the caption.
  3. Tap Tag people, then Invite collaborator.
  4. Pick the planner and the venue (you can add several accounts, but check the limit in the app).
  5. Share. They get an invite in their messages. When they accept, the post shows on their page too.
  6. Forgot someone? Open the post, tap the three dots, Edit, Tag people, and invite them.

Good manners

  • Message them a day or two before, so the invite isn’t a surprise.
  • Put their work in the post: the flowers, the tables, the view.
  • Credit every vendor in the caption, even the ones you didn’t invite.
  • Send them the full-size photos too, so they can post on their own.
  • If they say no or don’t answer, tag them anyway and move on.
See the collab message
Hi [Name], we loved working with you on [couple]’s wedding at [venue]. We’re posting a carousel this week with your beautiful work in it. Would you like us to add you as a collaborator so it shows on your page too? We’re happy to send you the full-size photos either way. [Your name], Berg Berg Photography

Optional, low priority: wedding groups

Only do this if you have extra time. When someone posts “looking for a photographer” in a big wedding group, they often get 100 replies, and we’ve never booked a wedding this way. Venues, planners and the Monday Golden posts are a better use of your time.

Try it for one month. If nothing comes of it, stop and forget it.

  • Join two or three groups from your own account. Colorado Wedding Vendors is one, and planners post requests there.
  • Read the rules. Most bride groups don’t let vendors advertise, so only reply when someone asks for a photographer.
  • Reply fast, and change one line so it fits their post. Groups kick out vendors who paste the same thing everywhere.
  • Don’t use a bot to post or reply. Facebook shuts down accounts that do.

You still post the replies yourself.

Get Out of the Bubble

Right now your posts mostly reach people who already follow you. These put your photos in front of new people for free:

  • Collab posts. When a venue or planner accepts, the post shows up on their account too. This is the biggest free reach you have. See Collab Posts above.
  • Visit Golden. Their blog runs guest posts, and their Instagram has a big local and visitor audience. Your business is already listed with them, and they’ve asked to use your photos before. Your contacts there are in Gmail.
  • Team up with LANZ Photography. Visit Golden doesn’t want to feature just one photographer. LANZ does many of the weddings at the Rose. Pitch Visit Golden together: a guest post called “Getting Married in Golden,” with photos from both of you, shared on Visit Golden’s Instagram.
  • Tag the location every time. People browse places like Evergreen Lake and Lookout Mountain.
  • Helpful posts. “The 5 best photo spots at Evergreen Lake House” gets saved and shared more than a single pretty photo.

Paid Ads, If You Try Them

Last time a boost got lots of views and no clicks. That usually means two things: the ad was set up to get views, and the post didn’t give people a reason to click. Fix both before spending again.

Boost or Ads Manager?

  • Boost is the button under a post. It’s quick, but it gives you few choices.
  • Meta Ads Manager takes longer to set up. You get to pick exactly who sees it, where it shows up, and what counts as success. Use this one.

How to Get Clicks

  • Pick “website visits” or “messages” as the goal, not reach or profile visits.
  • Give one clear reason to click: “See the full Evergreen Lake House wedding” or “2027 dates are open: meet us for free.”
  • Send people to one matching page, like the blog post or the booking page, not your home page.
  • Run it in the feed. Feed ads usually get more clicks than Reels or Stories.
  • Don’t target people interested in “wedding photography.” Target people who are engaged, in Colorado and nearby states, since many of your couples come from out of town.
  • Use photos where a couple thinks, “That’s us.” Real moments beat perfect poses.

Start Small

  • Ads are for weddings only. Senior, family and holiday photos are side work, so they get the free Monday Facebook posts, not ad money.
  • Run ads during engagement season, January through March, when couples are choosing photographers.
  • Spend $10 a day for two weeks. That’s $140.
  • Check how many messages or inquiries you got, not likes. If there are none after a month, stop.

YouTube

Your YouTube channel holds client slideshows, the same photos as the blog posts, and the last ones went up a year ago. Don’t catch up on old ones, the blog already covers them. A slideshow with music and no words doesn’t help you show up in search, because Google has nothing to read. But keep the channel. YouTube is the site Google’s AI answers cite most, and small channels get cited too.

  • Only when it’s quick. If a slideshow takes a few minutes after the blog post is done, post it. If not, skip it. Title each one like “Couple · Venue Wedding · Golden, Colorado.”
  • Link the blog post in the video description.
  • Put the video in the blog post. In Squarespace, add a Video block and paste the YouTube link.
  • If you ever have time: a few 5 to 10 minute talking videos answer what couples search for, like “Best photo spots at Evergreen Lake House” or “How to plan your wedding day timeline.” These do more than any slideshow.

Pinterest, Lightly

Pinterest is a search engine for pictures, and pins keep working for months. January through March is the busiest wedding-planning season there. Keep it simple:

  • Make boards named the way people search: “Evergreen Lake House Weddings,” “Boettcher Mansion Weddings,” “Headwaters Center Weddings,” “Colorado Mountain Wedding Photos,” “Golden Colorado Wedding Venues,” “Colorado Winter Wedding Ideas.”
  • Pin three tall photos from every new blog post, each linking to that post.
  • Write the title with the venue first: “Evergreen Lake House Wedding Photos | Colorado Mountain Wedding.”
  • Schedule a week of pins in one sitting with Pinterest’s free scheduler or Hootsuite.
