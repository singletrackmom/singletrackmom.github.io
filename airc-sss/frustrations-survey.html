What this data is

In 2025 the Glendale Community College Faculty Senate asked faculty and staff to record the barriers they watched students hit. Respondents described the barrier in their own words, named the area that owns it, marked whether it originates inside the college or outside it and whether it is a local (micro) or systemic (macro) problem, and rated it on two 1 to 5 scales: how frustrating it is to the student (F) and how much it affects the student’s ability to continue (I). Not every respondent supplied ratings, and unrated rows are marked “not rated.”

This is a single-college, self-reported, staff-observed dataset. It is the evidence that motivated the scaled district study, and it is not a substitute for it. Nothing here has been collected across the other nine colleges, and nothing here is a finding of the district study, which has not been run.

Respondent names and email addresses are withheld. The barriers are reproduced as written, condensed for length where a respondent wrote at length, with no reordering or reweighting of what they said. Source: Glendale Community College Faculty Senate student frustrations survey, 2025.

How this survey shaped the study

Three properties of this data drove the design of the district instrument. First, the reported barriers cluster in findability, hours, and hand-offs rather than in the absence of a service: the help usually exists and the student cannot reach it. Second, the highest-rated items are concentrated in advising, enrollment, financial aid, and basic needs, which is where the study puts its first scenarios. Third, the ratings here are single-rater and unadjudicated, which is exactly the weakness the district study corrects by defining severity on the Nielsen 0 to 4 scale (0 not a usability problem, 1 cosmetic, 2 minor, 3 major, 4 catastrophic), judging it on frequency, impact, and persistence, and having two independent raters score each finding.

The routine, repeatable questions in this list, hours, deadlines, where to go, who to ask, are the ones automation can absorb so that advising and financial aid staff spend their hours on the students whose situations require a person. No one is replaced.

How this feeds the district study

The barriers above populate the task taxonomy behind the scenario bank, and they are the reason the bank weights advising, enrollment, financial aid, technology access, and basic needs so heavily. They also supply the demographic and situational raw material for the persona library, which is why the personas include working parents, food-insecure and housing-insecure students, veterans, Spanish-dominant students, and students who need Disability Resources.

What this survey cannot do is tell the committee whether these barriers exist in the same form at the other nine colleges, how severe they are on a comparable scale, or where in the journey they land. That is the work the district study is designed to do, and it has not been run.
