Student Journey Gap Analysis

Case study · AI Resource Center, Student Support and Success

A ten-college UX study of the student journey across Maricopa, built on demographic-grounded persona agents that walk the real path from finding Maricopa to work, so support staff can lift routine work off their desks without anyone being replaced.

Open the study →

Across the Maricopa district, ten colleges each run advising, financial aid, basic needs, and other support in their own way. Before a student can even apply, they have to find Maricopa, and from there the path from a felt need to the right person is full of dead ends. This study walks that whole path on purpose, at scale, and turns what breaks into located, severity-rated evidence the district can act on. Every option is screened against a hard no-student-data line. It covers the student-services journey up to and around the classroom, not inside Canvas or the coursework itself, since the in-course experience belongs to the Teaching and Learning domain, Domain 2.

Goal

Set a baseline of how many students use each service now, then find and rank where students hit barriers reaching support, from finding Maricopa through to work, by how many students each gap touches, and hand staff located, severity-rated evidence they can act on. AI is aimed at the routine, repetitive work that eats staff time, so advisors and support staff are freed for the students who need a person. A successful AI tool answers routine questions without a booking or an office visit, so help is counted through the tool even as office traffic falls, and paid student teams build the tools, not the staff workflows, which the ARC team and the service owner lead.

Audience

The Student Support and Success domain of the district AI Resource Center, made up of advisors, financial-aid, and other support staff across all ten colleges, plus the district, its students, and the leaders who decide where support goes. The AI Resource Center coordinates AI across the ten colleges, which together serve roughly 140,000 students.

Process

Build fifty demographic-grounded personas as specialist agents, coordinate them with one orchestrator agent, and run the full synthetic matrix across the journey. Every run records the outcome, the time on task, the assists needed, the barrier, an ease score, and a severity rating scored on the Nielsen 0 to 4 scale by more than one rater. Barriers are affinity-mapped into themes and ranked on severity against reach, then mapped to a proposed fix, an AI tool, a shared staff workflow, or a person. No student data is collected at any point: counts and aggregates only, never any PII.

The technology

The study is built on agents, not written profiles, and the architecture is deliberate: a single generalist agent pretending to be every student at once is weaker than many specialists plus a coordinator, so the design uses fifty specialists and one orchestrator. Each of the fifty personas is its own specialist agent, a specific student instantiated to mirror the district’s real enrollment demographics, race and ethnicity, first-generation status, Pell eligibility, food security, veteran status, parenting, disability, language, and enrollment modality. The set is proportioned to how Maricopa students actually enroll, including modality, with roughly a quarter fully online, a quarter mixed, and the rest primarily in person, so the online path is tested as heavily as the in-person one. A persona is kept whole and is never split into traits or sub-models; the whole student runs the task. Each persona agent is a synthetic student that autonomously browses the live college websites the way any member of the public would in a browser, no login and no insider shortcuts, so it hits the same dead ends a real student would.

Above the specialists sits one orchestrator agent that owns sequence and coverage. It decides what has to run first (a student has to find Maricopa and get in the door before there is anything to pay for), picks which persona, task, and college to run next, routes each run to the right specialist, and records what comes back. Every agent returns a structured, inspectable finding, the outcome, the path it took, where it broke, a first-person quote, and a candidate severity, so each result traces back to a specific page and can be compared across runs and checked by a person. This many-specialists-plus-one-coordinator design is informed by Western Governors University’s synthetic course-validation model, in which agent-based synthetic students validate real course experiences at scale. Findings are then validated against real students, and humans run the in-person walk-ins and decide what every finding means.

The scale

The synthetic students carry the online findability testing at scale: the full synthetic matrix runs every persona through every task at every college, 50 personas times 50 tasks times 10 colleges, 25,000 synthetic runs, a scale only synthetic agents make possible. Humans do two things the synthetics do not, the 80 in-person walk-ins at the physical offices and a small online validation sample, about one tester per campus and roughly ten in all, that runs the same online tasks to check the synthetic findings, so the AI findings are checked, not trusted blind. The real testers work as themselves, not in a persona: the personas ride on the synthetic agents, while the human validators simply attempt the same tasks and report what they hit, so their instrument is not the persona brief the synthetic runs use.

Status: planning and instrument-building. Fieldwork has not started and no runs have been logged yet, so nothing here reports results. The full committee plan is maintained as a separate working document; this page is the readable case study.

Approval and guardrails: this study is going through the district’s approval and data-governance review right now. Before any live testing begins, the permissions, the sanctioned test environment, and the guardrails are being established with the Office of Information Technology and the AI Resource Center. Nothing runs until it is approved. What follows describes the plan, not completed work.
