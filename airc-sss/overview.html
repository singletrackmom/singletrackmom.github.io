District barriers study

AI tools & strategy

A 10-college UX study of the student journey across Maricopa, mapping barriers and turning them into prioritized, human-in-the-loop AI pilots.

Open the study → Methodology → Pilot findings →

Goal

Locate the friction that pushes students out, then close it with AI where AI helps and route the rest to a person. The aim is more human contact where it matters, measured against a baseline captured before anything is deployed.

A UX study of the student journey across all 10 Maricopa colleges, which together serve roughly 140,000 students. It maps where students hit barriers as they move from application to the goal they came for, pairing expert heuristic evaluation against Nielsen’s ten usability heuristics with task-based usability testing, Nielsen 0 to 4 severity rating, journey mapping, and service blueprinting. The findings become prioritized, human-in-the-loop AI pilots, and every option is screened against a hard no-student-data line.

Audience

The district, its students, and the administrators who decide where support goes. The work is run for the district AI Resource Center, which coordinates AI across the 10 colleges.

Process

Research personas walk the live student journey, one persona, one campus, one task at a time, thinking aloud, so a failure is attributable to a specific barrier. Every run records the outcome, the time on task, the assists needed, the barrier, an ease score, and a severity rating scored by two independent raters. Barriers are affinity-mapped into themes and ranked on severity against reach, then mapped to a proposed AI fix. No student data is collected at any point: counts and aggregates only, never any PII.
