1. The problem this study addresses

Across the ten Maricopa colleges, the support that would keep students enrolled already exists on every campus. The failure is findability. Students cannot reach advising, financial aid, basic needs, accommodations, or the right person at the moment they need them.

A study with three personas walking thirty journeys can describe that problem. It cannot map it at the resolution the committee needs in order to prioritize where AI should close the gaps. This document scales the diagnosis into a structured, repeatable instrument that is designed to produce a college-by-college, stage-by-stage map of where students hit barriers, with severity attached to each one.

3. The persona-per-test model

The unit of the study is a single run: one persona, at one campus, attempting one task. The tester, human or AI, opens the instrument and receives a brief. This is who you are, these are your barriers, this is the one thing you are trying to do, and this is your campus. The tester then attempts the task from a real felt need. They are never told where to go. The persona must find the path the way a real student would, usually starting from a search on the college website.

3.1 Why a single task per run

A single task isolates one barrier, so a failure is attributable to a specific point in the journey. It also keeps runs short, parallelizable, and easy to assign to many testers at once, which is what scale requires.

3.2 Why a persona library rather than a few personas

A page that works for a digitally fluent transfer student can still fail a phone-first, Spanish-dominant parent on a slow connection. Only a varied library surfaces those intersections. The 36 personas reflect who actually enrolls: ESL and non-native speakers, veterans, autistic and ADHD students, food-insecure and first-generation students, working parents, returning adults, students who need Disability Resources, swirling multi-campus students, and dual-enrollment high schoolers.

3.3 Why problems, not destinations

Naming the office tests nothing. The whole barrier is that students cannot find the office. By giving a goal and withholding the destination, the run measures findability directly: the path taken, the dead ends, whether a human was needed, and where the tester finally landed.

4. The scenario bank and the path to 5,000 runs

A taxonomy of 46 base tasks covers the whole journey: apply and admissions, MEID, residency, orientation, self-enrollment, and the student ID, then financial aid, scholarships, the bookstore, adding and dropping classes and the aid impact, transcripts, tutoring, Disability Resources, counseling, basic needs, campus tech, veterans benefits, transfer, swirling, and graduation offboarding.

Each base task is run by several personas, because different barriers reveal different failures. That multiplication lifts the bank toward roughly 500 distinct persona and task scenarios. Each scenario is then attempted at each of the ten colleges, where the local service name and the page layout differ. Five hundred scenarios across ten colleges is about 5,000 task-runs.

The bank is the data backbone. It is delivered as a spreadsheet with one row per scenario and fields for status, tester, date, result, and severity. Those fields are empty until runs begin.

4.1 Human and AI split

The plan assigns roughly 250 scenarios to human testers and 250 to AI testers, each set spread across the ten colleges, for about 2,500 task-runs each. AI testers walk the public, no-login paths at scale and produce a first map quickly. Human testers cover the login-walled flows that require an authenticated test account, and they validate what the AI surfaced. The two halves reinforce each other: the AI finds breadth, the humans confirm depth and catch what an agent cannot feel.

4.2 Scenarios that cannot run yet

Some scenarios cannot be tested until the district advising tool exists: find or get assigned an advisor, book an advising appointment, see the aid impact at the moment of dropping a class, and confirm consortium aid at a second college. These are flagged in the bank as a later wave and excluded from the runs that can start now.

5. Operations, tracking, and coverage

The coordination problem is real. Many testers, one scenario each, no duplication, every result logged, and a clear view of what remains. The approach layers three pieces that already mostly exist.

  1. The bank is the single source of truth for assignment. Each scenario carries an ID. A college representative assigns one scenario to one tester by entering initials and a date on that row, so nothing is run twice and coverage is visible in the sheet itself.
  2. The existing Jotform capture form records a run: persona, college, task, path taken, time, ease, and severity. It captures results well but does not assign work, so it stays as the capture instrument and its entries key back to the Scenario ID.
  3. The coverage dashboard reads the bank and shows, per college and per journey stage, how much has been walked and where the gaps are. It is how the co-chairs and the steering committee see progress without reading the sheet.

Recommendation: keep the Jotform capture form for recording results, and use the scenario bank as the assignment and coverage layer above it. This avoids building a new assignment system, reuses the live form, and keeps one ID linking a planned scenario to its logged result.

Recommended stack: Google Forms and Sheets as the backbone, with the Optimal Workshop free tier reserved for targeted findability exercises. For a no-budget, multi-tester, unmoderated study at this scale, Forms and Sheets carry no response caps and no per-seat cost, and the bank, the capture form, and the coverage dashboard all read from one place. The paid platforms throttle exactly what this study has a lot of, which is runs, so their free tiers are best spent on small, high-value card sorts and tree tests where the built-in analysis earns its limits. The live Jotform capture form already follows this model.

7. Guardrails

7.1 What the study holds to

  • No PII. Test accounts and initials only. Personas are research instruments, not real people.
  • Translate, do not standardize. Each college keeps its own service names. The study tests findability, not conformity.
  • AI-only scope. Where the fix is automation, it takes routine, repetitive work off advising and financial aid staff so their time goes to the students who need a person. No one is replaced.
  • Human contact is the metric of success.

7.2 What the study routes elsewhere

  • Process and policy barriers go to the owner who can fix them. They are documented here, not solved here.
  • Classroom, curriculum, and faculty-tool issues go to Domain 2, Teaching and Learning.
  • Data-governance and security questions, including the vendor advising-tool data concern, go to Domain 1.
  • Retention and institutional-research data needs are coordinated with Domain 3.

8. What is needed to proceed

8.1 Per-campus test accounts

GCC can run now on an existing GCC student login. The other nine colleges each need one test-student account scoped to that college’s live systems, because a student login only reaches its own college. The district test-student accounts referenced in earlier work may be the source. What is needed is confirmation of which test accounts reach each college and whether they can authenticate the login-walled flows. The accounts-we-need list, one line per college, is in the scenario-bank workbook.

8.2 Real-student persona validation

The 36 personas are designed from known demographics and the GCC frustrations data. They should be validated against real students before fieldwork, through the design-studio students and the Student AI Group. Validation confirms the barriers are real and catches any that were missed.

8.3 The advising-tool wave

Advising assignment and booking, the aid impact shown at the moment of dropping a class, and consortium-aid confirmation cannot be tested until the district advising tool is in place. These scenarios are flagged in the bank and held for a second wave. The data-governance concern around that tool belongs to Domain 1.

8.4 Tester capacity

Reaching about 5,000 task-runs requires the human half staffed across the ten colleges, one scenario per tester at a time. The AI half can begin on the public paths immediately. One college representative per campus, to assign scenarios and confirm coverage, is the lightest workable structure.
