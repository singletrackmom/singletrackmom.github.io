# ARC Domain 5 · Student Support and Success
## Working meeting · Thursday, July 9, 2026 · 2:00 to 3:00 PM (Arizona) · Google Meet

**Goal:** turn the June 25 open action item (everyone helps with the usability study) into something concrete. By the end of this hour, every member knows exactly how to run one 15-minute test at their college and log it, so we start collecting real human data, not just AI runs.

**Audience:** Domain 5 members, the support-services staff and faculty across our colleges.

**How we run today:** short recap, one live demo, then you self-assign and go. Light and practical.

---

### 1. Welcome and where we are (5 min)
- Quick roll call by college.
- Since June 25 the study has grown into a bigger, more credible model we can take to the ARC steering committee. Same guardrails, more coverage. This meeting is where members plug in.

### 2. The study in one breath (5 min)
- We are walking the real student journey across our colleges to find where students get stuck, then turning those barriers into AI helpers that take routine work off staff plates.
- The unit of the study is simple: one persona, one college, one task. A tester becomes that student and tries to do one real thing, starting from scratch, never told where to go.
- Framing that matters: this makes your job easier, not smaller. The aim is to hand the repetitive, “let me get back to you in a few days” questions to a tool, so your time goes to the students who actually need a person. Nobody is being replaced.

### 3. Two kinds of testing, and why we need you (10 min)
- **Anything with no login, the AI does.** Finding public pages, reading policy, locating where a service lives on a college website. That runs across all 10 colleges without your time.
- **Anything behind a student login, only a real account can reach.** Registering for a class, checking financial aid and holds, add and drop and its aid impact, Canvas, transcripts, advising, offboarding. That is your part.
- You each have student-side access at your college that the AI does not. That is the whole reason we need committee members on this.

### 4. Live demo: one test, start to finish (10 min)
- Pick a college, become the assigned persona, run one task out loud: start from a plain search, get lost on purpose, narrate the friction.
- Then open the form and log it: the path taken, where it got hard, a severity score, one idea for where AI could help.
- The point to land: we record what the student actually did, not where they were supposed to go.

### 5. How you will do this (10 min)
- **The form assigns you.** Open the form, pick your college, and it tells you: here is who you are (your persona), here is your one task for this session, here is what done looks like. Nothing to design.
- **Stay in character, use your own student login, problems not destinations.** Start like a confused new student. Do not use insider knowledge.
- **Document as you go, then submit.** About 15 minutes. Every submission is one task and flows straight into our study sheet.
- **Run it more than once if you can.** Next time you can be a different persona at a different college; the form re-assigns you each time.
- If a service does not exist at your college, that is a finding too. Log "not offered here" and move on. Screenshot dead ends and error pages.

### 6. Self-assign and timeline (10 min)
- Everyone: do at least one 15-minute run at your college before our next meeting (about two weeks out).
- We will look at the first results together next time and start naming the top barriers.
- Access check: flag now if you cannot get a working student login at your college, so we solve it before you start.

### 7. Guardrails (5 min)
- No student PII. Personas are test profiles. Use a test or your own non-sensitive account. Initials only.
- Translate, do not standardize. Use your college's own names for services. We are never asking anyone to rename what they do.
- Nobody is being evaluated. We are testing the systems and the signage, not the staff.

### 8. Open discussion (5 min)
- Questions, concerns, login access, who wants a hand getting started.
