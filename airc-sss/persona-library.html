Persona library

Student journey study

← District barriers study overview

The 36 research personas the scaled study runs on, one persona per test, at one campus, on one task.

Personas are research instruments, not real people. No PII. Each persona is a designed set of barriers that makes a specific failure visible, and each maps to a no-PII test account. They have not yet been validated against real students, and no runs have been completed.

1. How a persona is used

Every test is one persona, one campus, one task. A tester, human or AI, opens the instrument and receives a brief stating who the persona is, what barriers the persona carries, the one thing the persona is trying to do, and the campus. The tester then attempts the task from a real felt need. The brief states a problem and never a destination, so the persona has to locate the service.

The library reflects who enrolls across the ten colleges: ESL and non-native speakers, veterans, autistic and ADHD students, food-insecure and first-generation students, working parents, returning adults, students who need Disability Resources, swirling multi-campus students, and dual-enrollment high schoolers. Variety is the purpose of the library. A page that works for a digitally fluent transfer student can still fail a phone-first, Spanish-dominant parent on a slow connection, and only a varied library surfaces that.

2. Generating a fresh persona on demand

For large unmoderated runs, a tester does not have to reuse a fixed persona. A fresh persona is generated by combining attribute pools, so each run probes a different intersection of barriers. The method is repeatable and it keeps the no-PII rule intact, because nothing in it is a real person. A short generator prompt can produce these briefs in batches for AI testers, drawing one value from each pool. The fixed library in section 3 is the core to be validated, and the generator extends it for breadth.

2.1 Draw the attributes

  • Age band: 17 to 19, 20 to 24, 25 to 34, 35 to 49, 50 and over.
  • Status: first generation, returning adult, veteran, international on an F-1 visa, DACA or Dreamer, dual enrollment, foster-care alumna, reentry.
  • Home language: English, Spanish, Arabic, Somali, Mandarin, Vietnamese, Korean, Japanese, other.
  • Access need: Disability Resources, autistic, ADHD, low bandwidth, phone only, after hours only.
  • Life pressure: food insecure, housing insecure, working parent, caregiver, full-time job.

2.2 Set the goal

  • Pick one goal that fits the draw: enroll, pay, plan, set up, fix, find support, transfer, graduate, or swirl.
  • Pick one base task from the taxonomy that serves that goal.
  • Assign one campus from the ten.

2.3 Write the brief

  • One paragraph stating who the persona is, the barriers the persona carries, the one thing the persona wants, and the campus.
  • No destination is named. The tester has to find the path.
  • The run is recorded in the capture form against a new Scenario ID.
