Initiative 2 · the scaled model

Persona library
36 students, one instrument each

The scaled study moves from three personas to a library. Every test is one persona, one campus, one task. A tester (human or AI) opens the tool and is told: this is who you are, these are your barriers, this is the one thing you are trying to do, at this campus. Then they attempt it from a real felt need. Problems, not destinations: the persona has to figure out where to go. The library below reflects who actually enrolls across the ten colleges, ESL and non-native speakers, veterans, autistic and ADHD students, food-insecure and first-generation students, working parents, returning adults, students who need Disability Resources, swirling multi-campus students, dual-enrollment high-schoolers, and more.

Personas are research instruments, not real people.
No PII. Each persona is a designed-in set of barriers that makes a specific failure visible. They are validated against real student input (design-studio students and the Student AI Group), and they map to no-PII test accounts. The point of the library is variety: a page that works for a digitally fluent transfer student can still fail a phone-first, Spanish-dominant parent on a slow connection, and only a varied library surfaces that.

Generate a fresh persona on demand So every test gets a slightly different student

For large unmoderated runs, a tester does not have to reuse a fixed persona. A fresh persona is generated by combining attribute pools, so each run probes a different intersection of barriers. The method is simple and repeatable, and it keeps the no-PII rule intact because nothing here is a real person.

Step 1 · draw attributes
  • Age band: 17 to 19, 20 to 24, 25 to 34, 35 to 49, 50+
  • Status: first-gen, returning adult, veteran, international (F-1), DACA/Dreamer, dual-enrollment, foster-alumna, reentry
  • Home language: English, Spanish, Arabic, Somali, Mandarin, Vietnamese, Korean, Japanese, other
  • Access need: DRS, autistic, ADHD, low-bandwidth, phone-only, after-hours-only
  • Life pressure: food-insecure, housing-insecure, working parent, caregiver, full-time job
Step 2 · set the goal
  • Pick one goal that fits the draw (enroll, pay, plan, set up, fix, find support, transfer, graduate, swirl)
  • Pick one base task from the taxonomy that serves that goal
  • Assign one campus from the ten
Step 3 · write the brief
  • One paragraph: who you are, your barriers, the one thing you want, the campus
  • No destination named. The tester must find the path
  • Record the run in the capture form against a new Scenario ID

A short generator prompt can produce these briefs in batches for AI testers, drawing one value from each pool above. The fixed library below is the validated core; the generator extends it for breadth.
