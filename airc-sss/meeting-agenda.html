An ARC tri-chair opens by helping the group hone in on focus and goals. With newer members joining, we get clear on what the domain should be prioritizing, then look at where the study stands, and close with the one concrete thing members can help with, which is the test form.

1. Agenda

  1. Welcome and quick names. A fast round, especially for anyone new to the committee.
  2. Focus and goals. The tri-chair opens by reining us in on what the domain should be prioritizing and what success looks like, including thoughts on training and on working with the people already doing training across the district. This sets direction and keeps scope tight.
  3. Why this domain exists, briefly. For anyone new: the ARC is working out how AI can genuinely help across Maricopa. Student Support and Success (Domain 5) covers the student journey from applying to graduating, and making services easier to find and use. The aim is to take routine, repetitive questions off staff so their time goes to the human work only they can do. No one is being replaced.
  4. Where the study stands. The service crosswalk is mapped across all ten colleges and the usability test form is built. Honest status: nothing has been tested yet.
  5. Carried over from prior meetings. The vendor advising tool: still no response from that team to set up a meeting or a rundown of what the platform and its admissions bot actually do. The staff training thread and the student AI-literacy and buy-in ideas are also still open, and both connect to the focus point above.
  6. Wrap and next steps. A quick recap and when we meet next.

2. The test form, what to do

This is the one concrete way to help. When you have about thirty minutes this week, or now if there is time, open the test plan, take the persona you are given, and try one real task on your own campus. Use your own student account (MEID, maricopa.edu), not your work login, so you see what a student sees. Note where you get stuck, and log each task in the form.

One task helps. Do as many as you like. Going into fall we would also welcome a volunteer per campus and one test-student account per college. All of it is optional, and AI testers can run some of the scenarios in parallel.

The short instructions and the task list live on their own page, so we do not read through them in the meeting. That page also carries the link for logging what you find. Open the test plan
