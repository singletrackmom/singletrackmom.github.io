An ARC tri-chair opens the session on focus and goals. With newer members joining, the group first establishes what the domain should be prioritizing, then reviews where the study stands, and closes on the task members can take up, which is the test form.

1. Agenda

  1. Welcome and introductions, for members new to the committee.
  2. Focus and goals. The tri-chair sets what the domain should be prioritizing and what success looks like, including training and coordination with the people already delivering training across the district. This sets direction and keeps scope tight.
  3. The purpose of the domain, in brief. The ARC is establishing where AI can help across Maricopa. Student Support and Success (Domain 5) covers the student journey from application to graduation, and the findability and usability of services along it. The aim is to take routine, repetitive questions off staff so their time goes to the work only a person can do. No one is replaced.
  4. Where the study stands. The service crosswalk is mapped across all ten colleges and the usability test form is built. Nothing has been tested yet.
  5. Carried over from prior meetings. The vendor advising tool: no response yet from that team to a request for a meeting or a walkthrough of what the platform and its admissions bot do. The staff training thread and the student AI-literacy and buy-in items remain open, and both connect to the focus item above.
  6. Close and next steps, including the next meeting date.

2. The test form

The test form is the concrete task members can take up. A run takes roughly fifteen minutes: open the test plan, take the assigned persona, and attempt one real task at the member’s own campus. Testers use their student account (MEID, maricopa.edu) rather than the work login, so that they see what a student sees, and a login reaches only its own college’s systems, which is why each member covers the campus they can reach. Each task is logged in the form, including the point at which the tester got stuck.

One task is a useful contribution, and members take as many as their capacity allows. Going into fall, the study needs one volunteer per campus, each testing the campus whose systems that member can already reach. Participation is optional, and AI testers run the same scenarios in parallel to extend coverage.
