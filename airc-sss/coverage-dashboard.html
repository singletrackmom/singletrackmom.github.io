Sample data. Every coverage figure, severity rating, and barrier on this page is a placeholder seeded to show what the dashboard looks like once it is populated. None of it is a finding. No run has been completed, and nothing has been tested. In use, the page reads the scenario-bank tracking sheet, and the tables fill in as testers log real results.

Coverage is the share of a college’s or a stage’s scenarios that have a completed run. The dashboard exists so that the co-chairs and the steering committee can see progress and gaps without reading the tracking sheet row by row.

How severity is scored

Severity is rated 0 to 4, following Nielsen: 0 not a usability problem, 1 cosmetic, 2 minor, 3 major, 4 catastrophic (a student cannot complete the task and would plausibly give up). A rating combines three factors: frequency, meaning how many students would meet the barrier; impact, meaning how hard it is to overcome once met; and persistence, meaning whether a student learns around it once or hits it again every time. Two raters score each finding independently and disagreements are adjudicated in a short review.

Pilots are prioritized on severity against reach, not on severity alone. Where the fix is automation, it takes routine, repetitive work off advising and financial aid staff so their time goes to the students who need a person. No one is replaced.

Scenarios held for a later wave

Some scenarios cannot be run until the district advising tool is in place: find or get assigned an advisor, book an advising appointment, see the aid impact at the moment of dropping a class, and confirm consortium aid at a second college. They are flagged in the bank, counted separately above, and excluded from the runs that can start now.
