Maricopa AI Resource Center · Domain 5

Student Support & Success
Using AI to build connection, not replace it

The Student Support & Success domain spans all 10 Maricopa colleges and the roughly 143,000 students the district serves each year. Our premise comes straight from the research: students who feel connected to their college and supported by its services are far more likely to persist to completion. The services largely exist. Students just cannot always find them. This domain is four coordinated initiatives that map what exists, diagnose where students hit barriers, baseline who is actually being reached, and turn that into prioritized, human-in-the-loop AI pilots. The usability study is one of the four, not the whole of it.

Why this work exists Connection and support drive persistence

Community college completion rates are low, and the research is consistent about why. Fewer than six in ten community college students return for a second year at the same institution, and part-time students, the majority of community college enrollment, persist to a second fall at only 53 percent. The common thread is not ability. It is that students did not feel connected enough, or supported enough, to push through when life got hard. Decades of work from Astin and Tinto through recent national studies point the same direction: belonging and service connection predict whether a student stays. That gap is closable, and it is what Domain 5 exists to close.

Two problems shape the work. First, students often do not recognize their situation as something a service was built for: a student behind because of a disability may never think to call Disability Resources; a first-generation student falling behind may not know advising is even allowed to them. Second, the staff who run those services would benefit from AI that streamlines their day, but caseloads are full and the time to learn new tools is scarce. Our guardrail is simple. If a tool shrinks human contact instead of expanding it, we do not recommend it. Success looks like more student contact, not less.

Each of the 10 colleges is independently accredited with its own programs and names. Nothing here asks colleges to align or rename. Our crosswalk is a translation layer, not a standardization effort.

Domain scope Staying in our lane (per the ARC Domain Groups Overview)

In scope (ours)

  • The student journey, application to graduation
  • Advising, counseling, financial aid, enrollment, registration
  • Retention, persistence, completion
  • Well-being & engagement outside the classroom
  • Making student-facing services accessible & equitable

Out of scope (other domains)

  • Classroom instruction, curriculum, pedagogy, faculty tools/training → Domain 2
  • Governance, security, policy → Domain 1
  • Employee training & Institutional Research → Domain 3
  • Credentials & badges → Domain 4

One clarification that matters: a faculty member who checks in on a struggling student or points them to a resource outside the classroom is doing student-support work. That is in our lane. The question is not who connects, but whether the connection happens outside the instructional relationship.
