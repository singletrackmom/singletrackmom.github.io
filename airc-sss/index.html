Maricopa AI Resource Center · Domain 5

Student Support & Success
Using AI to build connection, not replace it

The Student Support & Success domain explores how AI can responsibly enhance the non-classroom services that shape student retention, progression, and well-being, including advising, counseling, financial aid, registration, and student life. The goal is to make these services more accessible, responsive, and equitable across all 10 Maricopa colleges, while keeping human connection and student agency at the center. Students help shape the work directly, because they understand best where services fall short.

The work runs as three coordinated initiatives: map the services and baseline who uses them, diagnose where students hit barriers, and turn that into prioritized, human-centered AI pilots, all grounded in the evidence on why students leave.

Why this work exists Connection and support drive persistence

Community college completion rates are low, and the research is consistent about why. Fewer than six in ten community college students return for a second year at the same institution, and part-time students, the majority of community college enrollment, persist to a second fall at only 53 percent. The common thread is not ability. It is that students did not feel connected enough, or supported enough, to continue when circumstances became difficult. Decades of work from Astin and Tinto through recent national studies point the same direction: belonging and service connection predict whether a student stays. That gap is closable, and it is what Domain 5 exists to close.

Two problems shape the work. First, students often do not recognize their situation as something a service was built for: a student with a disability such as ADHD may not realize Disability Resources and Services is there for them; a first-generation student falling behind may not know advising is even available to them. Second, the staff who run those services would benefit from AI that streamlines their day, but caseloads are full and the time to learn new tools is scarce. Our guardrail is simple. If a tool reduces human contact instead of expanding it, we do not recommend it. Success looks like more student contact, not less.

This is why the work has to be tested the way students actually experience it, not the way the institution imagines it. An application a college believes is an 8-step process can prove to be a 30-step experience once someone walks it end to end. We do not know what we cannot see, and we see it only from the student’s perspective.

Each of the 10 colleges is independently accredited with its own programs and names. Nothing here asks any college to rename, align, or change anything. We are simply inventorying which student-facing services exist and how many students use each one.

Domain scope Staying in our lane (per the ARC Domain Groups Overview)

In scope (ours)

  • The student journey from application to graduation: advising, financial aid, enrollment, and registration
  • Student retention, persistence, and goal completion
  • Student well-being, personal development, and engagement outside the classroom, including student life and extracurriculars
  • Making student-facing services more accessible, responsive, and equitable across the district

Out of scope (other domains)

  • Classroom instruction, curriculum, pedagogy, or faculty tools and training → Domain 2
  • Governance, security, and policy → Domain 1
  • Employee development and training → Domain 3
  • AI credentials and badges → Domain 4

One clarification that matters: a faculty member who checks in on a struggling student or points them to a resource outside the classroom is doing student-support work. That is in our lane. The question is not who connects, but whether the connection happens outside the instructional relationship.

How we are organized Proposed working subcommittees

We work on one question only: where can AI improve student support and success. Not every barrier students face is ours to solve, and we remain honest about that line. To move from plan to action, we propose four working subcommittees, each with a lead and a first deliverable. Members sign up for one or two, and students help drive the work.

Subcommittee 1 · Initiative 1

Service inventory & baseline

Finish the inventory of student-facing services across the 10 colleges and gather the usage baseline through a committee member and their Dean of Student Life. Deliverable: a verified inventory and current-state usage numbers.

Subcommittee 2 · Initiative 2

UX study & student testers

Run the student-journey personas, recruit and train students to run them, stand up the tester form, and dry-run at one college. Michelle keeps refining the study itself. Deliverable: a working study and a student tester crew.

Subcommittee 3 · Initiative 3, first pass

Solutions & quick wins

Turn the most severe barriers from the study into the lightest AI fix that works: adopt a tool we already own, configure something we license, or flag a build. Rank by how many students it reaches and pursue the quick wins first. Deliverable: a prioritized AI-opportunity list with the easy wins already moving.

Subcommittee 4 · Initiative 3, second pass

AI agents & workflows

For the barriers no existing tool solves, scope the custom AI agents or workflows worth building into systems we already run. Students help prototype and build them, as real applied work. Deliverable: pilot scope for one or two builds, each keeping people in control.

Liaison · cross-domain

Student AI training

A couple of our members also sit on the group developing student AI training, so the two efforts reinforce each other. Students who know how to use AI well are more likely to succeed, so this is squarely in our interest. We support that work and bring the student-support perspective to it; we do not own it.

Students are part of this end to end: they run the journey study as testers and help prototype and build the pilots at the end. It is real, resume-worthy applied work, exactly the kind of hands-on student involvement this domain is built around. We want them paid for it, so a near-term action is to pursue funding: a grant, or an AmeriCorps proposal (through Arizona Serve) that could support several students with a stipend and an education award. We will contact the GCC grant writer for assistance.

The guardrail holds across all four: a recommendation counts only if it frees human time, protects human connection where it matters, and stays inside student support and success. Next move: name a lead for each subcommittee, name two liaisons to the student AI training group, confirm one representative per college, and start the student-funding ask (a grant or AmeriCorps) with the GCC grant writer.
