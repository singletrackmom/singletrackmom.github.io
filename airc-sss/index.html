Maricopa AI Resource Center · Domain 5

Student Support & Success
Using AI to build connection, not replace it

The Student Support & Success domain explores how AI can responsibly enhance the non-classroom services that shape student retention, progression, and well-being, including advising, counseling, financial aid, registration, and student life. The goal is to make these services more accessible, responsive, and equitable across all 10 Maricopa colleges, while keeping human connection and student agency at the center. Students help shape the work directly, because they know best where services fall short.

The work runs as four coordinated initiatives: map the services and baseline who uses them, diagnose where students hit barriers, understand why students leave, and turn that into prioritized, human-in-the-loop AI pilots. We map and baseline this summer, stand up the first pilots through the fall, and target a measurable jump in student success by spring.

Why this work exists Connection and support drive persistence

Community college completion rates are low, and the research is consistent about why. Fewer than six in ten community college students return for a second year at the same institution, and part-time students, the majority of community college enrollment, persist to a second fall at only 53 percent. The common thread is not ability. It is that students did not feel connected enough, or supported enough, to push through when life got hard. Decades of work from Astin and Tinto through recent national studies point the same direction: belonging and service connection predict whether a student stays. That gap is closable, and it is what Domain 5 exists to close.

Two problems shape the work. First, students often do not recognize their situation as something a service was built for: a student with a disability like ADHD may not realize Disability Resources and Services is there for them; a first-generation student falling behind may not know advising is even available to them. Second, the staff who run those services would benefit from AI that streamlines their day, but caseloads are full and the time to learn new tools is scarce. Our guardrail is simple. If a tool shrinks human contact instead of expanding it, we do not recommend it. Success looks like more student contact, not less.

This is why the work has to be tested the way students actually experience it, not the way the institution imagines it. An application a college believes is an 8-step process can prove to be a 30-step experience once someone walks it end to end. We do not know what we cannot see, and we only see it from the student’s seat.

Each of the 10 colleges is independently accredited with its own programs and names. Nothing here asks colleges to align or rename. Our crosswalk is a translation layer, not a standardization effort.

Domain scope Staying in our lane (per the ARC Domain Groups Overview)

In scope (ours)

  • The student journey from application to graduation: advising, financial aid, enrollment, and registration
  • Student retention, persistence, and goal completion
  • Student well-being, personal development, and engagement outside the classroom, including student life and extracurriculars
  • Making student-facing services more accessible, responsive, and equitable across the district

Out of scope (other domains)

  • Classroom instruction, curriculum, pedagogy, or faculty tools and training → Domain 2
  • Governance, security, and policy → Domain 1
  • Employee development and training → Domain 3
  • AI credentials and badges → Domain 4

One clarification that matters: a faculty member who checks in on a struggling student or points them to a resource outside the classroom is doing student-support work. That is in our lane. The question is not who connects, but whether the connection happens outside the instructional relationship.
