The approach Why this is real UX research, not a survey

Method

Problems, not destinations

We never tell a persona where to go. They get a real felt need ("I'm failing math," "I need to register") and have to find the path themselves. The gap between self-serve and giving up to call a human is the barrier data.

Reality

Swirling built in

Two personas enroll at a second college — a second application, residency re-proof, consortium aid, making credits count back home. Maricopa is not "one Maricopa."

Evidence

Grounded in real data

Primary research across all 10 college sites, the GCC Faculty Senate Frustrations Survey (83 barriers, 4.5/5), and Rio Salado Student Senate notes — all cross-walked into the tasks.

Outcome

Barriers → AI priorities

Every documented barrier maps to a proposed, human-in-the-loop AI fix in the AI Opportunity Map, ranked by impact on student success.

The personas Three stress-tested students · mapped to Test Student 1–3

Built with real quirks so the study breaks where students actually break. Drafts here are pending validation with real students (design-studio students and a Student AI Group via student government).

Test Student 1

Marisol Reyes · 19

First-gen · pre-Nursing / CTE · works ~25 hrs/wk
ContextLives with mom and younger siblings; Spanish at home; navigates English institutional jargon slowly. Pell-dependent — one missed step can end her semester. Does almost everything on her phone.
Quirks
  • Screenshots everything; asks her cousin before she asks the college.
  • Avoids phone calls (anxiety) — wants text or chat.
  • Rarely checks her Maricopa email; won't ask a "dumb" question.
Breaks atFAFSA verification holds, the word "prerequisite," developmental-math placement, DUO without a reliable phone, English-only pages, transportation. Swirls for a CNA course offered elsewhere.
Test Student 2

Darnell Carter · 34

Veteran · Business AA → ASU transfer · full-time job + 2 kids
ContextHas old credits from years ago. Studies 9pm–midnight. Capable and impatient; expects systems to work like consumer apps and will self-advise rather than wait.
Quirks
  • Power-skims; abandons any process that takes more than ~10 minutes.
  • Keeps his own planning spreadsheet; emails in bullet points.
  • GI Bill makes every drop/refund decision higher-stakes.
Breaks atUn-evaluated transcripts, prerequisite blocks from self-enrolling, veteran enrollment-certification lag, "if I drop, do I owe money or lose my GI Bill?" Swirls for an evening/online course his college doesn't offer this term.
Test Student 3

Alex Nguyen · 18

Exploring / digital media · straight from high school
ContextDigitally fluent but institutionally naive — assumes everything works like an app. Undecided major. ADHD/anxiety; an executive-function dip mid-semester is likely.
Quirks
  • Ignores email; lives in Canvas notifications and Discord.
  • Does everything at 11pm; ghosts the moment something is confusing.
  • Motivated by belonging — clubs, esports, finding their people.
Breaks atDoesn't know who their advisor is, can't tell counseling from advising, Field-of-Interest confusion, an opaque accommodations process (doesn't know the word), Canvas onboarding, and saving work before the account closes. Swirls for a media course offered only at another campus.

Initiatives & working groups ARC model — real working groups with deliverables, not just meetings

P1 · Map the landscape — tool inventory + service crosswalk (each function's local name at each college). Translate, don't standardize.
P2 · Student Journey UX Study — personas live the journey at all 10 colleges; capture barriers + severity; includes swirling, Canvas, and graduation offboarding.
P3 · Usage & workflow baseline — who actually uses each service; how departments think students find them vs. how they really do. cross-domain: IR / Domain 3
P4 · AI recommendations & pilots — turn barriers into prioritized, human-in-the-loop AI pilots; "I am AI" buy-in.
Salesforce Discovery — what the un-purchased admissions bot does, whether it can avoid collecting student data, and one-time cross-campus ID/residency verification for swirling students. cross-domain: Governance / Domain 1
Student AI literacy & course-design barriers — we contribute the student-barrier lens (incl. a Canvas/OSCQR course-barrier review); we do not own it. cross-domain: Teaching & Learning / Domain 2

Our lane: the non-classroom student journey (advising, counseling, financial aid, enrollment, registration, student life, well-being, retention). Classroom/curriculum/faculty-tools work belongs to other domains; we join their groups and bring the student voice.

Domain scope Staying in our lane (per the ARC Domain Groups Overview)

In scope (ours)

  • The student journey, application → graduation
  • Advising, counseling, financial aid, enrollment, registration
  • Retention, persistence, completion
  • Well-being & engagement outside the classroom
  • Making student-facing services accessible & equitable

Out of scope (other domains)

  • Classroom instruction, curriculum, pedagogy, faculty tools/training → Domain 2
  • Governance, security, policy → Domain 1
  • Employee training & Institutional Research → Domain 3
  • Credentials & badges → Domain 4
