What this is

The purpose of the study and the method it uses.

Students rarely leave because a policy was wrong. They leave because they could not find an advisor, could not tell what dropping one class would do to their financial aid, or could not get a printer to work the day a paper was due. Those barriers are invisible in aggregate data and observable within minutes of walking the journey.

The study therefore walks the journey. A tester, human or AI, is given a persona, a campus, and one thing that persona needs, and is never told where to go, because a student is never told where to go. What breaks, and how badly, is logged and scored. The result is a map of where the district loses students, ranked by how many students each barrier touches, with a proposed fix attached to each one: work an AI can absorb, or work that belongs to a person.

The rules this study runs on

Four constraints govern the design of the study.

  • Problems, not destinations. A tester is given a need, never an office. A tester who cannot find advising has recorded a finding.
  • Translate, do not standardize. Every college keeps its own names and programs. The study maps them to one another and asks no college to align.
  • No student data. Runs are recorded against a scenario identifier and initials, never a student record. Any AI option that would collect student data is excluded before it is evaluated, so the preferred path is building data-sovereign tools rather than buying data-collecting ones.
  • No one is replaced. Automation takes the repetitive factual questions off advising and financial aid staff so their hours go to the students with real problems. Human contact is the measure the study is trying to raise.

Who runs the tests

The domain membership is the instrument.

Every district employee holds a Maricopa account, but access to student-facing systems is scoped to the employee’s own college. No individual can reach all ten. The Student Support and Success domain draws its members from all ten colleges, which is precisely what makes district-wide coverage possible: each member runs scenarios on the campus whose systems that member can actually reach. Students test on their own real accounts.

A volunteer takes one scenario in one sitting, roughly fifteen minutes. The low burden is deliberate, because volunteer capacity is the real constraint on a study of this size. AI testers run the same scenarios in parallel, which is how coverage extends beyond what volunteers can carry.

Known constraints

Conditions that bound what the study can complete, stated rather than absorbed.

  • The district advising tool is not yet accessible to the domain. Scenarios that depend on it (advising assignment and booking, aid impact at drop, consortium aid) are deferred to a second wave, to be run during or after that tool’s pilot. This is a stated limit on completeness, not an omission.
  • Persona validation with students has not been arranged. Design-studio students and the Student AI Group are possible routes in the fall, and no student capacity is assumed until it exists.
  • Coverage depends on volunteer participation across all ten colleges. One member per campus assigns scenarios and confirms coverage for that campus.

CopaMigo, the pilot that preceded the study

A tool built from the same premise, now positioned to be tested by it.

CopaMigo was built before this study was designed. The premise was the same one the study now sets out to evidence: students do not fail to use support because support is absent, they fail because they cannot name what they need or find who provides it. The persistence literature and the district’s own withdrawal data show students leaving without ever reaching a service, but no evidence existed at the level of the individual barrier. Rather than wait for it, the tool was built as a working hypothesis: a bilingual triage layer that takes a student’s plain-language problem and routes it to the correct human service, collecting no student data.

The study supplies what the tool has lacked. Barriers logged and severity-rated across ten colleges give CopaMigo an evidence base it was designed on faith, and the tool in turn gives the study a live candidate to evaluate against its own severity-by-reach ranking rather than a hypothetical one. Whether CopaMigo, a purchased alternative, or no tool at all is the right answer is a question the fieldwork should decide, and the same no-student-data line applies to every option.
