Nothing here reports findings. The study has not been run. This document describes how the fieldwork is organized, what is asked of each college, and who owns which piece.

1. Gathering the crosswalk and the usage baseline

The crosswalk requires two data points from each college for each service: the local name that college uses for the service, and roughly how many students it serves in a year. One contact, the service owner, answers those two questions for their own service. There is no long survey, no student-level reporting, and no audit.

1.1 The agent drafts the row, the owner corrects it

The AI-agent walkthrough reads each college’s public site and drafts a first-pass crosswalk row for every service, so an owner who opens the intake finds the row mostly filled in. The owner is not building a grid from scratch. The owner is checking one row, correcting the local name where the draft is wrong, and adding an approximate annual figure. That reduces the task from about an hour to a few minutes, which is what makes it feasible for a busy advising or financial aid office.

1.2 The usage baseline is the before-measure

Gathering usage now, in Phase 1, sets up a pre and post impact evaluation. For each service two figures are captured: how many students use it today (the per-service count, which comes from the department, because Institutional Research does not hold it) and the college’s total enrollment (public, from IPEDS and the college fact books). Together those are the baseline. About a year after any AI tools and processes launch, the same measures are taken again at the same services to establish whether more students are reaching them. The whole loop stays inside the no-student-data line: counts and aggregates only, no PII.

The intake never asks for names, records, or anything that identifies a student. The only number requested is roughly how many students a service serves in a year, a planning figure the office already holds or can estimate. An approximate count is sufficient, and where an office cannot share a number, the local-name correction alone is still useful.

2. The delegation plan

Domain 5 has about ten members, many of them advisors, financial aid specialists, and other support staff with full day jobs, and the committee rarely convenes everyone at once. The work is therefore divided into small subcommittees, each with one lead and a first deliverable small enough to complete without a coordinating meeting. Members contribute asynchronously, in units of work they own, on their own schedule, so the study advances between meetings rather than depending on them.

The same membership is what makes coverage possible. Every district employee holds a Maricopa account, but no individual has access to the student-facing systems of all ten colleges, because access is scoped to a person’s own college. Domain 5 draws its members from all ten, so the domain membership constitutes the instrument: each member can test and confirm the campus whose systems that member can actually reach. Volunteers take a single scenario in a single sitting, roughly fifteen minutes, which is a deliberate response to volunteer capacity, and AI testers run the same scenarios in parallel to extend coverage beyond what volunteers can carry. No fixed tester count is set. The district advising tool (Salesforce) is not yet accessible to the domain, so scenarios that depend on it are deferred to a second wave, to be run during or after that tool’s pilot.

2.1 The design returns time to support staff

Every subcommittee is staffed by the people who do student support, and the work is aimed at removing routine, repetitive load from them. They are the experts on where students get stuck and which service answers which question, and their input is what would let any future tool route a student to the right person. The study exists to return time to these offices. No one is replaced.

3. The intake letter

A committee member copies the text below, fills in the bracketed fields, and sends it. It requests two data points, the local service name and an approximate annual headcount. It states that the study is about helping students find what already exists and removing routine work from the office, and that no student data is collected. The reply should take a few minutes.

3.1 The text to send

Hi [first name],

I am reaching out from the Student Support and Success domain (Domain 5) of the district AI Resource Center. We are putting together a simple map of the student support services across all ten colleges so that students can find the help that already exists on every campus. The goal is to make it easier for a student to reach the right office, and to take some of the routine “where do I go for this” questions off your team so your time goes to the students who really need you.

This is not an audit, and it is not about replacing anyone. It is the opposite. The better we map what you offer, the more visible your service becomes and the fewer students fall through the cracks before they reach you.

I need two quick things about [service name] at [college], and you are the right person to confirm them.

  1. Local name. What do you officially call this service on your campus? We have a draft from your public site, so you may just be confirming or correcting it.
  2. Rough annual reach. About how many students does it serve in a year? A ballpark is fine.

That is the whole ask. We are not collecting student names, records, or personal data, only the service name and an approximate count. If a number is hard to pin down, the name alone still helps. You can reply to this email with something as short as the local name, the approximate students served per year, and anything the draft got wrong.

Thank you for the few minutes. I am happy to answer questions, and glad to share the finished map with you when it is done.

Best,
[your name]
[your title], [your college]
Student Support and Success, Domain 5, AI Resource Center

No student PII is requested or collected anywhere in this intake. The only data points are a service’s local name and an approximate annual headcount. For committee use.
