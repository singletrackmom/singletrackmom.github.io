## S042 · P41 Jasmine Williams · CGCC · Life gets hard
**Task:** Find food or a food pantry on campus.
**Suspected barrier (bank):** Basic-needs hub exists but unadvertised, eligibility unstated.
**Persona constraints:** 24, single working parent, food insecure, drives 35 minutes, does not know basic-needs help exists, no margin for a wasted trip.

- **Outcome:** found
- **Path:** searched the felt need, "free food for students." CGCC's own Food Resources page is result 1.
- **Why it worked, and this is the important part:** the page text reads *"CGCC's food pantry is called the Coyote Cupboard."* Both the plain word and the local brand name sit in the same sentence, so the plain-need search matches even though the service has a mascot name. **The branded name did not block the search because the page translates itself.**
- **Where it broke, minor:** the Basic Needs landing page opens with a statistic (59 percent of students experience basic-needs insecurity) before it says how to get food. Hours and locations are one more click down. Emergency Support states *"GPA requirements vary based on funding,"* which is vague enough that an anxious student may read it as "I probably do not qualify."
- **Severity candidate:** 1
- **AI opportunity:** none needed for findability here. **This page is the working pattern the study should point at.** The recommendation is to reproduce the self-translating sentence wherever a service carries a brand-only name.

## S064 · P03 Destiny Carter · SMCC · Pay for it
**Task:** Get emergency aid fast during a crisis.
**Suspected barrier (bank):** Emergency aid exists but unfindable, slow turnaround, no clear ask.
**Persona constraints:** 19, foster-care alumna, no family safety net, housing insecure, distrusts institutions, needs an answer now.

- **Outcome:** found
- **Path:** searched "I need emergency money help now." Result 1 is SMCC's Verna McClain Student Emergency Fund, and the snippet itself carries the answer: *"apply now and you can receive up to $500 within 48 hours."*
- **What this college does better than any other run so far:** the amount and the turnaround are both visible before the student clicks anything. For a student who distrusts institutions, that removes the reason to give up.
- **Where it broke, one. CONFIRMED ON THE PAGE 25 Aug, and this is the finding.** The page opens in large type with *"If you have an emergency, apply now and you can receive up to $500 within 48 hours!"* Below the eligibility list it says: ***"The Application Cycle is currently closed, please check back soon!"***

  Destiny reads the promise, reads the eligibility rules, and only then learns she cannot apply. Google's result carries the promise and truncates before the closure, so the search never warns her either.

- **Where it broke, two. Also confirmed, and it matters more for this persona.** Eligibility states: *"Requests cannot be used to pay off previous debts, tuition, or rent."* Destiny is housing-insecure. **Even with the cycle open, this fund does not cover her stated need,** and nothing earlier in the path says so. She must also be enrolled in at least three credit hours.

- **Where it broke, three. Confirmed by the breadcrumb.** The page sits at **Home / About / Foundation and Development / Verna McClain Student Emergency Fund**. It is filed in the donor and advancement section of the site, not in student services. That explains the search results: of ten pages carrying the fund's name, most are Donations, Annual Reception, Annual Fundraiser, Giving to SMCC, and Heart of the Mountain. **The fund is catalogued as a fundraising asset and reached as one.**

- **Where it broke, four.** Two sponsored private-loan results ran alongside the college results, a loan comparison site and Sallie Mae. A nineteen-year-old in crisis is served loan advertising beside her college's own free assistance.

- **Severity candidate:** 3
- **AI opportunity, and this is the strongest in the set.** Every failure above is a routing and state problem over content that already exists. An assistant asked "I need emergency money" can state the cycle status before the student invests hope, say plainly that rent is excluded and route her to Housing and Shelter instead, reach the fund without her having to pass through the donor section, and keep the loan advertising out of the path. **Nothing is renamed and no page is rebuilt.** The fund keeps its name, its section, and its donor pages.

## Batch 2, additional scenarios from the bank

**Method note for this batch:** these are **search-layer runs**, scoped to each college's own domain with a persona-realistic felt-need query. Where a result was consequential the page was opened; where it was not, the cell is marked search-layer and should not be quoted as a page-content claim. ### Two cross-cutting observations from this batch

**Campus tech answers live on library subdomains.** Confirmed at Glendale (`lib.gccaz.edu` outranking the college tech FAQ), Scottsdale (`library.scottsdalecc.edu` for printing and wi-fi), and Mesa (Textbooks and Tech under the library). > ### And look at where ADHD *does* appear
> Faculty referral pages. Teacher-training syllabi. Clinical ebooks. External provider lists. Alumni marketing. News items. **Every one of those is written *about* students with ADHD rather than *to* them.** The institutions have plenty of language for this condition. Almost none of it is addressed to the person who has it.

## The mechanism, and the exceptions that prove it

The three cases where a felt-need query **succeeded** explain the rule:

- **Scottsdale food.** Artie's FIT Market ranked first on the hungry query because its page says *"our **hungry** students."*
- **Chandler-Gilbert food.** Ranked because the page says *"CGCC's **food pantry** is called the Coyote Cupboard."*
- **Estrella Mountain disability.** Ranked because they publish a page titled **ADD/ADHD**.

> **Pages that contain the student's word rank on the student's query. Pages that contain only the service's name do not.**

That single sentence explains nearly every failure in this document: the MEID page that answers "MEID" but not "how do I log in the first time"; the DRS pages that say "accommodations" but never "ADHD"; the counselling FAQ that says "kept private" but never "confidential"; the food pantries that say "Coyote Cupboard" and "Gecko Grocery" but never "hungry."

## Why this matters more than any single barrier

**The study's own method has testers start from a felt need.** The run sheet says so: *"Begin each task the way the student would... Write down the exact first search terms they'd type."* So the study is testing precisely the failure mode, which is the right design.

And the students who only possess felt-need vocabulary are the study's own equity population: first-generation, English-learner, newcomer, and the fifteen personas whose disability is suspected or undisclosed. **A student who already knows the institution's words finds the service everywhere. A student who only knows what is wrong finds it almost nowhere.**

> **One detail that illustrates it exactly.** Estrella Mountain's **Mariposa Literary Review** surfaced as a top result for three separate crisis queries: unsafe, hungry, and cannot pay rent. It is a student literary magazine. It ranks because it is the only place on that domain where anyone writes about being broke, frightened, and hungry **in the first person.** The services exist. The language of need exists. They are in different documents.

## What this tells the committee to build

This is not a content-writing project and not a renaming project. It is a **vocabulary-to-service mapping**, and the evidence says exactly what it must contain:

1. **Felt-need phrases to service names.** "I'm hungry" to Coyote Cupboard, Gecko Grocery, Mesa Market, Artie's FIT Market, Puma Pantry, The Village.
2. **Condition names to services.** ADHD, anxiety, dyslexia, autism to Disability Resources. Estrella Mountain has already proved this works.
3. **Plain words to institutional terms.** Confidential to private, log in to MEID, rent to emergency assistance.

Three colleges have already solved a piece of it by accident, simply by writing a student word into a page. That is the cheapest possible pilot: **take the felt-need phrases this study collected, and check them against each college's own search.** No new content, no renaming, no site rebuild.

## A method note for the run sheet: click, never retype

Three URLs transcribed from search-result breadcrumbs returned 404 during this batch. The display drops path segments: Scottsdale's F-1 page shows as `scottsdalecc.edu › international › how-maintain-your-f-1-status` but actually lives at `/students/international/how-maintain-your-f-1-status`.

**A tester who retypes a URL from a search result will record a broken link that is not broken.** Volunteers should always click through, and any 404 in a log should note whether the tester clicked or typed. This belongs in the instructions before fieldwork starts.

## Infrastructure already deployed on every college site

Observed directly on GateWay, South Mountain, and Mesa, and consistent with the instructions on the Chandler-Gilbert and GateWay Spanish hubs:

## What this batch says

**Four of seven ran clean once the pages were opened**: CGCC food, SMCC disbursement, GateWay EMT, MCC verification. All four work for the same reason. The page states the plain fact in plain words, on the page, where the student is.

**Method note, and it changed the results.** Three findings in the first pass were produced by reading Google's summary of a page instead of opening it. Two of those collapsed on inspection: GateWay states its cost, length, location, and aid ineligibility, and Mesa's verification page is one of the better pages in the study. **All three errors ran the same direction, overstating the barrier.** Every run above was re-checked in a browser; the ones that survived, survived on screen.

**Test both search surfaces, and say which one failed.** The single clearest result of this batch: Sofia Ramirez fails on Google and succeeds on Phoenix College's own site search, using identical words. Any finding logged as "the student could not find it" is incomplete until it names the surface. This belongs in the run sheet before the volunteer testers start.

**The three findings that survived browser verification.**

1. **A state problem at SMCC.** The emergency fund page promises $500 within 48 hours in large type and says the application cycle is closed further down, and excludes rent, which is the persona's actual need. It is filed under Foundation and Development, the donor section, not student services. Severity 3, and the strongest AI case in the set.
2. **A vocabulary gap at PVCC.** Counseling answers Jordan's question with the words *kept private*. He searches *confidential*. The answer is one word away and he never reaches it. Severity 2.

None is a missing service. None is fixed by renaming a department. All three are answerable over content that already exists, on surfaces the district has already deployed.
