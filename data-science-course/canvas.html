Advanced Data Science

GRADUATE · 3 CREDITS · 15-WEEK ONLINE COURSE · ASSESSED THROUGH PROFESSIONAL SIMULATIONS

Plan about 9 hours per week. One real dataset of your choosing runs the length of the course. Every module moves from a recorded introduction and an interactive model, through a low-stakes exercise, to a professional simulation you defend on your own data. Every link below is live.

Course learning outcomes

  • Acquire data from varied structured and unstructured sources (files, databases, APIs, and the web), documenting provenance and access method.
  • Apply preprocessing strategies to complex, messy, and dynamic datasets: cleaning, missing-data handling, outlier treatment, and feature engineering.
  • Differentiate among machine-learning approaches, regression, classification, and clustering, and select a method appropriate to a problem and its data.
  • Evaluate the predictive power and validity of statistical and machine-learning methods using resampling, appropriate metrics, and the bias-variance trade-off.
  • Communicate findings to a non-technical decision-maker through sound visualization and a defensible data narrative.
  • Assess the ethical dimensions of a data problem using a recognized governance framework.
  • Construct a comprehensive data-science project from inception to presentation, and defend its decisions.

Modules

Module 1Acquisition and the reproducible workflowWeeks 1–2 · Module 1 · about 18 hours
Objective: acquire data from files, databases, APIs, and the web, with documented provenance, in a reproducible environment. Serves O1, O7. RSI: direct instruction, discussion, feedback.
▶ Instructor intro (record) + slide deck (build): the data-science lifecycle, why provenance and reproducibility are first-class, and the ethics of scraping.
◎ Interactive (formative): Select Star SQL (run SQL in-browser on real data)CC BY-SA / CC0; a REST API run-through with Hoppscotch against JSONPlaceholder; the toscrape sandbox for responsible scraping; Play with Docker for a reproducible environment.
  • Read: Software Carpentry, Version Control with Git (type-along)CC BY 4.0 · adapt; Data Carpentry, Databases & SQLCC BY · adapt; Ethical Web Scraping (robots.txt, rate limits, ToS)link
◆ Authentic assessment (simulation) — The Provenance Incident. A client ticket asks the learner to assemble metrics from four sources, including a live, rate-limited, paginated API whose schema silently changes mid-window. Deliverable: a reproducible ingestion pipeline, a datasheet-style provenance log for every field, and a memo noting the schema change and how it was detected. AI-resistant because the API is private and moving; the grade rests on interacting with it and documenting lineage only the student’s own run produced.
Module 2Preprocessing messy and dynamic dataWeeks 3–4 · Module 2 · about 18 hours
Objective: clean, handle missing data, treat outliers, and engineer features on messy data, with defended decisions. Serves O2. RSI: direct instruction, discussion, feedback.
▶ Instructor intro (record) + slide deck (build): cleaning as defensible decisions; MCAR/MAR/MNAR; why a transformation must be reversible and documented.
◎ Interactive (formative): Kaggle Learn: Data Cleaning and Feature Engineering (auto-graded notebooks)link; missingno in a notebook to see missingness patternsMIT · adapt; an outlier-influence explorable.
  • Read: Python Data Science Handbook Ch.3 (missing data, joins, grouping)text link, code adapt; NIST e-Handbook §1.3.5.17 (outlier detection)public domain
◆ Authentic assessment (simulation) — Recover the Signal. Each learner gets a personalized messy dataset generated from a clean process the instructor holds privately, corrupted per-student with MNAR missingness, unit inconsistencies, duplicates, and outliers of known distribution. Cleaning quality is scored numerically by how closely the recovered statistics match the hidden truth. Pasted generic solutions score poorly because the answer key is private to each student’s seed.
Module 3Exploratory analysis, visualization, and the stakeholder storyWeeks 5–6 · Module 3 · about 18 hours
Objective: explore data and communicate a defensible, honest narrative to a non-technical decision-maker. Serves O5, O2. RSI: direct instruction, discussion, feedback.
▶ Instructor intro (record) + slide deck (build): the difference between a chart and a decision a manager can act on; encoding, color, and the honest figure.
◎ Interactive (formative): Google Facets for interactive EDA in the notebook (sort features by distribution, surface missing and skewed values across thousands of points)Apache 2.0; the Data-to-Viz chart chooser and its catalog of misleading-figure pitfallsMIT; the Financial Times Visual Vocabulary (choose an encoding by communicative intent).
  • Read: Fundamentals of Data Visualization (Wilke), Ch. 4 color and Ch. 17 the principle of proportional ink, with the pitfalls of misleading axesCC BY-NC-ND · link; and a structured critique of a live stakeholder dashboard on Tableau Public and Our World in DataOWID CC BY.
◆ Authentic assessment (simulation) — The 10-Minute Executive Brief. A non-technical VP persona sends a real decision (“east side or west side, you have 48 hours and 5 minutes of my time”). The learner does EDA and delivers a one-page memo and a five-minute briefing, no jargon, every chart titled with its plain-language takeaway, uncertainty stated in words. Defended live against a skeptical executive who asks “what would change your mind?” AI prose collapses under unscripted follow-ups about this dataset.
Module 4Supervised learning: regression and classificationWeeks 7–8 · Module 4 · about 18 hours
Objective: differentiate regression, classification, and tree ensembles, and select a method that fits the problem and data. Serves O3, O4. RSI: direct instruction, group discussion, feedback.
▶ Instructor intro (record) + slide deck (build): choosing the problem type before the model; interpretation comes with the fit.
◎ Interactive (formative): MLU-Explain Linear Regression, Logistic Regression, and Random Forest (tree-count and accuracy sliders)link; TensorFlow PlaygroundApache 2.0; R2D3, A Visual Introduction to ML.
  • Read + run labs: ISLP Ch.3, 4, 8.1–8.2 with the Python lab notebooksfree to read · link; Harvard CS109A runnable notebooks (regression, classification, trees)MIT · adapt.
◆ Authentic assessment (simulation) — Private-Leaderboard Competition. A hosted competition with a hidden test set and a public/private leaderboard split on a client prediction task. The grade weights the private score, a public-versus-private gap analysis that rewards disciplined validation (a small drop is evidence of a candidate who did not overfit the public board), and a model-selection memo comparing at least three algorithm families with justification tied to the data. This is exactly how Stanford’s STATS 202 and DrivenData run graded competitions, and the hidden labels defeat copy-paste pipelines.
Module 5Rigorous model evaluationWeeks 9–10 · Module 5 · about 18 hours
Objective: evaluate predictive power with resampling, appropriate metrics, and the bias-variance trade-off, and quantify uncertainty. Serves O4. RSI: direct instruction, group discussion, feedback.
▶ Instructor intro (record) + slide deck (build): fitting versus generalizing; why we hold data back; honest metrics under class imbalance.
◎ Interactive (formative): MLU-Explain Cross-Validation, ROC & AUC (move the threshold, watch the confusion matrix), and Bias-Variancelink; Google MLCC Thresholding (imbalanced-data slider)CC BY 4.0; Interpreting Confidence Intervals for uncertainty on a metricCC BY.
  • Read + run labs: ISLP Ch.5 (resampling) with labslink; Elements of Statistical Learning Ch.7 (model assessment)link.
◆ Authentic assessment (simulation) — The Data-Incident Autopsy. A departing contractor left a “production-ready” model that scores 0.97 AUC and contains planted defects: target leakage, train/test contamination, a metric wrong for the class balance, and a subgroup on which performance secretly collapses. The learner audits it, redesigns the resampling, re-scores, decomposes bias and variance, and issues a go/no-go. Graded on which planted defects they catch, defects specific to a private artifact with no web answer. Includes a held-out private-test evaluation of the learner’s own project.
Module 6Unsupervised learning: clustering and PCAWeek 11 · Module 6 · about 9 hours
Objective: apply clustering and dimensionality reduction and defend the choices. Serves O3, O4. RSI: direct instruction, discussion, feedback.
▶ Instructor intro (record) + slide deck (build): structure without labels; how to argue for a number of clusters rather than eyeball it.
◎ Interactive (formative): Setosa PCA (drag points, watch components)link; Visualizing K-Means (step through Lloyd’s algorithm)link; How to Use t-SNE Effectively (perplexity sliders)CC BY.
  • Read + run labs: ISLP Ch.12 with labslink; Harvard CS109B clustering notebooksMIT · adapt.
◆ Authentic assessment (simulation) — How Many Segments, Really? A customer dataset with real structure, correlated noise, and non-spherical shapes. Marketing asks how many distinct groups exist and how to describe them. Judged the way segmentation is actually defended in practice, on cluster stability (bootstrap and consensus), interpretability, and a written argument for why this number of segments serves the marketing decision, since a real client never has a ground-truth number of clusters. Agreement with the generating structure is reported only as a secondary diagnostic, not the score.
Module 7Data and AI ethics and governanceWeek 12 · Module 7 · about 9 hours
Objective: assess bias, fairness, privacy, and governance using a recognized framework, applied to the learner’s own model. Serves O6, O4. RSI: facilitated group discussion, feedback.
▶ Instructor intro (record) + slide deck (build): ethics as an engineering requirement; the impossibility of satisfying every fairness metric at once.
◎ Interactive (formative): PAIR Measuring Fairness (threshold sliders show fairness metrics conflict)Apache 2.0; MLU-Explain Equality of Oddslink; Survival of the Best Fit (hiring-bias game)link; Aequitas bias-audit toolkitMIT; Google What-If ToolApache 2.0.
  • Read: Fairness and Machine Learning, intro + classificationlink; Data Feminism, Examine Power + Consider Contextopen access; NIST AI RMF (Govern, Map, Measure, Manage)public domain; the Data Science Code of Professional Conductlink.
◆ Authentic assessment (simulation) — The Model-Governance Review Board. A role-played governance board reviews the learner’s own audited model from the Data-Incident Autopsy, whose subgroup-performance table shows real disparate impact. Rotating roles: proposing data scientist, compliance officer, community representative. The learner completes a governance dossier, a Model Card and a Datasheet for the Dataset, maps the harms to the four NIST AI RMF functions (Govern, Map, Measure, Manage), and votes to approve, reject, or condition, then defends the vote live. Grounding the review in the student’s own model, and in the actual RMF functions, defeats generic AI-ethics essays.
Module 8The RiverCity consulting engagement and defenseWeeks 13–15 · Module 8 (Capstone) · about 27 hours
Objective: construct and defend a complete project end to end. Serves O7 and integrates O1–O6. RSI: direct instruction, feedback, live defense.
▶ Instructor intro (record) + slide-deck template (build): what a defensible, decision-maker-facing project looks like, and how the stakeholder defense works.
◆ Authentic assessment (simulation) — The RiverCity Engagement (threaded from week one). The learner is a solo consultant retained by a recurring client, and the engagement opens the way real sponsored capstones do (UChicago, CMU Heinz, Harvard IACS): a one-page problem brief from the client, two named points of contact, and a schedule of biweekly check-ins logged as a real engagement. The decision is genuine and ambiguous, the data messy and multi-source. Partway through, a new batch arrives with an unannounced distribution shift (a real two-timepoint pull from FRED or Census, or a per-student drift injector) that quietly breaks the earlier models; the learner detects it with Evidently, diagnoses it, and adapts. Deliverables, all client-facing: an executive decision memo (≤3 pages, non-technical, quantified impact and uncertainty), a reproducible repository in a standard structure (README, data lineage, pinned environment, model card, the layout Harvard IACS requires), a resampled evaluation and drift analysis, and a working Streamlit app the client can explore, defended in a 20-minute live stakeholder meeting where faculty and peers play the executive, the compliance officer, and the engineer. It is graded the way real sponsors and faculty grade a capstone, on the quality of the decision, the validity of the evaluation design, the business impact, and the live defense.
