# Data Science Course, Content Review

Thank you for looking this over. Here is what I am asking for and, just as important, what I am not.

**Goal.** Confirm the technical content is accurate, current, and rigorous enough for a graduate, MS-level Advanced Data Science course. This particular course was built using AI-generated (synthetic) subject-matter expertise, with no human data scientist in the loop. Your review is the check on whether that content actually holds up to an expert. Finding problems is the point, not a failure.

**Audience.** You, as the data science subject-matter expert. You verify the content and, in effect, own the result.

**Process.** Work through the course module by module. The full modules, readings, and schedule are here: the course page (data-science-course/canvas.html). For each module, tell me whether the content is sound and flag anything that is off.

## What is in your lane (please focus here)

- Technical accuracy. Is anything simply wrong or stated imprecisely?
- Currency. Does it reflect current tools, libraries, and practice, or is it dated?
- Depth and rigor. Is it deep enough for an intro course, or oversimplified to the point of being misleading?
- Terminology and examples. Correct terms, realistic datasets and examples, sensible workflows.
- Gaps. Anything important an intro course should cover that is missing.

## What is not in your lane (I own these, please skip)

- Teaching approach, sequencing, and instructional design.
- Accessibility, seat time and credit-hour math, point values and rubric structure.
- Page layout and formatting.

Please do not spend time on any of the above. If something there jumps out, a one-line note is plenty.

## How to flag things

For each module, a quick verdict plus specifics is all I need:

- **Accurate**, or **Minor issues**, or **Major issues**
- One or two lines on what is wrong or missing, and where (module and topic), so I can find it.

No need to rewrite anything. Just point at it. Be blunt. The more you break it, the more useful this is to me. If a module is solid, saying so briefly is genuinely helpful too.

You can send notes back however is easiest for you: inline in an email, a shared doc, or a quick call.
