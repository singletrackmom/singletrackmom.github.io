Ridgeline Wind: diagnosing target leakage in a predictive-maintenance model

AI Tools & Strategy · Learning Design

OverviewThe courseInstructor prepSimulation

An authentic, performance-based assessment from Module 5 of my graduate Advanced Data Science course. A learner interrogates a condition-monitoring dataset, identifies target leakage in a baseline failure model, and defends a maintenance decision under uncertainty. The graded evidence is the reasoning, not a retrievable answer.

Scenario

A wind-farm operator streams condition-monitoring data, gearbox temperature, drivetrain vibration, oil-debris particulates, and power output, from five turbines. One unit is degrading. Before end of shift the learner must recommend which turbine to remove for maintenance and whether to keep the remaining units in service through a high-load weekend. Removing a unit forfeits a day of generation; an undetected gearbox failure costs orders of magnitude more.

Methodological note

The scenario simplifies a problem normally posed as remaining-useful-life or survival analysis into a single decision, in order to isolate the reasoning being assessed.

The assessed competency: detecting target leakage

A baseline classifier reports near-perfect held-out accuracy. That performance is an artifact of target leakage: the model’s dominant feature, an open work-order flag, is recorded only after a technician has already flagged the machine, so it is a proxy for the label and is unavailable at prediction time. The learner is assessed on detecting the leak, recognizing that accuracy is uninformative under class imbalance, separating genuine condition signals from the artifact, and justifying a decision under uncertainty.

Assessment design

Targets the Analyze and Evaluate tiers of Bloom’s taxonomy (Anderson & Krathwohl). Deliberately AI-resistant: each learner receives a per-seeded dataset, so the graded object is judgment over private evidence, not a shareable answer (Wiggins, authentic assessment).

Assessment sequence

  1. Monitor the fleet dashboard and identify the genuine degradation signal.
  2. Evaluate the baseline model, interrogating its metrics and feature importance rather than accepting its output.
  3. Recommend a maintenance action, stating the accepted risk and a calibrated confidence.
  4. Defend the decision against stakeholder challenge.
  5. Review the outcome and the competency feedback against the rubric.
