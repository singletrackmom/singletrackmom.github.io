How Many Segments, Really? auditing a clustering claim

AI Tools & Strategy · Learning Design

OverviewThe courseInstructor prepSimulations

The graded summative for Module 6 of my graduate Advanced Data Science course. Marketing wants to build campaigns for a fixed number of customer segments, and a junior analyst says a k-means elbow gives five. Your job is to audit how that number was produced and defend how many segments really exist.

What this is

A performance-based summative assessment for this module, worth 100 points. The interactive below takes about 30 to 45 minutes and covers the diagnosis and the defense. The full assessment also includes the reproducible clustering analysis and the written segment profiles you submit, which is where most of the hours go. Each learner receives a uniquely seeded dataset, so the true number of segments differs from anyone else’s and no answer can be shared.

Why you are doing this

Unsupervised results are hypotheses, not answers. With no labels to hold back, a cluster count cannot be read off a single elbow plot; it has to be earned through stability, internal validity, and interpretability. This assessment tests whether you can refuse to hand a stakeholder a clean number until the evidence actually supports it, the core discipline of honest unsupervised analysis.

What you are being assessed on (100 points)

15Cluster stabilityBootstrap co-clustering, not one elbow
15Agreement with ground truthAdjusted Rand (0 to 1 label-agreement score) vs held-out labels
14Rigor of the count argumentConverging evidence for the number
14Preprocessing and methodStandardize, PCA, more than one algorithm
14Actionable segment profilesDescribed for a marketer to use
14ReproducibilityRuns from a fixed seed
14Honesty about uncertaintyDo not overclaim a precise count
What you submit: this interactive covers the diagnosis and the defended count (57 of the 100 points). The other 43 come from your reproducible clustering notebook, the agreement check against the held-out labels, and the written segment profiles you upload to the Module 6 assignment in Canvas. Do all of it.
