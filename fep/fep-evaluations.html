Student Evaluation Summary & Reflection

FEP appendix · Michelle Blomberg · Spring 2026
How to read this: the charts and comments below focus on the teaching items, instructor clarity, feedback, communication, care, and the work students produced. Logistics and institutional questions (whether a student knew their Enrollment Center advisor, used a loaner laptop, and so on) are not measures of teaching and are set aside. Four evaluations are documented: two official department reports and two of my own in-course surveys.
A student who nearly withdrew
“I have never had a 4.0 GPA until I started this degree and I have worked so hard to keep it... I spent two weeks out at sea... When I got back I [was] behind on 70 assignments and the idea of still having my 4.0 was history. I got so discouraged that I reached out to every teacher asking if it was possible to even pass. One teacher was kind of rude, but you and [a colleague] both hyped me up and told me what to focus on, and you two are the reasons I did not drop all my classes. I may not get the perfect grade I was hoping for, but I will finish, and I got to be there for my parents on their bucket-list trip.”
A Digital Media Arts student, April 2026. This is what student support and human connection look like in practice, and why the AVC 100 redesign is built around them.

Strengths and growth

What students named

  • Care and support; feeling cared for helped them succeed.
  • Communication and responsiveness, on email and in Discord.
  • The step-based project structure made the workload manageable.
  • Constructive, growth-oriented feedback.
  • Real, portfolio-ready work.
  • Innovation, including AI use.

Where students wanted more

  • Grading and feedback turnaround on every step of a large class.
  • Slightly clearer assignment instructions in a few places.
  • More even peer critique on Discord.

Reflection

The relational and structural parts of my teaching are clearly working: students feel supported, know I will respond, value the step-based structure, and notice the AI innovation. The student who nearly withdrew says it most directly, and it is exactly the outcome the AVC 100 redesign is built to produce, connection and support that keep students enrolled.

The one fair, recurring growth area is grading and feedback turnaround in 24-student online courses. I am acting on it: for the formative steps of a scaffolded project I am moving to a whole-class video critique in Discord, the online version of a studio critique wall, with checkmark complete-or-incomplete grading, and reserving full individual written feedback for the summative final work where it matters most. That keeps the interaction frequent and substantive while making turnaround sustainable, and it is grounded in regular and substantive interaction, which I keep refining and love comparing notes on with other faculty.
