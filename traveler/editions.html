Editions

Experiential Learning · Case study

OverviewAR editionEditions

Fifteen editions, each designed by a new student team around its own theme. Each one opens with its theme and builds its own way for readers to find their place. Select a cover to read the full edition in the library archive.

Volume 58 · The sketchbook

A celebration of human creativity and the quiet journey of art in the making. The book is designed as a multi-medium sketchbook, and it follows how an idea becomes something tangible: the marks, revisions and decisions beneath every finished piece, sketched, tested, reworked and refined.

Read Volume 58 ↗

Volume 57 · 60th anniversary

The experience of being a Glendale Gaucho, decade by decade. The team researched their predecessors through the library archive and an alumni questionnaire to find what has changed in campus life and what still ties students together. Each chapter is a decade of the college’s history, set in that decade’s design language.

Navigation. Each decade is told through what students cared about then: fashion, the price of coffee, how they listened to music, and hairstyles. A running timeline crosses the covers, and the college red carries through every decade’s palette.

Read Volume 57 ↗

Volume 56 · Artificial intelligence and dreams

Artificial intelligence keeps growing in popularity and use, even as some people hesitate. The book follows a character, Destin, through a series of dreams, and shows how easily it can all get out of hand. Each chapter is a different dream.

Read Volume 56 ↗

Volume 55 · Busy town campus

The book travels to a busy town of a campus where students are creating art, writing, procrastinating and drinking too much coffee. No matter where you are on your path, everyone is on the journey together, lifting each other up, supporting, encouraging and inspiring one another.

Read Volume 55 ↗

Volume 54 · Rising from the ashes

A theme of resilience and renewal after unprecedented times. Like the phoenix that bursts into flames and returns stronger than before, students rise, pursuing their dreams by overcoming adversity.

Read Volume 54 ↗

Volume 53 · An illustrated field guide

An illustrated field guide to the plants and animals native to Arizona. More than a “giant oven” of cactus and sand, Arizona holds six biomes and wildlife that has adapted to the state’s vast range of conditions.

Read Volume 53 ↗

Volume 52 · The Bauhaus at 100

The volume coincided with the 100th anniversary of the Bauhaus, which opened in Germany in 1919, so the theme was an easy choice for the design team. The book borrows the school’s geometry, primary color and modernist type.

Read Volume 52 ↗

Volume 51 · Area 51

The fifty-first edition takes on secrecy. Those in power often control access to information, and Area 51 is the best-known case. The team co-opted the symbols and language of secrecy and restriction and turned them on their heads, giving open access to students’ creative work.

Read Volume 51 ↗

Volume 50 · Surveillance

Media, messages, cameras and data. Whether it is the phone in your pocket or the security camera in a store, someone is always watching, and our data is collected constantly. The design is inspired by the devices, technology and information that connect and track everyday life.

Read Volume 50 ↗

Volume 49 · Childhood aspirations

Everyone must start somewhere. As children we are told to dream big, and somewhere in college that gets traded for making money instead of finding a passion. The book asks why dreams and reality can’t coexist, and argues that for artists they can.

Navigation. Stories run along the bottom of the pages about the things we wanted to be when we grew up: astronauts, artists, marathon runners and scuba divers.

Read Volume 49 ↗

Volume 48 · Patterns

Patterns are everywhere: in the biology of nature, in concepts woven through college textbooks, in the framework of architecture and in the fibers of our clothing. The book uses pattern as its design system.

Read Volume 48 ↗

Volume 47 · Growth

A theme of growth, built on the centuries-old Book of Kells and Celtic art, with its interlaced knots and illuminated forms.

Read Volume 47 ↗

Volume 46 · Steampunk

A trip back in time into the science fiction world of steampunk, inspired by industrialized Western civilization and an alternate nineteenth century of Victorian machinery.

Navigation. Gears move around the pages and light up when a reader is in that chapter.

Read Volume 46 ↗

Volume 45 · Year of the Dragon

Inspired by the Chinese zodiac. The dragon is the mightiest of the signs, symbolizing dominance and ambition, and dragons prefer to live by their own rules. Because the book is designed and written by student creatives, the team used this sign as its design metaphor.

Navigation. Symbols from Chinese astrology mark each chapter.

Read Volume 45 ↗

Volume 44 · Drawing in the margins

The doodles you probably made while you were thinking about a story or sketching out a piece of art when you were bored in another class. Type and images look drawn in the margins, and the artwork carries the same weight as the writing.

Navigation. The book has two covers. Flip it over and the other cover is the same design, so art and literary work share the hierarchy. A play in the middle runs vertically, so readers start holding the book one way, turn it for the play, and finish on the opposite side. The page numbers are a flip book that moves around the book.
