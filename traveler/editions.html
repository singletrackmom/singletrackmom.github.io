Volume 58 · The sketchbook

This volume of The Traveler is a celebration of human creativity and the quiet journey of art in the making. From the first spark of an idea to its final form, we explore how imagination becomes something tangible. By rediscovering the value of time, skill, and intention, this edition highlights the creative process as an evolution shaped by trial, error, refinement, and growth.

Across disciplines and mediums, each work reveals what is often unseen, the marks, revisions, and decisions beneath the finished piece. Ideas are sketched, tested, reworked, and refined. What begins as a fragment develops through patience and care into something whole. Creation is not instant. It is built through time, persistence, and the presence of the human hand. This is the nature of the handmade. Work shaped slowly, through touch, repetition, and intention. Craftsmanship is not only seen in the final form, but in every step that leads to it, in the choices, adjustments, and care that define the process.

In a world that often rushes toward outcomes, this collection lingers in the process. It honors the drafts, the in-between moments, and the subtle shifts that give work its depth and meaning. Creativity is not only a result, but a practice. It is shaped by experience, perspective, and a willingness to continue. Traces of thought appear throughout in the form of student quotes. These reflections are not tied to any one artwork; instead, they offer general insight into the creative process, giving voice to the ideas, intentions, and experiences that shape artistic growth. Nothing here is rushed. Nothing here is accidental. This is where ideas take form. This is where process meets imagination.

Volume 57 · 60th anniversary

The experience of being a Glendale Gaucho for each decade in time. While we think the 1960s may have nothing to do with us, the experience of being at this special place ties us together. For this edition we researched our predecessors to find the similarities and differences in campus life. Each chapter represents a different decade in the design style as well as rhetorical visual elements. There is a running timeline in the front and back cover that calls out some of the significant points in GCC and US history. Each new section starts a time period with icons representing symbols pervasive to the college experience.

Navigation. Each decade speaks in the vernacular of the things college students cared about then: fashion, the price of coffee, how they listened to music, and hairstyles.

Volume 53 · An illustrated field guide

Volume 53 of The Traveler is an illustrated field guide that showcases the diverse flora and fauna native to the state of Arizona. More than just a “giant oven” filled with cactus and sand, Arizona is home to six different biomes and a plethora of plants and animals. The resourceful wildlife of Arizona has adapted to the state’s vast range of elevations and temperatures. Volume 52 · The Bauhaus at 100

When Volume 52 of The Traveler coincided with the 100th anniversary of the Bauhaus, the theme for this year was an easy choice for our design team. The story of Bauhaus began in 1919, with the opening of the Staatliches Bauhaus school of design in Germany. Despite the school itself only being open for fourteen years, the ideas of Bauhaus spread across the world, and it became the most influential design movement of the century.

The foundation of Bauhaus was the idea of intentional design that could achieve both usefulness and beauty. This concept was applied to everything from architecture to typography to stagecraft. Founding director Walter Gropius envisioned Bauhaus as a utopian craft guild that united all of the arts into a single creative expression. When the political aspects of the school increased with its next director, Hannes Meyer, the teachings shifted toward meeting people’s necessities. During this time, function became the primary goal of Bauhaus design. The focus shifted once again with the final director of the Bauhaus, Ludwig Mies van der Rohe, who emphasized aesthetic above the other aspects of Bauhaus. Design merging the useful and the beautiful, utilizing simple lines, and appreciating negative space have endured over the years as essential concepts of design. The philosophy of Bauhaus remains just as relevant today as it was 100 years ago.

Volume 51 · Area 51

Often, those in power work to control access to information so that they can maintain the status quo. One of the most well-known cases of information control surrounds the Air Force base that is popularly known as Area 51. In this fifty-first edition of The Traveler, we have coopted the symbols and language of secrecy and restriction by turning them on their heads. We are providing open access to and celebrating the creative expression of the students of the Glendale Community College’s English and Art departments.

Additional images in this book reflect a timeline of events surrounding the alleged 1947 alien incident in Roswell, New Mexico. These images narrate the story to the subsequent involvement of Area 51 in the supposed government conspiracy and cover-up. Through this incident and other secret projects, Area 51 has become integrated into the cultural identity of America.

Volume 50 · Surveillance

Media, messages, cameras, data… Whether it be the phone in your pocket, the laptop in your room or the security camera in your favorite store, their eyes are always on you. Messages are being streamed while you pump gas or stand in line at the grocery store. As a society, we are under constant surveillance and our data is being collected constantly. In this day and age, it is made easier to document our activities since we are all connected by technology and the grid that keeps track of our everyday life. The design theme for this book was inspired by the devices, technology and information that bombards us and the data collected from every click or dollar spent.

Volume 49 · Childhood aspirations

Everyone must start somewhere. Every dream must first be dreamt, then aspired to, cultivated, matured and then finally realized; always with the knowledge that we never truly stop growing, just as we never truly stop dreaming. As children we are encouraged to be all we can be; dream big and we will do great things. Why does adulthood kill that notion? Why can’t dreams and reality coexist? As artists we know that they can. We know that our words hold great power and that the stroke of a brush can change a nation. So with this year’s Traveler let’s bring people back to the time when they dreamed about all they could be. Let’s awaken the ballerinas, the firemen, and the astronauts of yesterday and remind them that the only way to kill a dream is to forget about it.

Navigation. Stories run along the bottom of the pages about the things we wanted to be when we grew up: astronauts, artists, marathon runners and scuba divers. Somewhere in college we forget them, focused on making money instead of finding a passion.

Volume 46 · Steampunk

Volume 46, 2013 takes its readers on a trip back in time into the science fictional world of Steampunk. The Steampunk fantasy is inspired by industrialized Western civilization and is often set in an alternate 19th century British Victorian or American Wild West era. Its design emphasizes a balance between form and function. Featured elements often include gears and cogs, brass, and steam-powered machinery.

Navigation. A compass helps readers navigate the book, with icons for F (Fiction), N (Non-Fiction), P (Poetry) and A (Art) in place of the traditional cardinal directions. Gears move around the pages and light up when a reader is in that chapter.

Volume 45 · Year of the Dragon

The theme for Volume 45, 2012 is inspired by the Chinese Zodiac “Year of the Dragon.” The dragon is the mightiest of the signs. Dragons symbolize such character traits as dominance and ambition. Dragons prefer to live by their own rules and if left on their own, are usually successful. As The Traveler is designed and written by student creatives, we have chosen to create our story using this inspiring sign as a design metaphor.

Chinese Astrology is based on the balance of five elements. Each animal of the Zodiac is derived from a combination of these elements. The Dragon is comprised of Earth, Water and Wood.

Navigation. Symbols from Chinese astrology mark each chapter.

Volume 44 · Drawing in the margins

The theme is the idea of drawing in the margins, and the book is read by moving around it. The type and images represent the doodles you probably made while you were thinking about the story you were writing, or the art you were sketching while you were bored in another class. We wanted the artwork to be just as important as the written work, so art and literary work share the hierarchy for their content.

Navigation. The book has two covers. Flip it over and the other cover is exactly the same, one for art and one for literary work. In the middle of the book is a play that runs vertically, so readers start holding the book one way, switch for the play, and end up on the opposite side from where they started. The page numbers are a flip book that moves around the book.
