The Traveler

Experiential Learning · Case study

OverviewAR editionBack issues ↗

Glendale Community College’s award-winning student literary and arts magazine, designed end to end by students. Fifteen editions over sixteen years, Volumes 44 to 58, each with its own concept, and the next edition adds augmented reality.

Goal

Give students authentic, end-to-end publishing experience: a real production cycle with real deadlines, a real client, and a finished printed piece for their portfolio. The magazine is the curriculum.

Audience

AudienceWhat the magazine does for them

The campus communityA yearly record of the college’s creative work, presented to the college president
The public recordBack issues live in the GCC library archive and the Arizona state library’s Arizona Memory Project

Process

The Traveler runs as a long apprenticeship. Every year a new student team takes the magazine through a full production cycle, and my role as faculty advisor is to mentor them through it: modeling the professional process, coaching through critique, and holding the deadlines. The team does the work itself, from concept to the printer’s proof.

Every edition has a concept

No two editions look alike. Each team starts by choosing a theme that drives the whole design system. For the 60th anniversary edition, each chapter became a decade of the college’s history, set in that decade’s design language, with a running timeline across the covers, a three-column grid and body type held constant throughout, and the college red carried through every decade’s palette. Each decade was assigned to its own student designer.

Technology

  • Adobe InDesign for layout and typesetting, Photoshop for preparing art and photography, and Illustrator for icon libraries.
  • Miro for concept boards, assignments, and the production timeline.
  • Augmented reality in the next edition: readers point a phone at a printed artwork and the piece appears in 3D, in its true color. Students build the models with photogrammetry and the STEAMworks 3D scanner. See the AR edition page for how it works.

Outcomes

Fifteen editions, each taken to press by a new cohort. The 60th anniversary edition won three Pinnacle Awards from the College Media Association, including College Literary Magazine, and the magazine has earned recognition at the AIGA Design Week competition. Students who led the design team have gone on to design jobs, and every edition leaves an authentic audience and a permanent record, not an assignment that ends at a grade.
