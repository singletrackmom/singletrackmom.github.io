The next edition coincides with the opening of the college’s new STEAMworks building, so we are brainstorming how much science, technology, engineering, art and math a printed book can carry, and what a book can be beyond the page. Everything here is on the table; we will narrow it down as the team forms.

What’s on the table

IdeaWhat it could addWho might make it
3D artwork in augmented realityReaders point a phone at a printed sculpture or ceramic piece and turn it around in 3D, in its true color3D modeling students with the STEAMworks scanner, or the production team using photography

An RFID chip in the coverReaders tap the cover with a phone to reach the 3D models, videos and podcasts, instead of scanning a QR code; a QR code stays as the backupThe design team, with the printer
STEAM storiesPieces that connect art and writing to science, technology, engineering and math, to celebrate the new buildingThe whole team

Ways for students to get involved

The more layers the book has, the more students can put their name in it: 3D modelers, photographers, videographers and editors, journalism students recording podcasts, illustrators and graphic designers on the design team, and the writers and artists whose work it all starts from.

How it could start

In January, a call for participation would go to the contest winners. Each 3D artist chooses whether to have the work modeled by the 3D modeling class or captured in 3D with photography for the AR pages, and each creator can opt in to a short video interview about process or inspiration, or a podcast conversation.
