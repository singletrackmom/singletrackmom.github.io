Gemini for Students Under 18

Usability study · early stage, in development

Overview Programs Assignments Predicted results Requirements References

How does Google Gemini differ for a student designated under 18 compared with a student 18 or older, and does the difference put minors at a disadvantage in ordinary coursework? A study for the Maricopa Faculty Executive Council, by the Student Support and Success domain of the AI Resource Center.

Early stage, in development. Everything on these pages comes from Google’s published documentation and from scenarios written for the study. Testing began on 3 October 2026 and no results are posted here yet. They will be added side by side when both accounts are done.

Goal

The main goal is to find out where, or whether, students under 18 are blocked from meeting the competencies of an assignment when a course uses Gemini. The final results answer that same question, assignment by assignment. If students under 18 can meet the competencies even with the restrictions, the council has what it needs to sign off.

What the study will report
OutcomeWhat the council will see
Competency met, yes or noFor every assignment, whether the course competency can be met in each account, shown side by side.
Where a student is blockedThe exact step where the under-18 account is stopped, and the message Gemini gives.
Whether there is another wayWhether the competency can still be met with the restriction in place, for example by another route through the assignment or an alternate subject or scenario.
What faculty would adjustWhat needs to change in the assignment so a student under 18 can meet the competency, or whether an alternate assignment is needed.
Final tallyA count of any competencies that could not be met, and which ones.

Give the Faculty Executive Council evidence about how Gemini differs for students designated under 18 and students 18 and older, so the council can decide whether and how to roll out Gemini accounts to Maricopa students. The question is not only what minors can do, but whether the differences disadvantage them when they sit in the same sections as adults.

The district is working to get students access to Gemini. One of the steps is the council’s sign-off, which the council asked for at its last meeting. This study answers that request, and the hope is that the evidence lets the accounts be turned on as soon as possible.

Audience

The Faculty Executive Council first, then district administration and the AI Resource Center steering committee. Members of the domain also use these pages to run the same assignments by hand and check the results.

Process

How the study runs

  1. Audit the features. Walk through every capability in Google’s age table in both accounts and record what is actually available.
  2. Write one authentic assignment per program. Each is built from a real course competency, using the same agent method as the course builder: a subject-matter expert and an instructional designer.
  3. Run each assignment in both accounts. Synthetic students run it, and domain members log in and run it by hand to back up the result.
  4. Record what happened. Worked the same, lost a feature, blocked, or gave a different answer. Human and synthetic runs go in the same log.
  5. Analyze by subject. A data-science agent reports which programs are most likely to see under-18 students blocked or given a different result.

Status

Early stage, in development. Testing started on 3 October 2026. Twenty-six assignments are written from official course competencies. The eleven high-risk content courses have been run in the under-18 test account, and the 18 and older runs are in progress. The fourteen AI course assignments have not been run yet. Results will be posted here side by side when both accounts are done. The Predicted results tab is a forecast, not a finding.
