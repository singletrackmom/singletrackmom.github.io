Examples of What Gemini Blocks for Students Under 18

AI Strategy · Case study

OverviewAssignmentsResultsPRDReferences

Product requirements for the study: what it measures, how a result is decided, and what it reports.

In committee review. The district approved Gemini in other areas. Access for students under 18 was the open question, and this study was commissioned to answer it. The report is with the Student Support and Success domain for feedback before it goes to the Faculty Executive Council. Testing ran from 3 to 4 October 2026. Both accounts were run on all 27 example assignments: 14 AI courses, 11 high-risk content courses, and 2 courses added for Deep Research and music generation. The assignments are examples built for demonstration. The results come from one run per step.

1. Summary

A meaningful share of Maricopa students are under 18, and Google gives those students a restricted version of Gemini. The district is working to give students access to Gemini. This study shows what the restriction changes in real coursework, and what an instructor adds so every student can finish the assignment.

2. Goal

The study asks one question: can a student under 18 complete an assignment that uses Google Gemini, as written? Where the student is stopped, the study records what was blocked and what the student does instead. The instructor teaches to the course competency either way, so each alternate step is written to keep it.

What the study reports
OutcomeWhat the reader sees
Completed, or notFor every assignment, whether each step can be completed in each account, shown side by side.
What is blockedThe step where the under-18 account is stopped, whether Gemini refused the content or the tool is missing, and Gemini’s message.

What the instructor adjustsWhat changes in the assignment, or whether the student needs a route outside Gemini.

What the study measures and what it leaves out
MeasuredNot measured

4. How a result is decided

Definitions used to decide each result
TermDefinition
Completed as writtenEvery Gemini step in the assignment completed in that account.
Completed with an alternate stepOne or more steps were blocked, and the student can still finish with the “Under 18: do this instead” clause.
Not completable in GeminiThe steps the assignment depends on were blocked. The student needs a route that does not depend on Gemini.
BlockedGemini refuses in words, or the step fails on every attempt, up to five.
Not a blockA general error that clears when the prompt is sent again. These happened in both accounts.
Missing toolA tool that is not in the account’s menu. The same prompt is then sent in a standard chat, or the assignment’s fallback is used.
Under-18 restrictionA block that happens in the under-18 account and does not happen at the same step in the 18 and older account.

7. Proposed solution

The blocks found so far follow patterns, and each one has a workable alternate. So the fix does not have to be found one assignment at a time. The proposal is an under-18 check that an instructor runs on any assignment that uses Gemini. It is proposed, not built, and not yet tested.

Proposed under-18 check for an assignment

  1. Compare the assignment to the patterns this study found. Does a step use Deep Research, music, image editing or video? Does it ask for a role-play with violence or thoughts of death, an analysis of one person’s drug use, a clinical report on sexual health, or a security scan evaluation?
  2. Draft the alternate. A prompt, or a check inside the Dial Your Course tool, writes an “Under 18: do this instead” clause for each step that matches a pattern, keeping the same competency.
  3. Verify in an under-18 test account. Run the flagged steps once. This is the only reliable test.
  4. Add the clause to the assignment. Every student gets the same assignment, and the clause tells a student under 18 what to do at that step.

Why the third step is needed: Gemini cannot reliably predict its own under-18 filter. In this study the same account answered one prompt and refused the next on the same subject. A prompt can draft a good alternate. It cannot promise the original will be blocked or that the alternate will pass.

8. Limits

Limits of these results
LimitWhat it means for the results
Two different domainsThe under-18 account is in Google’s demonstration domain and the 18 and older account is in Maricopa’s. A difference could come from the age setting or from how each domain is set up.
The 18 and older side is a staff accountIt is assumed to match what an 18 and older student gets.
One run per stepGemini’s answers vary. The content refusals were not consistent even within one account.
Role-plays ran in a standard chatThe role instructions were pasted into a chat. Saved Gems were not tested.
Deep Research did not finishIt was started twice on IFS110 in the 18 and older account and did not produce a report in 28 to 50 minutes. The other nine assignments that name it were run in a standard chat in both accounts.
Tools outside the Gemini appGoogle Workspace Studio was not tested. Maricopa does not have Google AI Studio, so that step in CIS218 does not apply. Hand checks in Sheets, Colab or a library database were not run.
Example assignmentsThe 27 assignments are examples built for demonstration, drafted with AI assistance from official course competencies. No students took part.

Open questions: whether Google can supply an 18 and older test account in the same domain for a like-for-like check, and whether faculty can use an under-18 test account to check their own assignments.

Controls
Same conditionsBoth accounts are run in the same browser, over the same two days, with the same prompts and the same retry rule.

9. Ethics and governance

Safeguards
No minors take partAdults use the under-18 account. The under-18 condition comes from the account designation.
Fictional adults onlyEvery case, patient, client and victim in the assignments is a fictional adult, and the content stays at textbook level.
Google set up the test accountThe report says so plainly, so the findings are not read as Google’s own evaluation.
Not publicThese pages are not indexed and are not linked from the site.

10. Open questions

  • Whether Google can supply an 18 and older test account in the same demonstration domain, for a like-for-like comparison.
  • The age label and admin settings Google applied to the under-18 test account.
  • Whether Maricopa’s own setup for students under 18 will match the test account.
  • Whether faculty and instructional designers can be given an under-18 test account to verify their own assignments.
