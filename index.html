Innovation & AI in Higher Ed

A portfolio of sorts, some of it finished, some still in progress.

Welcome. I’m a Digital Media Arts faculty member with 25+ years in higher ed. I teach design, a field where the tools never stop changing, so the real skill is the thinking underneath: design principles, visual communication, how people process information. What prepares students for a future that doesn’t exist yet is learning to apply that to whatever tool comes next, and if you’ve ever been told art isn’t a practical major, the work here is the counter-argument.

AI is the biggest version of that shift yet, and the best way to learn it was to build with it, to expand what is possible rather than replace the creative work. So alongside teaching I make tools for student success. Render is a learner platform I took from a blank page to a working pilot, mapping the student journey and designing the loops that bring learners back. More and more the work is agentic: I design and run my own AI agents, scheduled assistants that watch, decide, and act on their own, in a setup where the AI can work across my whole computer rather than a single chat window. I give each one reusable Markdown instructions so I am not starting from scratch every time, which is how a whole suite of personal agents became part of my daily practice rather than a stack of one-offs.

The judgment call I make over and over is where AI accelerates learning and where human connection is what keeps a student motivated and moving, measured not by features shipped but by learning, retention, and engagement. My district student-journey study is my League for Innovation Fellows project and biggest effort. The rest of the work here is experiential learning, students doing real work for real audiences: campus murals and client projects my students take on, the internship program I oversee, the Campus Cares brand system, and The Traveler.

Student Support & Success

Goal: Implement AI to remove the barriers that keep students from succeeding, and improve success and completion.

Audience: The more than 140,000 students across all 10 colleges of the Maricopa Community College District.

Process: Three coordinated initiatives. First, map and baseline the non-classroom services (advising, financial aid, enrollment, student life). Then run a student-journey UX study, where personas walk the real path to find and rank the barriers. Then turn the findings into prioritized AI pilots.

This is my biggest project and my League for Innovation AI Fellows capstone. I co-chair the Student Support and Success domain of Maricopa’s district AI Resource Center, working across all 10 colleges. It keeps human connection and student agency at the center while AI removes friction from the non-classroom services that decide whether students stay, and it is grounded in the evidence on why students leave.

1 Map & baseline Inventory the services and baseline who uses each one.
2 Student Journey UX Study Personas walk the journey to find and rank real barriers.
3 Recommendations & pilots Turn barriers into prioritized AI pilots, ranked by reach.

AI-Native Learner Platform · Pilot planned Fall 2026
Render

Goal: Give students a personal learning environment that makes career readiness something they keep using, built around the engagement and retention loops that bring them back.

Audience: Students launching their careers, built in partnership with campus Career Services.

Process: A connectivist learner platform with seven interconnected modules (profile, goals, job log, resume vault, skills tracker, networking, interview prep) mapped to a formal career-launch taxonomy, with AI-assisted, learner-facing surfaces (interview rehearsal, resume and skills coaching) powered by the Claude API. AI Agentic Routing · Early prototype · In testing
CopaMigo

Goal: Point students to the right human support, fast. More and more, a stuck student turns to ChatGPT for an answer instead of asking their teacher or advisor, and many stop seeking help at all because they do not know what to call what they need. CopaMigo gets them to the right person.

Audience: Students who are not sure where to go for help, at a Hispanic-Serving Institution. It is not just bilingual, multiple languages are built in.

Process: A student describes their situation in plain language and CopaMigo routes them to the right human service with a handoff card showing contact info, hours, and what to ask for. Agentic routing patterns, 14 service modules, 100-plus verified GCC program URLs. Next: a campus picker for district-wide use.

In the Classroom

Teaching with AI · AVC 183 · Digital Graphic Design 1
Graphic Design History: 1900 to the Present

Goal: Students learn graphic design history by teaching it, everyone becomes the expert on their own era, then learns the rest from their peers (a jigsaw model).

Audience: AVC 183 Digital Graphic Design 1 students.

Process: Each student pair researches one era and builds a tool to teach it to the class. The instructor example covers 1910 to 1920 with a visual slideshow tour and an interactive drag-and-drop sorting game.

The standard project is a narrated slideshow. The extra credit version is an AI-assisted vibe coding challenge: build an interactive, period-accurate teaching tool from scratch using a prompt-based workflow. No prior coding experience required. Prompt craft counts.

Student vibe-coded examples:

  • 1919–1933 by Jo Miranda + Dominic A · interactive game
  • 1960–1979 by Devon Adams · narrated video with game demo

AI Agentic + Static Site · Personal build · PRD drafted May 2026
Wayfinder

Goal: Plan an ambitious multi-state van trip in one living page, and turn anyone’s interests into a trip like it.

Audience: Van travelers and road-trippers (built first for our 15-day Colorado, Utah, Nevada, and California loop: Great Basin, Tahoe, Yosemite, Zion, Bryce).

Process: It pairs a hand-built itinerary with a live weather feed, a fuel strategy with red gas-stop warnings, van-clearance and dimension awareness (Sprinter height, the Zion tunnel limits), direction-aware maps on every stop, a real budget, geography sections that segment the route by climate zone and elevation, color-coded boxes for camping, hot springs, hikes, climbing, and water, and drive-time chips with no-drive days. v1 is a questionnaire-driven generator that builds a page like this for anyone from their interests, accommodations, and budget.

Companion agent, campground watcher. A scheduled agent that runs on its own each morning and solves the problem every Colorado camper knows, that the best sites book out for months. It watches for the narrow moments when a hard-to-get spot frees up and quietly drops the find onto my calendar with what I need to grab it, so there is nothing to refresh and no dashboard to open. No public view by design, it works in the background and posts straight to my calendar.

AI Agentic · Daily Job Board · Personal build · Scheduled agent
Summer Work Board

Goal: Help a college student home for a short summer land high-paying, flexible, gig-style work fast.

Audience: A young adult who wants top pay per hour, weekend-friendly hours, and a hard five-week window.

Process: A scheduled agent runs each morning as a rolling board, it adds newly posted openings, expands to more local employers (event caterers, staffing firms, DJ companies, venues, rec centers), removes anything no longer posted, and re-checks every link. Leads are grouped by type with pay labeled hourly or per event, on one permanent link the content refreshes behind.

Design rule: Every link goes straight to the company’s own careers page, verified live, never Indeed or a logged-out job-board search.
