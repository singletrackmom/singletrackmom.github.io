Innovation & AI in Higher Ed

Welcome to my sandbox. I'm a Digital Media Arts faculty member with 25+ years in higher ed, exploring what AI and technology-driven innovation can do for student success. These are working prototypes and active experiments, not finished products.

Sometimes the best way to learn something new is to jump in. The tools in creative fields have always been changing, and what survives every shift is the underlying thinking: design principles, visual communication, how people process information. What prepares students for a future that doesn't exist yet is learning to apply those concepts to whatever tools come next, not just the ones we're teaching today.

AI is the latest version of that story and the most significant one yet. Not to replace creative work, but to expand what's possible. If you've ever been told art isn't a practical major, this is the counter-argument. The future belongs to creative thinkers who know how to use new tools. Jump in. Several of the projects below (Design Studio, Campus Cares, The Traveler) are experiential learning at scale, students doing real work for real audiences.

Early prototype · In testing
CopaMigo

A bilingual student support routing prototype. Students stop asking for help not because resources don't exist, but because they don't know what to call what they need. CopaMigo lets a student describe their situation in plain language and routes them to the right human service with a handoff card showing contact info, hours, and what to ask for. Uses agentic routing patterns: 14 service modules, 100+ verified GCC program URLs. Next: a campus picker for district-wide use.

Feeder School Outreach · Semester Newsletter
Rough Cut

For-profit and private colleges recruit in 9th-grade classrooms, so by the time community colleges arrive at college fairs, students have already chosen. Rough Cut is the response: a semester newsletter for feeder schools and prospective students, with recurring features on new labs and program updates, student award winners, faculty and alumni spotlights, paid student work, and community ed and summer camp offerings. Paired with a research spreadsheet covering 36+ feeder high schools across 8 districts (art teachers, yearbook advisors, CTE coordinators, counselors) and a visit-form-to-Sheet pipeline routing inquiries to dmarts@gccaz.edu. Built with Claude as the production partner.

In the Classroom

AVC 183 · Digital Graphic Design 1
Graphic Design History: 1900 to the Present

Each student pair researches one era of graphic design history and builds a tool to teach it to the rest of the class. Everyone becomes an expert on their decade, then learns the other eras from their peers, following a jigsaw model. The instructor example covers 1910–1920 and includes a visual slideshow tour and an interactive drag-and-drop sorting game.

The standard project is a narrated slideshow. The extra credit version is an AI-assisted vibe coding challenge: build an interactive, period-accurate teaching tool from scratch using a prompt-based workflow. No prior coding experience required. Prompt craft counts.

Student vibe-coded examples:

  • 1919–1933 by Jo Miranda + Dominic A · interactive game
  • 1960–1979 by Devon Adams · narrated video + game (link coming)

Curriculum & Courses

Digital Media Arts & Animation
Stackable Fast Track Microcredentials

A redesign of the Animation certificate into three stackable Fast Track blocks, each under the 15-credit cap. Students come from many starting points, some retooling skills after earning a degree elsewhere, some exploring college for the first time, some still in high school. Stackable credentials give every student a meaningful early win and a clear path forward. Each block counts toward either the Animation & Time-Based Media or Digital Media Arts AAS, so nothing is wasted. Curriculum paperwork is underway; implementation expected 2027–2028.
