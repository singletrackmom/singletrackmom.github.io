eCourses Coordinator

Adoption · Case study

Faculty developer for online teaching in the Art and Humanities department: training requirements, accessibility, every LMS change, and the move of the whole department online during COVID-19.

Goal

Every Art and Humanities instructor, full-time and adjunct, is prepared to teach online, not simply handed a course.

Audience

More than 45 Art and Humanities faculty, about 12 full time and the rest adjunct, and the students in their courses.

Process

I was one of the founding members of the campus eCourses Committee, part of the group that wrote its bylaws and set it up as faculty-governed. The committee set the online-teaching training requirements, and each department had a coordinator to carry that work into it.

As the Art and Humanities coordinator, I tracked each instructor’s required training and helped them finish it, carried accessibility mandates and LMS changes through the department, and for several years organized the campus-wide lunch and learn series as my committee charge.

When COVID-19 sent the college remote with one day left on campus, the same role meant a condensed training that day, covering video and Canvas, then one-on-one mentoring until every course was online.

Technology

  • Canvas, for the courses themselves.
  • Zoom, for live class sessions and for the mentoring.
  • Google Drive tools, for shared files and materials.

Outcomes

Department faculty met the online-teaching training requirements the college’s accreditation depends on. When COVID-19 closed the campus, more than 45 Art and Humanities faculty, most of whom had never taught online, moved their courses fully online within weeks.
