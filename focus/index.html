Real inspection work over $20 an hour (the honest distinction)

Staff / hourly only

Property, insurance, real estate, and infrastructure inspection

Colorado-wide, staff or hourly roles only

There is a real version of “drive out and photograph a property to inspect it” that pays well, and a fake-sounding version that does not. The real version is a staff or hourly field inspector role (property, insurance, real estate or architectural documentation, infrastructure or drone inspection) that pays roughly $20 to $30 an hour as actual wages. Those are fine and a good fit for the inspection and survey background. What stays off this board on purpose is the per-task gig-app inspection work that pays about $5 to $15 a stop, no matter how steady it looks, because it does not clear the floor. When an inspection posting turns up, the test is simple: is it an hourly or salaried wage, or is it paid per inspection? Only apply to the hourly or salaried ones, on the hiring company’s own site. The CoStar role above is the cleanest verified example of this kind of scheduled, employer-paid documentation work right now.

06

Get found online

Low-pressure ways to get the wedding and portrait business in front of people, including the new world of AI search. Pick one, ignore the rest. None of this is urgent.

The big new one

Get cited when someone asks an AI

More and more couples ask a chatbot “who is the best Golden wedding photographer?” before they ever open Google. To be the name it answers with, the signals are surprisingly concrete: a complete, active Google Business Profile (every field filled, reviews answered); the exact same business name, address, and phone everywhere it appears online; a simple landing page for each prime venue (so the AI can match “Evergreen Lake House photographer” to a real page); a steady trickle of real client reviews; and getting listed on venues’ preferred-vendor pages and reputable local Golden roundups, which is what the AI tools actually read and quote. Right now only about one in a hundred local businesses gets recommended this way, so the early movers lock in the spot. Nothing here costs money, it is just consistency.

Old-school local SEO, still works

The same venue landing pages feed traditional Google ranking too. He used to rank well for the prime Golden and Evergreen venues; rebuilding that is mostly about having one clear, plain-language page per venue (“Evergreen Lake House wedding photographer,” “Mount Vernon Canyon Club weddings,” and so on), each linked to a real gallery or blog. Do them one venue at a time. This is the slow, durable channel that keeps paying off long after the work is done.

Pinterest, one board at a time

If he is not on Pinterest yet, it is worth setting up, because couples and traveling families plan there and the platform feeds both Google and AI search. The trick is to keep it small and steady: one board at a time, organized by venue or theme, with 2 to 3 sentence pin descriptions that name the venue, city, and state (for example “Evergreen Lake House wedding, Evergreen, Colorado”). No hashtags, the plain description is what gets read and matched. One board done well beats ten half-built ones.

Facebook photography groups (worth a light try)

Honest framing on this one: local Facebook wedding and photography groups sometimes have couples posting “looking for a photographer” or “our photographer fell through.” Replying to those can occasionally land a booking. But be realistic, it is a low-yield, scattershot channel, not something to count on, and it can eat time. Treat it as a five-minute-now-and-then scan, reply warmly when a real request shows up, and do not let it become a chore. Worth a light try, not a strategy.

Growth ideas to think about

Bigger moves for a slow wedding year: turn the Colorado-destination angle into portrait and event work for travelers. None of this is urgent. Pick one that sounds good and ignore the rest.

The core idea

Colorado vacation portraits for visiting families

Families and couples who travel to Colorado want photos with the mountains behind them, and that is a year-round market that does not depend on wedding bookings. Lean into the places tourists actually go, not just Golden, Boulder, and Denver: Rocky Mountain National Park and Estes Park, Grand Lake, Winter Park, and the Frisco, Breckenridge, and Dillon area. Since weddings are thin this year, point the existing venue and planner contacts toward elopements, and add destination family and couple sessions as the bread and butter.

Where to run paid ads

Instagram and Facebook (Meta) ads are the best fit here: you can target the travelers’ HOME markets and interests (Colorado travel, national parks, family trips) before they even arrive. Google Search ads catch the high-intent searches like “Estes Park family photographer.” Start small: a low-budget Meta test aimed at Texas families plus two or three Google search keywords, see what actually books, then put more behind whatever works. For inspiration-style demand, Meta wins; for people already searching, Google wins.

Advertise in Texas and other feeder markets

Texas sends huge numbers of summer visitors to the Colorado mountains, so running ads to Dallas, Houston, and Austin puts him in front of families while they are still planning. Other strong feeder markets to test: Oklahoma, Kansas, Nebraska, Arizona, and Chicago. This is the piece most local photographers miss, they only market inside Colorado. Print and visitor-guide ads in the mountain towns (Visit Estes Park, Visit Grand County, GoBreck) can supplement this, but treat print as a backup to the web pages and Google, since it is pricey and hard to track.

To-do, not set up yet

Airbnb photo sessions (the stay-then-activity hookup)

Airbnb relaunched Experiences and Services in 2025, and Photography is one of the service categories. A traveler booking a Colorado stay now gets offered local photo sessions right in the booking flow, exactly the “book a place, then add an activity” idea. Airbnb vets service hosts and looks for around ten years of experience and credentials, which he clears easily. This has NOT been set up yet, it is a real next step: apply to host a photography Service in the mountain markets.

Not exhausted, and a good low-effort fit. There are 20-plus years of Colorado scenics, venue, and lifestyle frames already shot and sitting unused. Adobe Stock turns that archive into slow passive income, and the contact who already does Adobe Stock is a warm way to learn the keywording and upload workflow without starting cold. Also worth licensing Colorado images to tourism boards. Be realistic: stock is a trickle, not a paycheck, but it is found money from work already done.

Lodging and venue partnerships (the offline version)

Vacation-rental managers and lodges in Estes Park, Grand Lake, Winter Park, and Frisco can offer their guests an “add a family photo session” referral, and resort concierge desks can do the same. Same model as the Airbnb hookup, just built on local relationships, and it pairs naturally with the destination pages above.
