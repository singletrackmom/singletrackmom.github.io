05

Growth ideas to think about

Bigger moves for a slow wedding year: turn the Colorado-destination angle into portrait and event work for travelers. None of this is urgent. Pick one that sounds good and ignore the rest.

The core idea

Colorado vacation portraits for visiting families

Families and couples who travel to Colorado want photos with the mountains behind them, and that is a year-round market that does not depend on wedding bookings. Lean into the places tourists actually go, not just Golden, Boulder, and Denver: Rocky Mountain National Park and Estes Park, Grand Lake, Winter Park, and the Frisco, Breckenridge, and Dillon area. Since weddings are thin this year, point the existing venue and planner contacts toward elopements, and add destination family and couple sessions as the bread and butter.

Get found in Google and AI search

Build one simple page per destination on the website (for example “Rocky Mountain National Park family photographer,” “Estes Park vacation portraits,” “Winter Park family photos”), each in plain, clear language. That is what ranks on Google AND what tools like ChatGPT pull from when someone asks “Colorado portraits while we are on vacation.” AI assistants favor businesses that show up consistently across Google Business Profile, Yelp, The Knot, and Thumbtack with real reviews, so keep those profiles complete and ask happy clients for reviews. Add a Pinterest board per destination, since travelers plan trips there.

Where to run paid ads

Instagram and Facebook (Meta) ads are the best fit here: you can target the travelers’ HOME markets and interests (Colorado travel, national parks, family trips) before they even arrive. Google Search ads catch the high-intent searches like “Estes Park family photographer.” Start small: a low-budget Meta test aimed at Texas families plus two or three Google search keywords, see what actually books, then put more behind whatever works. For inspiration-style demand, Meta wins; for people already searching, Google wins.

Print ads and visitor guides in the mountain towns

Tourists really do browse the local visitor guides and tourism websites before and during a trip, so an ad where they are planning can work. The places to look: Visit Estes Park (estes.org / the Estes Park visitor guide), Visit Grand County (for Grand Lake and Winter Park), and the Town of Frisco, Breckenridge (GoBreck), and Summit County tourism guides. Each tourism office publishes an annual print guide and runs a website, ask for their media kit and ad rates. Honest note: print is pricey and hard to track, so treat it as a supplement to the destination web pages and Google, not the main spend, and look at the guides’ websites and local wedding/elopement vendor directories first, since those are cheaper and easier to measure.

Advertise in Texas and other feeder markets

Texas sends huge numbers of summer visitors to the Colorado mountains, so running ads to Dallas, Houston, and Austin puts him in front of families while they are still planning. Other strong feeder markets to test: Oklahoma, Kansas, Nebraska, Arizona, and Chicago. This is the piece most local photographers miss, they only market inside Colorado.

Airbnb vets service hosts and looks for around ten years of experience and credentials, which he clears easily. Adobe Stock (monetize the archive)

Not exhausted, and a good low-effort fit. There are 20-plus years of Colorado scenics, venue, and lifestyle frames already shot and sitting unused. Adobe Stock turns that archive into slow passive income, and the contact who already does Adobe Stock is a warm way to learn the keywording and upload workflow without starting cold. Also worth licensing Colorado images to tourism boards (Visit Estes Park, Visit Grand County). Be realistic: stock is a trickle, not a paycheck, but it is found money from work already done.

Vacation-rental managers and lodges in Estes Park, Grand Lake, Winter Park, and Frisco can offer their guests an “add a family photo session” referral, and resort concierge desks can do the same. Same model as the Airbnb hookup, just built on local relationships, and it pairs naturally with the destination pages above.

Ways to put Claude to work (take this and run with it)

Open your own Claude and pick any of these. You can paste this whole page in and keep iterating on it yourself, no need to wait on anyone: shape the bike-maintenance app, plan a Pinterest page, set up a small Texas ad test, dig up more local bike-brand and product-photography leads, draft the outreach notes, or rough out the destination-portrait plan. Below are ready-to-paste starting prompts. There is no wrong one, and if none of it fits, the last prompt just opens a conversation.

Bike-maintenance app: “You are my brainstorming partner. I am a serious mountain biker in Golden, Colorado and I have an idea for a bike-maintenance app. Ask me about it one question at a time, help me figure out exactly what it does and who it is for, then outline a simple first version we could build together, no coding experience needed. Keep it concrete and low-pressure.”
Start a Pinterest page: “I am a professional photographer and I want to use Pinterest to get found by couples and families planning Colorado trips and weddings. Give me a step-by-step plan: how to set up a business account, how many boards to start with and how to name them (should boards and pins use the city, state, and destination, like ‘Estes Park Wedding Photographer, Colorado’?), what to pin and how often, and how to write pin descriptions so they show up in Google and AI search. Walk me through it like I am brand new to Pinterest.”
Reach Texas travelers with ads: “I am a Colorado photographer who wants to reach families and couples in Texas (Dallas, Houston, Austin) who travel to Colorado for vacations and weddings. Where do I start? Compare Instagram and Facebook ads with Google Search ads for this, then walk me through setting up one small first test on whichever is best: budget, who to target, and what the ad should say and show. Assume I have never run an ad before, and tell me how to know if it is working.”
If none of this looks good: “None of these ideas feel quite right, so let’s just brainstorm. I am a photographer in Golden, Colorado with 20-plus years in weddings, product, and architectural work, a background as a production manager, and deep mountain-bike knowledge. I want steady side income that fits an introvert who likes scheduled, defined work. Ask me questions and help me figure out what kind of work or small business actually fits me.”
