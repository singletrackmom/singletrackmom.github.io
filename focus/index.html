Read this first · licensing

What a CDL is, and when you do (and do not) need one

A CDL is needed only for vehicles over 26,001 lbs, built for 16-plus passengers, or hauling hazmat, so the 14-seat tour vans above need none. School-bus and big-shuttle jobs do need one (usually Class B) plus a P endorsement, and an S endorsement for school buses. RTD and Jeffco above train you and pay for it.

Branch out later · guidance

Real inspection work over $20 an hour (the honest test)

Not a posting, just guidance for when an inspection role turns up. There is a real version of “drive out and photograph a property to inspect it” that pays well, and a fake-sounding version that does not. The real version is a staff or hourly field inspector role that pays roughly $20 to $30 an hour as actual wages, a good fit for the inspection and survey background. Get found online

Low-pressure ways to get the wedding and portrait business in front of people, including the new world of AI search. Pick one, ignore the rest. None of this is urgent.

Skill to add · the big new one

Get cited when someone asks an AI

More and more couples ask a chatbot “who is the best Golden wedding photographer?” before they ever open Google. To be the name it answers with, the signals are surprisingly concrete: a complete, active Google Business Profile (every field filled, reviews answered); the exact same business name, address, and phone everywhere it appears online; a simple landing page for each prime venue (so the AI can match “Evergreen Lake House photographer” to a real page); a steady trickle of real client reviews; and getting listed on venues’ preferred-vendor pages and reputable local Golden roundups, which is what the AI tools actually read and quote. Right now only about one in a hundred local businesses gets recommended this way, so the early movers lock in the spot. Nothing here costs money, it is just consistency.

Skill to add

Old-school local SEO, still works

The same venue landing pages feed traditional Google ranking too. You used to rank well for the prime Golden and Evergreen venues; rebuilding that is mostly about having one clear, plain-language page per venue (“Evergreen Lake House wedding photographer,” “Mount Vernon Canyon Club weddings,” and so on), each linked to a real gallery or blog. Do them one venue at a time. This is the slow, durable channel that keeps paying off long after the work is done.

Skill to add

Pinterest, one board at a time

If you are not on Pinterest yet, it is worth setting up, because couples and traveling families plan there and the platform feeds both Google and AI search. The trick is to keep it small and steady: one board at a time, organized by venue or theme, with 2 to 3 sentence pin descriptions that name the venue, city, and state (for example “Evergreen Lake House wedding, Evergreen, Colorado”). No hashtags, the plain description is what gets read and matched. One board done well beats ten half-built ones.

Skill to add · organize the work

Build four focused portfolios, not one catch-all

Split your work into four focused portfolios so each audience sees what it wants: (1) architecture, (2) product and beer, (3) bike and action, (4) family and travel portraits. Each can be a Pinterest board or small digital brochure, with plain descriptions naming the place, city, and state. One focused board per theme beats one crowded gallery.

Set them up one at a time, start with whichever has the most ready images ›

Growth ideas to think about

Bigger moves for a slow wedding year: turn the Colorado-destination angle into portrait and event work for travelers. None of this is urgent. Pick one that sounds good and ignore the rest.

The core idea

Colorado vacation portraits for visiting families

Families and couples who travel to Colorado want photos with the mountains behind them, and that is a year-round market that does not depend on wedding bookings. Lean into the places tourists actually go, not just Golden, Boulder, and Denver: Rocky Mountain National Park and Estes Park, Grand Lake, Winter Park, and the Frisco, Breckenridge, and Dillon area. Since weddings are thin this year, point the existing venue and planner contacts toward elopements, and add destination family and couple sessions as the bread and butter.

Growth idea

Where to run paid ads

Instagram and Facebook (Meta) ads are the best fit here: you can target the travelers’ HOME markets and interests (Colorado travel, national parks, family trips) before they even arrive. Google Search ads catch the high-intent searches like “Estes Park family photographer.” Start small: a low-budget Meta test aimed at Texas families plus two or three Google search keywords, see what actually books, then put more behind whatever works. For inspiration-style demand, Meta wins; for people already searching, Google wins.

Growth idea

Advertise in Texas and other feeder markets

Texas sends huge numbers of summer visitors to the Colorado mountains, so running ads to Dallas, Houston, and Austin puts you in front of families while they are still planning. Other strong feeder markets to test: Oklahoma, Kansas, Nebraska, Arizona, and Chicago. This is the piece most local photographers miss, they only market inside Colorado. Print and visitor-guide ads in the mountain towns (Visit Estes Park, Visit Grand County, GoBreck) can supplement this, but treat print as a backup to the web pages and Google, since it is pricey and hard to track.
