Drone license

Get your drone license (FAA Part 107)
  • Any drone flight for pay, including real estate, architecture and wedding photos, needs the FAA Part 107 Remote Pilot Certificate.
  • The FAA publishes its study materials online. The knowledge test itself is taken in person at an FAA-approved Knowledge Testing Center and costs about $175 (FAA figure). First get an FAA Tracking Number by creating an IACRA profile, and bring a government photo ID to the test. I could not confirm a specific testing center near Golden or Denver this week, so use the FAA’s testing center list when you book.
  • The drone must be registered at FAADroneZone: $5 per drone, good for three years.
  • Flying in controlled airspace near an airport needs an airspace authorization through LAANC. Golden sits near Rocky Mountain Metropolitan Airport, so check the airspace map before every flight.
  • Wedding venues and some parks have their own drone rules, so check before the day.
  • This week: 0 of 1 photo postings required drone work; HomeJab lists aerials as an optional add-on.

Gutter, leaf and snow work for neighbors
  • Gear is modest: an extension ladder with a stabilizer, leaf blower, rakes, heavy bags, gloves, a snow shovel and a truck or trailer for hauling.
  • Ladder work is where liability insurance matters most. Ask an insurance agent about general liability coverage before the first paid job.
  • A local business license may apply. I could not confirm the rule for Golden this week, so ask the City of Golden before you advertise.
  • Start with a few neighbors by word of mouth; fall leaf cleanup and first snows pay the best.
