A little study guide

Level Up: Drone & Video

turn the cameras you already own into the two skills real estate keeps asking for

Denver real estate media has changed: clients now want stills, video, and aerials from one shooter. You already have the eye, the architectural chops, and the cameras. These are the two add-ons that open the most doors, and neither one needs a big new investment to start. Here is the plain-English path for each.

Why this is worth it (the jobs prove it)

Jeeves Drones (Denver) requires an active Part 107 drone license plus interior walkthrough video. Rocky Mountain Photography, Virtuance, and Crestline all push drone and video as core services. Adding these two skills moves you from “stills only” into the full-package shooter most listings now want, and it lets you charge for three deliverables instead of one.

Skill 1 · the license

Drone: the FAA Part 107

Flying a drone for any paid work (real estate, construction, anything commercial) legally requires the FAA Part 107 Remote Pilot Certificate. It is a single knowledge test, no flight exam, and most people pass in 2 to 3 weeks of casual study. This is the credential that unlocks the drone half of those jobs.

What it covers

Eight topic areas: regulations, airspace and how to read sectional charts, weather, drone loading and performance, and operations (the biggest chunk). It is more “rules and charts” than math, and the FAA gives you the exact study material for free.

The steps, in order

  • Make a free IACRA account and get your FTN (your pilot ID number).
  • Study the free FAA Remote Pilot Study Guide then take a structured course and timed practice tests until you score in the 85s consistently.
  • Schedule the exam at a nearby PSI testing center and pass it ($175).
  • Apply for the certificate in IACRA, then register your drone on FAA DroneZone ($5, good for 3 years).
  • Add drone liability insurance before paid gigs (many shoots, like Jeeves, require it).

Recurrent training afterward is a free online course every 24 months. No retest fee.

The drone itself

For real estate, a DJI Mini 4 Pro or Air 3 is the sweet spot: light, sharp, and the Mini class skips the heavier registration tier. Some clients (Jeeves) specify a Mini 3 or better, so aim there.

Skill 2 · no new camera needed

Real estate video on your DSLR

You can shoot listing video today on a DSLR you already own. Real estate video is forgiving: short, slow, steady clips set to music, no dialogue, no fancy color work. The skill is smooth movement and clean exposure, both very learnable.

Camera settings cheat-sheet

The shutter rule (double your frame rate) is what makes motion look natural instead of stuttery. In bright rooms you may need an ND filter to keep that shutter while staying exposed, it is a cheap screw-on filter.

Gear that makes it easy

  • A gimbal stabilizer (DJI RS 3 Mini or similar) is the single biggest upgrade, it turns shaky walking into a smooth glide.
  • A simple ND filter set for bright interiors and exteriors.
  • No microphone needed, real estate video runs on a music bed.

How to shoot it

  • Slow, deliberate moves only: gentle push-ins, reveals around doorways, a steady glide down a hallway.
  • Shoot 5 to 10 second clips, more than you think you need.
  • Follow the buyer’s walk: approach, entry, main living, kitchen, primary suite, outdoor, then a drone exterior to bookend.
  • Keep verticals straight (your architectural instinct already does this).

Editing (free)

Cut a 60 to 90 second walkthrough to a licensed music track. DaVinci Resolve is free and pro-grade; CapCut is the fast, simple option. Drop clips in buyer-walk order, trim to the beat, add a title card with the address, export at 1080p or 4K.

A simple 30-day plan

  • Week 1: Start the Part 107 course and the FAA study guide. Same week, shoot one practice video walkthrough of the house with your DSLR using the cheat-sheet.
  • Week 2: Keep studying (practice tests). Edit your practice walkthrough in DaVinci Resolve, start to finish.
  • Week 3: Hit 85%+ on practice tests, schedule and take the Part 107 exam. Shoot a second, cleaner video.
  • Week 4: Register your drone, line up insurance, and add “FAA Part 107 certified” plus a sample video to your portfolio. Now apply to the full-package roles.

Honest cost and time

Drone license: $175 test + $5 drone registration, about 2 to 3 weeks of light study. Course is optional ($50 to $150) but worth it. Video: $0 to start (you own the camera), and a gimbal plus ND filters later runs roughly $250 to $450. Payoff: you go from one deliverable to three, and you qualify for the Denver real estate media jobs that now require exactly this combo.

You already have the hard part: the eye and the cameras. These two skills are just the paperwork and the practice that let you charge for them. One step this week beats a month of thinking about it.
