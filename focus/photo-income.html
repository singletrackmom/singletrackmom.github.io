Photography income beyond the wedding season

Money from your archive

Twenty years of skate, travel, and product photos, turned into income, with as little marketing as possible.

Straight talk first. A job is your fastest, most certain money, so keep chasing the driving, warehouse, and bike-brand roles on the main page. This page is the slower, quieter money: your huge photo library and your real skill, earning on the side. You are not a marketer, so everything here is sorted with the least-marketing options first. The two that need almost no selling are second shooting for other photographers (uses your skill, near-zero setup) and stock photography (upload once, earn passively). Start there.

01

Second shooting (fastest, least marketing)

Someone else finds the client and runs the day. You just shoot. This is the closest thing to quick photo money.

Start here

Be a second shooter for other photographers

$150 to $400 / day

Denver, Golden, Boulder wedding & event photographers

Why it fits you: you already do this work, you own the gear, and there is no client hunting. The lead photographer books the wedding, you show up and shoot alongside them for a flat day rate. Established shooters constantly need reliable seconds, especially in the busy season.

What to do: make a short list of 15 to 20 active wedding and event photographers within an hour of Golden (Instagram and Google are enough). Send each a two-line note: you are a working photographer offering second-shooter availability, here is your portfolio, here is your day rate. Join the Facebook groups “Colorado Wedding Photographers” and “Second Shooters & Associates” and post your availability. Ten emails usually lands one or two regulars.

Honest money: $150 to $400 a day to start, more once a photographer trusts you. A handful of regulars can mean steady weekend income without ever marketing to a couple.

02

Stock photography (upload once, earn passively)

You have thousands of images sitting on drives. Stock is the most hands-off way to make them earn. It is a slow trickle that builds with the size of your catalog, not a paycheck, so upload big and forget it.

One thing to know first: photos with recognizable people, logos, or brands (skate footage, event crowds, branded product) can usually only be sold as editorial, not commercial, unless you have signed model or property releases. Alamy is the most editorial-friendly, so send your skate and travel archive there. Clean landscapes, textures, and objects with no people or logos can go anywhere as commercial.
Best for a big archive

Alamy

~40% to 50% commission

Editorial-friendly, accepts almost everything

What to do: open a contributor account, bulk-upload your skate, travel, and event archive, tag it well, and mark it editorial where needed. Highest commission of the big libraries and the least picky about what it accepts, which suits a deep, varied archive like yours.

Alamy contributor signup ›

Adobe Stock

~33% commission

Huge buyer base, easy uploader

What to do: become a contributor and upload your cleanest commercial-safe images (landscapes, nature, objects, textures). Adobe has an enormous built-in buyer pool through Photoshop and Lightroom, so good keywording gets found. Best for your no-people, no-logo shots.

Adobe Stock contributor ›

Shutterstock & Getty / iStock

Volume plays

The other two big libraries

What to do: upload the same commercial-safe set to Shutterstock (simple, high traffic) and, if you want the premium tier, apply to Getty/iStock. The same photo can live on several libraries at once, so mirror your catalog across all of them to multiply the trickle.

Honest money: stock pays cents to a few dollars per download. A large, well-tagged catalog might earn $20 to a few hundred a month over time, growing as you add more. Think slow compounding, not fast cash.

03

Prints & wall art (they print and ship, you keep a margin)

Your best travel and skate shots as wall art. These marketplaces handle printing, shipping, and some of the traffic, so you are not running a store. Skate photography has a real collector niche.

Easiest start

Fine Art America / Pixels

You set the markup

The biggest print-on-demand for photographers

What to do: open a free account, upload 20 to 40 of your strongest travel and skate frames, set your markup over their base price, and they print and ship every order. No gatekeeper, live the same day. This is the one to set up first.

Fine Art America, sell your art ›

Displate & Society6

Royalty per sale

Metal prints (Displate) and decor (Society6)

What to do: Displate turns images into metal posters, and the bold, high-contrast look suits skate and action shots. Society6 leans home decor and has a built-in browsing audience. Upload to both, they handle everything, you earn a royalty. Great low-effort second and third channels.

Displate, sell your art ›

iCanvas & Great Big Canvas (curated)

Royalty, if accepted

Featured-artist licensing brands

What to do: these curate who they carry, so submit a portfolio of your best travel and landscape work for review. If accepted, they market and sell your prints for you and you keep the copyright. More prestige and reach, more selective. Submit once you have a strong, tight set.

iCanvas, submit a portfolio ›

Etsy (your own shop, or printable downloads)

$8 to $40 / print

Buyers already hunting for wall art

What to do: two ways to run this. Sell physical prints (you or a lab fulfill), or sell digital printable downloads, the buyer prints it themselves, which is nearly passive once listed and needs no shipping. Etsy shoppers are already searching for travel and skate art, so the platform brings some traffic. It needs a little setup and light upkeep, but the digital-download route is the least work.

04

Real estate & architectural photography

This is more active money per shoot and you already have the eye and the gear. The main job board above lists companies hiring photographers; this is how to make it a steady side income on your own terms.

Shoot real estate on contract

$150 to $400 / shoot

Denver metro, strong and steady demand

Why it fits you: your architectural and building experience is exactly what this needs, and homes are a quick, repeatable, well-paid shoot. It flexes around weddings and your gear is ready to go.

What to do, the low-marketing way: sign onto the platforms that hand you the jobs instead of finding clients yourself. Apply to PlanOmatic, HomeJab, Hometrack, and CoStar (all already on the main board), plus list yourself on Thumbtack. They send you addresses, you shoot, they handle the client and often the editing. Once you have a few agents who like your work, they rebook you directly.

Honest money: $150 to $400 per home depending on size and whether it includes drone or 3D. Two or three a week around weddings adds up fast.

05

The honest plan

If you only do three things: (1) email 15 photographers offering second-shooter availability, that is the fastest real money and no marketing. (2) Bulk-upload your archive to Alamy and Adobe Stock, set it and forget it. (3) Put 30 of your best frames on Fine Art America. Everything else on this page is a bonus you layer in later. The paycheck job stays the priority; this is the money your photos make while you sleep.
