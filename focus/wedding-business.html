Let’s work on your wedding business

A free plan to get found online and stay first on every list.

Couples find you in two ways: through search (Google and the AI tools) and through people who already know you (venues, planners, caterers and past couples). Both have slipped this year, and both can be fixed for free with about an hour or two a week. Start with the audit, then work the weekly plan below.

Why the blog matters

Blogs drive your search traffic, and Google Business keeps it fresh

Every wedding post you write becomes its own page on Google. A couple searching “Evergreen Lake House wedding photographer” or “Boettcher Mansion wedding” can land on your post from that exact venue, see a whole real wedding there, and find you. Your home page can only rank for a few searches; each post adds another venue, town and season you can be found for.

  • More ways in. Each post is another page that can rank for the venue names and towns couples type.
  • Fresh means active. Google and the AI tools favor businesses that keep adding real work. A blog that stops in July looks like a business that slowed down.
  • Links back to you. When a venue or planner shares or links to a post, search engines count it as a vote for your site.
  • Google Business Profile. Weekly photos and posts there tell Google you’re open and busy, and that profile is what shows on the map when someone searches “Golden wedding photographer.” Post every new blog there the day it goes up.

The photos from this season are already shot. Posting them is the fix.

Every other week

How to write each blog post

1. Title: names, venue and town. “Brinna and Stephen’s Evergreen Lake House Wedding in Evergreen, Colorado.” 2. Say the town in the first paragraph. Write about 600 words: the timeline, the light, where you took portraits on the property, a tip for couples at that venue, and every vendor. 3. Link to that venue’s page on your site, and link every vendor’s website so they share it. 4. Rename image files before you upload them: “evergreen-lake-house-wedding-evergreen-co-01.jpg.” 5. Give every photo alt text (below). 6. Same day: share it to Google Business Profile and Pinterest, and email the link, plus the venue’s page of all your weddings there, to the venue and the planner.

You need alt text

Alt text: the words Google reads for each photo

Google and the AI tools can’t see your photos. They read a short line of text attached to each image, called alt text. Right now almost every photo’s alt text is just its file name, like “016 Brinna Stephen Wedding Berg Berg Photography.jpg,” which tells Google nothing about the venue. Good alt text says what is in the photo and where: “Bride and groom on the dock at Evergreen Lake House, Evergreen, Colorado.” It also helps people who use screen readers.

1. Install Claude in Chrome (the Claude browser extension) from claude.ai, and sign in to Squarespace in that same Chrome. 2. Starting with the newest post, replace the alt text on every image that is empty or just a file name. Write one plain sentence per photo that says what is in it, the venue and the town. Also give the post its own SEO title and SEO description naming the couple, venue and town. Do one post at a time and show me the changes before you save.” 3. Watch the first post, approve it, then let it work through the rest, one post a sitting. Claude only clicks inside your own Squarespace account, and you can stop it any time.

Every Monday

Venues: be the first name on every list you’re already on

You lose work to vendors who aren’t even on the list because they check in more. Pick the venue for the week (rotation below) and send a short note with:

  • An offer you’re running right now.
  • “Do you need more brochures?”
  • A link to your newest blog post from their venue, and the link to your page of every wedding you’ve shot there. Your site already has a page per venue, so this takes seconds.
  • Free use of a few images of their space, with credit.
  • “Do you have an open house or vendor preview coming up? We’d love to be there.”

Albums: the album at Mount Vernon brings in business. An album for Applewood would likely do the same, at $500. When money allows, it’s worth it. Until then, keep Applewood stocked with brochures and send them your Applewood page link.

Denver Botanic Gardens: they don’t publish a photographer list that we could find (only caterers and rentals). Follow up with your friend who works there, send the Canva portfolio you already paid for, and ask how photographers get recommended for weddings and events.

Every Wednesday

Planners: make a few of them your planners

You used to have planners who sent you couples. Planners recommend whoever lowers their risk and whoever they heard from last. A short personal note, not a sales email:

“Hi [name], I was thinking about the [venue] wedding we did together. Here’s the gallery, and you’re welcome to use any of these with credit. We have a few 2027 dates open. If a couple asks you for a photographer, we’d love to work with you again.”
  • Evergreen Lake House planner: Evergreen is your busiest venue, so this is your most important planner. Your March plan names Flora Moon Events. Lead with your best Evergreen gallery.
  • Yeti or Knot: she used to recommend you. Send a warm note and an unconventional-couple gallery, her specialty.
  • Sweetie Pie: you did a styled shoot together. Send a fresh gallery and one clear ask: “Could we be on your recommended list?”
  • Next styled shoot: agree before you shoot on delivery in two weeks, a full credit list, a joint blog or Instagram post, and one specific ask, such as an introduction to her next two couples. If two follow-ups bring nothing back, spend your time elsewhere.

Your vendor circle

Caterers, DJs, videographers and drone

  • Caterers get hired first and often get asked “who do you like?” Tag them on Instagram, photograph their setup before guests arrive, and send them the photos. Start with the caterers on both the Boettcher and Evergreen lists: Biscuits and Berries, Colorado Catering Co, Encore, Italic, Relish, Rocky Mountain Catering and Three Tomatoes.
  • DJs: reach out to the DJ from your last wedding and add him to your preferred DJ list.
  • Videographers: pick two or three you like and ask their starting prices once, so you have an answer ready when couples ask.
  • Drone: add the square on your pricing page for the drone photographer who has been training Jasper, linking to him. Or learn it yourself: more couples want drone shots. Drone learning page

Partner perks: offer this to every caterer, since couples usually book catering early (not always). Your side: couples who book a partner caterer get a free extra hour of your coverage, worth $400. Ask each caterer what they would add for free when a couple comes from you, something worth about the same that costs them little, like a late-night snack. A flat add-on works better than 10% off, which can be a lot on a catering bill. Low stakes, but it gets them talking you up. Once it’s working with caterers, offer the same to planners.
