About the job

Position Summary

The Senior Learning Designer serves as the lead designer for Innovative and Lifelong Learning (I&LL) and manages the full portfolio of I&LL courses, from development and launch through quality review, data review, and regular refresh. The primary focus of the position is Workforce Pell course development. The position also builds credentials for external audiences, including employers, maintains and develops I&LL self-paced online courses, supports the Summer Learning Series, and manages the learning platform for I&LL courses. The Senior Learning Designer uses a new model to work with faculty and subject matter experts that allows the designer to do 80% of the work, reducing the load on faculty to approximately 20 hours per course.

Workforce Pell

The main focus of this job: 20% of the role now, 50% by the end of year one. Nobody has built many of these yet. Know the rules cold so you can talk design, not policy.

What it is

  • Pell Grants for short-term job training, starting 1 July 2026, created by the 2025 reconciliation law (H.R. 1).
  • Students get a prorated award (about $2,200 a year on average; New America estimates $1,260 for 8 weeks up to $4,310 for 14 weeks). It counts toward their lifetime Pell limit.
  • Students can’t already hold a graduate degree.

What a program needs to qualify

  • Length: 150 to 599 clock hours (or 4 to 15 semester hours), 8 to 14 weeks of instruction.
  • Jobs: aligned to high-skill, high-wage or in-demand occupations; leads to a credential.
  • Track record: the institution must have offered the program for at least one year first.
  • Approval: the state (Governor, after the state workforce board) approves, then the U.S. Department of Education.

Outcomes it has to keep hitting

  • 70% completion within 150% of normal time.
  • 70% job placement, 180 days after completion.
  • Value-added earnings: median completer earnings minus 150% of the poverty line must be more than tuition and fees. First applies 2030–31.
  • Fail and the program loses eligibility, with a 2-year wait to come back.

Colorado

  • Run by the Governor’s Office with CDHE and the Colorado Workforce Development Council.
  • Applications go through the Eligible Training Provider List (ETPL).
  • Employer evidence accepted: the credential is on the List of Qualified Credentials, advisory committee minutes with several employers, or a multi-employer letter of support.
  • First approvals (October 2026): EMT certificates at Trinidad State; Arapahoe CC applied for early childhood, nurse aide, EMT, phlebotomy.

Where course design moves the numbers

  • Completion: orientation and onboarding module, clear weekly path, support easy to find in every module (AVC100), no busywork for working adults.
  • Placement: competencies from employers (advisory board), assessments that look like the work, portfolio or credential employers recognize.
  • Speed: a QM-indicator template so a course launches as soon as a program is approved; the program then needs a year of operation before approval, so build quality in from day one.
  • Stacking: noncredit course → credit → certificate → degree, which is Terry Bower’s Career Launchpad model.
  • Say it as an offer, not a claim: “I can help pressure-test which programs can realistically hit 70 percent completion and placement.”

What has been submitted so far

  • Colorado, first round (October 2026): five programs approved by the state, none with federal approval yet. Trinidad State Community College got two EMT certificate programs approved.
  • Arapahoe Community College applied for early childhood education, nurse aide, EMT and phlebotomy certificates, and hopes to cover more noncredit workforce programs like boot camps later.
  • Colorado's pattern: healthcare and public safety certificates first, because they already have licensure, employer demand and outcome data.
  • Nationally: NC community colleges (Wake Tech: BioWork biotech process technician, mechatronics), Forsyth Tech nursing aide; many existing programs miss the hour window (one CNA class was only 112 hours).
  • MSU Denver: nothing found yet. That's the job.
