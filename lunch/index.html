Where to eat, and how to get lunch on class days

Living at Williams Village, classes in the Engineering Center.

Goal

Get a real lunch into your backpack on the days you have class straight through, without paying extra or running out of swipes.

Audience

You, at Williams Village, on the 15-meals-per-week plan, riding the bus to main campus.

Process

Every place below has a number. Tap the map button on any of them to see exactly where it is on CU’s real campus map, then get directions from there. The badges tell you whether it takes a meal swipe or Munch Money.

The short version

  1. Buy lunch before you leave Will Vill. Village Market 2 opens at 8 a.m., is in the same building as your dining hall, and sells sandwiches. Use Munch Money. Put it in your bag and you are done thinking about lunch.
  2. Backup, and it is a good one: there is a cafe inside the Engineering Center 3. If you forget, food is already in your class building.
  3. Save your swipes for breakfast and dinner at Village Center Dining 1, right next door to you. Fifteen swipes a week is about two a day, so lunch should come out of Munch Money, not swipes.

Getting there, Route W

Your Buff OneCard is your bus pass. Just tap it. Regular fall service starts Thursday, August 20.

Route W runs a clockwise loop and stops at:

  • Bear Creek
  • Williams Village (yours)
  • IMIG Music
  • Physics
  • Math (closest to the Engineering Center)
  • Colorado and 30th, south side

How often: Mon to Fri, every 10 minutes from 7 to 8:30 a.m., then every 5 minutes from 8:30 a.m. to 6 p.m., every 8 minutes until 11 p.m. Weekends every 30 minutes, 10 a.m. to 11 p.m.

Heads up: there is no stop at the Engineering Center and no stop at the C4C anymore. Get off at Math or Physics and walk. Download the Transit app to see where the bus actually is.

About taking a box out of the dining hall

CU has free reusable steel takeout containers through an app called USEFULL, but it will not solve your six-hour day:

  • Only at Alferd Packer Grill (8) and the SEEC Cafe (14). Not at Village Center, not at the C4C, so you cannot box up dinner at your own dining hall.
  • Return within two days or it is $1 a day, then $25 if it is gone two weeks.
  • Not microwave safe. It is stainless steel.
  • Return stations are only in the UMC and the SEEC building, both on main campus, so you would have to carry it back.

Good for carrying a hot lunch across campus the same day. Not a way to stock your fridge.

Four things to ask the first week

  1. Does Violet Peak in the Engineering Center take Munch Money?Ask at the counter. If yes, your lunch problem is completely solved.
  2. Can I use two swipes in one visit at Village Center?If yes, breakfast plus something to carry solves everything before you get on the bus.
  3. Can I take a piece of fruit or a wrapped item out with my meal?The policy is not published anywhere. The staff will know.
  4. Does Village Center do order-ahead?Sewall advertises it. Worth asking whether yours does too.

Your plan, and why you should probably keep it

Both meal plans cost exactly the same. The cost is built into your housing rate, so 19 meals is not more expensive than 15. The only thing that changes is your Munch Money:

15 meals a week gets you $200 of Munch Money.
19 meals a week gets you $150.

So four extra swipes a week costs you $50 of Munch Money. That is the whole trade.

Stay on 15. Swipes cannot leave the dining hall, so they do nothing for a lunch you need to carry to campus. Munch Money is the part that buys a sandwich you can put in your bag, and the 15 plan gives you $50 more of it. Switch to 19 only if you find you are actually eating inside a dining hall more than twice a day.

If you do want to change it, September 14 is the deadline to switch for free. After that there is a $50 change fee. Email studentlifebilling@colorado.edu with your name, student ID, and the plan you want.

One more thing: Munch Money does not roll over to spring semester and is not refundable. Spend all of it.
