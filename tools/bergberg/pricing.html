The Problem

  • You booked 22 weddings in 2025 and 6 in 2026. Your prices went up, so price isn’t the main reason. Fewer couples are booking.
  • Couples on a tight budget are picking cheaper photographers, including budget brands run by bigger studios.
  • On small weddings you often pay a second shooter, and that eats your profit.

A Budget Option That Doesn’t Cheapen the Rest

The trick is to make the budget package smaller in ways that matter to a big Saturday wedding, so those couples still pick a regular package.

  • Limit the dates. Weekdays, Sundays and winter only. Couples on a budget often pick those days anyway, and you’d have those dates open otherwise.
  • Limit the size. Fewer hours, one photographer, 75 guests or fewer.
  • Fewer extras. No engagement session or slideshow. They can add them.
  • Put it last on the page. Show the regular packages first, so Vanilla Bean reads as a small-wedding option, not your real price.
  • Cap it. Take only a handful each year, so it fills empty dates and doesn’t replace full weddings.
  • Never discount the main packages to compete. That’s what makes a brand look cheap.

A second brand, like the budget brands Love and Lens and Antler Run run, would need its own website, listings and reviews starting from zero. That’s too much extra work, so skip it.

Second Shooters

  • Only bring a second shooter when the package includes one. Rocky Road and Vanilla Bean are Kevin only.
  • If a couple wants a second on a Kevin-only package, charge for it as an add-on. Set the price to what you pay your second, plus a little. Your website now prices an extra hour at $550 with two photographers and $400 with Kevin only, so a second is worth at least $150 an hour.
  • Use your new second-shooter contacts, so you aren’t stuck paying whoever is free.

AI for Culling

Culling means sorting thousands of photos down to the keepers. AI tools like Aftershoot do it in minutes, and you still pick the final set and hand edit every photo yourself.

  • Aftershoot’s culling-only plan costs about $10 to $15 a month. Its full AI editing plan is about $45 or more.
  • Photographers say culling that took a whole day now takes under an hour. That’s what makes a lower-priced package worth your time.
  • Most professional wedding photographers now use some AI in their workflow.
  • Try it on one wedding. If it saves you hours and you like the picks, keep it.

If you use it, change one line on Zola. It says, “We don’t dumb things down with AI, every photo is hand edited with expertise.” Change it to: “Every photo is hand edited by Kevin.” That stays true, because AI only sorts.
