Clients

Getting found is half the job. The other half is answering fast, delivering well, and asking for the review at the right moment. These scripts come from your old business plan, cleaned up.

Start here: Come here when an inquiry arrives, when a gallery goes out, or before a show. Each part stands on its own.

Wedding Client List

You have a spreadsheet of every wedding since 2021 and what each one paid. It’s called Wedding client list, and it’s starred in your Google Drive. It’s up to date through November 2026, and 2027 is at the bottom.

  • When a contract is signed, add a row under the right year: date, couple, price, venue.

Answer Fast

Most couples write to four to six photographers in one sitting. The first one to answer warmly usually gets the meeting, and the meeting is most of the booking.

  • Reply within 30 minutes when you’re awake. A short, warm reply tonight beats a perfect one tomorrow.
  • If they gave a phone number, send one text: “Hi, this is Berg Berg Photography. We just sent you an email. Feel free to text if that’s easier.” One text only.
  • Ask every couple, “How did you hear about us?” and write it down. That’s how you’ll know what’s working.

The First Email

Subject: Your [Venue] Wedding · Berg Berg Photography Hi [Name], So glad you reached out. We’ve photographed many weddings at [Venue] and would love to hear about yours. We’ve photographed weddings in the Colorado foothills for more than 20 years, and we’re ranked top 10 in Colorado by the Wedding Photojournalist Association. Our style is photojournalistic and timeless. Here’s a recent wedding at [Venue]: [link] The easiest next step is a quick, no-pressure meeting: [Calendly link] [Your names] P.S. One of our [Venue] couples said: “[one short line from a review].”

Couple Meetings

Start here: Use the same agenda for every meeting. If the couple brings questions, let them lead and use the practice answers below. If they don’t, start with the 30-second intro. Read the intro out loud a few times before your first meeting so it sounds like you.

Before the Meeting (15 minutes)

  • Reread everything they’ve sent you: their inquiry, their emails, and your notes if you met them at a show. Bring up one thing they told you, so they know you were listening.
  • Google their names. Look for a wedding website (The Knot, Zola or Minted) and LinkedIn, so you know a little about them. Don’t mention LinkedIn in the meeting, just use what you learned.
  • Check their date in ShootProof.
  • Open the blog post for their venue, plus one full gallery from that venue that shows the dancing.
  • Have the pricing page open.
  • Have a notebook ready to write down their answers.

The Agenda (30 to 45 minutes)

  1. Say hi and congratulations. Ask how they found you, and write it down.
  2. Ask, “Do you have questions you’d like to start with?” If they do, let them run the meeting. Go to step 5 when they’re done.
  3. If they don’t, give the 30-second intro.
  4. Ask your questions for them.
  5. Show a full gallery from their venue, or one like it. Include the dark dancing photos.
  6. Share the advice on choosing a photographer.
  7. Talk packages. Then say the next step: “We’ll send the contract and invoice tonight.”

After the meeting, send the contract the same day. Then use the follow-up plan above.

The 30-Second Intro

Thanks for meeting with us. A quick intro, then we want to hear all about you. We’re Berg Berg Photography. We live in Golden, and we’ve photographed weddings in the Colorado foothills for more than 20 years. Kevin has photographed more than 200 weddings. We both have art degrees, and the Wedding Photojournalist Association ranks us top 10 in Colorado. Our style is photojournalistic. We catch real moments as they happen, and we coach you through the posed photos so they feel easy. On your wedding day, Kevin is calm. He won’t boss you around, and he’s ready for any weather. We shoot digital and 35mm film. So tell us about your wedding.

Questions to Ask Them

  • How many guests are you expecting?
  • Do you have a venue? If not, where are you thinking?
  • Are people coming in from out of town?
  • Will kids or pets be in the wedding?
  • What time is the ceremony? Do you want getting-ready photos?
  • What matters most to you about your photos?
  • We shoot photojournalistic, with coached posing for the portraits. Does that feel right for you?

Advice on Choosing a Photographer

Couples like this because it helps them, even if they don’t pick you. Say it in your own words:

Ask every photographer to show you a full wedding, not just their best shots. Then look at the dark, inside dancing photos. Almost anyone can take good photos in daylight. Using flash well, with the right exposure, takes an artist. If the dance photos look flat, harsh or blurry, that tells you a lot.

Then show them your dancing photos.

Practice Answers

These are questions couples ask a lot. Many come from lists on The Knot. Tap one to see the answer. Practice them out loud until they feel natural.

What’s your style?

Photojournalistic. We catch real moments as they happen. For the posed photos, like family and wedding party, we coach you so it feels easy and quick.

Have you shot at our venue?

Show them the blog post for their venue. We’ve photographed more than 100 weddings at Boettcher Mansion, and many at Evergreen Lake House and other foothills venues. If you haven’t shot there, show a venue that looks like it.

Will there be two photographers?

Yes. Every wedding package has Kevin and Michelle or a second photographer. The second one catches the other side of the day, like the other partner getting ready and guests’ faces during the vows. For weddings of 50 people or fewer, Kevin can shoot alone, and that takes $500 off a 5+ hour package.

What if it rains or snows?

Kevin stays calm and plans for it. We find covered spots ahead of time and bring our own lights. Some of our favorite photos happened in the snow.

When do we get our photos?

You get a sneak peek within a few days. Your online gallery and slideshow come within 3 weeks.

Do we get all the photos and print rights?

Yes. You can download and print all your images. An engraved wood USB drive with every photo and the slideshow is a $50 add-on.

Do you help with the timeline and family photos?

Yes. We’ll ask for your timeline and a list of the family photos you want, and we’ll help you plan enough time for them.

Do you do engagement photos?

Yes. Every wedding package comes with a free engagement session.

Do you charge travel fees?

Travel is included within 1 hour of Golden. If it’s farther, we’ll talk about it.

Do you shoot film?

Yes. 35mm film starts at $300, for daylight hours during the ceremony and your portraits. We scan the film and deliver it digitally. Film has a warm, classic look that digital can’t copy.

Do you know a good videographer?

Yes. We have someone we love working with, and we’ll connect you after you book with us.

Can you do drone photos?

Yes. We work with a licensed drone pilot, and we’ll connect you after you book with us.

How do payments work?

A deposit holds your date. The rest is paid in parts through ShootProof, and it’s all paid before the wedding.

Do you make albums?

Yes. Heirloom fine art albums start at $750, and we design them after the wedding.

How many photos will we get?

Kevin, fill this in with your usual number for a 6-hour wedding.

What do you bring for backup?

Kevin, fill this in with your backup cameras, memory cards and lights. Couples ask this a lot.

The check-in email

This is the step that was missing. You get more reviews when you wait for their happy reply and then ask.

Subject: Checking in on your photos Hi [Name], Just checking in to make sure everything arrived safely and that you’re happy with your photos and slideshow. We put a lot of care into your gallery and want it to feel like your day the way you remember it. Is there anything you’d like us to look at or adjust? Hope you two are settling into newlywed life. [Your names]

The review request

Send this only after they reply happy. Ask every couple, and never offer a discount for a review.

That makes us so happy to hear. Thank you for telling us. If you’re willing, it would mean the world to us if you’d share a quick review: Google: [your Google review link] Zola: [your Zola review link] No pressure at all. We’re grateful for your kind words either way. [Your names]

Keep your Google review link and Zola link in your phone notes so you can paste them in seconds.

Albums

  • Albums feel like a separate decision at the wrong moment. Offer them in the check-in email, when the couple is happiest with the photos.
  • Try a small parent album as an easy yes when you deliver the gallery.
  • Think about putting a smaller album into your biggest package for free.
  • Finish any album a couple is already waiting on before you start new marketing. A waiting couple won’t write a review.
  • Display albums at venues are your best salesperson. The Venues tab says which venues need one.

Wedding Shows

What to bring

  • Printed albums, ideally from that venue.
  • Your competition awards.
  • A screen with your portfolio running.
  • Two iPads with your Calendly booking page open, so a ready couple can book a meeting right there.
  • A sign-up sheet: name, email, phone, venue, wedding date.
  • Brochures and business cards. Count them before every show.
  • A show discount card and a QR code card to your website.

Within 48 hours after a show

Email everyone on the sign-up sheet personally. Mention their venue or date, and include a link to a wedding you shot at their venue. If someone booked a meeting at the booth, send a short confirmation that same night.

Which shows

  • Venue open houses are better than big expos. The couples there already picked the venue, and you can show them their venue in your album.
  • Keep doing the Evergreen Lake House and Baldoria shows, the brochures at Applewood and Mount Vernon, and the Golden Wedding Show.
  • Skip the giant expos with hundreds of booths. You book through real conversations, and those rooms are too loud for that.

More Ways to Book Work

  • Mini engagement sessions. Twice a year, three or four Saturday slots on Lookout Mountain or at Evergreen Lake. Announce them on Instagram and Google six weeks ahead. Couples who book engagement photos often book the wedding.
  • Anniversary sessions. Three months before a past couple’s first anniversary, write: “Your first anniversary is coming up. Want to mark it with an hour of photos?” Past clients say yes far more often than strangers.
  • Couples who met later in life. You understand them, because you two met later in life too. Write the blog post for them (Blog tab), and tell planners who do small weddings and vow renewals that you love this work.
  • Styled shoots. A great way into a venue you haven’t shot. Before you agree, settle the delivery date, a full credit list, a joint blog or Instagram post, and one clear ask, like an introduction to the venue coordinator.
  • A trusted second shooter. Line one up before spring, so you never scramble the week of a wedding.
  • Drone. More couples ask for drone shots at mountain venues. A partner with a commercial drone license (FAA Part 107) is the easy way to offer it.
