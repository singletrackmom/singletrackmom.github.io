Syllabus Checker

AI tools & strategy

An automated compliance workflow that verifies submitted syllabi against district requirements and approved institutional language, returns actionable feedback to the instructor, and gives the department a single view of compliance across the term.

Open tool → PRD

Goal

Every term, each syllabus is reviewed by hand against the MCCCD required elements, program-specific additions, and approved institutional language that must appear verbatim rather than paraphrased. The review is slow, its consistency depends on who performs it, and the feedback loop is long enough that an instructor may learn in week six that a required statement was absent in week one. Meanwhile the department has no consolidated view of who has complied.

Audience

Two distinct users with distinct needs. The instructor must act, and therefore requires specific, actionable feedback. The department must monitor, and therefore requires an aggregate view. Conflating these is the design error most compliance systems make.

Process

Faculty submit through the department’s existing Google Form, preserving established practice. Submission triggers a review against the MCCCD Regulation 3.6 required elements, program additions, and the approved seat-hour and Regular and Substantive Interaction language. The instructor receives a response within seconds: confirmation of completeness, or an itemized account of what is absent, with the correct approved wording supplied inline so that no further search is required.

The result is simultaneously written back into the form’s existing response spreadsheet as status columns, giving the department a single sortable view of compliance across all sections without generating an inbox of individual reports.

Design rationale

The system is read-only. It reports; it never revises. No instructor should discover that an automated process has silently altered a document for which they hold professional accountability.

Approved language is read from its source at runtime rather than cached. When the department revises the reference document, the check is current immediately. A stored copy that silently diverges from its source is the failure mode of every compliance checklist ever written.

Verification is deterministic. Confirming that mandated language appears verbatim is a string comparison, not an inference, and a language model would perform worse at it by accepting paraphrase as equivalence. On an instrument subject to external review, a confident incorrect result is the worst available outcome.

The architecture requires no infrastructure. The workflow runs within the Google Workspace account that already owns the form, eliminating any need for authentication infrastructure, hosted services, credentials, or licensing. Ownership rests with the department rather than an individual, and the system persists through personnel change.

Known limitation

Document and word-processing formats parse reliably; PDF text extraction does not. A verbatim comparison against imperfectly extracted text produces false negatives, which are worse than no check, since they train users to disregard the instrument. Submissions in PDF are therefore flagged as potentially unreliable rather than silently failed. The durable remedy is a policy decision regarding accepted formats.

Status

Complete, and awaiting installation on the department account that administers the submission form. No data is collected, nothing leaves the district’s Google environment, and no student data is implicated at any stage, as a syllabus contains none.
