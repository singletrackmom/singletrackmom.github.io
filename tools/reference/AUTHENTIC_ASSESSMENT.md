# Authentic Assessment · Reference for Dial Your Course

**Goal:** Give Dial Your Course a defensible framework for flagging assessments that no longer measure anything, and for suggesting what to replace them with.

**Audience:** Me, and faculty who did not ask to have their assessment design questioned.

**Process:** Do not argue about pedagogy. Argue about **validity**. Then hand them a replacement.

---

## The question the tool asks

> **Can an AI complete this assignment, correctly, in under sixty seconds?**

If yes, **you are no longer assessing the student.**

This is not a preference. It is a **validity failure**. A multiple-choice quiz and a take-home essay are not old-fashioned instruments, they are **broken** ones. They have measured nothing since roughly 2023, and the profession has been politely not saying so.

Framing it this way matters. "Your assessment isn't authentic" invites a debate about educational philosophy that a busy faculty member will decline. **"Your assessment no longer measures anything" is a defect report.**

## Where OSCQR lands, and where it stops

| Standard | What it says | Limit |
|---|---|---|
| **31** | "Activities that emulate real world applications of the discipline, such as experiential learning, case studies, and problem-based activities." | Checks whether authentic work **exists**. Says nothing about the inauthentic assessments sitting next to it. |
| **45** | "Frequent, appropriate, and **authentic** methods to assess the learners' mastery of content." | Same. It is a presence check, not a redesign. |
| **47** | "Opportunities for learners to review their performance and assess their own learning." | Good, but self-tests are the *most* AI-vulnerable format. |

**OSCQR was written before generative AI made the question urgent.** It asks whether authentic assessment is present. It does not ask whether the *other* assessments still work. **That gap is where this section of Dial Your Course lives.**

## ⚠️ The trap: authentic ≠ AI-resistant

**The artifact never protects you.** A student can have AI write their portfolio case study, their project rationale, their reflection, their "personal" narrative. A polished final artifact proves nothing about who made it.

**What actually resists AI is process evidence.**

| Resistant, because | Example |
|---|---|
| **AI produces a finished thing, not a trail** | Thumbnails, drafts, version history, working files |
| **AI cannot respond to feedback it never received** | "What did your classmates say, and what did you change?" |
| **AI cannot defend a decision live and unrehearsed** | Walkthrough video, critique, oral defense |
| **AI has no access to a situated context** | A real local client, a campus problem, a place the student actually went |
| **AI cannot make a physical object** | Paper sketches, a photo they took, a mural, a printed piece |
| **AI cannot be a person in a room** | Peer critique given *and received*, in a named community |

> **The rule: assess the process, not just the product.** The product can be faked. The process cannot, at least not cheaply.

## The replacement ladder

When the tool flags a broken assessment, it offers a replacement at the same cognitive level. **Never just "this is bad."**

| Broken instrument | Why it fails now | Replace with |
|---|---|---|
| **Multiple-choice knowledge quiz** | AI answers it perfectly, instantly. Measures nothing. | **Applied identification.** Do not ask "what is hierarchy." Ask them to *find* hierarchy in a classmate's actual poster and *name the element* carrying it. Same concept, ungoogleable, and it feeds critique. |
| **Definition matching** | Same. | **Vocabulary in use.** Require the terms *inside* a critique post. The assessment is whether they used the word correctly about real work. |
| **Take-home essay / research paper** | AI writes a competent one in 30 seconds. | **A decision memo with evidence.** "Here were three options. I chose this one. Here is what changed after critique." Requires a real process to have happened. |
| **Reflection prompt** | AI writes beautiful, empty reflection. | **Reflection anchored to artifacts.** "Point to the specific change you made between v2 and v3 and explain the critique that caused it." Requires the v2 and v3 files. |
| **Summative final exam** | Broken by definition now. | **Portfolio + live walkthrough.** They present their own work and defend one decision. |
| **"Describe the process"** | AI knows the process. | **Show the process.** Submit the working files, the thumbnails, the version history. |
| **Case study analysis** (canned) | AI has read every published case. | **A real, local, current problem.** A campus client. This term. No published answer exists. |

## What makes an assessment survive

A checklist the tool scores against:

1. **Does it require a personal or local context** that could not be known by a model?
2. **Does it require process evidence** (drafts, iteration, version history), not just a final artifact?
3. **Does it require responding to feedback** that a specific human gave to *this* student?
4. **Does it require a live, unrehearsed defense** of a decision?
5. **Does it produce something with an audience beyond the instructor** (a published portfolio, a real client, a public artifact)?
6. **Would a student be able to use it after the course ends?**

**Three or more yeses and the assessment is probably doing its job.** Zero or one, and it is decorative.

## AVC100, scored against this

**The course is already AI-resistant, and it was never framed that way.**

| Assessment | Survives? | Why |
|---|---|---|
| Thumbnail sketches (20 of them, on paper) | ✅ | Process evidence, physical, iterative |
| Discord peer critique, given and received | ✅ | Specific humans, specific work, not fakeable |
| Part 3 revision from critique | ✅ | Requires feedback that actually happened |
| Miro board with process assembled | ✅ | The trail *is* the deliverable |
| Poster / postcard / animation builds | ✅ | Their own destination, their own choices, working files |
| Published Behance portfolio | ✅ | Real audience beyond the instructor |
| **Vocabulary Check quizzes** | ❌ | **An AI answers these in ten seconds.** Multiple choice + matching. |

**The only AI-vulnerable assessments in the entire course are the two vocabulary quizzes.** And they are also the two that are corrupted.

**That is not a coincidence worth ignoring.** The instrument that measures the least is the one that broke, and nobody noticed for a term, because nobody was learning anything from it.

**Replace them with applied identification**, inside the critique:

> *"In your classmate's poster, find contrast, hierarchy, and alignment. Name the element carrying each one, and describe the effect on your eye."*

Same vocabulary. Same content. **Ungoogleable, un-promptable, and it feeds the critique component instead of sitting beside it.**

## To build: the examples library

**This should exist and it should be public.** A repository of authentic assessment patterns, by discipline, that faculty can steal from. Nothing like it exists as an open resource, and it is exactly the kind of thing that gets a district AI committee to move.

**Structure:** for each pattern, the broken version, the replacement, what makes it resist AI, the discipline it came from, and a rubric.

**Seed it from:** AVC100 (design), the AVC248 walkthrough videos (portfolio defense), the simulation-as-assessment research in `stem-project/`, and whatever references Michelle has.
