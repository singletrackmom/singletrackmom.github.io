# OSCQR / Quality Matters crosswalk, and where the tool is weak

Grounding for honest scope claims. The tool is deep on the *countable/deterministic* portion of both rubrics and largely silent elsewhere. State this plainly.

## The rubrics

- **OSCQR (SUNY):** 6 categories, 50 standards, CC BY 4.0. Categories: Course Overview & Information; Technology & Tools; Design & Layout; Content & Activities; Interaction; Assessment & Feedback. Formative (3-2-1 scale + action plan), not pass/fail.
- **Quality Matters (Higher Ed Rubric, 7th ed., 2023):** 8 General Standards, 44 Specific Review Standards, 101 points, essential-standards threshold to pass. The 8 GS: (1) Course Overview & Introduction; (2) Learning Objectives/Competencies; (3) Assessment & Measurement; (4) Instructional Materials; (5) Learning Activities & Learner Interaction; (6) Course Technology; (7) Learner Support; (8) Accessibility & Usability. 7th ed. added an academic-integrity SRS + inclusivity language.

## Where a tool doing only seat-time + measurable objectives + accessibility + AI-resistance is WEAK

- **Course overview / getting-started clarity** (QM GS1, OSCQR 1-10): start-here, stated purpose, navigation instructions, self-intro. Mostly invisible to keyword parsing.
- **Learner support & tech requirements** (QM GS6/7, OSCQR 11-15): links to tech support, accessibility services, academic support; clearly stated tech requirements. Only partly covered.
- **Instructional materials currency & purpose** (QM GS4): current, cited, tied to objectives. Not checked.
- **Learner interaction & community / RSI** (QM GS5, OSCQR 38-44): the whole interaction dimension is "enumerated, not scored." **Biggest gap.** Where the RSI writer earns its place.
- **Grading-policy transparency & feedback** (QM GS3, OSCQR 45-50): stated grading policy, timely feedback, self-checks. Partial.
- **Accessibility as usability of the whole experience** (QM GS8): v1 does structural a11y (alt text, headings, tables), not holistic usability/navigation/readability judgment.
- **UDL:** both rubrics now lean on it; v1 names it but doesn't deliver it. See `UDL_3.md`.
- **Objective→assessment→activity alignment as students experience it** (QM GS2↔3↔5): v1's strength, but depends on outcomes surviving export, which currently they don't (PRD section 11).

Net: v1 covers roughly a third of both rubrics (the deterministic third). v2's generative + writer + UDL + builder features aim at overview, interaction/RSI, learner support, materials, and holistic usability.
