# GCC Strategies for Effectively Teaching Online — the model

**Goal.** Capture, section by section, the standard that every GCC/Maricopa online faculty member is trained and measured against, so any course we design or audit (Dial Your Course, the Advanced Data Science case study, Render, CopaMigo) is built to this model and not to a reading list.
**Audience.** Me (Claude) as a working reference, and Michelle when checking a course against the GCC standard.
**Process.** Walked the live Canvas course *GCC Strategies for Effectively Teaching Online: Online Version* (course 1153910) page by page, July 2026, and transcribed the actual requirements. Governing rubric named *in this (older) course*: **Quality Matters (QM)**. GCC's current standard is **OSCQR** (open-source, SUNY/OLC); QM and OSCQR are closely aligned. Align our work to **OSCQR** (see OSCQR_QM_CROSSWALK.md).

---

## Course shape
Getting Started module + 3 content modules, each: Overview → Units (numbered pages) → "Complete" assignments → Wrap-up → Reflection quiz.

- **Getting Started 👋** — welcome, meet instructor, meet facilitators, course expectations, introduce yourself (discussion), make the most of this training.
- **Module 1 Preparing to Teach** — Unit 1 online environment & learners; Unit 2 the online syllabus; Unit 3 the Getting Started module.
- **Module 2 Course Design** — Unit 4 online course design + alignment/backward design; Unit 5 designing engaging environments (discussion boards, collaborative tools).
- **Module 3 Teaching Online** — Unit 6 online presence & communication; Unit 7 supporting students (accessibility, challenges); Unit 8 course improvement.

## Two recurring spines
1. **Every assignment states an anticipated time as a range** (e.g., Backward Design Map "avg 45 min, as little as 10, up to 4 hours"; Online Teaching Plan "avg 1h30m"). Confirms Michelle's "every assignment states its time" rule — GCC does exactly this.
2. **"Add to your Online Teaching Plan"** — a cumulative instructor-facing planning doc built across the whole course. Reflection/planning assignments say **"AI cannot be used for this assignment"** (process evidence).

## The required Getting Started Module (assignment 8.3, rubric-scored)
A course must contain all six:
1. **Instructor welcome and introduction** — min = image + written intro; better = video (make one video, make it this); also audio or avatar (Voki) options. Approachable, personal + professional.
2. **Course orientation or tour** — explain course structure and where to find things; recommend a **short screen-recording video** walking the Canvas layout; option: syllabus scavenger hunt.
3. **Course expectations** — student expectations (attendance/participation, computer & digital-literacy skills, readiness quiz), faculty expectations (response time, grading), technology requirements, course/institutional policies, netiquette.
4. **Student resources** — GCC support links (help desk, library, tutoring, student services, disability, registration, bookstore) + course-specific.
5. **Student introduction activity** — icebreaker/community (partner interview, share-something, four corners); tie to course content; low-stakes.
6. **Student success assignment** — a low-stakes "do everything they'll do in the course" practice (syllabus quiz, a post, a check-in email, an intro).

GCC data point: students who pass the eCourses Student Orientation at 80%+ were **1.4x more likely to complete** their online course. Maps to QM standards (crosswalk to OSCQR) 1.1–1.6, 1.8, 1.9, 5.3.

## Course design (Unit 4)
- Canvas **modules**, each with: intro page (measurable objectives written **from the student perspective**) → relevant content → **practice activities** → **assessment** measuring those objectives.
- Consistent design/terminology across the course; **chunk** content; create a repeating pattern (e.g., prepare → assess → learn → interact → reflect); module intro page includes objectives + overview + **checklist of what's due**.
- **Backward design / alignment (4.4):** Course Dream (overall goals) → learning outcomes/competencies → assessments → activities → course materials, all aligned. Uses Maricopa Course Competencies. No AI.

## Engagement & RSI (Unit 5) — the "not just textbooks" core
- **"If your course is merely static online content, then you have created a virtual correspondence course."**
- Three interaction types required: **student↔content** (interactive lessons/SoftChalk, interactive video/EdPuzzle, read+reflect), **student↔peer** (discussion, peer review, group projects), **student↔instructor** (1:1s, email/phone, rich feedback incl. audio in SpeedGrader).
- **RSI (federal):** at least **two of five** substantive activities — (1) direct instruction, (2) assessing/feedback on coursework, (3) answering content questions, (4) facilitating group discussion, (5) other approved — delivered on a **scheduled/predictable** basis with timely responses. Courses cannot be "set it and forget it."
- **Discussion boards (5.2):** not required, but **peer-to-peer interaction is**; use boards only where they serve outcomes, else use reflection/other tools; write convergent/divergent/evaluative questions; require a **specific response task** (offer a counterview, ask a question, give an example, connect to content).
- **Collaborative tools (5.3):** Padlet, VoiceThread, Prezi, EdPuzzle, Poll Everywhere/Mentimeter/Kahoot, Google Docs, mind-mapping, etc.

## Online presence & communication (Unit 6)
- **Presence (6.1):** instructor roles — amplify, curate, aggregate, model; pre-course intro email, in-course welcome, **audio/video lessons of you explaining concepts**, personalized language, multiple contact avenues.
- **Communication (6.2):** email students **~1 week before** start; **weekly Canvas Announcements** on a schedule; **respond within 24–48 hours**; state availability/boundaries; **"Message Students Who"** in the gradebook to reach non-submitters/low/high grades (early alert); shared FAQ.
- **Netiquette (6.3):** a policy placed in the **Getting Started module + syllabus + touchy activity instructions**.
- **Feedback (6.4):** tell students what they did well, what needs improvement, and **next steps**; use written + **audio/video/screencast** feedback; SpeedGrader.

## Supporting students & improvement (Units 7–8)
- **Accessibility (7.1):** build accessible content (benefits all students, not only documented disabilities).
- **Challenges (7.2):** check-ins (a "how are you doing" item on a weekly quiz), **flexibility** (grace days, make-up, late penalties), **multiple due dates per week**, stick to a schedule/boundaries.
- **Improvement (8.1):** end-of-semester student evaluation, collect advice for future students, reflect and keep a change list, keep learning (CTLE, Faculty Focus, Chronicle).
- **Syllabus (2.1):** MCCCD Admin Reg 3.6 required items + GCC template; online-specific format/attendance/third-party tools; a **syllabus quiz** with an "I have read and agree" final item.

## Capstone artifact
**Online Teaching Plan** — instructor fills it across the whole training: presence, course organization/navigation, engagement/interaction, discussion boards, communication (whole-class + individual + grades), diverse-learner access, feedback, supporting struggling students. Scored complete / clear / comprehensive.
