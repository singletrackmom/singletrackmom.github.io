Dial Your Course

Reviews your course against all 50 standards of the SUNY OSCQR rubric, applies a consistent visual system to every page, and checks that the course fits the student hours its credit load allows.

Roughly half of OSCQR can be verified from a course file. The rest depends on what you do during the term. Those standards are listed at the end with guidance, never a score this tool did not earn.

Step 1 · Add your course

In Canvas: Settings, then Export Course Content, then Course. Studio and YouTube videos export as links, so no video files are needed.

Authentic assessment  OSCQR 31, 45 · and beyondAsks one question of every graded item: could an AI do this, correctly, in under a minute? If yes, it is no longer measuring your student. Flags multiple-choice quizzes, definition matching, take-home essays, and generic reflection prompts, and offers a replacement at the same cognitive level. Not a lecture. A defect report with a fix. The trap: authentic does not automatically mean AI-resistant. A student can have AI write a portfolio case study. The artifact never protects you, the process does. What a machine cannot check, and what to do instead

AI can only do so much. Roughly half of OSCQR is about what exists in the course file. A machine can read that. The other half is about what you do during the term or it needs a human to judge quality, and no tool can see either one. This is not an apology. It is the half of the rubric that matters most, and pretending to check it would be worse than saying so.

So instead of a fake score, here are the lessons. Each row below is a standard a machine cannot verify, why not, and the specific thing to do this week to satisfy it. Do them in order of how much they will change your course, not how easy they are.

OSCQR standardWhy a machine cannot check itHow to verify it yourself
38 · Regular and Substantive Interaction (RSI) RSI is a behavior not a file. It is about how often you show up, respond, and initiate. A course export contains none of that. Pull your own data. In Canvas: Course Statistics, and the Gradebook history. Ask: did I post an announcement most weeks? Is my median feedback turnaround under 72 hours? Did I initiate contact, or only respond? Write the answer down. If you cannot evidence it, it did not happen.
19 · Instructions are well written A machine can find missing steps. It cannot tell whether a beginner can follow them. Hand your directions to one person who has never used the software and watch them try. Do not help. Every place they hesitate is a defect.
29 · Variety of engaging resources “Engaging” is a judgment. Counting media types is not the same thing. Read your own course as a student for one hour. Note where you got bored. That is the finding.
35 · Captions on video The export contains links, not video files. There is literally nothing in the package to inspect. Open every video. Confirm human captions, not auto. YouTube labels auto-generated ones. Auto-captions run 70-90% accurate and fail hardest on your technical vocabulary.
40 · Learners get to know the instructor A “Meet Your Instructor” page can exist and be lifeless. Presence is not a file. Ask a student what they know about you. If the answer is your office hours, the page is not working.
45 · Authentic assessment of mastery Whether an assignment actually measures the objective is a pedagogical judgment. For each objective, name the assignment that proves it. If you cannot, one of them is wrong.
49 · Working gradebook Gradebook setup lives in Canvas settings, not the content export. Open the gradebook as a Test Student. Are weights right? Do totals make sense mid-term?
50 · Course feedback channel A machine can find a survey. It cannot tell whether anyone reads it. Add a mid-semester check-in, and act on one thing from it publicly. That is what makes students answer the next one.
13 · Unused tools removed from the menu Course navigation settings are not fully represented in an export. Look at your left-hand nav as a student. Hide everything you do not use. Every dead link costs you trust.
3, 4, 5, 44 · Syllabus, policies, grading These usually live in Syllabus+ or a linked doc, not in the course package. Open the syllabus. Confirm: campus policies, late-work consequences, accommodations, the approved seat-hours statement, and (for online) the RSI statement.

The rule this tool runs on: it will never tell you that you passed something it did not actually check. A course-quality tool that reports a fake pass is worse than no tool, because it buys you false confidence right before a review.

Nothing is uploaded anywhere. Your course package is opened, read, and rewritten on your own machine. There is no server, no account, and no vendor in the middle. A Canvas export contains no enrollments, grades, or student submissions by design, so there is no student data for this tool to receive.

Every estimate is a draft for you to confirm, never published silently. A wrong number in a module intro is worse than no number, because students plan around it. The tool proposes, you decide.
