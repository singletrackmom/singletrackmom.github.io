Step 2 · Choose what to check

Accessibility handoffCounts the videos, PDFs, and images that need attention and routes each to the right tool. It does not remediate PDFs, write alt text, or check captions — YuJa Panorama and Canvas Studio already do that, and this will not half-rebuild them.
Due-date patternConfirm every assignment is due on the days and time you choose. at
Measurable learning objectivesFlags unmeasurable verbs (“understand,” “know,” “appreciate”), suggests observable replacements from the right Bloom’s level, and checks the level fits the course. Uses a verb bank, not a model. “Understand” is a valid Bloom’s level and an invalid objective verb. The level names the cognition; the verb has to name the observable evidence of it. If you cannot observe it, you cannot assess it. Revised Bloom’s (Anderson & Krathwohl, 2001).
Align to competenciesEvery activity maps to an official course competency. Competencies are looked up live from the Maricopa curriculum site, so they are always current, no pasting. Course Maricopa curriculum ↗
Spelling and grammarFlag typos and grammar issues across pages and assignments.
Directions and accuracySteps are complete, in order, and match what is being asked.
Consistent formattingHeadings, buttons, and page structure are consistent throughout.
Apply a style guide to every pageRestyles every page, assignment, and rubric in the course to one palette and typeface, then exports it Canvas-ready. Contrast is checked against WCAG AA as it goes, so it cannot ship a failing combination. Only five typefaces, on purpose. Canvas strips the <link> tag that loads a web font, so Google Fonts fail silently and students see a default. These five are installed nearly everywhere. Everything exports as inline styles, the only kind Canvas does not strip on save. See the preview ↓
Seat hours: estimate student time, check the budget, verify the required languageEstimates how long each module takes, writes it into the module intro, checks the course total against its seat-hour budget, and confirms the approved seat-hours language appears verbatim. Department reference sheet. Total student hours = credits × 40, always. Load hours do not change the total, they change the split between direct instruction, in-class studio time, and outside work. Source: GCC Art & Humanities, Required Syllabus Language on Seat Hours and Direct Instruction. Other departments may differ; profiles can be added per department.

Style guide preview

This is what every page in your course will look like after the audit. It updates as you change the palette and typeface above. This is the fix, not another thing that will get stripped. Canvas sanitizes HTML when you save. It throws away <style> blocks, <link> tags, and CSS classes with no stylesheet behind them, which is why formatting you paste in today arrives plain. It keeps inline style="" attributes. Everything below is fully inline, with the styling attached directly to each element, so there is nothing for the sanitizer to remove. It holds. Two things to know: after a page is styled, edit it in HTML view, not the visual editor, which can reflow inline styles. And before you trust a whole course to this, test one page — paste, save, reload, look. Five minutes and you will know for certain.

Step 3 · What it found, and what it fixed

Status: prototype. This interface shows the workflow and a sample report. The audit engine uses no student data and keeps no PII by design; a live version pairs this interface with a parser and an AI model to read the exported course and generate the findings.

On time estimates: every estimate is a draft for the instructor to confirm, never published silently. A wrong number in a module intro is worse than no number, because students plan around it and credit-hour compliance depends on it. The auditor proposes, you approve.
