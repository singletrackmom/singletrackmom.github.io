Critique Helper

In the classroom

The words and the structure to give real critique, not just “looks good.” Open this before you post feedback on a classmate’s work.

How to give critique: describe, don’t prescribe

Your job is to describe your honest response as a viewer, what’s working and what isn’t quite landing, using the vocabulary below. You never tell the designer what to do. No “move this here,” no “make it blue.” You name what you see and feel, and they decide what to change. A suggested fix can also just be wrong, and then someone changes good work for the worse. Naming the effect keeps the solving with the person who made it.

What’s working

Name a specific choice that’s succeeding, in the vocabulary.

“One thing that’s working for me is ___ because ___.”

What’s not landing

Describe where your eye or attention got lost, as your experience.

“The part that isn’t quite landing for me is ___.”

Ask, don’t tell

Ask an open question about what they were going for. No instructions.

“What were you going for with ___?”

The elements · the nouns

The Elements are the raw material you have to work with. The Principles below are what you do with them. You cannot talk about contrast without value, or balance without shape, so learn these first. When you critique, name the element, then name the principle it is serving or failing.

Element of Design

Line

A mark with direction and length. It leads the eye, divides space, and carries energy: horizontal is calm, diagonal is tense.

aimless
directed

“The lines here pull my eye toward ___.”

Element of Design

Shape

A closed, flat area. Geometric shapes read as ordered and man-made; organic ones read as natural and relaxed.

scattered
deliberate

“The shapes feel ___ to me, which makes the whole thing read as ___.”

Element of Design

Value

How light or dark something is. Value, not colour, is what actually creates contrast and readability. Squint at your work: if it all goes grey, your value range is too narrow.

narrow
full range

“The value range feels ___, so my eye ___.”

Element of Design

Color

Hue, saturation, and value together. Colour carries mood and meaning before anyone reads a word, and it is the fastest thing to get wrong.

unrelated
a palette

“The colour makes this feel ___, which fits / fights the message because ___.”

Element of Design

Texture

The implied surface quality: rough, smooth, grainy, glossy. Texture adds richness, and too much of it adds noise.

noisy
Read me
restrained
Read me

“The texture is competing with / supporting the type because ___.”

Element of Design

Space

The area around and between things, and the depth you imply with overlap and scale. Space is a material, not what is left over.

flat
depth

“The space around ___ gives it room to / crowds it so that ___.”
