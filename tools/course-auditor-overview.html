Dial Your Course

AI tools & strategy

A course design instrument. It reconciles the workload a course actually demands against the hours its credit allows, tests whether stated outcomes are written in observable terms and evidenced by graded work, and evaluates accessibility and assessment validity. The SUNY OSCQR rubric is one of the standards it applies, not the frame it works in.

Choose what to check. Every check is optional, and the ones that matter most for compliance need no AI at all.

Goal

Course design carries a set of obligations that are checkable properties of the course itself, and are nonetheless checked by reading: whether the required work fits the hours the credit allows, whether objectives are stated in verbs a student can be observed performing, whether the outcomes a course claims are evidenced by anything it grades. Dial Your Course performs that deterministic work in a single pass, so instructor attention is reserved for the judgment that cannot be automated.

Its central concern is constructive alignment: whether stated outcomes, instructional activities, and assessment instruments cohere, and whether the evidence a course collects actually substantiates what it claims. Accessibility and rubric conformance follow from that, rather than preceding it.

Audience

Faculty and instructional designers preparing an online course for launch or quality review, including practitioners with no background in markup, accessibility standards, or credit-hour policy.

Process

The instructor exports the course from Canvas and opens the package in the tool. Seventeen checks span seat-hour compliance, the observability of learning objectives, constructive alignment, competency coverage, assessment validity under generative AI, Universal Design for Learning, and the SUNY OSCQR rubric. Each finding is reported against its governing standard. The tool then applies a unified visual system and returns a package prepared for reimport.

Execution is entirely client-side. No content is transmitted, no account is required, and no third-party processor is involved. A Canvas export excludes enrollments, grades, and learner submissions by design, so no student data enters the tool at any stage. The privacy position is architectural rather than contractual, and a district evaluating it can verify the claim by watching the network panel.

Design position

The tool reports only what it can substantiate. Twenty-three of the fifty OSCQR standards concern instructor conduct during term delivery rather than artefacts recoverable from a package; these are surfaced with the established method for verifying each, never with a score the instrument did not earn. A quality tool that issues a false pass is worse than no tool, because it manufactures confidence immediately before a review.

It is a faculty-facing self-review instrument. It reports to no one but the instructor and revises nothing without being asked. The distinction is deliberate: an instrument that reports upward is one faculty learn to route around, and a review process faculty avoid produces no improvement at all.

Alignment is not applied as a purity test. Graded work serving an institutional outcome rather than a course outcome, onboarding, help-seeking, advising, belonging, is not misalignment; it is co-curricular design, and the persistence literature is consistent that it belongs early and belongs graded. The standard the tool enforces is not that every assessment map to a course outcome, but that the designer be able to state what every assessment is for. A declared exception is a design decision. An undeclared one is an accident, and a reviewer cannot tell the difference from the outside.

Status

In testing across Digital Media Arts courses. The tool applies OSCQR, the SUNY Online Course Quality Review Rubric, released under a Creative Commons Attribution licence and therefore lawfully incorporable into derivative instruments. OSCQR is one of three bodies of criteria evaluated here, alongside constructive alignment and criteria no published rubric yet addresses.
