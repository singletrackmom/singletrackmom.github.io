Dial Your Course

AI tools & strategy

Drop a Canvas course in. Nineteen checks run against it: seat hours, learning objectives, alignment, accessibility, and whether a student could pass your assignments with AI. Approve the fixes, get the course back.

Choose what to check. Every check reads your actual course. Nothing is generated, and nothing that cannot be computed is reported.

Goal

Online course review is done by reading, so it is done inconsistently and usually incompletely. Dial Your Course checks the things that are actually checkable, in one pass, and hands the course back fixed.

This did not begin with a rubric. I have reviewed online courses since 2012, as a peer and lead reviewer for Quality Matters and OSCQR, and the checks here are the ones I kept performing by hand, cycle after cycle. The tool automates the countable part of that review and declines to automate the part that takes judgment.

It answers three questions. Does the work fit the hours the credit allows? Are the objectives written in verbs a student can be observed performing, at the level of Bloom’s Taxonomy the course reaches? Is every outcome the course claims actually evidenced by something it grades? Accessibility and assessment validity follow from there.

Audience

Faculty and instructional designers preparing an online course for launch or review. No technical background assumed.

Formal course review happens once a year, and for most faculty it is the first time anyone reads their course closely. That is a hard moment to discover a missing alt attribute, an outcome that was never attached to a rubric, or a module that quietly drifted past the hours the credit allows. This is a rehearsal for that review, not a replacement for it. It runs on your own machine, whenever you want, as many times as you want, and it reports to you and nobody else. Nothing is submitted, scored, or sent to an administrator. The point is that a course should arrive at its review already correct on everything a script can check, so the reviewer’s time goes to the judgment only a person can make.

Process

Export the course from Canvas, drop the file in, choose the checks. Nineteen of them run. Every finding names the standard it comes from and where in the course to fix it. Approve the changes you want, and the tool writes them back and hands you a package ready to reimport, plus a short list of the things only Canvas can do.

Nothing is transmitted. No account, no server, no vendor. A Canvas export contains no student data by design, so none enters the tool.

Some things a course package cannot carry. Attaching an institutional outcome to a rubric is one of them, because it is an account-level object that only Canvas holds. Those become a checklist, handed to you after the download, with the click path for each. This is the real list from AVC100:

After you export · 6 actions only Canvas can do

  1. Attach ILO-VC5 · Analysis: Critique & InterpretationTo the critique criterion on the last graded project of the term.
  2. Attach ILO-TE1 · Acquiring: Evaluates Creative ProcessTo the criterion where a learner articulates what they changed after critique, and why.
  3. Attach ILO-VC3 · Execution: Technical SkillsTo the build step with the fullest toolchain.
  4. Attach ILO-VC4 · Delivery: Visual ProductionTo the criterion where a file is prepared for output: resolution, color mode, bleed, delivery size.
  5. Attach ILO-W4 · Job Search: Marketing Self to EmployersTo the published portfolio, and specifically its written description.
  6. Attach ILO-TE2 · Risks: Seeks Untested ApproachesNot currently evidenced anywhere in the course. Consider adding a criterion.

Each item carries the full click path (Rubric → pencil → Find Outcome → domain → Import) and stays ticked when you come back.

Two versions

Version 1 works. Every check runs and it uses no AI. Use it today.

Version 2 is a preview and does not run. It shows what four capabilities would look like with an institutional AI model. Version 1 finds all four problems already. It cannot write the fix.

  • Alternative text. V1 finds every image missing it, and every image whose alt text is present but meaningless. V2 would draft it.
  • Learning objectives. V1 flags unmeasurable verbs and names observable substitutes. V2 would rewrite the objective in your discipline’s language.
  • Authentic assessment. V1 identifies which assignments a model could complete on its own, and what process evidence each rubric already collects. V2 would propose worked alternatives in the discipline: a rubric criterion that asks for the working file rather than the export, a recorded walkthrough of a decision the student made, a revision traced to named critique, a brief written against a local client instead of a generic prompt.
  • Caption quality. Automated checkers, including this one, confirm that captions exist. Confirming they are accurate is a different problem, and auto-captioning tends to fail on the exact terminology a technical course teaches. V2 would read the track against the audio.

The tool was built with AI and does not run on it. That is why one faculty member could build it, and why it cannot invent a finding. Version 2 needs a sanctioned model endpoint the college controls, and the options are set out in the PRD.

Seat time

The Higher Learning Commission has flagged seat time in online courses here. A credit carries a fixed number of student hours and the syllabus promises them, but in an online course there is no classroom to observe, so the course package is the only evidence the hours were delivered.

The tool estimates the time each assignment takes, lets you correct every estimate, and writes the total into each module overview. It then asks students at submission whether the estimate held, so the published hours can be corrected against the people who did the work.

What it will not do

It reports only what it can prove. Twenty-three of the fifty OSCQR standards are about what you do during the term, not what is in the file, so it lists them with the method for checking each and does not score them. A check that cannot be computed is never reported as a pass.

It reports to you and nobody else, and changes nothing without your approval.
