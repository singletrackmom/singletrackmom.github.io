Dial Your Course

AI tools & strategy

Reviews a whole online course against all 50 OSCQR standards, then fixes what it can. It gives every page a consistent, designed look, flags assessments that AI has quietly made pointless, and answers the question no instructor has ever been able to check: is my course actually too much work?

Choose what to check. Every check is optional, and the ones that matter most for compliance need no AI at all.

Goal

Catch the alignment, clarity, consistency, accessibility, and seat-hour gaps in a whole course in one pass, then fix them, so the tedious part is done in minutes and human judgment goes where it actually matters.

Audience

Instructional designers and faculty getting an online course ready for launch or a quality review.

Process, how it works

Export your course from Canvas and drop the file on the tool. Choose what to check: the six OSCQR sections, measurable objectives, seat hours, due-date pattern, competency alignment, and authentic assessment. It reads every page, reports what it finds against each standard, applies a style guide across the whole course, and hands back a package you import back into Canvas. File in, file out, and nothing is uploaded anywhere, because it all runs on your own machine.

It fixes rather than only flagging. It gives every page one consistent look, checks readability as you choose your colors, and repairs formatting that Canvas quietly discards, which is why some pages look plain even though they did not look plain when you built them.

It also refuses to score what it cannot honestly check. About half of OSCQR is about what you do during the term, not what is in the course file. Those standards get a lesson and a specific thing to try this week, not a fake pass.

The workload sliders

Every faculty member has quietly wondered whether their course asks too much, and until now there has been no way to check. This tool lists every assignment with an estimated student time, and lets you drag any of them. You know your students; the tool does not. Correct the estimates and the totals move live.

Then it does the arithmetic nobody does by hand. A course’s student hours are fixed by its credits, and a syllabus promises those hours. Dial Your Course adds up what the course actually asks for and compares it against that budget, per module and overall. It catches the week that is quietly overloaded, and the deliverable assigned twice that bills students for the same hours in two places.

Each module’s total is written into that module’s introduction, so students know what they are signing up for before they start. Nothing is published without the instructor’s approval: a wrong number in a module intro is worse than no number, because students plan around it. The tool proposes, the human confirms.

Status

In pilot. I run it on my own courses and I am sharing it with colleagues in the department. It is built on OSCQR, the SUNY Online Course Quality Review Rubric, which is openly licensed and therefore legal to build a tool on. It runs entirely on your own machine: no server, no account, no vendor in the middle. A Canvas course export contains no enrollments, grades, or student submissions by design, so there is no student data for the tool to receive. Read the PRD →
