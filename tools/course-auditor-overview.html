Dial Your Course

AI tools & strategy

A course quality assurance instrument that evaluates an online course against the fifty standards of the SUNY OSCQR rubric, tests constructive alignment across learning outcomes, activities, and assessments, and verifies that the enacted workload conforms to the course’s credit-hour allocation.

Choose what to check. Every check is optional, and the ones that matter most for compliance need no AI at all.

Goal

Course quality assurance is labor-intensive, inconsistently applied, and typically incomplete. An instructor verifies what time permits, and the remainder surfaces during formal review or an accreditation cycle. Dial Your Course automates the deterministic portion of that review so that instructor and designer attention is reserved for the interpretive work that genuinely requires professional judgment.

Its central concern is constructive alignment: whether stated learning outcomes, instructional activities, and assessment instruments cohere, and whether the evidence a course collects actually substantiates the outcomes it claims.

Audience

Faculty and instructional designers preparing an online course for launch, quality review, or program-level certification, including practitioners with no background in markup, accessibility standards, or credit-hour policy.

Process

The instructor exports the course from Canvas and opens the resulting package in the tool. They select the checks to run across the six OSCQR domains, measurable learning outcomes, seat-hour compliance, due-date consistency, competency alignment, and assessment design. The tool parses each page, reports every finding against its governing standard, applies a unified visual system across the course, and returns a package prepared for reimport.

Execution is entirely client-side. No content is transmitted, no account is required, and no third-party processor is involved. A Canvas course export excludes enrollments, grades, and learner submissions by design, so no student data enters the tool at any stage. The privacy position is architectural rather than contractual.

Scope

Dial Your Course is a faculty-facing self-review instrument. It is not an institutional quality-assurance system, it does not report to anyone but the instructor, and it revises nothing without being asked. The distinction is deliberate: an instrument that reports upward is one faculty learn to route around, and a course-review process faculty avoid produces no improvement at all.

Design rationale

The tool reports only what it can substantiate. Approximately half of the OSCQR standards concern instructor behavior during term delivery, notably Regular and Substantive Interaction, rather than artifacts recoverable from the course package. Those standards are surfaced with guidance and a concrete verification procedure, never with a score the instrument did not earn. A quality tool that issues a false pass is worse than no tool, as it manufactures confidence immediately prior to review.

Remediation accompanies diagnosis. The tool applies a single color system and typeface across the course, validates every foreground-background pair against WCAG 2.1 AA as it does so, and reconstructs markup that the Canvas editor discards on save, a silent failure that leaves carefully designed pages rendering as undifferentiated text.

Learning outcomes are evaluated for observability. The instrument tests each stated outcome against the revised taxonomy of Anderson and Krathwohl, identifying verbs that describe internal states rather than demonstrable performance and proposing observable substitutes at the appropriate cognitive level. An outcome that cannot be observed cannot be assessed, and an outcome that cannot be assessed cannot be aligned.

Compliance verification is deterministic by design. Confirming that mandated language appears verbatim is a string comparison, not an inference. A language model performs worse at this task, as it may accept paraphrase as equivalence. Generative methods are reserved for genuinely interpretive checks, and the instrument remains fully functional without them.

The system is human-in-the-loop by construction. No estimate, no rewrite, and no compliance judgment is published without instructor confirmation. Professional accountability for a course rests with its instructor, and an instrument that acts autonomously on that course misplaces it.

Workload and credit-hour compliance

A course carries a fixed allocation of student learning hours determined by its credit load, and its syllabus commits to that figure. Whether the enacted curriculum actually corresponds to it is rarely examined.

Dial Your Course estimates the time demand of each assessment, presents every estimate as an adjustable parameter on the premise that the instructor holds knowledge the tool does not, and reconciles the aggregate against the course budget at both module and course level. It identifies weeks of disproportionate cognitive load and deliverables assigned twice. Each module total is written into the corresponding module overview, addressing OSCQR Standard 2 and giving learners an accurate account of the commitment before they begin. No estimate is published without instructor confirmation.

Alignment, and the limits of alignment

The instrument constructs an alignment matrix from the course package: every stated outcome, and the graded evidence that substantiates it. It reports outcomes that are claimed but never assessed, and graded work that is attached to no outcome. Canvas stores outcomes as structured objects bound to rubric criteria, so this matrix is recovered from the course rather than reconstructed by hand, which is what makes it repeatable across a program rather than a once-per-review-cycle exercise.

Alignment is not applied as a purity test, and this is a deliberate design position. Graded work that serves an institutional outcome rather than a course outcome, onboarding, help-seeking, advising, academic integrity, belonging, is not misalignment. It is co-curricular design, and the persistence literature is consistent that it belongs early, that it belongs graded, and that optional support resources are not used by the students who most need them. Removing the credit does not improve alignment; it removes the mechanism by which the intervention reaches the student.

The instrument therefore requires that unaligned graded work be declared rather than deleted. The standard it enforces is not that every assessment map to a course outcome, but that the designer be able to state what every assessment is for. A declared exception is a design decision. An undeclared one is an accident, and a reviewer cannot tell the difference from the outside.

Universal Design for Learning

The instrument evaluates the three principles of the CAST framework against what a course package can actually evidence. Universal Design for Learning is not accessibility, and the conflation of the two is the most common error in the field. WCAG conformance removes barriers for learners with disabilities; UDL designs for learner variability from the outset. A course may be fully conformant and still afford a single narrow path through it.

Three indicators are recoverable from the package. Multiple means of action and expression is the most consequential and the least examined: Canvas records the submission modes each assessment accepts, so a course in which every graded item permits exactly one is a course in which learners have exactly one route to demonstrate competence. Where submission format is not itself the construct under assessment, it is functioning as a barrier. Multiple means of representation is evaluated by content modality, and multiple means of engagement by the presence of substantive learner choice.

The remaining indicators, whether options are meaningful rather than nominal and whether scaffolds sustain self-regulation across a term, are matters of professional judgment. They are surfaced for review and never scored.

Assessment validity

The instrument evaluates each graded item against two conditions: whether a generative model could produce a passing response within sixty seconds, and whether the item carries consequential weight. Only their conjunction constitutes a finding. A low-stakes, repeatable formative check is not a defect, and the tool does not treat it as one; its function is rehearsal, not measurement.

Where an item fails both conditions, the finding is framed as a threat to construct validity rather than a pedagogical preference, since an instrument a generative model can satisfy is no longer measuring the learner. The tool proposes a substitution at equivalent cognitive demand and supplies discipline-specific exemplars drawn from the authentic assessment literature.

The operative caution is that authenticity of task does not by itself confer resistance to generative substitution. A learner may direct a model to produce a portfolio rationale as readily as an examination response. What resists substitution is process evidence: iteration and drafting, documented response to critique actually received, situated local context, and live defense of a decision. The artifact does not protect the assessment; the record of its making does.
