Course Polish

AI tools & strategy

A tool that reviews a whole online course in one pass, then fixes what it finds. It restyles every page, rewrites the formatting Canvas keeps stripping, and answers the question no faculty member has ever been able to check: is my course actually too much work?

Choose what to check. Every check is optional, and the ones that matter most for compliance need no AI at all.

Open the tool Read the PRD

Goal

Catch the alignment, clarity, consistency, accessibility, and seat-hour gaps in a whole course in one pass, then fix them, so the tedious part is done in minutes and human judgment goes where it actually matters.

Audience

Instructional designers and faculty getting an online course ready for launch or a quality review.

Process, how it works

Export the course from Canvas. That export is an .imscc file, which is really a zip of HTML. Drop it on the tool and choose what to check: due-date pattern, measurable learning objectives, alignment to the official Maricopa competencies, spelling, directions, consistent formatting, accessibility, and seat hours. The tool opens the package, reports what it finds, applies a style guide across every page, and hands back a new package to import into Canvas. File in, file out, nothing uploaded anywhere.

It fixes rather than only flagging. It restyles the whole course to one accessible palette and typeface, and converts the formatting Canvas has been silently stripping into inline styles that survive.

The workload sliders

Every faculty member has quietly wondered whether their course asks too much, and until now there has been no way to check. This tool lists every assignment with an estimated student time, and lets you drag any of them. You know your students; the tool does not. Correct the estimates and the totals move live.

Then it does the arithmetic nobody does by hand. A course’s student hours are fixed by its credits, and a syllabus promises those hours. Course Polish adds up what the course actually asks for and compares it against that budget, per module and overall. It catches the week that is quietly overloaded, and the deliverable assigned twice that bills students for the same hours in two places.

Each module’s total is written into that module’s introduction, so students know what they are signing up for before they start. Nothing is published without the instructor’s approval: a wrong number in a module intro is worse than no number, because students plan around it. The tool proposes, the human confirms.
