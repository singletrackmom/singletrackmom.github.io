The honest framing (use this if Scrum comes up)

You are pursuing PSPO I this summer not certified yet. That is a strength when you frame it as deliberate, current learning, which is exactly the builder mindset this role wants. Never imply you already hold it.

“Finishing my Professional Scrum Product Owner certification is on my list for this summer. I have been working through the Scrum Guide and the Product Owner material, and what drew me to it is that the role is really about maximizing value and ordering work around outcomes which is how I already run my pilots.”
If pressed on what you have learned so far, answer with substance from the cards below. Confidence plus accurate vocabulary reads as “already doing the work,” which is true.

Vocabulary to drop naturally

TermPlain meaning you can say out loud
EmpiricismDecisions come from what you actually observe, so you test and learn rather than guess.
Inspect and adaptCheck the real result often, then adjust. The rhythm under everything in Scrum.
Product OwnerThe single person who maximizes value and decides the order of the work.
Product BacklogThe ordered, living list of everything that would improve the product.
Product GoalThe long-term objective the backlog is working toward.
SprintA short, fixed timebox (a month or less) that produces a usable increment.
IncrementA working, usable step toward the goal, shippable as soon as it is valuable.
Definition of DoneThe quality bar an increment must meet to count as finished.
Outcomes over outputsMeasure the value and results, not how many features you shipped.
MVPThe smallest releasable slice that lets you learn from real users.
Backlog prioritizationOrdering work by value, risk, and impact so the most important thing ships next.
Evidence-Based ManagementMeasuring value across current value, unrealized value, time-to-market, and ability to innovate.

Ready answers

“Why are you pursuing the Scrum Product Owner cert?”

“Because the Product Owner role names something I already do: maximize value and order the work around outcomes. I wanted the shared language and the discipline of it, especially for running AI pilots where you want to ship a thin slice, see real evidence, and adapt.”

“How would you run an AI pilot here?”

“I would order a backlog by student impact, ship a thin increment, and inspect it against real efficacy data before scaling, the same empirical loop WGU Labs uses. Outcomes over outputs, with a human in the loop and student data protected the whole way.”

“Tell me about managing a complex project start to finish.”

“As a director of instructional technology I led a campus LMS evaluation and RFP end to end, and earlier as a product manager at ProQuest I owned web-delivered software from requirements through launch. I think in terms of a prioritized backlog, a clear goal, and shipping in increments so stakeholders can react to something real.”

“What does a Product Owner actually own?”

“Value. Concretely, the Product Goal, the content and order of the Product Backlog, and making sure it is transparent and understood. The Product Owner can delegate the work but stays accountable, and only they decide the order.”

Three things not to say

Do not call yourself “certified” or “a certified Scrum Product Owner.” It is in progress.
Do not say the Product Owner runs the Daily Scrum or assigns tasks. The Daily Scrum is the Developers’, and the team is self-managing.
Do not describe Scrum as a rigid methodology with required documents or extra roles. It is a lightweight framework, and adding rules that are not in the Scrum Guide is the classic wrong answer.
