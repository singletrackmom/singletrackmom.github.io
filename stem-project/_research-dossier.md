## Why this file exists (the frame)

The goal: replace the AI-cheatable exam with a personalized, authentic task the student performs, where AI is the scenario engine, the simulated client or patient, or the examiner. The strategic target is Mines Online, which is 100% graduate and professional, so the pieces worth copying are the ones pitched at grad or professional level, and the method (personalized simulation-as-assessment) is the star, not the subject.

Two things to watch for as you read: (1) which examples truly personalize (adapt or generate a per-student scenario) versus same-for-all, and (2) how each one turns performance into a competency score. The best ones score the trace of what the student actually did, not a separate quiz bolted on after.

## A. Immersive and simulation-based labs with embedded assessment

**ASU Dreamscape Learn (DSL / NeoBio)** · undergrad intro biology
- Personalized: yes, two ways. The student's name and an avatar are inside the VR story, and a paired adaptive courseware layer (NeoBio) keeps testing and makes them re-master weak skills before moving on.
- Assessment: students demonstrate scientific reasoning and quantitative analysis through in-world tasks plus a linked 3-hour lab; the lab grade is the assessed artifact. Six 10 to 15 min VR episodes map to six canonical bio topics.
- Evidence: controlled study of 486 BIO 181 students (spring 2022), DSL median lab grade 96% vs 87% conventional, enjoyment 4.4/5, engagement about double. Scaled to about 5,800 intro-bio students by spring 2023, with carry-over gains into later courses. Licensable to other institutions.
- Links: https://dreamscapelearn.asu.edu/student-experience/ · https://news.asu.edu/20221021-creativity-vr-biology-lab-experience-leads-student-success
- Steal: put the student's name and avatar into the scenario so the task is personally theirs, and end each episode on a mystery to force discussion. Note the ratio: 10 min of immersion motivates 3 hours of real cognitive work outside it.

**PhET Interactive Simulations** · intro college physics, chem, bio, math
- Personalized: partial. Not per-student generated, but "implicit scaffolding" adapts to how each student explores, guiding without feeling like it is guiding.
- Assessment: formative and exploration-based, dynamic real-time feedback rather than auto-scored exams. Best embedded in an activity where the sim behavior is the evidence.
- Evidence: CU Boulder nonprofit, founded 2002 by Nobel laureate Carl Wieman. Design refined via think-aloud interviews; large peer-reviewed base (sims can beat real equipment for conceptual understanding in intro physics).
- Link: https://phet.colorado.edu/en/research
- Steal: "implicit scaffolding," design the environment so the productive path is the intuitive one, instead of bolting on instructions.

## B. Business and professional decision simulations with auto-scored assessment (closest to the thesis)

**CapsimInbox-XM** · undergrad + MBA · THE BEST TEMPLATE FOR THE BRIEF
- What: after running a business simulation, the student becomes CEO and must answer board-member emails ahead of a shareholder meeting.
- Personalized: yes, strongly. "Snowflake" technology gives each student unique, dynamically generated data to respond to, explicitly for academic integrity. No two exams match.
- Assessment: auto-scored across Strategy, Marketing, Finance, Accounting, HR, Operations, Sustainability, with an instant per-concept dashboard. Usable as a graded final or a diagnostic, about 2 hours.
- Evidence: used at BGSU, Penn, Florida, Tennessee; common for AACSB assurance-of-learning.
- Link: https://www.capsim.com/capsiminbox-xm
- Steal: this is the single best match to "replace the exam with a personalized authentic task." An authentic role task (answer real-looking emails as the CEO), auto-scored to competencies, with per-student generated data so no two exams are identical.

**Marketplace Simulations (Capstone / Advanced Management)** · advanced undergrad, MBA, EMBA
- Personalized: emergent. Each team's market diverges based on their decisions (branching by consequence); alternate storylines exist (3D-printed bikes vs early-PC industry).
- Assessment: Balanced Scorecard grading, plus assurance-of-learning and career-readiness competency reports built from gameplay data. AI coaching tools.
- Evidence: 700+ schools; assessment instruments validated at University of Tennessee and West Virginia University.
- Link: https://www.marketplace-simulation.com/capstone-simulations/
- Steal: consequence-based branching (earlier choices reshape the later problem) as an authenticity engine, and mapping telemetry to a career-readiness competency report.

**Forio (Harvard Business Publishing / Harvard Business Impact sims)** · undergrad + MBA
- What: browser decision simulations (Everest leadership, Data Analytics, a device-recall Organizational Behavior sim, Design Thinking food truck).
- Personalized: yes, branching decision paths, single and multiplayer, outcomes depend on choices.
- Assessment: embedded decisions scored against models, plus debrief analytics for instructors. Content authored by Harvard, MIT, Wharton faculty. Over 60% of MBAs worldwide use a Forio sim before graduating.
- Link: https://forio.com/hbplanding/
- Steal: role-play a specific job (a product manager handling a device recall) in the browser, where the assessment is the sequence of decisions and their downstream effects.

**Forage** · undergrad + early career · the "outlasts the semester" model
- What: free virtual job simulations built with real employers; students do 3 to 6 real-work tasks.
- Personalized: light. Onboarding recommends sims; tasks are fixed; students self-assess against employer model answers.
- Assessment: self-checked against model answers, 3 to 8 hours, verifiable completion certificate. Not auto-graded but authentic-task based.
- Evidence: 5M+ students, 125+ employers (Citi, JPMorgan, BCG, GE Aerospace), 300+ sims. Embedded in career services at Yale, Penn, Colorado State, Indiana; Pearson partnership. Flag: the "3.3x more likely to land a job" stat is Forage marketing.
- Link: https://www.theforage.com/
- Steal: the "compare your work to a model answer" self-assessment loop plus a portable, verifiable credential, a low-lift, no-PII authentic task the student keeps. Directly on your "tools that outlast the semester" through-line.

## C. AI simulated patients, clients, and examiners (professional programs)

**Oxford AI-assisted Simulated Patient (AI-SP)** · postgrad medicine / clinical pharmacology · closest academic cousin to AVC183
- What: an LLM chatbot role-plays a patient in an OSCE-like format; the student practices history-taking, communication, and clinical reasoning, then gets AI feedback against a rubric.
- Personalized: yes, the conversation branches on the student's questions; varied case library. Students asked for "decoy information," varied personalities, and patient-initiated questions.
- Assessment: AI-driven structured feedback against a published marking rubric (prompts and rubric are posted as appendices). Honest caveat from the study: AI scoring of interpersonal skill was the least reliable part, and tutor-facilitated live sessions worked far better than unsupervised self-use.
- Link: https://www.ctl.ox.ac.uk/simulated-patient-chatbot-for-medical-students-to-practice-communication-and-reasoning
- Steal: AI plays the client the student must handle, and a published rubric drives structured automated feedback. This is your AVC183 "AI as fictitious client" pattern, turned into assessment. Keep a human in the loop for the soft-skill scoring.
- Lead to chase (link not verified this pass): "ECOSBot" (Clinical Kidney Journal, 2025), a GPT-4o tool that simulates BOTH the standardized patient AND the standardized examiner for nephrology OSCEs, reported patient fidelity around 98%. Pull the paper for the "AI examiner" angle.

**Shadow Health (Elsevier), Digital Clinical Experiences** · undergrad + grad nursing
- Personalized: partial. The natural-language patient answers millions of unique free-text questions so each interview path differs, but cases are authored, not generated per learner. Mapped to AACN Essentials.
- Assessment: a "Student Performance Index" gives an immediate, quantified clinical-reasoning score built from what the student gathered, decided, and documented.
- Evidence: 700,000+ nursing students (vendor claim); case studies at Virginia Commonwealth and NorQuest (the latter cut faculty grading time).
- Link: https://www.elsevier.com/products/shadow-health
- Steal: the "Performance Index," a single immediate competency score built from the trace of what the student actually asked and did.

**i-Human Patients (Kaplan)** · undergrad + grad nursing, med, PA
- Personalized: yes on feedback. A new AI Tutor gives real-time individualized coaching on weak areas without adding faculty workload. Cases are deliberately standardized for objectivity.
- Assessment: every investigation and decision scored against best-evidence pathways for critical thinking, efficiency, and diagnostic accuracy; aligns to the NCSBN Clinical Judgment Measurement Model.
- Evidence: Kaplan's own 2023 to 2025 study (about 500 respondents), 87% said cases built confidence and competence, honestly labeled by Kaplan as self-report.
- Link: https://kaplan.com/institutions-educators/higher-education/nursing-schools/i-human
- Steal: decouple "the assessment is the same for everyone" from "the coaching is tailored to you." Standardize the case for fair scoring, then layer personalized AI coaching on top.

**Body Interact (Take The Wind)** · med, nursing, PA, EMS
- Personalized: partial. A physiology engine reacts in real time (untreated conditions deteriorate), so each run differs; cases are curriculum-authored. Adding AI natural-language conversation. Has dedicated OSCE scenarios.
- Assessment: a time-stamped report of every action, a performance score with penalties, and a list of competencies achieved vs conditions left untreated, built to drive a structured debrief.
- Link: https://bodyinteract.com/
- Steal: the minute-by-minute timeline report ("here is exactly what you did, when, and what it cost") as the backbone of the debrief, process evidence, not just a final grade.

**Oxford Medical Simulation (OMS) + OMS Assess** · nursing, med, PA, allied health
- Personalized: partial. Unlimited practice before evaluation; individualized feedback and progress tracking. Scenarios standardized for rater consistency.
- Assessment: learner actions auto-matched against clinically weighted scoring matrices for an immediate, transparent, objective score by domain; competency mapping and tracking flags gaps early. Formative or summative.
- Links: https://oxfordmedicalsimulation.com/virtual-simulation-for-assessment/ · https://oxfordmedicalsimulation.com/competencies/
- Steal: "scoring matrices" that make grading transparent and rater-independent, freeing the human examiner to observe and give pointed feedback.

**UbiSim (a Labster company)** · pre-licensure nursing (VR)
- Personalized: partial. A no-code "Intuitive Editor" lets faculty build and customize scenarios; not learner-generative.
- Assessment: customizable scenarios, recorded sessions, aligned to AACN Essentials, PEARLS debriefing, the NCSBN judgment model, and Next Gen NCLEX.
- Evidence: adopters include University at Buffalo, Community College of Denver, Texas Tech, Boston Children's.
- Link: https://www.ubisimvr.com/
- Steal: the no-code scenario editor for faculty is the real adoption lever (a director reportedly built a branching sim in about 90 minutes). Authoring barrier is the thing to lower.

**Full Code (Minerva Medical Simulation)** · med students, residents, nurses (CME)
- Personalized: no (case library), but replayable, with an AI patient for open history-taking.
- Assessment: a score across six core clinical competencies per case, plus a full breakdown. 260+ peer-reviewed cases; 0.5 AMA Category 1 CME credit per case.
- Link: https://apps.apple.com/us/app/full-code-medical-simulation/id1207424206
- Steal: tie a competency score directly to an accredited credit, the assessment IS the credential.

## D. AI roleplay / simulated humans, and "generate a scenario from objectives" builders

Honest status: a mature academic tool where you pick learning objectives and it auto-builds a scored branching simulation does not fully exist yet in the ID mainstream. The live instances of this pattern today are the generative-roleplay builders below. This is the white space your project could plant a flag in.

**Mursion** · enterprise + higher-ed / educator training
- Personalized: yes, and it is the differentiator. Three tiers: Mursion Live (a human "simulation specialist" voices the avatar in real time, human-in-the-loop), Mursion On-Demand (fully generative AI, adapts to input, no human puppeteer), and 1:1 Coaching.
- Assessment: "Mursion Insights" analytics, AI scoring tied to tested rubrics, records exactly what the learner said, latency, hesitation, and improvement across reps; refined on 18M+ minutes of first-party data.
- Link: https://www.mursion.com/platform/
- Steal: the explicit human-in-the-loop vs fully-AI split as a product decision, and rubric-anchored scoring where the rubric is ground truth and AI just applies it.

**Bodyswaps** · higher ed, further ed, healthcare, workforce
- Personalized: yes. LLM-powered roleplays customizable by the educator; performance data builds individual learning paths; captures semantic, speech-rate, and body-language signals. Signature "body swap" where learners re-watch their own performance as the avatar.
- Assessment: customizable AI roleplays + MCQ + open-ended voice-to-text, with aggregated class results for educators. 35+ scaffolded modules.
- Evidence: UCLA, Liverpool, Cornell, Yale, UNC Chapel Hill nursing, Fanshawe; NHS and Meta research.
- Link: https://bodyswaps.co/features/pedagogy
- Steal: the three-part stack (SME-scripted learning + educator-customizable AI roleplay + create-your-own assessment) and perspective-taking (watch yourself as the other person) as a reflection mechanic.

**Second Nature** · corporate sales
- Personalized: yes on generation. Describe a scenario in freeform text or upload content and the AI builds a full roleplay (persona + evaluation topics) in minutes. Fully AI.
- Assessment: a per-dimension breakdown (value articulation, question quality, objection handling) rather than one composite.
- Link: https://secondnature.ai/product
- Steal: "describe it in plain text, AI builds the persona AND the rubric together." Generating the evaluation criteria alongside the scenario is the clever part, and the closest thing to your "pick what you want them to do, it builds the sim" vision.

**Hyperbound** · B2B sales, call centers, also hiring assessment
- Personalized: yes. Turns an ideal-customer-profile description into a custom AI buyer in under 2 minutes; supports MEDDIC, BANT, Challenger, Sandler, SPIN, or custom frameworks. Fully AI. Multi-party roleplays simulate cross-functional stakeholders.
- Assessment: instant AI scorecards (talk ratios, objections handled, key moments) mapped to any chosen methodology; the SAME scorecard also grades real recorded calls. Explicit "certification via roleplay."
- Evidence: 45,000+ users, 7,000+ companies (vendor). Notably states it does NOT train on customer data.
- Link: https://www.hyperbound.ai/product/ai-sales-roleplays
- Steal: one rubric, two surfaces, the same scorecard grades the practice roleplay and the learner's real-world call, so practice and performance are measured identically. Plus certification-by-roleplay, and the no-PII data stance.

**Virti** · healthcare, life sciences, enterprise
- Personalized: partial to yes. AI "Virtual Humans" adapt in conversation; authorable content; instant per-learner feedback.
- Assessment: analytics on speech, text, and (where available) eye movement to pinpoint gaps and track improvement.
- Link: https://www.virti.com/
- Steal: multimodal capture (speech + gaze) as competency evidence for communication skills that are otherwise hard to grade objectively.

**Praxis AI** · higher ed + enterprise (adjacent, "AI coach," not an examiner)
- Personalized: yes. A "Human-First Digital Twin" turns a faculty member's expertise and teaching style into an AI agent inside Canvas, delivering personalized lessons and 24/7 interventions.
- Evidence: Notre Dame (15,000 prompts, 40+ faculty, student prompts climbing Bloom's over a semester), DeVry, Clemson, Per Scholas, ASU. Growth figures are vendor-reported.
- Link: https://praxis-ai.com/education/
- Steal: the "digital twin of the instructor" framing (personalization that preserves a specific human's voice, not a generic bot) and the security-first, deployed-inside-the-institution stance, which maps onto your no-PII, data-sovereign through-line.

## E. AI tutors and teaching assistants at universities (with assessment signal)

**Georgia Tech Jill Watson** · grad (OMSCS) + undergrad · directly relevant to CopaMigo
- Personalized: yes, per-student dialogue grounded in that course's own materials; verifies each answer with textual entailment before responding.
- Evidence: fall 2023 randomized A/B in a 600+ student OMSCS course. Jill accuracy 75 to 97% vs an OpenAI Assistant around 30%; more A's, fewer C's, higher teaching presence; harmful-error rate 2.7% vs 14.4%.
- Link: https://research.gatech.edu/georgia-techs-jill-watson-outperforms-chatgpt-real-classrooms
- Steal: restrict a generative tutor to verified course content and gate every answer through an entailment check. That is exactly the no-hallucination bar for CopaMigo.

**University of Michigan U-M Maizey / MiMaizey** · undergrad + grad · the build-vs-buy argument
- Personalized: yes. No-code platform; each faculty builds a context-scoped bot on their own data, behind a privacy-protecting "closed AI" wall (queries not shared out).
- Evidence: about 15,000 daily AI users; a Ross School tutor answered 1,000+ questions before exams, "94% as effectively as or better than" course staff, saving 5 to 12 instructor hours/week. 49+ institutions asked U-M for the playbook. EDUCAUSE case study, Feb 2024.
- Link: https://er.educause.edu/articles/2024/2/how-and-why-the-university-of-michigan-built-its-own-closed-generative-ai-tools
- Steal: the data-sovereign "closed AI" model (walled LLM, FERPA-ok, no vendor harvesting) is the exact build-not-buy case for the ARC work, plus the no-code "faculty build their own bot" pattern.

**Stanford M-Powering Teachers + Code in Place** (Piech, Demszky) · at-scale CS
- Personalized: yes for the instructor (feedback cites their own session); a separate LLM style-grader gave feedback to 8,000+ students who rated it slightly higher than human feedback.
- Evidence: peer-reviewed (EEPA 2023), improved teaching plus higher assignment completion. Code in Place reached about 40,000 learners.
- Link: https://acceleratelearning.stanford.edu/story/feedback-from-an-ai-driven-tool-improves-teaching-stanford-led-research-finds/
- Steal: auto-graded process feedback that quotes the learner's own transcript back to them, and the finding that students accept specific, nonjudgmental AI feedback.

**Stanford Tutor CoPilot** · human-AI live tutoring (K-12 study, model transfers)
- Evidence: first RCT of a human-AI system in live tutoring, 900 tutors / 1,800 students. +4 pts topic mastery overall, +9 pts for students of the lowest-rated tutors, about $20/tutor/year.
- Link: https://nssa.stanford.edu/studies/tutor-copilot-human-ai-approach-scaling-real-time-expertise
- Steal: "co-pilot, not autopilot." AI raises the floor for the weakest performers most. Reinforces the staff-buy-in framing: it makes existing staff better, replaces no one.

**Carnegie Mellon OLI / Simon Initiative** · undergrad adaptive courseware
- Personalized: yes, adaptive, continuous per-student feedback loops; OpenSimon adds intelligent-tutor authoring.
- Assessment: continuous embedded assessment ("learn by doing" with immediate feedback) feeding analytics dashboards. Long-cited "six times the improvement in half the time."
- Link: https://www.cmu.edu/simon/open-simon/toolkit/tools/learning-tools/oli.html
- Steal: assessment as a continuous stream woven into instruction, no distinction between learning and testing.

**MIT RAISE** · mostly K-12 / teacher PD / workforce; new PATH targets 2- and 4-year colleges
- Personalized: yes, adaptive context-aware tutor tools; curriculum designed to adapt to local context.
- Assessment: project and build-based (students create AI apps), portfolio not exam. Flag: strong reach numbers (Day of AI 2M+ learners) but thinner causal outcome evidence than CMU/Stanford/GT.
- Link: https://raise.mit.edu/
- Steal: "computational action," assess by having students build a working AI artifact that solves a real problem. Exactly the Render / CopaMigo ethos.

- Personalized: yes at the team level. A Training Management Tool is being built for automated team assessment, feedback, and after-action reviews, with adaptive synthetic characters that adjust to trainee behavior.

- Steal: biometrics as an engagement and competence signal (are they actually cognitively loaded and coping), plus a persistent individual learning record that follows the trainee. Your "data-informed iteration + tools that outlast the course" idea, at scale.

## Patterns to steal (the synthesis)

1. **Score the trace, not a separate quiz.** The best tools (Capsim, Shadow Health Performance Index, Body Interact timeline, OMS scoring matrices, Hyperbound scorecards, Army STE after-action review) build the competency score from the log of what the learner actually did. Design the sim so the graded thing is the reasoning path, which is also what makes it AI-cheat-resistant.

2. **Decouple standardization from personalization.** i-Human keeps the case identical for fair scoring but personalizes the coaching. That resolves the fairness-vs-adaptivity tension: standardize what you grade, personalize the help and the surface details.

3. **Same rubric for practice and reality.** Hyperbound grades the practice roleplay and the learner's real recorded call with one scorecard. The strongest "outlasts the semester" mechanic here, the assessment keeps working after the course.

4. **Generate the rubric WITH the scenario.** Second Nature's "describe it in text, AI builds persona + evaluation topics" is the emerging authoring pattern closest to your vision of "click the competencies, it builds a personalized sim." This is the white space in the academic ID world.

5. **Per-student generated data.** Capsim's "snowflake" data (every student gets unique numbers) is the cleanest anti-cheat move and the simplest form of personalization to build.

6. **Human-in-the-loop is a deliberate dial, not a limitation.** Mursion productizes both (Live = human-voiced, On-Demand = fully AI). Naming the tradeoff is itself a design decision, and the Oxford study is the honest warning that AI scoring of soft skills is still weak.

7. **Personalize from the learner's own materials.** LearnLM and Khanmigo both pull the student's real content or history into the task. Personalization does not have to mean generating a world; it can mean using what the learner already brought.

## Candidate builds for the Mines-adjacent flagship (grad / professional level)

The subject should sit in a program Mines Online actually runs at volume (Data Science or Engineering and Technology Management), with the method as the star. Two concrete directions, each borrowing specific patterns above:

- **ETM decision simulation.** Model on CapsimInbox-XM + Forio: the student is a manager inside an evolving project, budget, and risk scenario, answering stakeholder messages and making a sequence of interdependent decisions. Steal: per-student generated data (Capsim snowflake), consequence-based branching (Marketplace), auto-scored to competencies from the decision trace, AI plays the executive/client/team-lead (Oxford AI-SP pattern), transparent scoring matrix (OMS).

- **Data Science scenario case.** The student gets a messy realistic dataset and a real question, frames it, chooses a method, catches the traps, and defends the analysis to an AI stakeholder who keeps asking "why this model, what would change your mind." Steal: score the reasoning transcript (Shadow Health Performance Index idea), AI stakeholder as examiner (Hyperbound multi-party), rubric published up front (Oxford), personalize by letting the student bring or pick the domain (LearnLM pattern).

Either way, the buildable core is unchanged from the project plan: outcomes to scenario to rubric to AI examiner to illustrated UI to accessibility to a 60 to 90 second demo. Only the subject skin changes.
