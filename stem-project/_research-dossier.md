## The frame: what we want, what we don't

Goal: replace the AI-cheatable exam with a personalized, authentic task the student performs, where AI is the scenario engine, the simulated client, or the examiner. The method is the star, not the subject.

**We want** a visually impressive, browser-based (headset optional) immersive scenario where the student does real work and is graded on the reasoning. **Not** tutor bots or study modes: they help learning, they do not show a student doing the job, and they are not impressive; they are kept below only as context. Michelle already has the "AI tool students use" covered by CopaMigo and Render.

Two paths, and the sweet spot:
- **Do real professional work in a browser** (SimScale, DataCamp, cyber ranges). Authentic and un-fakeable, but it already exists and a notebook or spreadsheet does not look impressive. Low novelty.
- **Immersive narrative world** (Dreamscape). Impressive, memorable, novel if done browser-first for a non-biology subject. Risk: style over substance, and Dreamscape leans on VR and a Hollywood budget.
- **The sweet spot:** a browser-first immersive scenario (Dreamscape's look) whose GRADED core is an authentic judgment call (the do-the-work rigor), translating a healthcare mechanic onto a science subject, released as OER, tied to a PLE. Nobody has combined all of this for these subjects. That combination is the product.

**What NeoBio and Dreamscape do NOT do well (our opening).** NeoBio is ASU's adaptive courseware paired with the Dreamscape VR labs. It needs headsets and a studio production (not from-anywhere, not authorable by a normal designer); the grading underneath is still a lab grade plus remediation quizzes; the episodes are on-rails; it is closed, proprietary, and biology-only; its "personalization" is remediation, not a PLE; and the story can outrun the science. We beat it by being browser-first, grading the reasoning, giving real agency with consequences, going OER, tying to a PLE, and putting an SME behind the domain.

## Concept directions to react to (each novel, undergrad or general is fine)

1. **The Core Sample (field geology).** You are a geologist reading evidence (rock face, core, map, sensor) to make a real call: where to drill, is the slope safe, what happened here. AI plays a field client and a skeptical senior geologist. Pro: very visual, novel, unmistakably Mines, reasoning-gradeable, works for intro earth science. Con: needs strong art and a geologist SME (Claire?).
2. **The Scientific Method (general first-year science).** Meet a phenomenon, form a hypothesis, design the test, an AI "nature" reacts, revise and defend. Pro: universal, a pure instructional-design and assessment showcase, undergrad-friendly, no polished immersive version exists. Con: risk of feeling abstract, less industry flavor.
3. **Diagnose the Failing System (engineering or earth science).** The virtual-patient loop translated: a system on screen is failing; gather evidence, name the root cause, recommend a fix, defend it to an AI senior engineer. Pro: borrows the strongest healthcare mechanic, un-fakeable, visual. Con: can slide toward "real work in a browser" unless wrapped in a world.

Also still on the table (earlier grad-level leads, same build, subject skin swapped): an **ETM decision simulation** (be a manager in an evolving budget-and-risk scenario, CapsimInbox style) or a **data-science scenario case** (defend an analysis to an AI stakeholder).

**Recommendation for Claire:** concept 1 or 3, wrapped in a browser-first immersive world, general enough for a first-year student, graded on reasoning, released as OER, ending in a keep-it artifact for the learner's PLE.

**Translate each model to a Mines or science topic:**
- Dreamscape (immersive bio lab) to a geology expedition or a scientific-method world.
- AI-patient-you-diagnose (Shadow Health, i-Human) to an AI failing system (slope, aquifer, dataset); the specimen becomes the patient.
- AI simulated patient / examiner (Oxford) to AI as a field client, mine operator, or lab partner you question and advise.
- CapsimInbox (be the CEO, per-student data) to being the site engineer or analyst on your own unique data.
- Body Interact (live-reacting timeline) to a slope destabilizing or a reaction running away, with a defended decision log.
- Falstad (see the invisible) to visualizing stress, groundwater, a reaction, or a field.
- Cyber ranges (auto-checked, framework-mapped) to the rigor layer: competencies mapped to a rubric, reasoning auto-checked.

## The examples (catalog)

### Immersive labs (the model to emulate)
- **ASU Dreamscape Learn (DSL / NeoBio)** · undergrad biology. Named/avatar VR story plus adaptive NeoBio courseware; graded via a linked 3-hour lab. Evidence: 486-student controlled study, DSL median 96% vs 87%; scaled to ~5,800 by spring 2023. https://dreamscapelearn.asu.edu/student-experience/  Steal: name and avatar make the task personally theirs; end on a mystery; 10 min of immersion pulls 3 hours of real work.
- **PhET** · intro physics/chem. Grab-and-see sandbox with "implicit scaffolding"; formative real-time feedback. https://phet.colorado.edu/en/research  Steal: design the environment so the productive path is the intuitive one.

### Business and professional decision sims (closest to the thesis)
- **CapsimInbox-XM** · UG/MBA. Be the CEO answering board emails; **per-student "snowflake" data so no two exams match**; auto-scored to competencies, ~2 hrs. https://www.capsim.com/capsiminbox-xm  Steal: the single best "personalized authentic task replaces the exam" template.
- **Marketplace Simulations** · advanced UG/MBA. Run a company over rounds; each team's market branches by their decisions; Balanced Scorecard plus career-readiness reports from gameplay. https://www.marketplace-simulation.com/capstone-simulations/  Steal: consequence-based branching; telemetry to a competency report.
- **Forio (Harvard Business Publishing)** · UG/MBA. Browser decision sims (Everest, a device-recall OB sim); scored on the decision sequence. Over 60% of MBAs use one. https://forio.com/hbplanding/  Steal: role-play a specific job where the assessment is the decisions and downstream effects.
- **Forage** · UG/early-career. Free virtual job simulations with real employers; do 3-6 real tasks, self-check against model answers, keep a certificate. https://www.theforage.com/  Steal: model-answer self-assessment plus a portable credential (outlasts the semester).

### Engineering and physical-science visual sims (on-target subject examples)

- **Falstad Circuit Simulator** (free/open). Current and voltage as animated color and motion; real-bench-instrument feel. https://www.falstad.com/circuit/circuitjs.html  Steal: real-time visualization of an invisible quantity (the strongest "I can see it" here).
- **SimScale** (browser CFD/FEA/thermal). A real professional engineering tool, zero install, public gallery of real simulations. https://www.simscale.com/projects/  The closest thing to authentic graduate engineering work in a browser.

- **DataCamp Projects.** Real Python/R/SQL on real datasets in-browser, auto-graded. https://www.datacamp.com/projects  Authentic-work-plus-assessment for Data Science.

### AI simulated patients and examiners (healthcare, MODELS only)
Mines is an engineering school, so these are here for their mechanics, not their subject. Translate the mechanic onto geology, data science, chemistry, physics, or engineering. Never pitch a healthcare subject.
- **Oxford AI Simulated Patient** (closest cousin to your AVC183 "AI as client"): an LLM plays a patient the student questions and advises, scored against a PUBLISHED rubric. Caveat: AI scoring of interpersonal skill was the weakest part and facilitated sessions beat unsupervised ones, so keep a human in the loop. https://www.ctl.ox.ac.uk/simulated-patient-chatbot-for-medical-students-to-practice-communication-and-reasoning  (Lead: "ECOSBot," 2025, simulates patient AND examiner.)
- **Shadow Health** and **i-Human**: the score is built from the trace of what the student asked and decided (Shadow Health's "Performance Index"); i-Human standardizes the case for fair scoring but personalizes the AI coaching on top. https://www.elsevier.com/products/shadow-health · https://kaplan.com/institutions-educators/higher-education/nursing-schools/i-human
- **Body Interact** and **Oxford Medical Simulation**: a live-reacting system with a minute-by-minute decision timeline (Body Interact), and transparent, rater-independent "scoring matrices" (OMS). https://bodyinteract.com/ · https://oxfordmedicalsimulation.com/virtual-simulation-for-assessment/
- **UbiSim** and **Full Code**: a no-code editor so an instructor can author a branching sim in ~90 minutes (UbiSim), and tying the competency score to accredited credit so the assessment IS the credential (Full Code). https://www.ubisimvr.com/ · https://apps.apple.com/us/app/full-code-medical-simulation/id1207424206

The one mechanic to steal across all of them: score the trace of what the learner actually did, against a rubric they can see, with a human confirming the judgment.

### AI roleplay and "generate-a-scenario" builders (the authoring white space)
A tool where you pick objectives and it auto-builds a scored branching sim does not fully exist in the ID mainstream yet; the live instances are these roleplay builders. That gap is the flag to plant.
- **Mursion** · avatar sims. Three tiers: Live (human-voiced), On-Demand (fully AI), Coaching; rubric-anchored AI scoring, refined on 18M+ minutes of data. https://www.mursion.com/platform/  Steal: human-in-the-loop vs fully-AI as an explicit product dial.
- **Second Nature** · sales. Describe a scenario in text and AI builds the persona AND the evaluation topics. https://secondnature.ai/product  Steal: generate the rubric WITH the scenario (closest to "click competencies, get a sim").
- **Hyperbound** · sales/hiring. Custom AI buyer in under 2 min; one scorecard grades the practice roleplay AND a real recorded call; does not train on customer data. https://www.hyperbound.ai/product/ai-sales-roleplays  Steal: same rubric for practice and reality; certification-by-roleplay.
- **Bodyswaps** · higher ed/workforce. Educator-customizable LLM roleplays plus create-your-own assessment; "body swap" to watch yourself as the avatar. https://bodyswaps.co/features/pedagogy  Steal: SME-script + customizable roleplay + own assessment, plus perspective-taking.
- **Virti** · healthcare/enterprise. AI Virtual Humans; multimodal analytics (speech, gaze). https://www.virti.com/  Steal: multimodal capture as evidence for hard-to-grade communication.
- **Praxis AI** · higher ed (coach, not examiner). "Digital twin of the instructor" inside Canvas; security-first, in-perimeter. https://praxis-ai.com/education/  Steal: personalization that preserves a specific human's voice, plus the data-sovereign stance.

## Patterns to steal (synthesis)

1. **Score the trace, not a separate quiz** (Capsim, Shadow Health, Body Interact, cyber ranges, Army STE). Grade the reasoning path; that is also what resists AI cheating.
2. **Decouple standardization from personalization** (i-Human): standardize what you grade, personalize the coaching and surface details.
3. **Same rubric for practice and reality** (Hyperbound): the assessment keeps working after the course.
4. **Generate the rubric WITH the scenario** (Second Nature): the authoring white space, closest to "pick competencies, get a sim."
5. **Per-student generated data** (Capsim snowflake): cleanest anti-cheat and simplest personalization to build.
6. **Human-in-the-loop is a deliberate dial** (Mursion), with the honest caveat (Oxford) that AI scoring of soft skills is still weak.
7. **Personalize from the learner's own materials** (LearnLM, Khanmigo): use what they brought, no need to generate a world.

Design implication for our piece: an immersive browser scene, per-student data, score the decision trace against a published rubric, AI plays the client and examiner, a human confirms, and it ends in a keep-it artifact.
