Every online course has to clear a quality bar. At my college that standard is OSCQR, along with seat time, accessibility, and regular substantive interaction, and faculty check it by hand or not at all. Dial Your Course takes a Canvas course as input and returns what to fix. Four of the six tools are rule-based checks with no AI in them. Only the last two, authentic assessment and finding Open Educational Resources, use a model. The standard is not fixed to OSCQR either. Point the tool at whichever rubric your institution uses.

The six tools

1Syllabus Builder1 of 6 · no AIProduces a compliant syllabus from the course data you already have, and tells you what is missing. 2Quality Check2 of 6 · no AIRuns a course against a published quality standard and returns what fails, by module, with the fix. 3Style Guide3 of 6 · no AIGenerates Canvas-safe course HTML in a chosen palette, every style inline. 4Seat Time4 of 6 · no AIEstimates the real time a module asks of a student against what the credit hours allow. 5Authentic Assessment5 of 6 · uses a modelTakes an assignment a student can finish with a chatbot and returns one they have to perform and defend. 6OER Finder6 of 6 · uses a modelFinds openly licensed material for a thin module and verifies the license.

The take-home essay and the multiple-choice exam stopped being reliable the moment students had AI. The replacement is a task the student performs and defends. Agreeing with that is easy. Rebuilding a course around it, and writing simulations a student cannot shortcut, is the work, and this is the part I build by hand.

Authentic AssessmentAI-resistant simulationsReplace the AI-cheatable exam with an authentic task the student performs and defends, including a built suite of graduate data-science simulations.

The graduate data-science simulation suite was written by hand, scenario by scenario. It is not generated by a tool, and that is the point: the assessment has to be specific enough that a general-purpose model cannot answer it.

Students rarely fail because the help is missing. They fail because they cannot find it, or because the name the college uses is not the word the student would think to type. This work locates where students hit a wall and routes them to the person who can actually help.
