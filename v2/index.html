Work

Every online course has to clear a quality bar. At my college that standard is OSCQR, along with seat time, accessibility, and regular substantive interaction, and faculty check it by hand or not at all. Dial Your Course takes a Canvas course as input and returns what to fix. All four checks are rule-based and contain no AI, so the same course gives the same answer every time. The standard is not fixed to OSCQR either. Point it at whichever rubric your institution uses.

Read the full Dial Your Course case study

The four checks

1Syllabus Checker1 of 4 · no AIChecks a finished syllabus against program and college requirements, and tells you exactly what is missing. 2Quality Check2 of 4 · no AIRuns a course against a published quality standard and returns what fails, by module, with the fix. 3Style Guide3 of 4 · no AIGenerates Canvas-safe course HTML in a chosen palette, every style inline. 4Seat Time4 of 4 · no AIEstimates the real time a module asks of a student against what the credit hours allow.

Dial Your Course tells you what is wrong. This builds the replacement. These two tools use AI, because writing an assessment or judging an open licence is judgement rather than a lookup, and nothing is applied until a person accepts it. The same method builds a course from nothing, with a human subject-matter expert reviewing and owning every decision.

Design students graduate with a portfolio and no method for finding work. Building a career agent for one specific person, then another, is how I have been working out what that method is. Render is the version students build across a capstone, so they leave with a working agent of their own rather than a folder of assignments. The rest were built one at a time for individual people with no career services at all: a career-exploration letter, an aerospace watch, a seasonal work board, a job board for a photographer. Different people, different fields, the same underlying pattern, and each one taught the next.
