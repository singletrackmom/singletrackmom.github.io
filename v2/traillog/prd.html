TrailLog

Product requirements

OverviewLive buildProduct requirements

A Carfax for high-end mountain bikes. Every service, at any shop, logged against the specific bike, so the history follows the bike for its whole life and especially when it is sold.

1. Summary

Every service, at any shop, gets logged against the specific bike. The maintenance record follows the bike rather than the owner, which is what makes it worth something at resale.

2. Goal

Tell a rider what is due and when, based on how much they are actually riding right now, and keep a history credible enough to raise what the bike sells for.

3. Users and context

Primary: the owner who does not wrench. Owns a nice bike, rides regularly, relies on shops, wants to be told. This is the marketable audience.

Secondary: the bike shop. Shops add verified entries. That is the trust layer and the business angle. A rider may use any shop, though most have two or three regulars.

4. How it works

Pick a bike first. Forks, shocks, tires and wear all differ per bike, so each has its own profile.

Three clocks, not one. Suspension is tracked by ride hours, drivetrain and pads by miles, brake bleed and sealant and bearings by calendar months. Intervals vary by make and by use, since an e-bike eats chains and pads faster than a cross-country bike.

The projection uses a four-week trailing average, never an all-time one. Riding is seasonal. An all-time average produces reminders that match nobody’s actual summer or winter.

Ride hours are estimated at about 8.5 mph for mountain biking, which includes climbing, and replaced with real Strava ride time when that is available. Status is OK below 80 percent of an interval, soon between 80 and 100, overdue above.

The proof layer. Every entry shows as verified by shop, receipt on file, or self-logged with no receipt. That distinction is the whole product.

Suspension tunes. Fork and shock setup codes plus baseline pressure, rebound, compression and tokens, so a rider never loses their tune and a shop can reproduce it.

5. Data, privacy, and governance

The build holds its data in memory and resets on reload, on purpose, so it behaves identically as a local file or a hosted page. No accounts, no database, no personal data collected. Real Strava sign-in would need a small backend to hold the keys, which is exactly why it is not in version one.

6. Build and portability

One self-contained HTML file. No build step, no server, no dependencies. It opens by double-clicking. That constraint is deliberate for a first app: it can be shown to a shop owner on a phone with no setup.

7. Competitive landscape

Researched June 2026. Honest read: all four pieces of this exist somewhere, but no product combines them for the high-end mountain bike owner.

SAGLY is the closest and the one to study hardest. Suspension setup storage, Strava-synced reminders, change history. No shop or receipt verification, and the record does not transfer to a new owner. ProBikeGarage is the strongest general tracker, with intervals, component lifecycle and self-uploaded receipts, but no suspension tune fields and no resale transfer. Frame-Book and VELOWALLET chase the bike-passport angle with owner-to-owner handoff, but are general cycling and store no tunes. Strava My Gear is the data source, not a competitor. Theft registries like Bike Index own bike identity rather than service history, and are more likely partners than rivals.

The wedge: mountain-bike specific, shop-verified or receipt-backed records, that also hold suspension tunes, and that transfer to the next owner. That exact bundle is unowned.

The honest weakness: the moat is thin. SAGLY could add verification and a passport startup could add tunes. The hard part is bike-shop adoption, which is the same chicken-and-egg problem Frame-Book has. The realistic value before any shop network is a clean personal record with receipt photos that make a resale honest.

8. Open questions and risks

Are the suspension intervals right? A shop will say in five minutes. What makes a receipt photo trustworthy enough to matter at resale, a photo alone or a later shop confirmation? Is the business model a rider subscription, a shop partnership, or shop-branded so shops give it to their customers? And given SAGLY and Frame-Book exist, is this bundle a big enough wedge, or is integrating with a theft registry for bike identity the better play?

9. Roadmap

Out of scope for version one and deliberately so: real Strava sign-in, accounts and a database, a shop-side dashboard, in-app booking, and push notifications.

The order that matters: change the sample bikes to real ones, correct the intervals with a shop, then show it to two or three shop owners and write down what they say. Only after that does real Strava sync, accounts and a database earn the investment.
