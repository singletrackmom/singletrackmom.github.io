TrailLog

A service record that follows a mountain bike for its whole life, so the maintenance history survives the sale.

Open the running toolTrailLogA working multi-screen build. Sign in, pick a bike from the garage, and see what is due against that bike’s own service intervals. Sample data, no accounts.

Goal

People buy mountain bikes costing five to fifteen thousand dollars and then do not maintain them on schedule, because the schedule is genuinely complicated. Suspension is due by ride hours. Drivetrains and tires wear by miles. Brake bleeds and sealant are due by the calendar. Three different clocks on one bike, and almost nobody tracks any of them. Shops see the result constantly: good bikes arriving neglected because nobody told the owner what was due.

Audience

The owner who does not do their own wrenching. They ride regularly, rely on shops, and want to be told when something is due. The bike shop is the second user, because a shop-verified entry is what makes the record worth more than a self-reported one.

Process

A working build in a single HTML file. Pick a bike from the garage, then see what is due against that bike’s own intervals. Miles come from Strava, converted to ride hours at roughly 8.5 mph, and projected forward on a four-week trailing average rather than an all-time one, because riding is seasonal and an all-time average tells you nothing useful in March.

It also stores suspension tune codes, the fork and shock setup a rider loses every time they forget to write it down, and keeps a permanent service history marked as verified by a shop, receipt on file, or self-logged.

Status

Built and running on sample data, and a written specification behind it including a competitive scan. Strava is simulated. Nothing persists between reloads, deliberately, so it runs the same as a local file or a hosted page.
