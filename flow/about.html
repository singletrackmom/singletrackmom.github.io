Goal

Find Your Flow exists to take the overwhelm out of choosing a career for one young person. The problem is not a lack of options, it is too many at once. Standard career advice arrives as one giant list, assumes a four-year degree, and is written for confident, linear job-seekers, which produces exactly the freeze it is meant to fix. So the tool does the opposite: it serves up one realistic, low-school path at a time, in small doses, each with a verified outlook, a real pay number, a concrete first step to get qualified, and a real job that proves the field is hiring. The aim is to move from “I can’t think about this” to one small, real step: touring a school, stacking a free certificate, or applying to a single job.

Audience

One person, Jillian, and every decision is filtered against who she actually is: outgoing, a people person, happiest moving rather than at a desk, ADHD and school-averse but open to a short program or a single certificate, strong at AI prompting, not confident in math, not interested in working with kids, and near Golden, Colorado, cost-smart with family able to help pay for the right short program. If a career does not fit that profile, it does not get written. The voice stays low-pressure and neutral throughout, presenting each path as hers to choose, never a parent deciding her life.

Process

Find Your Flow is a small static site with a deliberate three-surface split: a router, the dated letters it archives, and a single living jobs board it never archives. A scheduled Claude agent, defined by a SKILL.md spec, runs around 6:00 AM daily, with a catch-up run when Michelle signs on if the machine was off overnight, so no day is skipped.

SurfaceRole and lifecycle
Routerindex.html reads today’s date and shows the letter scheduled for that day. Edited only to add a new edition’s date.
Daily letterOne dated vol-NN.html per day, a single career built as a five-card deck with a qualify box and a real-job box. Saved permanently and listed in the archive.
Jobs boardjobs.html, a single living page of apply-today, part-time roles she can land now. Updated in place, never versioned.
Archivearchive.html, an index of every past letter, newest first.

A full run picks a fresh career never covered before, screened against her fit profile and drawn from the U.S. Bureau of Labor Statistics fastest-growing list. It pulls the BLS growth rate and median pay, finds the real, accredited Colorado training path (preferring the least-school, most local, most affordable public option), and finds one real, currently-open job on an employer’s own careers page, verified live that day. It composes the letter in her voice, refreshes the running jobs board (adding still-open roles, dropping closed ones), publishes, and adds the edition to the archive and the router schedule. The letters are mobile-first, a single column built to read on a phone, in plum, sage, rose, and gold on cream, solid colors only, curly quotes, no em dashes.

Privacy

Find Your Flow collects nothing. It is static, with no forms, logins, or tracking, and nothing about her is stored or transmitted. It is noindexed and unlinked, a private page just for her.

Status

Find Your Flow is a personal build for family, in daily use. It is not a pilot, not a product, and not headed for any rollout. It stays factual by default: real BLS numbers, no inflated pay or outlook, no degree-inflation, and where a career needs school or a move, it says so plainly. It stays one gentle letter a day, her choice, never a list to drown in.
