Medical device sales · the people person in the operating room

What it is

You help doctors use the tools

Medical device companies make the things surgeons and clinics use every day: cameras, implants, surgical tools, monitors. A sales rep looks after a territory of hospitals and clinics. Most days you drive between them, stand in on surgeries to make sure the equipment works and the team knows how to use it, answer questions, show new products, and build the kind of trust that makes people call you first. Pharmaceutical sales is a close cousin: the same idea, visiting clinics about medicines. Job titles to search: associate sales representative, sales associate, clinical specialist, onsite specialist.

Why it might fit you

Outgoing, on your feet, and fluent in clinic

This job is mostly talking with people and moving: the car, the hospital, the operating room, the next clinic. Very little desk time. Your medical assistant certificate gives you the medical words, anatomy basics, HIPAA, and a feel for how a clinic day runs, which is exactly what new reps have to learn on the job. Your customers are grown-ups: surgeons, nurses, and office managers. The honest part: sales has goals, some days start early with a surgery, and pay often includes a bonus tied to sales. The math is mostly keeping track of your own numbers.

The money and the outlook

About $105,000, with a bachelor's

The government says reps who sell technical and scientific products, which includes medical instruments and pharmaceuticals, earn a median of $104,920 a year (May 2025). Across all wholesale and manufacturing sales reps the median is $76,460. The number of these jobs is projected to stay about the same over the next ten years, but there are still about 123,400 openings a year. For medical and scientific products, a bachelor's degree is the usual ask.

A way in before the degree

Support surgeries while you study

Some device companies hire people with a high school diploma to support their equipment in the operating room, like the job below. It is a paid way to learn the products, get used to the OR, and meet the sales reps, all while you work toward a bachelor's. Healthcare experience, like yours, helps here.

One real job, live right now

OnSite Specialist, Stryker

Denver area hospital · $23.74 to $32.12 an hour (posted range) · full time

Stryker is one of the biggest medical device companies. In this job you are based at a hospital: you set up Stryker camera towers, lights, and screens the way each surgeon likes, fix problems during surgery, clean and restock afterward, and teach the OR staff how to use the products. They ask for a high school diploma, and they prefer an associate's, a bachelor's, or a certification, plus operating room experience. Heads up: shifts can rotate and can include on-call, nights, and weekends, and Stryker says the exact pay range depends on location. Posted October 2, checked live today.

Where to study, close to home

Two years at Red Rocks, then a bachelor's

  • Red Rocks · Business associate (AA designated degree), 60 credits, about 2 years, in Lakewood or online. Built to transfer into a bachelor's.Start
  • Red Rocks · Bachelor of Applied Science in Business Administration, 120 to 121 credits, built on a Red Rocks business AAS, so you can finish a bachelor's without leaving Red Rocks. The page does not list the cost, so that is a question to ask.Option
  • MSU Denver · Professional Selling B.A., on campus or fully online, $326.25 a credit for Colorado residents (2026-27 rate). A nationally recognized sales center with networking events and role plays with real companies. The page does not list total credits; most bachelor's are about 120.Finish

Rather major in something else, like psychology? MSU Denver also offers a Sales Minor and a Sales Certificate you can add on. You do not need a bachelor's for hospital support jobs like the one above. You will want one for most medical device and pharmaceutical sales rep jobs. Everything here stops at a bachelor's.
