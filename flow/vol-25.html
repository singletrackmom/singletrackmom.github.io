Just one job today

Court reporter

Still in the law world you liked, but from the best seat in the room. A court reporter captures every single word said in a trial or a deposition and turns it into the official record. Here is the exciting part: there is a nationwide shortage, so Colorado courts are actively hiring, and a newer digital path lets you skip the old two-year steno school.

What you actually do

You are the official record

You sit in on hearings, trials, and depositions and capture every word, either speaking quietly into a covered mic (voice writing) or running the digital recording setup, then you review it into a clean transcript. Nothing is on the record until you say it is. It rewards a sharp ear, focus, and being cool under pressure, and no needles, no blood, ever.

Who hires them

The courthouse is right here in Golden

The Colorado Judicial Branch hires court reporters for courts in every county, and the First Judicial District courthouse for Jefferson County sits right in Golden, minutes from the house. Law firms and reporting agencies also hire for depositions, and captioning work you can do from home. Because of the shortage, they want people now.

The life · the perk

Good pay, and you can freelance

A court job comes with state benefits and steady weekday hours. But the real perk is freedom: many reporters go freelance, pick their own days, and the fastest realtime and captioning reporters clear well past $100K. You can build a life around it, more when you want it, less when you want a snow day.

How to get in

Skip the two-year steno school

The old way was two years of stenography. The new way is far shorter: a digital court reporter or voice-writing program you can do largely online in months, then a certification exam. Front Range Community College runs a Digital Court Reporter program, and Colorado allows voice writers in its courts. One online program starts this, not a whole degree.

How to get qualified (just this one job)

The big news: you do not need the old two-year stenography school. Colorado is one of the states that lets voice writers and digital reporters work in its courts, and that path is mostly online and measured in months.

The smallest first step: the Digital Court Reporter program at Front Range Community College (online), which preps you for the AAERT Certified Electronic Reporter (CER) exam, the credential courts and agencies look for. Colorado State University runs a similar online program if you want to compare.

A real job in this field, and what it pays

Court Reporter, Colorado Judicial Branch

State of Colorado · courts across all 22 judicial districts, Golden included · field median about $67,310, top freelance reporters clear $100K, plus state benefits

The Colorado Judicial Branch keeps court reporter roles open at several levels because of the shortage, capturing verbatim records of hearings and trials for judges across the state, including the Jefferson County courthouse in Golden. You apply right on their careers site with a resume, and higher levels reward certification, which is exactly what the digital program above builds toward.
