Find Your Flow

Case study · Personal Projects

OverviewLive toolPRD

A gentle career-exploration agent that serves one realistic, low-barrier career path a day, each with a real open job showing the true pay and day-to-day.

Goal

Take the overwhelm out of choosing a career by offering one honest option at a time, in small doses. The aim is to move from “I can’t think about this” to one small real step: touring a school, stacking a free certificate, or applying to a single job.

Find Your Flow is a daily career-exploration newsletter for a young person figuring out what to do next. A scheduled agent publishes one short, designed letter a day, each built around a single realistic, low-barrier career. Every edition shows the real job outlook and pay, a concrete way to get qualified, and one real, currently-open job posting so the field is always paired with proof it is hiring.

Audience

A young person exploring careers, someone who freezes at “what do you want to do with your life?” when it arrives as one giant list. The tone stays gentle and encouraging, presenting each path as an option to consider rather than an instruction to follow.

Process

It is a personal build, a daily agent that runs on a schedule. Each morning it picks a fresh career, verifies the growth rate and median pay against the U.S. Bureau of Labor Statistics, finds a real local training path, and confirms one live job on an employer’s own careers page. It then composes the letter into a locked, phone-first template and keeps a running board of jobs to apply to today. The site is static, with no forms, logins, or tracking, and nothing personal is collected.

Outcomes

The tool did what it was built to do, so it has been retired.

The reader worked through a run of daily letters, each one a different career with verified pay and a real local training path, and used them to narrow rather than to decide all at once. She settled on a direction, enrolled in school after twenty-one, and has since taken a part-time job while she studies.

That sequence is the whole design working. The letters were never meant to produce a decision on any given morning. They were meant to make the question small enough to look at, one option at a time, until a direction became obvious. Twenty-seven volumes went out. The one that mattered was whichever one landed on a good day.

What it says about the pattern

This is the only tool in the set with a finished outcome rather than a status. It is also the smallest and the least technically interesting, which is the useful lesson: the constraint that made it work was one honest option per day, delivered where the reader already was, with nothing to log into and no dashboard to check.

Everything in it was verified before it was sent. Growth and median pay against the Bureau of Labor Statistics, a real local training path, and one live posting confirmed on an employer’s own careers page. A career newsletter that recommends a job that does not exist is worse than no newsletter.

Status

Retired, having succeeded. The daily agent is switched off and the twenty-seven volumes remain readable in the archive. It can be restarted for a different reader by changing the location and the starting interests, which is the only configuration it ever needed.
