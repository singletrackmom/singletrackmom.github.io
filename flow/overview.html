Find Your Flow

Personal Agents · Case study

OverviewLive toolPRD

A gentle, scheduled career-exploration letter: one realistic career at a time, each with verified pay, a Colorado path to get there and one real open job.

Goal

Take the overwhelm out of choosing a career by offering one honest option at a time. The aim is to move from “I can’t think about this” to one small, real step: reading a program page, attending an info session or talking to someone in the field.

Audience

A young adult exploring careers who freezes when the question arrives as one giant list. Presenting one option at a time keeps the cognitive load small, and each letter builds career self-efficacy by showing a path that is reachable, not just possible.

Process

A scheduled agent picks a career not yet covered, verifies the outlook and median pay with the U.S. Bureau of Labor Statistics, maps the Colorado path from the least schooling and most affordable public option up (certificate, two-year or bachelor’s, never beyond), and confirms one live job on an employer’s own careers page. It publishes the letter in a fixed five-card, phone-first format and adds it to the archive.

Technology

A scheduled AI agent working from a written rules file, publishing static, noindexed pages with no logins, forms or tracking.

Outcomes

Thirty letters so far, each a different career. The letters were never meant to produce a decision on a given morning. They make the question small enough to look at, one option at a time, and the reader has used them to narrow rather than to decide all at once.

References

Lent, R. W., Brown, S. D., & Hackett, G. (1994). Toward a unifying social cognitive theory of career and academic interest, choice, and performance. Journal of Vocational Behavior, 45(1), 79–122.
