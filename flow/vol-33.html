Healthcare recruiting · the person who finds the people who run the clinic

What it is

You match people with healthcare jobs

A healthcare recruiter finds and hires the nurses, medical assistants, techs and front desk staff that clinics and hospitals need. Most days you are calling and messaging candidates, doing quick phone and video interviews, explaining the job and the pay, setting up interviews with managers, and checking in after someone starts. Some recruiters work inside a hospital system and go to career fairs and school events. Others work at staffing companies that place traveling nurses and techs all over the country. Job titles to search: recruiter, healthcare recruiter, talent acquisition coordinator, talent acquisition specialist, sourcer.

Why it might fit you

You will know what the job really is

Once you finish your certificate, you will know what a medical assistant actually does all day, and that is exactly what a recruiter has to explain to every candidate. It is a real edge: you can tell a strong MA from a resume that only sounds good, and candidates trust someone who has done the work. It is a people job from start to finish, and AI tools are now a big part of recruiting, for writing job posts and sorting applications. The honest part: most of the day is phone, video and messages, so there is more desk time than in a clinic. Hospital recruiters do get out to career fairs and campus events, and the math is light, mostly tracking how many people you hired.

The money and the outlook

About $76,000, with a bachelor's

The government counts recruiters as human resources specialists, who earn a median of $75,940 a year (May 2025). In healthcare and social assistance the median is $64,130. Jobs are projected to grow 6 percent from 2025 to 2035, faster than average, with about 73,700 openings a year. A bachelor's degree in human resources, business, communications or a related field is the typical ask, though some staffing companies hire and train recruiters without one, like the job below. The government does not list pay for recruiters on their own.

A way in before the degree

Customer service is a real starting point

The government says people often build experience first as HR assistants or customer service representatives, and that internships during college help. Staffing companies often train new recruiters from scratch and care most about drive and people skills. Hospital recruiting teams are more likely to want the bachelor's. A good question for any clinic you work at: could you spend a day with the person who hires the MAs?

One real job, live right now

Healthcare Sales Recruiter, Medical Solutions

Denver · $57,784 a year base, plus uncapped commission (posted) · full time, on site

Medical Solutions is a healthcare staffing company that places traveling nurses and other clinicians at hospitals around the country. New recruiters go through the company's Recruiter Academy training. The posting says you do not need healthcare staffing experience, and that recruiting, sales, or customer service experience helps but is not required. It does not list a degree. Most of the day is building relationships with clinicians over the phone and working toward goals, so this is the phone-heavy, competitive side of recruiting. Checked live today.
