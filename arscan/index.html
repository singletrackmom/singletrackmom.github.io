Traveler AR Scanning Guide

Experiential Learning · Case study

How to turn each contest artwork into a 3D model, so the piece pops up in 3D when a phone points at its photo in The Traveler.

Goal

Turn each contest art piece into a 3D model, with every step practiced before the art arrives.

Audience

The Traveler production team: the people taking the photos, building the models, and testing the printed pages.

Process

  1. Take pictures of the art from all sides.
  2. An app turns the pictures into a 3D model.
  3. The model goes on a web page.
  4. A phone points at the printed page, and the 3D art appears.

Read each step all the way through before starting it. If something looks wrong, stop and ask the faculty advisor.

Shopping List

First, ask STEAMworks and the Digital Media Arts photo studio to borrow a light tent, softboxes, LED lights, or a motorized turntable. Buy only what cannot be borrowed.

Equipment

  • Lazy Susan, 12 to 18 inches across, that spins smoothly
  • Tripod with a phone holder
  • Light tent (24 inches or bigger), or the parts to build one (next section)
  • 2 or 3 LED lamps, all the same kind, marked “daylight” or “5000K”
  • Clip-on circular polarizer for the phone camera (it cuts shine)
  • Newspaper or cloth with a busy pattern, to go under the art
  • Cotton gloves, for touching metal and jewelry

Free phone apps

  • RealityScan makes the 3D model.
  • Polycam is the backup if RealityScan has trouble.
  • Blackmagic Camera takes the spin photos with settings locked.

Practice pieces

  • A plain clay pot with no shine
  • A shiny coffee mug
  • A ring or necklace

The Light Box

A light box is a box with thin white paper walls. Lamps shine through the paper, and the paper spreads the light so it is soft. Soft light means no bright shiny spots on the art, which helps the app build a good model. If a light tent can be borrowed, skip to the next section. Building one takes about 30 minutes.

Materials

A big cardboard box (at least 2 feet on every side), a box cutter, a ruler, a marker, white tissue paper or parchment paper, clear tape, one big sheet of white poster board, and the LED lamps.

Steps

  1. Stand the box up so the open side faces you. This open side is the front. Leave it open.
  2. On the left side of the box, use the ruler and marker to draw a line 2 inches in from every edge. This makes a big rectangle with a 2-inch border.
  3. Cut out the big rectangle with the box cutter. Keep the 2-inch border, because it holds the box together.
  4. Repeat steps 2 and 3 on the right side and the top.
  5. Tape tissue paper over all three holes. Pull it flat and smooth, with no wrinkles. Use 2 layers if the lamp bulb shows through.
  6. Slide the poster board into the box. Tape its top edge high on the back wall. Let the bottom curve down gently and lie flat on the floor of the box, toward the front. Do not fold it. A smooth curve means no line shows behind the art.
  7. Put one lamp outside the left window and one outside the right window. Point them at the paper, about 1 foot away.
  8. Turn the lamps on and look inside. If the top of the box looks dark, add the third lamp above the top window.

Check: hold a hand inside the box. A sharp, dark shadow means the lamps need to move back or the paper needs another layer. The shadow should look soft and fuzzy.

Use the same lamps in the same spots every day. Mark the lamp spots on the table with tape so they never move.

The Scanning Station

The phone stays still on the tripod. The art turns on the lazy Susan. Photos are taken from 3 heights.

Setup steps

  1. Put the light box on a sturdy table, in a spot where it can stay for the whole scanning period.
  2. Put the lazy Susan in the middle of the box floor.
  3. Lay the patterned paper flat on top of the lazy Susan. The pattern helps the app see the art turning.
  4. Set the tripod in front of the open side, about 2 feet from the art.
  5. Clip the polarizer onto the phone’s main camera lens.
  6. Put the phone in the tripod holder, sideways or upright, whichever fills the screen with the art.
  7. Turn on the lamps. Turn off the room’s overhead lights if they make shiny spots.
  8. Put a strip of tape on the edge of the lazy Susan and another on the box floor next to it. When the two tapes line up, the art is back at the start.
  9. Mark where the tripod legs go with tape on the floor, so the tripod is in the same spot every day.

Turning the art: turn the lazy Susan a little between photos, about the width of 2 fingers at its edge. That is about 10°. It takes about 36 photos to go all the way around once.

Using the polarizer: watch the phone screen and slowly twist the polarizer ring. Stop when the shiny spots look smallest.

Part 1: Learn to Scan

By the end of this part, an easy pot becomes a 3D model that shows up on a phone. Each lesson takes about 90 minutes. Time every lesson and write the time down.

Lesson 1: Scan a plain clay pot

  1. Set up the station. Put the plain clay pot in the middle of the lazy Susan.
  2. Line up the two tape marks.
  3. Open the RealityScan app. Start a new scan and name it test-pot.
  4. Move the phone to the low spot on the tripod. Point it at the middle of the pot. The whole pot should fit on the screen with a little space around it.
  5. Take 1 photo. Turn the lazy Susan 2 fingers. Take 1 photo. Keep going until the tape marks line up again, about 36 photos.
  6. Move the phone to eye level and repeat step 5.
  7. Move the phone high, tipped down a little at the pot, and repeat step 5.
  8. There are now about 100 photos. Tell the app to make the model and wait for it to finish.
  9. Spin the model with a finger. Look at every side, the top, and the bottom edge.
  10. Export the model as a GLB file named test-pot.glb. If the app will not offer GLB, write down what it does offer and tell the faculty advisor.

If the app gets confused by the turning lazy Susan (it says it lost track, or the model looks smeared): leave the pot still and walk slowly in a full circle around it, holding the phone at the same 3 heights. Write down which way worked better.

  • The model has no holes.
  • It looks like the real pot, with the right colors.
  • The time is written down.

Lesson 2: See it in AR on a phone

  1. Send test-pot.glb to the faculty advisor. The file gets made smaller and put on a test web page.
  2. Open the test link on a phone.
  3. Tap View in AR and point the phone at a table. The pot should appear sitting on the table.
  4. Walk around it. Pinch to make it bigger and smaller.
  5. Try it on an iPhone and an Android phone, on Wi-Fi and on cell data.
  • The pot shows up in less than 5 seconds.
  • It sits still on the table and does not slide around.
  • It works on both kinds of phones.

Lesson 3: Make a 360° spin, the backup plan

Some art will not scan well, like shiny pieces and jewelry. Those get a spin instead: 36 photos that a viewer drags to turn the art around on a screen.

  1. Put the shiny mug on the lazy Susan and line up the tape marks.
  2. Put the phone at eye level.
  3. Open the Blackmagic Camera app. Turn off everything marked Auto (white balance, ISO, shutter, focus), so every photo looks exactly the same.
  4. Set white balance to 5000K, the same as the lamps.
  5. Tap the mug on the screen to focus, then lock the focus.
  6. Take 1 photo. Turn 2 fingers. Take 1 photo. Keep going until the tapes line up, 36 photos. Do not skip any, and do not take extras.
  7. Move the photos to the computer, into a folder named test-mug-spin.
  8. Open the photos in Lightroom. Fix the color and crop on the first photo only.
  9. Copy those settings from the first photo and paste them onto all the others, so they all match.
  10. Export them as JPG files named mug-01 to mug-36 and send the folder to the faculty advisor.
  11. Open the spin link on a phone and drag the mug around.
  • The spin turns smoothly.
  • The color does not jump from one photo to the next.
  • The time is written down.

Part 2: Page Triggers

By the end of this part, a printed page makes the 3D pot pop up, and the hard pieces have been timed.

Lesson 4: Make a printed page show the pot

  1. Make a pretend Traveler page with a big photo of the test pot. Save it as a PNG or JPG.
  2. Send the page image and test-pot.glb to the faculty advisor, who builds the tracking file and the AR web page.
  3. Print the page on the same paper The Traveler uses.
  4. Open the AR link and allow the page to use the camera.
  5. Point the phone at the printed photo of the pot from about 1 foot away.
  6. The 3D pot should pop up on top of the page. Move the phone slowly around the page.
  7. Try it in a bright room and in a dim room.
  • The pot pops up in less than 2 seconds.
  • It stays stuck to the page during movement.
  • It works in dim light.

Lesson 5: Test the QR code backup

Every art photo in The Traveler also gets a small QR code beside it. If the pop-up has trouble, readers scan the QR code and see the art in 3D that way.

  1. Put the QR code small, next to the pot photo on the pretend page, and print it.
  2. Scan the QR code with the regular phone camera. It should open the 3D pot.
  3. Test it in dim light, on shiny paper and plain paper, and on an older phone.
  • The QR code works every single time.

Lesson 6: Practice the hard pieces

Scan each of these the Lesson 1 way and time each one.

  • The shiny mug, once without the polarizer and once with it
  • The ring or necklace, on plain matte paper
  • Something very tall
  • Something very small

For each one, write down how long it took and scan or spin. Scan means the model came out clean. Spin means it came out blobby and needs a 360° spin instead.

  • Every hard piece has a time and a scan-or-spin answer.

Part 3: Ready to Scan

By the end of this part, everything is set up, so scanning days are only scanning.

  • Permission form is ready. Every artist signs it before scanning. It covers photographing and scanning the art, posting the 3D version online, and handling notes for fragile pieces.
  • Tracking sheet has 40 rows. Columns: entry number, artist, form signed, scan or spin, done, link works on a phone.
  • Folders are made, one per entry, named entry-01 to entry-40.
  • Every entry has its web link before the art arrives, so QR codes can go on the pages early.
  • The station is set up in its scanning spot, with tape marks on the table and floor.
  • Practice day: 4 practice pieces, start to finish, in one timed session.

If the practice day takes more than 3 hours, tell the faculty advisor. The plan shifts to more spins and fewer scans.

Scanning Rules

Every piece gets one try at scanning. A scan that does not come out clean becomes a spin, so no single piece can slow everything down.

  1. Check the tracking sheet first. No signed form, no scan. Set that piece aside and tell the faculty advisor.
  2. Wear cotton gloves for metal or jewelry, and follow any handling notes from the artist.
  3. Take the spin photos and the scan photos in the same sitting, before the art leaves the lazy Susan. If the scan fails, the spin is already done, and the art is never handled twice.
  4. Scan for 20 minutes, no more. If it is not clean by then, it is a spin. Move on.
  5. Never spray anything on the art.
  6. Fill in the tracking sheet before the next piece goes on the lazy Susan.
  7. No second tries until all 40 pieces are done.
  8. Aim for 4 pieces a day.
