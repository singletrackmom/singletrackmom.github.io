Next Steps

Personal Agents · Case study

OverviewBoardPlannerPRD

A scheduled agent that builds one running board for a university student on a pre-health path: deadlines, paid patient care, shadowing, scholarships and study abroad.

Goal

Turn a long, loosely mapped path to a graduate health-professions program into clear next steps. The board puts every deadline, paid patient-care role, shadowing option and scholarship in one place, verified at the source, so the next action is always visible.

Audience

One university student on a pre-health path who also wants to study abroad. Research on guided pathways shows students progress further when the route is mapped and the next step is explicit, so the board leads with the nearest deadline rather than a list of everything.

Process

SectionWhat it holds
DeadlinesEvery verified deadline in the next 90 days, soonest first.
Paid patient careSummer roles that build the hands-on hours graduate programs require, with whether each counts as direct patient care.
ShadowingShadowing programs, clinical research roles and pre-health summer programs.
ScholarshipsOutside and private awards, sorted by deadline.
Two paths comparedDegree, prerequisites, tests and Colorado programs for two graduate health-professions paths.
Study abroadUniversity programs by term and the national awards that fund them.

A scheduled agent rebuilds the board on a schedule, opens every link, drops anything closed and flags what is new or closing soon. A companion planner holds the gap-year map and savings plan.

Technology

A scheduled AI agent working from a written rules file, publishing static, noindexed pages with no logins, forms or tracking.

Outcomes

In use. It began as a summer work board, became a study abroad board, and was reshaped again into a pre-health pathway board as the goal sharpened. The agent, rules and link carried over each time.

References

Bailey, T. R., Jaggars, S. S., & Jenkins, D. (2015). Redesigning America’s community colleges: A clearer path to student success. Harvard University Press.
