The two sentences

I co-chair the Student Support and Success domain of the Maricopa Community Colleges AI Resource Center, where we are mapping the student journey across ten colleges to find where AI can take routine work off staff. I also build the prototypes: a student career platform in its first pilot this fall and a multilingual support routing tool piloting in my department.

At every booth

  • Ask a real question about their product before you say anything about yourself.
  • Get a name and the right team, not just a card from whoever is staffing the booth.
  • Write one line on the back of each card about what you talked about.
  • Follow up within 48 hours.

Accurate versions, hold these

  • You co-chair a domain of the AI Resource Center. It is a center, not a committee, and you do not lead the whole thing.
  • The career platform is entering its first pilot. The routing tool is in a program-wide pilot in your department. The Quality Suite is in testing. Nothing is in production.
  • The AI opportunity intake is designed, not running. It has taken no requests.
  • The service crosswalk is in draft, out to campus representatives for verification.
  • You led the campus LMS team and contributed to the RFP that led to Canvas. You did not lead the migration.
  • Never say your prototype drove a district platform decision.
  • The domain is evaluating solutions. Keep vendor conversations as learning, never as a signal about what the district will buy.

Careful who hears it

Maricopa and GCC colleagues will be in the hall. Keep job conversations for the booths and the follow-up email, not the hallway.
