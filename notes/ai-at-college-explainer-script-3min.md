# AI at College: Red, Yellow, Green — 3-minute cut

Two-host explainer (Nia and Sam) · ~3 min · plain, spoken, phone-first.

**Built on open resources:** adapts the traffic-light AI Assessment Scale (Leon Furze / Mike Perkins, CC BY-NC-SA) and CC-licensed student guides. Recommend releasing this version under CC BY so any Maricopa college, or anyone, can reuse and translate it.

**Production notes:** burn-in captions; release a Spanish version or Spanish captions; show the Red / Yellow / Green colors on screen as they're named; end on a slow card with the one takeaway.

---

## 1 · The flip
**Nia:**  Ever used AI on your homework, then thought, wait, is this cheating?  
**Sam:**  Here's the surprise. In some college classes your professor WANTS you to use AI. In others, it's a hard no.  
**Nia:**  Same college, different classes. So how do you tell? Think of a traffic light. [on-screen: red · yellow · green]  

## 2 · The three lights
**Sam:**  Green light: use AI freely. The class is fine with it.  
**Nia:**  Yellow light: some AI is okay, but say how you used it. One honest line, like "I used AI to brainstorm."  
**Sam:**  Red light: no AI. The point is for YOU to do it, because doing it yourself is how you actually learn it.  
**Nia:**  Every class, sometimes every assignment, has its own light.  

## 3 · How to find the light
**Sam:**  So find the light before you start. Open the class in Canvas and read the syllabus, that's the class rules page. Search it for "AI." [on-screen: syllabus = the class rules page]  
**Nia:**  Can't tell? Ask your professor first. One line: "Is it okay to use AI for this assignment?" Teachers respect you asking.  
**Sam:**  And hear this clearly. On a red light, telling your professor afterward does NOT make it okay. Honesty is good, but it's not a free pass. The only safe move on red is not using it.  
**Nia:**  One more thing: this video doesn't set the rules. Your professor and your syllabus do. We just help you read the light.  

## 4 · Use it to learn, not to fake it
**Sam:**  Even on a red light, AI can still help you learn around the assignment.  
**Nia:**  Stuck on a hard reading? Ask it to explain it in plainer words, or in Spanish first. That's tutoring, not cheating.  
**Sam:**  Just remember: AI is your editor, not your author. It can sharpen what's yours. It can't be the one whose work gets graded.  

## 5 · Trust, but verify
**Nia:**  Last thing. AI sounds sure even when it's wrong, and it can invent sources that look real.  
**Sam:**  So check any fact against a real source before you trust it. Your name is on the work, so you own it.  

## 6 · The one rule
**Nia:**  That's it. Find the light. Ask when you're not sure.  
**Sam:**  [slow, big, on-screen] Ask first. Be honest. Put your name on your own work.  
**Nia:**  You've got this. See you in class.
