# OER submission sheet: Advanced Data Science

Everything you need to post the course to the OER banks. Copy-paste ready. Nothing here needs to wait on the domain fix: submit the github.io URL now, and when michelleblomberg.com goes live as the custom domain, the github.io links keep working (they redirect), so the records will not break.

## Where to post, in order

1. **OER Commons + the OERizona hub (do this first).** OER Commons is the big national repository, and OERizona is the Arizona network founded through Open Maricopa, so this hits both your home community and national discovery in one place.
   - Create a free account at oercommons.org.
   - Click Create in the header, choose "Submit from Web," paste the URL, and continue.
   - Fill the metadata (below). Submit.
   - OER Commons librarians review and approve in about 24 to 48 hours; they confirm the license, then email you when it is live.
   - Then add it to the OERizona hub (oercommons.org/hubs/oerizona) so it shows in the Arizona collection.

2. **MERLOT (second).** The CSU-run, peer-reviewed national collection, STEM-friendly, adds review credibility.
   - Free membership at merlot.org, then Contribute a Material as the author, using the same metadata.

3. **Your Maricopa path (do in parallel).** Email your college OER/library liaison or Maricopa Open Digital (MOD) Press / Open Maricopa to get it into the district repository, and ask about Z-Degree / Near-Z alignment (your Near Z Degree work is a real hook here). This creates an internal record and possible recognition.

I cannot submit these for you: each requires your own login, and account creation and passwords stay with you. Everything below is prepped so each submission is copy-paste.

- **Description (paste this whole block):**

  Overview: A complete graduate, MS-level Advanced Data Science course built for the age of generative AI. Rather than banning AI or policing it, the course assesses learning through authentic, AI-resistant simulations, interactive cases where students audit a model, make a call, and defend it in a recorded oral defense, work an AI cannot do for the student.

  Core learning objective: Across eight modules (data acquisition, preprocessing, exploratory analysis and communication, supervised learning, model evaluation, unsupervised learning, ethics and AI governance, and a capstone), students move from following steps to defending decisions. The grade rests on process evidence and a live defense, not on a deliverable a model could generate.

  What makes it authentic and AI-resistant: Four interactive simulations (a model-evaluation audit, a clustering decision, an AI-governance review board, and a capstone) each seed a real problem the student must diagnose, resolve, and defend. Because the assessment is the student's reasoning and defense rather than a submittable artifact, it holds up when anyone can generate a polished answer. The course also includes reusable learning objects and an on-device oral-defense practice tool, and each simulation can be dropped into an existing course on its own.

  Format: Zero Textbook Cost (ZTC) / OER. CC BY-NC-SA 4.0.

  Target audience: Graduate and upper-division undergraduate students in data, computing, and quantitative fields; adoptable whole or one simulation at a time.

  AI usage disclaimer: This Open Educational Resource was developed with AI. An AI subject-matter expert drafted the technical content, and an AI review panel (a PhD-level data scientist, a PhD-level instructional designer, and a graduate-student reviewer) checked it for accuracy, alignment, and clarity, with the human author owning every decision. As with any AI-assisted material, please verify the technical content against current sources and standards for your own context before using it, and report any corrections to the author so they can be fixed.
