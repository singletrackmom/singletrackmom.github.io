Written Aug 3, 2026. This is a private working plan, not a portfolio page. It consolidates the strategy conversation so nothing scatters.

**Goal.** Land one or two offers in a single clear lane by spring 2027, so next summer is OFF, not another job hunt. Get there as a current learning experience designer with published assessment research and a portfolio of real, built courses. This is a 9-month sprint (Aug 2026 to May 2027), not an open-ended plan.

**Audience.** Me. Secondary: the version of me who opens this in six months and needs to remember the decisions, not relitigate them.

**Process.** Pick one sharp lane, build the real artifacts that lane demands (courses, sims, a case study, a publication), create the missing current credential through a community of practice, and run the internal Sonal play as both a Plan B and a credential builder. Reassess in December and again in March.

## 5. OER: where the good stuff is, and RLOs (reference)

I should know this cold if I am selling myself as a learning designer. The canonical, reputable OER sources:

- **OER Commons** (oercommons.org). Largest cross-discipline repository, and its Open Author tool lets me build and host my own RLOs.
- **MERLOT** (merlot.org). Peer-reviewed learning objects, strong for STEM and interactive materials. This is literally a "learning objects" repository, the natural home for my sims.
- **OpenStax** (openstax.org). Free, peer-reviewed open textbooks, STEM heavy.
- **LibreTexts** (libretexts.org). Remixable open textbook libraries, STEM heavy.
- **Open Textbook Library**, U of Minnesota (open.umn.edu/opentextbooks). Vetted open textbooks with faculty reviews.
- **MIT OpenCourseWare** (ocw.mit.edu). Full course materials.
- **PhET Interactive Simulations**, CU Boulder (phet.colorado.edu). Free interactive STEM sims. This is my model for publishing sims as RLOs, and it is local (CU Boulder).
- **OASIS** (oasis.geneseo.edu) and the **Mason OER Metafinder** (mason.deming.gmu.edu). Federated search across many OER sources at once, the fastest way to find something for a specific topic.
- **Pressbooks** (pressbooks.com). Author and remix open textbooks.
- **SkillsCommons** (skillscommons.org). Workforce and CTE OER.
- Community-college focused: **CCCOER** and **Open Oregon**.

**Reusable Learning Object (RLO), yes it is a real, established concept.** A self-contained chunk of learning that is (1) built around explicit learning objectives, (2) openly licensed, (3) tagged with metadata, and (4) reusable across contexts. My sims qualify the moment I add objectives + a CC license + metadata and deposit them in OER Commons or MERLOT. Learn the Creative Commons license types (CC BY, CC BY-SA, CC BY-NC, and so on) so I can both use others' OER correctly and license my own.

Note: the `oer-course-finder` skill can pull real, license-verified OER for a specific course when I am ready to build one. Use it per course, not in the abstract.

## 6. The Community of Practice as the engine

This fixes the missing "learning experience design" bullet on my current job. I cannot fake an LXD line, but I can create a real one: lead a faculty community of practice doing design-based research on authentic and simulation assessment. That is a current, ongoing LXD-and-research credential, and it is the machine that produces the sims and a publishable piece.

- **Topic for the year:** authentic assessment (assessment a model cannot complete for the student). Simulation-as-assessment is the through-line.
- **Cadence:** we research, we build, we publish.
- **Hard scoping rule:** every CoP output maps to a portfolio artifact. Target for the year: one publishable piece (article, conference paper, or open white paper once there is real data) plus 2 to 3 built sims. If it does not produce an artifact, it does not go on the agenda. A CoP with no outputs is uncompensated service that eats a teaching year and yields nothing for the job hunt.
- This becomes a legitimate current bullet: "Founded and lead a faculty community of practice conducting design-based research on authentic and simulation-based assessment; produced [artifacts]."

## 8. The interview-to-offer fix (do not skip)

Portfolio work gets more interviews. It does not close them. In parallel:

- Request feedback from every interview already completed. Even a one-line answer.
- Prep a crisp fit narrative for the intersection lane: "I am the learning designer who also builds the tool," with one story that lands for a non-technical hiring manager (the Mines manager did not follow the AI talk, so the story has to work WITHOUT jargon).
- Name the seniority/salary reality up front so I am targeting roles I can actually close, not reaches that always go to the insider.
