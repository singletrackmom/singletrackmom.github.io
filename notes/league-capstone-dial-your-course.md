# AI Fellows Capstone Project Description

## Dial Your Course: An AI Course-Design and Quality-Assurance Assistant

Michelle Blomberg, Digital Media Arts, Glendale Community College

**Project Intent.** Dial Your Course is an AI-assisted instructional-design tool that helps faculty design and quality-check online and hybrid courses against recognized standards before students ever enter the course. Grounded in backward design and constructive alignment, the tool ingests a course, either imported as a Canvas course package or built new from a research-based template, and evaluates the coherence of the instructional system as a whole: whether learning outcomes are written as observable, measurable behaviors at the intended level of Bloom&rsquo;s revised taxonomy; whether assessments and learning activities are aligned to those outcomes; whether the course meets credit-hour and seat-time expectations, a Higher Learning Commission concern in online modalities where contact hours cannot be observed in a classroom; whether it satisfies OSCQR and Quality Matters design criteria and the federal requirement for regular and substantive interaction (RSI); and whether it applies Universal Design for Learning to reduce barriers and widen access. The tool offers two pathways, &ldquo;import and tune&rdquo; for an existing course and &ldquo;build from a template&rdquo; for a new one, so it serves as both an auditor of quality and a maker of it.

**Impact.** The primary beneficiaries are faculty, particularly new, adjunct, and first-time online instructors, who gain expert-level design feedback on demand rather than waiting on a small instructional-design staff. Students benefit from courses that are better aligned, easier to navigate, and more accessible. The college benefits from more consistent course quality, a stronger compliance posture around seat time, RSI, and accessibility, and relief of the instructional-design bottleneck that limits how many courses can be reviewed each term. Because the tool operates on a course package rather than on student records, it advances quality while collecting no student data.

**Current State of AI Use.** I am an active builder of AI, not only a user of it. I vibe-code working prototypes, engineering prompts and lightweight, rubric-driven LLM workflows grounded in learning science, and I have shipped several (a career-services tool, a bilingual student-services routing tool, and this course assistant). I co-chair the Student Support and Success domain of Maricopa&rsquo;s AI Resource Center and have completed the Google Cloud Generative AI Leader training alongside this Fellowship. My capability is that of an advanced practitioner-builder: I design and test agentic and rubric-based AI workflows and anchor them in instructional-design theory, while partnering with engineering expertise for production hardening.

**Barriers.** The most concrete technical barrier is that Canvas does not reliably export institutional outcomes and some course metadata into the .imscc package, which constrains automated outcome-alignment checking (a defect I have already researched and documented). Beyond that: variability in how faculty structure courses; the need to validate that the tool&rsquo;s instructional-design judgments are accurate and stable across runs, that is, instrument reliability; faculty trust and adoption; the discipline of keeping the tool free of student data; and the reality that this is a prototype, not yet production-hardened.

**Enablers.** The League AI Fellows program and its community of practice; Maricopa&rsquo;s OSCQR and Quality Matters resources and the Center for Teaching, Learning and Engagement; open, Creative-Commons standards such as OSCQR that can be built on directly; current LLMs capable of reading course content and applying explicit rubrics; my instructional-design foundation (M.Ed. in educational technology, grounded in connectivism and personal learning environments); and my existing prototypes and public build history.

**Measures of Success.** Success is a working prototype that (1) imports a real course and returns a structured, standards-aligned report covering outcomes and Bloom&rsquo;s level, constructive alignment, seat time, OSCQR and Quality Matters, RSI, and UDL, and (2) supports a build-from-template path. I will validate the tool on real courses, beginning with AVC 100, against expert instructional-design review, measuring the agreement between the tool&rsquo;s findings and a designer&rsquo;s, and I will pilot it with faculty using task-based usability testing (the Single Ease Question, time-on-task, and self-reported time saved). Longer-term indicators are faculty adoption, measurable gains in course-quality-review outcomes, and reduced instructional-design review time per course.
