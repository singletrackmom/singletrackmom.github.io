Student Support & Success

Goal: Implement AI to remove the barriers that keep students from succeeding, and help more of them reach the goal they came for, whether that is graduating, transferring, or entering the workforce.

Audience: The more than 140,000 students across all 10 colleges of the Maricopa Community College District.

Process: Three coordinated initiatives. First, map and baseline the non-classroom services (advising, financial aid, enrollment, student life). Then run a student-journey UX study, where personas walk the real path to find and rank the barriers. Then turn the findings into prioritized AI pilots.

This is my biggest project and my League for Innovation AI Fellows capstone. I co-chair the Student Support and Success domain of Maricopa’s district AI committee (the ARC), working across all 10 colleges. It keeps human connection and student agency at the center while AI removes friction from the non-classroom services that decide whether students stay, and it is grounded in the evidence on why students leave.

1 Map & baseline Inventory the services and baseline who uses each one.
2 Student Journey UX Study Personas walk the journey to find and rank real barriers.
3 Recommendations & pilots Turn barriers into prioritized AI pilots, ranked by reach.

Render · AVC 248 redesign

Goal: Give students a personal learning environment they keep building after graduation, anchored to a real reach job they choose on day one.

AI Agentic Routing · Early prototype · In testing
CopaMigo

Goal: Point stuck students to the right human support, fast, before they give up or default to ChatGPT because they do not know what to call what they need.

In the Classroom

Teaching with AI · AVC 183 · Digital Graphic Design 1
Graphic Design History: 1900 to the Present

Goal: Students learn graphic design history by teaching it, everyone becomes the expert on their own era, then learns the rest from their peers (a jigsaw model).

Audience: AVC 183 Digital Graphic Design 1 students.

Process: Each student pair researches one era and builds a tool to teach it to the class. The instructor example covers 1910 to 1920 with a visual slideshow tour and an interactive drag-and-drop sorting game.

The standard project is a narrated slideshow. The extra credit version is an AI-assisted vibe coding challenge: build an interactive, period-accurate teaching tool from scratch using a prompt-based workflow. No prior coding experience required. Prompt craft counts.

Student vibe-coded examples:

  • 1919–1933 by Jo Miranda + Dominic A · interactive game
  • 1960–1979 by Devon Adams · narrated video with game demo
How it works
Standard path
Pairs research an assigned era and build a narrated slideshow to teach it to classmates.
Extra credit: vibe code
Build a period-accurate interactive web tool using AI. Prompt craft counts.
Jigsaw learning model: each pair teaches their era, everyone learns from each other.
Student Success & Retention · AVC 100 · Intro to Digital Media · Redesign in progress
Rebuilding the Program’s Front Door

Goal: Lift success in the one course every digital media student takes, and historically our lowest-success course, by rebuilding it around student support and connection. The aim is a welcoming front door that markets our programs rather than scaring students away.

Audience: First-time and returning students in their very first program course, the moment that decides whether many of them stay.

Process: An easy-win final project students can be proud of, carried as a single piece across the creative pipeline. Around it: student support modules, introductions to the full-time faculty and program directors, clear guidance on where to get help, the message that the software is approachable rather than intimidating, and one short student-success video (about three minutes) per module. The redesign is grounded in retention research. It is in progress now; once the build is finished I will measure the change in success rate.

Connection

AI Agentic + Static Site · Personal build · PRD drafted May 2026
Wayfinder

Goal: Plan an ambitious multi-state van trip in one living page, and turn anyone’s interests into a trip like it.

Audience: Van travelers and road-trippers (built first for our 15-day Colorado, Utah, Nevada, and California loop: Great Basin, Tahoe, Yosemite, Zion, Bryce).

Process: It pairs a hand-built itinerary with a live weather feed, a fuel strategy with red gas-stop warnings, van-clearance and dimension awareness (Sprinter height, the Zion tunnel limits), direction-aware maps on every stop, a real budget, geography sections that segment the route by climate zone and elevation, color-coded boxes for camping, hot springs, hikes, climbing, and water, and drive-time chips with no-drive days. v1 is a questionnaire-driven generator that builds a page like this for anyone from their interests, accommodations, and budget.

AI Agentic · Scheduled Agent · Calendar-native · Personal build · Runs daily on its own
Campground Watcher

Goal: Catch the rare cancellations that briefly free up sold-out campgrounds, so a hard-to-get site lands in hand without anyone sitting on a booking page.

Audience: Colorado campers chasing sites that book out months ahead (Tahoe shore, Frisco and Dillon’s Peak One, and more), pointed at whichever campground I am hunting at the time.

Process: A scheduled agent wakes on its own each morning, checks the reservation system for the exact nights and sites I want, and the moment a spot frees up it drops the find straight onto my Google Calendar with the dates, the link, and what I need to grab it. It runs fully independently now (no longer tied to Wayfinder), can watch several campgrounds at once, and works entirely in the background, no dashboard to open and nothing to refresh. A clean example of an autonomous, calendar-native agent that monitors and acts without being asked. It only watches and alerts; booking the site is always mine to confirm.

Real result: it snagged us beachside sites at a Lake Tahoe state park, spots that had been fully booked.

AI Agentic · Daily Job Board · Personal build · Scheduled agent
Summer Work Board

Goal: Help a college student home for a short summer land high-paying, flexible, gig-style work fast.

Audience: A young adult who wants top pay per hour, weekend-friendly hours, and a hard five-week window.

Process: A scheduled agent runs each morning as a rolling board, it adds newly posted openings, expands to more local employers (event caterers, staffing firms, DJ companies, venues, rec centers), removes anything no longer posted, and re-checks every link. AI Agentic · Scheduled Agent · Calendar-native · Personal build · Runs daily on its own
Daily Focus

Goal: Turn a long, messy task list into a short, ordered plan for the day, so the most important and time-sensitive work always rises to the top instead of getting lost.

Audience: Me, juggling teaching, committee work, coursework, deadlines, and family logistics, who needs the right next thing surfaced, not the whole list.

Process: A scheduled agent reads my master task file every morning, distills it to the few priorities that matter most by deadline, and adds the day’s tasks straight to my Google Calendar, so the plan is in front of me without my having to build it. It re-reads the list as it changes, keeps the order honest against real due dates, and stays out of the way the rest of the time.

Design rule: The calendar is for action, not clutter, only the day’s real priorities go on, nothing busy.

About

Michelle Blomberg is Residential Faculty in Digital Media Arts at Glendale Community College, where she teaches graphic design, UX, and capstone career readiness. When she notices a gap in the student experience, she prototypes small tools with AI to help fill it. She also shares what she learns, through faculty workshops and a campus AI community of practice on using AI in teaching. Her background includes software product management at an EdTech startup, instructional technology leadership at GCC, and decades of teaching and course design in higher education. She led the campus LMS team during the Blackboard era and contributed to the LMS RFP that led to Canvas. She is co-chair of the Student Support and Success domain on the Maricopa Community Colleges District AI Resource Committee (the ARC), the work that surfaces where and why students hit those gaps.
