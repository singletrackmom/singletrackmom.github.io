The federal rule, Arizona’s draft state process and the evidence file a reviewer will ask for, applied to a credit-hour program. Part of a prototype program design.

The Rules in Brief

RequirementWhat it means for this design
LengthA credit-hour program must carry at least 4 and fewer than 16 semester hours over 8 to 14 weeks of instruction, under the final rule published May 19, 2026. The fast track is 7 credits in 14 weeks.
Credit, not clockWhile the conversion exemption holds, the program is measured in credit hours and the clock-hour rule that documents 50 of every 60 minutes does not apply (34 CFR 600.2). Without it, each credit would need 30 clock hours of monitored instruction, which this online design cannot show. Each online week still needs academically engaged work with instructor support.
No conversionA non-degree credit program avoids credit-to-clock conversion only when every course counts in full toward an associate degree of at least two academic years that has had a graduate in the current or two prior award years. The two technical courses meet this today. Career Launch must be added to the AAS, or offered as a course that already counts in full, before the 12-month clock can start.
No correspondenceCorrespondence programs are ineligible, and a course that is partly correspondence counts as correspondence in full. Every online section needs regular, substantive, instructor-initiated interaction, so the program runs as a cohort and is never marketed as self-paced.
Outcomes70% of learners complete within 150% of normal time (21 weeks here), and 70% are employed in the second quarter after exit. From 2029–30, placement counts only completers employed in the target or a comparable occupation.
ValuePublished tuition and fees may not exceed value-added earnings: the median earnings of working Pell completers, adjusted for regional prices, minus 150% of the poverty line for one person. The US Department of Education calculates it; until then Arizona declines programs whose costs likely exceed projected value-added earnings.
Who teachesNo more than 25% of instruction can be provided by an ineligible institution or organization under a written arrangement. Open textbooks and the hosted lab are tools, not providers.
HistoryThe program must meet the length and credit conditions for the 12 months before the college applies, and Arizona requires one year of operation and 70% placement over those 12 months. The first cohorts run on other funding; Workforce Pell comes after the outcome data exist.

Aid note. Cost of attendance includes tuition, fees, the exam voucher, lab access, transportation and a living allowance. Pell is the only federal student aid available for a workforce program, remedial coursework cannot be included, and a learner cannot receive Pell for this program and the AAS at the same time.

The Arizona Process

Arizona’s process is set out in Arizona’s Workforce Pell Program Policy, posted in July 2026 as a draft that takes effect once the Workforce Arizona Council approves it. As of 25 November 2026 no final version was found; confirm with the Office of Economic Opportunity.

WhoRole
Office of Economic OpportunityLead agency. Keeps the qualifying-occupation list by SOC code, calculates outcome metrics and runs annual monitoring.
Workforce Arizona CouncilThe state workforce board. Approves the occupation list, reviews applications and certifies programs, and attests to each program’s CIP and SOC codes. The Governor’s designated official makes the final decision.
Department of Economic SecurityRuns the Eligible Training Provider List on Arizona Job Connection, where approved programs can be listed.

The draft policy tests alignment to occupations that are high-skill, high-wage or in demand, and asks for signed employer validation, evidence that the credential is portable across employers and a documented path into academic credit. A secondary report says the first Arizona program the US Department of Education approved, in October 2026, was a phlebotomy program; this has not been confirmed against the Department’s own release.

Course Template

Every course in the fast track starts from the same Canvas structure, organized around the eight general standards of the Quality Matters Higher Education Rubric. The wording below is a design paraphrase, not rubric text.

StandardBuilt into the template
OverviewA Start Here module: purpose, how the three courses fit, the weekly rhythm, technology needs and the instructor’s welcome video.
ObjectivesThe official course competencies, crosswalked to Security+ V8 objectives and to the tasks of the target jobs, with weekly objectives that trace to them.
AssessmentAn alignment table, a rubric for every lab and project, a “Done when” line on every activity and timed checks that mirror the exam.
MaterialsOpen textbooks and public-domain government sources first, each with its license and published reviews, so books add nothing to the learner’s cost of attendance.
InteractionThe weekly live session, written feedback within five days, an instructor-posed discussion each unit and outreach to anyone who goes quiet.
TechnologyCanvas, Canvas Studio, Rise scenarios and a hosted lab environment, with privacy terms linked.
Learner supportTech support, disability resources, financial aid and Workforce Pell eligibility, college career services, ARIZONA@WORK and exam voucher instructions.
AccessibilityWCAG 2.1 AA, edited captions and transcripts, and a text alternative for every branching scenario.

Evidence File

What a state or federal reviewer will ask for, assembled while the first cohorts run rather than after.

Program design

  • Award name, six-digit CIP code (chosen from the state’s SOC to CIP crosswalk so that it maps to 15-1232; a security CIP may not) and target SOC codes (15-1232 first, with 15-1231, 15-1244 and 15-1212 as next steps).
  • Published length in weeks and credits, matching the catalog and every syllabus.
  • A credit-hour plan for each course, showing instructor contact, labs and out-of-class work against the college’s credit-hour policy.
  • A written interaction plan for each online section and the instructors’ qualifications.
  • The program director’s written prerequisite and corequisite policy for the security course.
  • Financial aid’s Pell formula, payment period, disbursement and return-of-funds plan.
  • The accreditor’s certificate program screening, the networking course’s credit-hour plan, the career course number and instructor qualifications.

Employer validation

  • Signed, dated letters or agreements from at least two Arizona employers that name the program, the roles they hire for, the starting pay range and whether they will interview completers, including staffing firms and managed service providers. Two employers is a design target; the draft policy does not set a minimum. Federal members of the advisory committee can review the curriculum, but signed hiring letters generally need to come from employers who hire for the target jobs. Federal employees may not use their title to endorse a program, so any letter from them goes through their agency’s ethics office.
  • Minutes from the program’s existing industry advisory committee, which includes federal and private-sector cybersecurity professionals, showing they reviewed the curriculum.
  • A crosswalk from course competencies to Security+ objectives to employer job requirements.

Credential and stackability

  • The certificate of completion, the Security+ voucher policy and pass-rate tracking.
  • Written notice to the Workforce Arizona Council within 15 days of replacing an interim career course with the approved one.
  • The guided pathway into the Cybersecurity AAS, naming the credits that apply and the shared CIP family.

Outcomes tracking

  • Entry, expected exit and actual exit dates, and the credential earned, captured at enrollment with consent.
  • Completion tracked against 70% within 21 weeks, with a supervised makeup window for individual learners inside 21 weeks. Makeups cannot become the norm, or the program counts as longer than 14 weeks.
  • Placement follow-up in the second quarter after exit, recording employer and job title alongside the state wage match.
  • Enrollment limited to learners located in Arizona unless an agreement with another state exists.

Risks to Approval

RiskMitigation in this design
Classified as correspondenceCohort schedule, weekly release, a weekly live session, five-day feedback and logged outreach.
Arizona policy changesThe policy is a draft and the occupation list was not published, so the design is checked again when the final policy appears.
Completion below 70%Weekly engagement checks, early alerts at the end of weeks 9, 11 and 13, a recoverable grading plan and a 21-week window for individual incompletes.
Placement below 70%Career Launch in every week, employer mock interviews, applications starting in week 9 and an honest first-job target: help desk.
Prerequisite gapThe networking course builds the base the security course assumes, and the program director’s written policy covers the Linux prerequisite and the Windows corequisite for the whole cohort, and the security course outlines Windows help desk practice in week 10.
