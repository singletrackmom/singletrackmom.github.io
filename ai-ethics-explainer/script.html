AI at College: Know the Rules

Two-host explainer script · about 4 to 5 minutes · read by two friendly voices (Nia and Sam)

Audience: new and returning college students Plain language, no jargon Works in any class, even ones that ban AI

Read at a relaxed, upbeat pace. Nia and Sam trade short lines like a friendly podcast. The matching animated version plays the same lines on screen.

A note for faculty. This explainer is built to back you up, whatever your class policy is. It tells students the rules change from class to class, that they must read your syllabus and ask you before using AI, and that in classes built on a craft or on critical thinking, like art or English, doing the work themselves is exactly what they are paying for. Nothing here pushes students to use AI. It fits a class that bans it, one that allows it, and one that requires it. Show it in the first week, then point students to your own AI statement.

Scene 1. The flip

Nia
Real quick. Back in high school, what happened if you used AI on your homework?
Sam
You got in trouble. They called it cheating.
Nia
So here is the thing that surprises a lot of new college students.
Sam
In some college classes, your professor actually wants you to use AI.
Nia
And in others, it is still a total no. Same college, different classes.
Sam
Give us five minutes and you will know how to tell the difference. Let us go.

Scene 2. Rule one: it changes by class

Nia
Rule one. The AI rules change from class to class.
Sam
Your math class might ban it. Your business class might require it.
Nia
Your English teacher might let you toss around ideas with AI, but not write the essay for you.
Sam
None of them are wrong. They are just teaching different things.
Nia
So never assume. What was fine in one class can flip in the next.

Scene 3. The point of the class (why some say no)

Nia
So why would a class ever say no?
Sam
Because some classes teach a craft, and doing it yourself is the point.
Nia
In an art class, you usually cannot have AI make the art. Making it is how you learn.
Sam
In English, you usually cannot have AI write your paper. Writing it builds your critical thinking.
Nia
You pay to build these skills and your own critical thinking. If your instructor okays AI, use it to learn, not to skip the work.

Scene 4. Rule two: read it, then ask

Nia
Rule two, and this is the big one. Check your syllabus.
Sam
Every class has a syllabus, and most have a part about AI.
Nia
Look for the words AI, artificial intelligence, or academic honesty. It is usually near the top.
Sam
It tells you straight up: can you use AI, when, and how much.
Nia
And if you cannot find it, or it just confuses you?
Sam
You ask your instructor. Before you turn anything in.
Nia
One quick message. Hi, is it okay to use AI for this assignment?
Sam
Thirty seconds like that can save your whole grade.
Nia
And honestly, teachers respect that question. It shows you are trying to do it right.

Scene 5. Rule three: some classes want it

Nia
Rule three. Some classes want you to learn AI on purpose.
Sam
Because a lot of jobs now expect it.
Nia
Knowing how to use these tools is becoming a basic skill, like email or spreadsheets.
Sam
So when a class teaches AI, they are getting you ready for that paycheck.
Nia
That is not cheating. That is training for the real world.

Scene 6. Rule four: a study buddy

Nia
Rule four. Even when you cannot use AI on the assignment, it can still help you learn.
Sam
Think of it like a study buddy that is awake at two in the morning.
Nia
Stuck on a hard idea? Ask it to explain it in simpler words.
Sam
Big test coming? Have it make you practice questions.
Nia
Juggling work and family? It can help you study smarter in the little time you have.
Sam
The trick is this. Use it to understand the material...
Nia
...not to turn in its answers as your own.
Sam
You still do the thinking. You still learn it. That is why you are here.

Scene 7. The one rule under all the rules

Nia
So here is the one rule underneath all the other ones.
Sam
When in doubt, be honest and ask.
Nia
If you use AI, do not hide it. Just tell your instructor how you used it.
Sam
Give it credit. A quick note like: I used AI to brainstorm, or to check my grammar.
Nia
And if AI hands you a fact, check it against a real source before you trust it.
Sam
AI can be wrong or out of date, and your name is on the work, so you own it.
Nia
Being upfront keeps you safe in any class, even the ones that ban it.

Scene 8. Close

Nia
So, three easy moves. Read the syllabus. Ask when you are not sure.
Sam
And use AI to learn, not to fake it.
Nia
Do that, and AI goes from something that gets you in trouble...
Sam
...to something that helps you get through college.
Nia
You got this. We will see you in class.
