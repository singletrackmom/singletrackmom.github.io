Dial Your Course, version 2

A picture of where the instrument is going: one guided setup, then a tabbed report that both checks a course and teaches the principle behind every check. Built on AVC 100, Introduction to Digital Arts, a one-credit, half-semester course.

This is a v2 preview, not a working pilot. It is a representation of the finished vision for a League for Innovation capstone and a Maricopa district conversation. Nothing here runs on a live course, no numbers are collected, and the reported figures are illustrative, drawn from the AVC 100 sandbox to make the interface feel real. It is not linked from anywhere and is set to no-index.

Objectives & Alignment

What this is and why it matters

Bloom’s revised taxonomy sorts thinking into six levels, from lower to higher: Remember, Understand, Apply, Analyze, Evaluate, Create. An objective should name a level and use a verb a student can be observed performing. “Design a colour palette” is observable and scorable. “Understand colour” is not, you cannot watch understanding, only the doing that reveals it.

Constructive alignment (and its cousin, backward design) means the objective, the assessment that measures it, and the activity that builds it all point at the same thing. You start from what the student should be able to do, then design backward to the assessment and the practice.

pass

12 of 14 objectives use observable, measurable verbs

Learning outcomes

Most objectives name a performance that can be watched and scored: composite, animate, storyboard, export, isolate, draw with the Pen tool. These sit at the Apply and Create levels, which is right for a studio foundation course.

A palette file plus a single appropriateness criterion. No rationale, no application to the student’s own project, no evidence of the reasoning the objective names.

Change the objective to “justify a colour palette for a stated audience.” Add a rubric line asking the student to name two colour relationships they used (complementary, analogous, value contrast) and why each fits the postcard’s audience. Now the reasoning is on the page.

2

Two-minute recorded walkthrough

Add a media-recording option: the student points at their palette and their composited postcard and explains one colour they changed after critique. A short spoken defense is the cheapest un-fakeable evidence of understanding available.

4

Compare-and-choose against a decoy

Give three palettes for one brief, one strong, two flawed, and have the student pick and defend. This moves the task to Analyze/Evaluate on Bloom’s, which is exactly what “understand relationships” was reaching for.

Assessments & Rubrics

What this is and why it matters

Criterion-referenced assessment scores the work against a fixed standard, not against other students and not against effort. A criterion should name a performance (“subject isolated cleanly, edges refined”), never effort (“worked hard”). Transparent assignments (the TILT framework) tell students the purpose, the task, and the criteria up front. Formative assessment is low-stakes practice; summative measures mastery, and a course needs a healthy balance.

Authentic assessment asks students to do the real thing a practitioner does. But authenticity alone is not AI-resistance: a model can write a portfolio case study or a client rationale. The thread that still counts as evidence of learning, once a model can make the artifact, is the record of its making, drafts, critique given to a named classmate, revision, a spoken defense.

pass

Rubrics present on every graded project, criteria name performance

Rubric criteria

All studio steps carry a rubric bound to the graded item, and the criteria describe observable output: subject isolated cleanly, file at correct resolution and colour mode, fade built with opacity keyframes. This is criterion-referenced and reads clearly to a student before they start.

review

Formative and summative balance skews to completion credit

Course-wide

The two projects are the summative core. Around them sit many completion-graded exercises (Student Success, Industry Inspiration, Introduce yourself). That is fine for a foundation course, low-stakes reps lower the temperature, but declare them as formative so their point weight is not mistaken for measurement.

fix

1 graded item could be completed by a generative model

Course Reflection, 10 pts

This is a construct-validity finding, not an integrity accusation. An assessment that something other than the student can satisfy has stopped measuring the student. The AI below reads the item and proposes authentic alternatives grounded in what AVC 100 already does.

Assessment: Course Reflection (10 pts)

What the AI read: a prose response to a general end-of-term prompt (“what did you learn?”), submitted as text or a single upload. The rubric rewards a thoughtful, complete reflection. There is no requirement to reference this critique, this project, or this revision, so the prompt is answerable without the student. A model produces a competent version in under a minute.

What the assessment is currently made of
One text box, one general prompt, one appropriateness-and-completeness rubric. No process evidence, no situated content, no defense.
1

Reflect on named artifacts, not in the abstract

Require the reflection to cite the student’s own Photographic Postcard and Social Media Animation by their choices: the subject they isolated, the keyframe timing they set. A reflection anchored to files only this student made cannot be written from the prompt alone.

2

Revision-history reflection

Ask the student to quote one piece of critique they received (from Discord or a recorded OBS review) and show, with before/after screenshots, what they changed because of it. The revision trail in response to a named classmate is un-fakeable.

3

Recorded two-minute walkthrough

Swap the essay for a screen recording where the student opens their files and narrates three decisions. Same rubric, but now graded on a performance. Two minutes of a student explaining their own work is the strongest single AI-resistant move here.

4

Forward-looking portfolio note

Have the student write the caption that will sit under these pieces in their Behance portfolio: audience, the problem the piece solves, one thing they would push further. Ties the reflection to the real, situated portfolio task the course already ends on.

review

Process evidence is strong on projects, thin on standalone exercises

Graded work

The Sketch / Build / Refine steps already collect physical capture, process artifacts, critique given, and revision, that is exactly the record that survives generative AI. The single-skill exercises (Illustrator, Photoshop, After Effects lessons) grade a file with little process around it. The cheapest fix is one criterion asking the student to name what they changed and why, in the vocabulary of the discipline.

Institutional Outcomes (ILOs) · optional

What this is and why it matters

Institutional outcomes go by many names, ILOs, GELOs, core competencies, program outcomes. They are the handful of abilities a college promises every graduate, no matter the major. A single course rarely owns one; it contributes evidence toward it. This tab shows which AVC 100 lesson would evidence which outcome, and recommends the one or two strongest to actually attach.

This outcome set is configurable. A school loads its own ILOs, GELOs, or core competencies, this is not hard-coded to one college. The example below uses the outcome set from the AVC 100 sandbox.

Marked optional because another course may formally own an outcome. But everyone should care: a defensible one-or-two attachment is worth more than claiming six.

Seat Time & Workload · HLC credit-hour compliance

What this is and why it matters

The credit-hour standard (the federal definition the Higher Learning Commission enforces) says one credit equals a fixed amount of student work, roughly 40 hours total for a one-credit course counting direct instruction and outside work. Online, there is no classroom to observe, so the course package is the only evidence those hours were delivered. That is the original reason this tool exists.

Cognitive load and pacing matter too: the same total hours land very differently if they pile into two heavy weeks. A workload budget lets you see the split before students feel it.

Interaction (RSI) · online only

What this is and why it matters

Regular and Substantive Interaction (RSI) is the federal and HLC line between a real online course and correspondence study. To count, interaction must be instructor-initiated, regular (on a predictable rhythm), and substantive (about the course content, not just logistics). A course that only posts materials and collects uploads is correspondence, and cannot be offered for credit as an online course.

The Community of Inquiry framework names the three presences a good online course builds: teaching presence (the instructor designing and directing), social presence (learners as real people to each other), and cognitive presence (the back-and-forth that builds understanding). RSI is mostly teaching presence made visible.

pass

“Every project runs a critique cycle: students post work in progress to Discord and Canvas, I record feedback with OBS and post it to YouTube, and students revise from it. I post a weekly announcement, hold optional live help sessions, and commit to returning graded feedback within 48 hours. The syllabus states the 48-hour response window and the weekly announcement rhythm.”

Assessment Cycle

What this is and why it matters

Course assessment is a loop, not a grade. You pick a competency, decide how you will measure it, collect the evidence, look at what it says, change one thing, and re-check next cycle. The last step, closing the loop, is the one most often skipped and the one accreditors most want to see: you did something with what you found. It often runs on a two-year cycle, which is why you choose one or two competencies to look at closely rather than all fourteen every year.

Accessibility & Style

What this is and why it matters

Accessibility (WCAG 2.1 AA) removes barriers for learners with disabilities: real alt text, descriptive links, table headers, heading order, captions on video, and text contrast of at least 4.5:1. The cheapest way to hit it is to build accessible from the first draft, not to retrofit later. This tool enforces contrast at the moment a colour is chosen, so a failing pair can never be applied.

Universal Design for Learning (UDL) is not the same thing. WCAG is a compliance floor; UDL is a design philosophy that plans for learner variability from the start, multiple means of representation, engagement, and expression. A course can be fully WCAG-conformant and still offer a single narrow path. Conflating the two is the most common mistake in the field.

pass

Contrast enforced at palette time

Persistent style system

The style system carries a fixed, accessible palette that is not stripped when HTML is pasted into a Canvas module. Every text pair is verified against WCAG AA before it can be used:

Ink on white · passes AA Red on white · passes AA White on red · passes AA Muted grey on grey · blocked before use
review

Alt text and PDF tagging need a human pass

Images and files

The tool locates every image and flags any with missing or empty alt text, but it will not write the sentence. Alt text depends on why an image is on a page, which a file cannot tell you, and a generated caption describes the picture instead of its function while looking finished. For PDFs, use YuJa Panorama (district-licensed, already in Canvas): it tags reading order, runs text recognition on scans, and produces alternative formats a student downloads without disclosing a disability. A scanned PDF is an image of text, a screen reader announces nothing, and the failure is invisible to a sighted instructor.

review

UDL: content offered in two modalities, most items accept one submission format

Course-wide

Computed from the package: 34 text pages, video references, 2 images, no audio-only path and no printable alternative. And 19 of 27 graded items accept exactly one submission format. Where the format is not itself the competency (a rationale need not be typed), a second mode, a recorded walkthrough or oral defense, opens a path for a learner who could show the same competency another way. Neither gap is a compliance failure; both are cheap options a learner does not currently have.
