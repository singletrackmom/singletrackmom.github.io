CopaMigo

Case study · AI Tools & Strategy

OverviewLive toolEmbedded examplePRD

A multilingual AI triage tool that answers in the student’s own language and routes their plain-language problem to the right human service with a warm handoff card.

Goal

Get a stuck student to the right person quickly, without making them name the office or know the vocabulary, at the moment that often decides whether they stay enrolled. Every successful handoff is a small persistence intervention.

Every campus already offers more support than its students can find. The services exist; students just do not know which office handles their problem, or what it is even called, and the ones who most need help are often the least likely to walk in and ask. CopaMigo is a stigma-aware triage tool that closes that gap. A student describes their situation in plain language, in the language they think in, English, Spanish, or any language they write in, and it routes them to the right service, such as financial aid, food, child care, or counseling, with a warm handoff card showing the contact, hours, and what to ask for. It is anonymous, with no login. What sets it apart from the chat widget on most college homepages, which only scans the website and hands back links, is that CopaMigo’s answers are curated by the experts in each student-service area, so a student gets what that office would actually tell them.

Audience

Students hitting life or academic barriers, especially first-generation and Spanish-speaking students, who are not sure where to go. The research is consistent: when students feel connected to their college and its services, they stay enrolled at higher rates. CopaMigo meets them in their own words first, and names the office only after it has met them with empathy.

Process

A student types or speaks their situation and CopaMigo reads it for urgency and need, then either answers directly from department-supplied content or points them to the right office with a warm handoff card. A live, in-tool connection to a staff member is a planned next feature. It is anonymous by design, no login and no student record. The full routing model, crisis handling, accessibility, and rollout plan live in the PRD.

Technology

  • A single self-contained HTML file: CSS and vanilla JavaScript, no framework, no backend.
  • Ships two ways from one codebase: a standalone page and an embeddable widget.
  • Multilingual by design: it answers in the student’s own language, not just English and Spanish. It runs on a model optimized for about a dozen languages that handles many more, so a student can write in Arabic, Vietnamese, Tagalog, or whatever they think in. Voice in and out via the Web Speech API.
  • Over one hundred verified GCC service links and a plain-JavaScript program checksheet lookup.
  • Routing answers curated by each student-service area.
  • The Claude API through a server-side proxy; no login and nothing stored about a student.
  • Hosted on GitHub Pages, mobile-first.

Status

Moving from build into a Fall 2026 pilot. CopaMigo will run inside the college’s Canvas course template, embedded in the Student Resources button every student already sees, so it meets students where they are instead of asking them to find a new tool. The pilot has two jobs: collect the real questions students ask, and check whether those questions align with the answers each student-service office has programmed. Where a question has no good answer yet, that gap goes back to the office that owns it.

The answers are staff-verified, not scraped. Each student-service area confirms the top questions students actually ask about their department, and the answer they would give in their own voice, through a staff intake form: the CopaMigo Question Intake form. Gathering the top questions per department is how every routed answer becomes one a real advisor stands behind.

Outcomes

No numbers are claimed yet; the Fall pilot is where they get measured. Success is defined up front: whether a student reaches the right office faster and with less friction than searching the college website, whether the questions students actually ask line up with the staff-verified answers, and where answers are missing so the office that owns the service can write them. The pilot inside the Canvas Student Resources button is the mechanism, real questions from real students, checked against what each department verified in the intake form.
