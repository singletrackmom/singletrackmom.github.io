What Gemini Blocks for Students Under 18

Usability Studies · early stage, in development

OverviewAssignmentsResultsPRDReferences

Can a student under 18 complete a course assignment that uses Google Gemini? A study by the Student Support and Success domain of the Maricopa AI Resource Center.

Early stage, in development. Testing ran from 3 to 4 October 2026. Both accounts were run on all 27 example assignments: 14 AI courses, 11 high-risk content courses, and 2 courses added for Deep Research and music generation. The assignments are examples built for demonstration. The results come from one run per step.

Goal

The study asks one question: can a student under 18 complete an assignment that uses Google Gemini, as written? Where the student is stopped, the study records what was blocked and what the student does instead. The instructor teaches to the course competency either way, so each alternate step is written to keep it.

A meaningful share of Maricopa students are under 18, and Google gives those students a restricted version of Gemini. The study shows what that restriction changes in real coursework, so instructors know where to add an alternate step.

Audience

Maricopa faculty and faculty leadership, district administration, the AI Resource Center, and Google. Faculty use the results to see whether their own assignments need an “Under 18: do this instead” clause.

Process

How the study runs

  1. Start from the competency. Each assignment is written from one official Maricopa course competency, as a simulation, case, portfolio or project.
  2. Run it in both accounts. The same prompts, typed the same way, in the under-18 account and the 18 and older account.
  3. Decide: could the assignment be completed? Completed as written, completed with an alternate step, or not completable in Gemini.
  4. Where it was not met, write the fix. The step that stopped the student, and an “Under 18: do this instead” clause for the instructor.

Content: what Gemini refused in the under-18 account

Google’s documentation does not list these. They were found by running the assignments. The 18 and older account completed each one with the same prompt.

Content the under-18 account refused, and what to do instead
Refused under 18Assignments it touchedUnder 18: do this instead
A role-play or analysis that involves violence or thoughts of deathPSY266, AJS275Hold the conversation with the instructor or a classmate, using the questions in the assignment.
A case brief built from one person’s drug use historyASD110Build the brief by hand from the intake and the federal guidance.
A clinical guidelines report on sexual healthPSY277Read the CDC guideline pages directly.
Evaluating a security scanITS240Use the course text, the lab manual and the lab platform. Gemini is not a route for this assignment.

Recommended practice for every student. AI deep research should never be the only way a student does research. In any assignment with a research step, students also search library databases and other vetted scholarly sources, and check what the AI cites against them. For a student under 18, the library databases replace Deep Research. For a student 18 and older, they verify it. In this study Deep Research was started twice in the 18 and older account and did not finish a report either time, which is one more reason not to depend on it.

Proposed solution

The blocks found so far follow patterns, and each one has a workable alternate. So the fix does not have to be found one assignment at a time. The proposal is an under-18 check that an instructor runs on any assignment that uses Gemini. It is proposed, not built, and not yet tested.

Proposed under-18 check for an assignment

  1. Compare the assignment to the patterns this study found. For example: a role-play that involves violence or thoughts of death, analysis of one person’s drug use, evaluating a security scan, a clinical guidelines report on sexual health, or a step that edits an image or generates music or video.
  2. Draft the alternate. A prompt, or a check inside the Dial Your Course tool, writes an “Under 18: do this instead” clause for each step that matches a pattern, keeping the same competency.
  3. Verify in an under-18 test account. Run the flagged steps once. This is the only reliable test.
  4. Add the clause to the assignment. Every student gets the same assignment, and the clause tells a student under 18 what to do at that step.

Why the third step is needed: Gemini cannot reliably predict its own under-18 filter. In this study the same account answered one prompt and refused the next on the same subject. A prompt can draft a good alternate. It cannot promise the original will be blocked or that the alternate will pass.
