1. Describe your experience with AI, automation, or related technologies in a professional setting.

What tools, platforms, or technologies have you worked with?

I build on the Claude API and Claude’s agent tools, with Google Apps Script and Sheets for workflow automation, GitHub Pages for publishing, and simple integrations such as Discord webhooks. I also work with ChatGPT, Google Gemini and Microsoft Copilot. None of this building was assigned to me. I took it on because I wanted to know firsthand what AI could do for the work around me. Four tools I designed and built:

  • Course quality suite (Dial Your Course). Audits a course against quality and compliance standards. The core checks are rule-based, with no model in them, because a compliance check has to give the same answer every time. In pilot. Overview · Requirements
  • Student journey study. A usability study run by 52 AI agents across ten colleges’ systems, in progress now. Described in question 2. Study
  • Job search agents. Six scheduled agents that find openings, verify each posting on the employer’s own site, filter by level and pay, and publish a matched list. Overview
  • Render. A career tool for the capstone course I teach, where students will build and keep their own career agent. First pilot this fall. Overview · Requirements

What was your role (e.g., project lead, business analyst, change leader, technical contributor, strategist, evaluator)?

Project lead and builder on each tool, and I write the product requirements document for each one. I am also a change leader and an evaluator. I spent seven years as Director of Instructional Technology in the Innovation Center, a unit of the college’s Office of Information Technology. I work in one of the largest community college districts in the country, the Maricopa Community Colleges, with ten colleges and more than 140,000 students. I sit on the steering committee of the district AI Resource Center, which decides how AI tools and practices roll out across the ten colleges, and I co-chair its Student Support and Success domain. On my own campus I convened an AI community of practice of early adopters from faculty, staff and administration. Earlier, I led more than 45 faculty to fully online teaching in a matter of weeks during the pandemic.

How were these technologies used to support business operations or decision-making?

Decision-making. Early findings from the journey study are shaping how we evaluate vendor, enterprise and build options for student support, and I bring that work to the district AI Resource Center steering committee. I have also designed an intake and scoring process for AI requests from across the district, which sorts each request into a pilot, more discovery, a training need, or a decline. The design is complete and it has not launched yet.

Operations, at pilot scale. These tools are pilots, not production systems. CopaMigo, a multilingual tool that routes students to the right campus service, is in a program-wide pilot in my department. The course quality suite, also in pilot, turns a manual course review into a consistent, repeatable check. The job search agents are the one piece that runs on its own every day. They automated searching and verifying I used to do by hand, and the one for our students has posted more than 60 verified jobs since August.

2. This role requires partnering with business stakeholders to identify opportunities for AI-enabled improvements. Share a specific example of a project or initiative involving AI, automation, or digital transformation.

What business challenge or opportunity were you addressing?

Across our ten colleges, students get stuck trying to reach basic services such as advising, financial aid and registration, and each college delivers those services differently. Before the district buys or builds AI for student support, we need to know where the real barriers are and which ones AI can responsibly address.

How did you work with stakeholders to evaluate a process, gather requirements, or drive adoption of a new technology or way of working?

As co-chair of the Student Support and Success domain, I built a cross-college service map and sent it to representatives at each campus to verify. That review is how I gather requirements and document the current state: each campus corrects how its process actually runs, not how the website says it runs.

What approach did you take, and what was the result? (Outcome or impact on the organization, team, or process)

I designed a usability study with 52 AI agents, fifty of them playing student personas, that work through real tasks on each college’s own systems and record where a student would get stuck. I rate each barrier by severity to separate where automation could help from where a student needs a person. The study is in progress, and early findings are already shaping how we evaluate vendor, enterprise and build options. I took them into vendor meetings at EDUCAUSE this week to research tools that could tie the district’s AI student success initiative together. Study

For a completed example of the same kind of work: I was Director of Instructional Technology in the Innovation Center, a unit of the college’s Office of Information Technology (OIT) where faculty came for help with teaching and learning ideas and projects. In that role I led our campus onto its first learning management system. We started with no platform, no standards and no faculty using one. I implemented it with district OIT and sat on the district LMS steering committee, made up of representatives from every campus and OIT leadership. On campus I created the course templates, worked with the training department to develop the faculty training, and taught a class for faculty on online learning. After launch I maintained the system, helped faculty build their courses, and coached them on quality standards.

3. How do you stay informed about emerging AI technologies and trends, and how have you applied that knowledge in your work?

How I stay informed

I learn by building. The first AI tool I built for myself was a professional development dashboard I use on my iPad to keep up with the field, and I maintain a custom RSS feed that follows what leading universities are doing with AI. I completed the League for Innovation AI Fellows program and the Google Generative AI Leader training, and I have taken AI courses through Coursera and Anthropic Academy. This week I attended EDUCAUSE in Denver, where I went to sessions on change management for universities rolling out AI initiatives and met with vendors about tools for our student success work. I am attending two more AI conferences in the next few weeks.

How I have applied it

Mostly by knowing when not to use a model. In the course quality suite I kept generative AI out of the core checks, because a compliance check has to give the same answer every time. In the journey study, four early findings fell apart once I opened the actual pages instead of trusting search results, so I wrote a two-tier evidence rule and applied it back across everything collected so far. Knowing what AI cannot yet be trusted to do is as useful to an institution as knowing what it can.
