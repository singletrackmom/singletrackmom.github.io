The one sentence that gets every fact right: the MEd in Learning Design and Technologies, 30 credits, ten 7.5-week courses fully online through ASU Online, capped by LDT 593 Applied Project and a professional portfolio, in the Mary Lou Fulton College for Teaching and Learning Innovation, MLFC.

Master’s or certificate

Both, and the master’s is the centre of gravity.

  • Learning Design and Technologies, MEd. 30 credits. It is an MEd, not an MA. Degree Search · ASU Online
  • Learning Design and Technologies, Graduate Certificate. 15 credits, five courses. Core is LDT 501, 502, 503, then two electives. Degree Search
  • Educational Studies (Instructional Design), BAE. Undergraduate, 120 credits. Angel Morgan coordinates this one. Degree Search
  • Instructional Design, minor. 15 credits. The college page calls it a certificate and Degree Search calls it a minor, so say “the undergraduate instructional design offering.”
  • Learning, Literacies and Technologies, PhD. 85 credits, Tempe campus, not online. A different program. Do not call it the LDT doctorate.

Program family page: education.asu.edu/degrees/technology-learning-design

Competency-based or seat time

Seat time. Credit hour. Verified on ASU’s own pages. 30 credit hours, ten courses, 7.5-week sessions, one course per session, six sessions a year, fully online through ASU Online. Roughly 1.5 calendar years to finish. Typical enrolment is 6 credits a semester.

The trap. The degree handbook does contain the phrase competency-based, and it is about subject matter rather than delivery. The full sentence says the program “focuses on the design and development of competency-based instructional materials and their application and evaluation in applied settings.” Students learn to design competency-based materials for other people. The program itself is a conventional graded credit-hour container.

If CBE comes up, that distinction is the sophisticated answer: LDT teaches CBE design inside a credit-hour container.

The curriculum

24 credits of required core, 3 elective, 3 culminating. LDT 501, 502, 503, 504, 506, 508, 513, 523, one elective, then LDT 593 Applied Project in the final semester, which requires a B or higher and produces a professional portfolio for job interviews or promotion. Students partner with an external organisation to design, develop and evaluate a real instructional program.

There is no thesis option. The MEd is applied-project only. The dissertation lives in the PhD.

The MEd does not lead to teacher certification, which shapes who enrols.

Who the students are

Broader than K-12, and ASU says so directly. The handbook names K-12 classrooms, higher education, non-profits, and business and industry. ASU Online describes graduates heading into organisational training, learning and development, or higher education. Corporate L and D is a first-class audience here, not an afterthought.

Admission: bachelor’s or higher, 3.00 GPA in the last 60 hours, personal statement in short answers of 150 to 200 words, resume, transcripts, one letter of recommendation.

ASU publicises a No. 1 ranking for best online master’s in educational and instructional media programs, U.S. News and World Report.

AI in the program

The program’s student-facing material says students work with AI as both a creative partner and a personal mentor, integrated across coursework and the portfolio. That connects directly to Steve Salik’s AI mentors work and to the 2026 paper all three panelists co-authored.

The college, get the name right

Mary Lou Fulton College for Teaching and Learning Innovation. The approved acronym is MLFC, not MLFTC. Formerly Mary Lou Fulton Teachers College. Public name change 13 January 2025, systems complete 21 August 2025, effective for Fall 2025. Older URLs still contain mlftc.

Dean Carole Basile framed the change as the college growing beyond teacher preparation into higher education, professional learning and lifelong learning, moving past credentialism and scholasticism. The mission statement reads: “ASU’s Mary Lou Fulton College for Teaching and Learning Innovation creates knowledge, mobilizes people and takes action to improve education.” Two academic divisions: Educator Preparation, and Policy, Practice and Leadership.
