Read this first

This is an adjunct teaching role, not a course-building role. ASU describes these pool positions as teaching one or two courses per semester under a course or program coordinator who supplies the syllabus and assignment guidelines. The master course already exists.

Lead with teaching, presence, feedback and student support. The instinct to lead with redesign and learning-experience work is the wrong opening here. Ask about the design line, do not assume it.

The jobAcademic Associate, pool position #178377

What the role is

A part-time pool appointment teaching one or two courses per semester in the Learning Design and Technologies program, under a course or program coordinator. Pool positions are filled as sections open, not on a fixed hiring cycle.

Classification and pay

  • ASU classifies Faculty Associates and Academic Associates as “Variable” employment, capped at 0.40 FTE or less. 0.41 to 0.48 needs Provost approval; 0.50 and above must use a different title.
  • Postings state non-benefits eligible, fixed-term appointment.
  • Per-course pay is not published anywhere findable. Ask in the interview, or at offer. Asking is normal for an adjunct pool.

The college name

Mary Lou Fulton Teachers College became the Mary Lou Fulton College for Teaching and Learning Innovation in 2025. Public name change 13 January 2025, systems complete 21 August 2025. Shorthand is MLFC. Older URLs still contain “mlftc.” Use the current name.

Yourself

I have been full-time community college faculty for a long time, teaching entirely online, and the through-line has been designing the experience rather than just the content. I did my M.Ed. in ed tech on connectivism and personal learning environments, and I have spent the last several years on the faculty-development and quality side, reviewing courses to OSCQR and Quality Matters, and lately building AI tools for our students and faculty.

Why the LDT program

Two reasons. One, I am the practitioner your graduates become. I do this work in a real institution, at scale, under real constraints, and I can bring that into a class of people who are mostly doing it in their own organizations. Two, the AI and accessibility work in this program is where I already live, so I would not be catching up on it.

Why part-time on top of a full-time job

They will ask. Be ready, be direct, do not be defensive and do not oversell the capacity.

I have taught fully online my whole career, so this is the same modality and the same rhythm I already run. A 7.5-week session at one or two courses is a load I have carried before alongside a full schedule. And I want to teach graduate students who are practitioners, which my current role does not give me.

The questions they are most likely to askAdjunct pool · 30 minutes · three people

1Tell us about your online teaching experience.

  • 20+ years teaching fully online, not emergency-remote, by design
  • Every course built from scratch: outcomes written first, assessments aligned to them, built in Canvas, videos written and produced herself, captioned
  • Range: a 1-credit foundation course up through a capstone, software fundamentals through career readiness
  • Lead reviewer on OSCQR and Quality Matters, so she teaches to a formal standard and reviews others against it
  • Led online transition support for 45+ arts and humanities faculty in 2020 and kept coaching after

2Have you taught at the graduate level?

This is the real gap. Answer it straight, do not dodge or inflate.

  • Honest version: my teaching has been undergraduate. What I have done at the graduate level is the work itself, and mentoring. I mentor probationary faculty through peer review, coach instructors on course design, and run faculty development for working professionals.
  • Pivot to what is actually true and relevant: LDT students are working practitioners, and the pedagogy for adult practitioners is what I do every day in faculty development. That is closer to this than a traditional grad seminar is.
  • My M.Ed. is in this field, so I have also been the student in a fully online graduate program like this one. I know what it feels like from that side.
  • Do not say “grad students are just like undergrads.” They are not, and they will know it.

3You would be teaching a course you did not design, with a coordinator’s syllabus. How do you feel about that?

The fit question. Get this right and a lot of the rest matters less.

  • Say yes clearly and first. Then show you understand why programs do it: consistency across sections, and it is the same reason I build our AVC 100 so any instructor can pick it up and teach it well.
  • Turn it into a credential: I have been on the other side of that. I build master courses that other people teach, so I know what it costs a coordinator when an instructor drifts from the design.
  • Where I would add value inside the guardrails: presence, feedback quality, discussion facilitation, and flagging accessibility or alignment issues to the coordinator rather than fixing them unilaterally.
  • Then ask rather than assume: “What is the line between what an associate can adapt and what stays fixed?”

4How do you establish instructor presence in a fully online course?

Salik’s territory. He researches presence and feedback.

  • Weekly short video, face and voice, made for that week, not evergreen. Presence is evidence you are actually there this week.
  • Feedback that names the student’s specific work, not a rubric echo
  • Announcements that respond to what is happening in the gradebook, not to a schedule
  • Discussion: respond to fewer posts more substantively, and synthesize the thread at the end so students see their thinking go somewhere
  • The Discord community: 14 channels across a whole program, students stay across semesters, which builds cross-cohort mentorship Canvas discussion boards do not replicate. Distinctive and true.
  • Tie to research if it fits: this is social presence in the community-of-inquiry sense, and presence is a live question in this program, not a settled one.

5A 7.5-week session is fast. How do you keep up with feedback?

  • Front-load: the feedback plan is part of the build, not improvised in week 3
  • Tiered feedback: rubric plus one specific personal note per student per major artifact. The personal line is the one that changes behavior.
  • Turnaround commitment stated in week 1 and kept, because in 7.5 weeks late feedback is useless feedback
  • Triage early: watch week-1 and week-2 engagement, reach out to the quiet ones before they are behind, not after
  • Honest: in a compressed term the biggest risk is a student disappearing silently. Early outreach is the single highest-value thing an instructor does.

6How do you handle students using AI in their work?

Very likely asked, and you are unusually strong here. Do not be preachy about it.

  • In an LDT program AI is the subject matter, not just an integrity risk. These students will deploy it professionally, so the class should be where they learn to use it well and critique it.
  • Position: design assessments where the thinking is visible and AI is a declared tool, not a hidden one. Ask for the process, the prompts, the revisions and the judgment calls, not just the artifact.
  • What she actually does: builds with Claude via LTI in live courses, uses NotebookLM, co-chairs the student support and success domain of the district AI Resource Center, which is about AI and student outcomes rather than policing.
  • Hook to Salik: his AI mentors work is exactly this. If it fits, say she read about it and ask how students have responded.
  • Accuracy: her tools are prototypes and in testing. Say that plainly. Nothing is in production.

7How do you approach accessibility and inclusive design?

Morgan’s lane. Your strongest technical overlap in the room.

  • WCAG 2.1 AA as the working standard, applied from the first draft rather than remediated later
  • Tooling actually used: YuJa Panorama, CidiLabs UDoIt, hand-written Canvas-safe HTML and CSS, captioned video produced herself
  • Design-side, not just compliance-side: as a graphic designer, hierarchy, contrast and type are accessibility decisions, and they also reduce cognitive load. Mayer’s signaling and coherence principles.
  • The accessibility checker built into her own course tool, which applies a style guide built to WCAG 2.1 AA and parses the Canvas cartridge
  • If Morgan is asking, use POUR language (perceivable, operable, understandable, robust) since that is her framing, and UDL for multiple means of representation, engagement and expression.

8How do you support a struggling online student?

  • Catch it in week 1 or 2 from the analytics, not at midterm
  • Personal outreach that names something specific, not a mass “checking in” email
  • Route to services rather than improvising: she designs student services into the course itself as embedded learning-design elements, advising, tutoring, disability services, counseling
  • The redesign example: rebuilt the department’s highest-enrollment, lowest-success course grounded in retention research, each module introducing a campus service. Early completion moved from about 44% to 60% in two small summer sections. Say it is early and small.
  • For graduate practitioners the failure mode is usually life and workload, not ability. Flexibility with a firm floor beats either rigidity or endless extensions.

9Which LDT courses do you feel prepared to teach?

Have three ready. Strongest first.

  • LDT 508, Design of Accessible and Inclusive Digital Learning. Closest match to what she does daily, and it is Morgan’s course.
  • LDT 504, eLearning Design and Development. Canvas depth, video production, cartridge-level work.
  • LDT 511, Emerging Trends in Technology and Learning Design. The AI committee work and the tools she builds.
  • Reasonable stretch: LDT 503, Design of Effective Communications, given the BFA and 20 years teaching graphic design. This is the one where her design background is an asset almost nobody else in the pool has.
  • Also honest: LDT 501 Foundations, because the M.Ed. coursework maps to it.
  • Do not claim LDT 506 Evaluation or LDT 513 Needs Assessment as strengths unless asked. Say she would want to teach them after teaching alongside them.

10What is your availability and how many courses?

Know the answer before Tuesday. Do not improvise this.

  • The role is capped at 0.40 FTE, typically one or two courses a session.
  • Safe answer: start with one, see how the session rhythm fits alongside the full-time load, and scale from there. Committing to two and delivering one is worse than starting at one.
  • Flag EDUCAUSE, 29 September onward, if scheduling comes up.

The Archambault hookUse once, do not force it

She published on supporting adjunct faculty across career stages at a small community college in 2026. You are full-time community college faculty who supervises and mentors adjuncts, and you would be entering ASU as an adjunct yourself.

Natural moment: if asked why this role, or at the end when asking questions.

“I saw your recent work on supporting adjunct faculty across career stages. I am on the other side of that at my college, I am the eCourses lead supporting about 30 instructors, most of them adjunct. I would be curious how the program thinks about onboarding and supporting associates here.”
