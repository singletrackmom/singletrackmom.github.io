The questions they are most likely to ask Adjunct pool, 30 minutes, three people
1Tell us about your online teaching experience.
  • 20+ years teaching fully online, not emergency-remote, by design
  • Every course built from scratch: outcomes written first, assessments aligned to them, built in Canvas, videos written and produced herself, captioned
  • Range: 1-credit foundation course up through a capstone; software fundamentals through career readiness
  • Lead reviewer on OSCQR and Quality Matters, so she teaches to a formal standard and reviews others against it
  • Led the online transition support for 45+ arts and humanities faculty in 2020 and kept coaching after
2Have you taught at the graduate level?
  • This is the real gap. Answer it straight, do not dodge or inflate.
  • Honest version: my teaching has been undergraduate. What I have done at the graduate level is the work itself, and mentoring: I mentor probationary faculty through peer review, I coach instructors on course design, and I run faculty development for working professionals.
  • Pivot to the thing that is actually true and relevant: LDT students are working practitioners, and the pedagogy for adult practitioners is what I do every day in faculty development. That is closer to this than a traditional grad seminar is.
  • My M.Ed. is in this field, so I have also been the student in a fully online graduate program like this one. I know what it feels like from that side.
  • Do not say "grad students are just like undergrads." They are not, and they will know it.
3You would be teaching a course you did not design, with a coordinator's syllabus. How do you feel about that?
  • The fit question. Get this right and a lot of the rest matters less.
  • Say yes clearly and first. Then show you understand why programs do it: consistency across sections, and it is the same reason I build our AVC 100 so any instructor can pick it up and teach it well.
  • Turn it into a credential: I have been on the other side of that. I build master courses that other people teach, so I know what it costs a coordinator when an instructor drifts from the design.
  • Where I would add value inside the guardrails: presence, feedback quality, the discussion facilitation, and flagging accessibility or alignment issues to the coordinator rather than fixing them unilaterally.
  • Then ask the question rather than assuming: "What is the line between what an associate can adapt and what stays fixed?"
4How do you establish instructor presence in a fully online course?
  • Salik's territory. He researches presence and feedback.
  • Weekly short video, face and voice, made for that week, not evergreen. Presence is evidence you are actually there this week.
  • Feedback that names the student's specific work, not a rubric echo
  • Announcements that respond to what is actually happening in the gradebook, not a schedule
  • Discussion: respond to fewer posts, more substantively, and synthesize the thread at the end so students see their thinking go somewhere
  • She can also mention her Discord community: 14 channels across a whole program, students stay across semesters, which builds cross-cohort mentorship Canvas discussion boards do not replicate. Good, distinctive, and true.
  • Tie to research if Archambault is on the line: this is social presence in the community-of-inquiry sense. His own recent work pairs AI-generated text with human-created video to build social presence, so presence is a live question for him, not settled.
5A 7.5-week session is fast. How do you keep up with feedback?
  • Front-load: the feedback plan is part of the build, not improvised in week 3
  • Tiered feedback: rubric plus one specific, personal note per student per major artifact. The personal line is the one that changes behavior.
  • Turnaround commitment stated in week 1 and kept, because in 7.5 weeks late feedback is useless feedback
  • Triage early: watch week-1 and week-2 engagement, reach out to the quiet ones before they are behind, not after
  • Honest: in a compressed term the biggest risk is a student disappearing silently. Early outreach is the single highest-value thing an instructor does.
6How do you handle students using AI in their work?
  • Very likely asked, and she is unusually strong here. Do not be preachy about it.
  • In an LDT program AI is the subject matter, not just a integrity risk. These students will deploy it professionally, so the class should be where they learn to use it well and critique it.
  • Her position: design assessments where the thinking is visible and AI is a declared tool, not a hidden one. Ask for the process, the prompts, the revisions and the judgment calls, not just the artifact.
  • What she actually does: builds with Claude via LTI in live courses, uses NotebookLM, and co-chairs the student support and success domain of the district AI Resource Center, which is about AI and student outcomes rather than policing.
  • Hook to Salik: his AI mentors work is exactly this, AI as supplement and as a thing students experience as learners while thinking about it as designers. If it fits, say she read about it and ask how students have responded.
  • Accuracy: her tools are prototypes and in testing. Say that plainly. Nothing is in production.
7How do you approach accessibility and inclusive design?
  • Morgan's lane. This is Michelle's strongest technical overlap in the room.
  • WCAG 2.1 AA as the working standard, applied from the first draft rather than remediated later
  • Tooling she actually uses: YuJa Panorama, CidiLabs UDoIt, hand-written Canvas-safe HTML and CSS, captioned video she produces herself
  • Design-side, not just compliance-side: as a graphic designer, hierarchy, contrast and type are accessibility decisions, and they also reduce cognitive load. Mayer's signaling and coherence principles.
  • The accessibility checker built into her own course tool: it applies a style guide built to WCAG 2.1 AA and parses the Canvas cartridge
  • If Morgan is asking, use POUR language (perceivable, operable, understandable, robust) since that is her framing, and UDL for multiple means of representation, engagement and expression
8How do you support a struggling online student?
  • Catch it in week 1 or 2 from the analytics, not at midterm
  • Personal outreach that names something specific, not a mass "checking in" email
  • Route to services rather than improvising: she designs student services into the course itself as embedded learning-design elements, advising, tutoring, disability services, counseling
  • The redesign example: she rebuilt the department's highest-enrollment, lowest-success course grounded in retention research, with each module introducing a campus service. Early completion moved from about 44% to 60% in two small summer sections. Say it is early and small.
  • For graduate practitioners the failure mode is usually life and workload, not ability. Flexibility with a firm floor beats either rigidity or endless extensions.
9Which LDT courses do you feel prepared to teach?
  • Have three ready. Strongest first:
  • LDT 508 Design of Accessible and Inclusive Digital Learning. Closest match to what she does daily, and it is Morgan's course.
  • LDT 504 eLearning Design and Development. Canvas depth, video production, cartridge-level work.
  • LDT 511 Emerging Trends in Technology and Learning Design. The AI committee work and the tools she builds.
  • Reasonable stretch: LDT 503 Design of Effective Communications, given the BFA and 20 years teaching graphic design. This is the one where her design background is an asset almost nobody else in the pool has.
  • Also honest: LDT 501 Foundations, because the M.Ed. coursework maps to it.
  • Do not claim LDT 506 Evaluation or LDT 513 Needs Assessment as strengths unless asked. Say she would want to teach them after teaching alongside them.
10What is your availability and how many courses?
  • Know the answer before Tuesday. Do not improvise this.
  • The role is capped at 0.40 FTE, typically one or two courses a session.
  • Safe answer: start with one, see how the session rhythm fits alongside the full-time load, and scale from there. Committing to two and delivering one is worse than starting at one.
