How this works

Goal

Give the domain a front door, and a consistent way to decide what happens to everything that comes through it.

Audience

Student services staff and leadership across ten colleges. Most are advisors and support staff. This exists to take routine work off people, not to replace anyone.

Process

Two tracks, one assessment, one register.

Track one, what exists

Inventory first, because the cheapest fix is usually something another college already runs. Each entry ends at one of: share it across colleges, consolidate to a district licence, replace with something we build, retire it, or leave as is.

Track two, what is wanted

Requests for a build, for training, or for a workflow audit. Each ends at one of: pilot, discovery, not an AI problem, or declined. The third answer is what makes the other three credible.

Six assessment dimensions

Student impact · staff time returned · readiness · risk · feasibility · scale. A conversation structure, not a formula. Feasibility carries the most weight, because it asks the question an enthusiastic requester will not ask themselves.

Scope

Recommendations stay tied to AI. Anything that turns out to be a process, policy or resource matter is documented and routed to whoever owns it.

On the tooling

In an enterprise IT environment this function lives in the service management platform, as a catalogue item with a workflow, an assessment record and a reporting layer. ServiceNow, TeamDynamix and Jira Service Management all do this. The district has no platform for it, so this prototype demonstrates the operating model rather than the tooling. Runs entirely in the browser, stores nothing on a server, and holds no student data.
