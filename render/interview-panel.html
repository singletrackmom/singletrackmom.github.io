The same four people interview you for that job, and coach every answer.

The student in every example on this page is Alex Rivera, a fictional Digital Media Arts student in motion design and video. They are applying to the Junior Motion Designer posting below. Render stores no student names, emails, or addresses.

The job posting everything here is built from

This posting is invented for teaching. It is written to the shape of a real Phoenix entry-level opening, and it follows the same rule the DMA jobs board uses: an associate degree or an equivalent portfolio, and no more than two years asked of a graduate. Every score and every interview question on this page is traced back to one of its minimum qualifications, so you can see where each one came from.

Junior Motion Designer · Saguaro Post, Phoenix, Arizona
On-site, hybrid after ninety days · $48,000 to $55,000 · Fictional posting, written for this course

A small post house cutting broadcast spots, social campaigns, and live event content for Arizona clients. You would be the second motion designer on a four-person creative team.

#Minimum qualification
1Associate degree in digital media, animation, or graphic design, or an equivalent portfolio.
2Working knowledge of Adobe After Effects and Premiere Pro.
3A reel of sixty seconds or less showing original motion work.
4Able to take direction and turn a revision round in one business day.
5Comfortable with type in motion, including kerning and tracking decisions.
6Available for occasional evening and weekend event work.

Preferred, not required: Cinema 4D or Blender · sound design or audio sync · vertical-first social work.

What this one is

The same four people who scored your application now interview you for that job, one question at a time, and coach every answer before moving on.

It opens the way real interviews open, with “tell me about yourself,” and it closes the way real interviews close, with “do you have any questions for me.” Both of those are questions students lose jobs on, and both are questions you can practice until they are boring.

This unlocks after your fix list. Practicing for an interview you are not going to get is the wrong hour to spend. Fix the application first, get your score up, then come back here.

How it works, step by step

One panelist asks→ You answer out loud→ It checks the shape→ Coaching→ Next question
  1. Questions are built from the posting you saved, so they are the questions this studio would ask, not a generic list off the internet.
  2. The panelists take turns. The creative director asks about judgment, the senior designer asks about craft, the recruiter asks about logistics, the account manager asks about clients.
  3. You answer out loud. Reading an answer in your head is not the same as saying it, and the difference shows up in the room.
  4. After each answer it checks the shape: did you name the situation, say what you actually did, and say how it turned out. It coaches the structure, not your personality.
  5. At the end you get a read on where you are strong, and the two answers to rebuild before the real thing.

The question bank, and where each question came from

This is the part students do not expect. The agent does not pull questions off a list. It reads the minimum qualifications one at a time and writes a question that tests each one. Cover the requirement, answer the question.

Try this yourself. Take any posting, number its minimum qualifications, and write one question per number. That is the whole trick, and it works in reverse too: if you cannot think of a good answer to the question you just wrote, you found the gap in your application before the interviewer did.

What a round looks like

Senior motion designerShow me a place where you adjusted tracking on type in motion. What was wrong before you touched it? (qualification 5)
AlexProbably the kinetic type piece. I really like how it turned out, the timing feels good and it syncs with the music.
CoachingYou described the result, not the decision. Nothing in that answer tells me you made a choice. Try again and include one thing you tried that did not work, and what you changed. That is the part that sounds like a designer instead of a student.
Alex, second passThe kinetic type piece. I cut it to the beat first and it felt mechanical, so I moved the type in slightly ahead of each hit, about two frames. That gave it a push instead of a thud. It took three passes to get the easing to stop feeling like a default curve.
CoachingThat is the answer. You named what you tried, what was wrong with it, and what you changed. Keep the two frames detail. Specifics are what make an interviewer believe you did the work.
