A panel built for one job interviews the student for it. Every question comes from the job description.

The student in every example on this page is Alex Rivera, a fictional Digital Media Arts student in motion design and video. They are applying to the Junior Motion Designer posting below. Render stores no student names, emails, or addresses.

The job description the questions were built from

Alex entered this job description in Render. The panel wrote every interview question on this page from it, one minimum qualification at a time, so each question can be traced back to a line in the posting. The posting is a teaching example, written to the shape of a real entry-level opening.

Junior Motion Designer · Saguaro Post, Phoenix, Arizona
On-site, hybrid after ninety days · $48,000 to $55,000 · Fictional posting, written for this course

A small post house cutting broadcast spots, social campaigns, and live event content for Arizona clients. You would be the second motion designer on a four-person creative team.

#Minimum qualification
1Associate degree in digital media, animation, or graphic design, or an equivalent portfolio.
2Working knowledge of Adobe After Effects and Premiere Pro.
3A reel of sixty seconds or less showing original motion work.
4Able to take direction and turn a revision round in one business day.
5Solid understanding of animation principles, including timing, pacing and easing.
6Available for occasional evening and weekend event work.

Preferred, not required: Cinema 4D or Blender · sound design or audio sync · vertical-first social work.

How the interview works

The four people who read the application for this job now interview the student for it. The panel asks one question at a time and the student answers out loud. After the last question, the panel coaches every answer, the way feedback would follow a real interview.

A panel member asks→ The student answers out loud→ Next question→ Each answer is checked against its type→ Coaching
  1. Every question is written from the job description the student entered. There is no general list.
  2. The four panel members take turns, each from their own seat: the human resources specialist, the hiring manager, a person who does the same job, and a person on the same team in a different role.
  3. The student answers out loud. Saying an answer is different from reading it silently, and the difference shows in the interview.
  4. After the last question the panel checks each answer against its type, STAR for a behavioral question, and coaches its structure.
  5. The student leaves with the questions to practice again and the changes to make before the real interview.

The STAR method

STAR structures a behavioral answer. Students apply it in written responses, then in recorded practice, then in the mock interview.

PartWhat the answer covers
SituationThe setting: the project, the class, the client or the job.
TaskWhat the student was responsible for.
ActionWhat the student did, step by step. This is the longest part of the answer.
ResultWhat happened, and what was learned or would be done differently.

The questions, and the line in the job description behind each one

The panel does not hand the student an answer. For each question it says what a strong answer includes, and the student writes and practices their own.

QuestionTypeFromWhat a strong answer includes

The same method works on any posting. Number the minimum qualifications, then write one question for each number. A question the student cannot answer yet identifies a gap in the application before an interviewer finds it.

One question, coached

The coaching names what is missing and what to include. It does not write the answer.

Hiring managerTell us about a time you received feedback that changed your direction close to a deadline. What did you do? (qualification 4, behavioral)
AlexI get feedback regularly in critique and I always take it well. I am comfortable making changes quickly.
CoachingThis describes a habit, not an example. A behavioral question asks for one specific time, answered with STAR. The answer has no situation, no task and no result. A strong answer includes: the project and the deadline, what the feedback was, what you were responsible for, the steps you took in order, and how it turned out. Choose one project and answer again.
Alex, second passAlex answers again with one project from the capstone.
CoachingAll four parts of STAR are present. The situation and task take two sentences, the action is specific and in order, and the result says what changed in your practice. To strengthen it further, state how much time the revision took, since the posting asks for a revision round in one business day.
