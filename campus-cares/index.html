1. Summary

A brand identity system for GCC Cares and its new Basic Needs Support Hub, built as live client work by an art director, a student designer, and a faculty mentor. The name, identity, mark, icon libraries, wayfinding signage, and open house materials are complete. The project closes with a brand manual handed off to GCC Cares.

2. Goal

Give GCC Cares a brand that makes the Hub feel like community, not charity, so students walk in because they want to, and give the department a system it can run on its own after the design team moves on.

Deliverables

  • The identity: the GCC Cares mark, palette, type, and icon libraries
  • Wayfinding signage
  • Open house materials: infographics, a brochure, and five display towers
  • A statistics system that keeps marketing and outreach current
  • Canva templates anyone on the GCC Cares team can update
  • A social media marketing campaign
  • Mural designs and concepts for the Hub
  • A brand manual that documents it all, handed off by May 1, 2027

Everything beyond these is a potential project.

3. Users and context

The brand serves GCC students first. GCC is a federally designated Hispanic-Serving Institution, 44% of its students are food insecure, more than half are first-generation, and the average age is 25. Many are single parents, veterans, unhoused, or navigating disability, mental health needs, housing, transportation, and childcare. Design decisions should reflect this community’s dignity, resilience, and diversity. The brand also speaks to donors, campus partners, and the GCC Cares staff who will use it every day.

GCC Cares is consolidating its services into one Hub in the former bookstore building. These are GCC Cares’ plans, still taking shape; our role is to design for whatever direction they choose. Current plans include:

  • Food access. A Market Style Pantry and Grab-N-Go Pantry stocked through a St. Mary’s Food Bank partnership.
  • Social services. Offices for the social worker and social work interns, with a quiet waiting area.
  • Community resources. A partner space for ACCESS and SNAP enrollment and referrals, a Clothing Boutique, and a conference room.
  • Kids Corner. A play area so student parents can use services without childcare.
  • Hangout space. Free coffee, microwaves, and seating.
  • Programs. Cooking demonstrations, Career Services presentations, and a free monthly farmers market.

4. How it works

Team

  • Client. The director of GCC Cares, a social worker, who approves all content.
  • Art director. A Digital Media Arts alumna now at ASU, an AmeriCorps member on a grant the GCC Cares director and I applied for.
  • Designer. A student from the GCC Design Studio.
  • Faculty mentor and creative director. Me.

The full Hub buildout is shaped by a campus committee I serve on; this brief covers only the brand and marketing system.

Design voice

Friendly, warm, and community-centered: hand-crafted and human, like a neighborhood market where people feel like regulars rather than recipients. Language is welcoming and first-person (“Come on in,” “Help yourself”), never procedural.

Identity

The GCC Cares mark, a five-color palette, the Spent VF type family, and hand-drawn icon libraries for every service area, with a kid-friendly set for the Kids Corner. The library works from a social media avatar to a wall, and new icons can be added without redesigning the system.

Scope

Print (brochures, flyers, menus, posters, handouts); digital (social media, website graphics within the college’s Drupal site, GauchoTV); interior signage for each zone; outdoor and event graphics for the farmers market; and mural designs.

Murals

The mural designs are part of this project. Where they go, how large they are, and who paints them are not yet decided. I have been part of both recent campus murals:

  • Pool mural. Designed by my class. I planned the project and managed the logistics, and a faculty member with a history of painting murals was hired to produce it with two students.
  • Math building mural. Created by the Art Club, led by its director, with me as advisor, helping get the artwork onto the wall and painting it.

5. Data, privacy, and governance

GCC Cares owns its sources-of-truth document and keeps it updated; every statistic is confirmed by GCC Cares before it is designed. AI is used for research, content synthesis, and keeping the hand-drawn icons consistent, never as the source of a number.

6. Build and portability

Identity and production work in Adobe Illustrator and InDesign. Templates are built in Canva with locked colors, fonts, and layouts so staff who are not designers can update them. The brand manual documents everything, including a Canva setup guide, so the system outlasts the design team.

7. Pilot and testing

The brand was tested in use at the open house: five towers, a brochure, and wayfinding signage. Canva templates will be built and tested with GCC Cares staff before handoff.

8. Definition of success

  • Adoption. GCC Cares staff produce their own flyers, posts, and signage from the templates, and the output stays on brand.
  • Consistency. The identity holds across every surface, from a social post to a six-foot tower.
  • Invitation. Students experience the Hub as a place they choose to walk into.

10. Open questions and risks

  • The Hub’s plans are still taking shape. The design follows GCC Cares’ direction as it settles.
  • The space is not yet designed. The refrigerators are waiting on an electrical upgrade, and the architects have not been hired; physical graphics for those zones wait until the space design is confirmed.
  • Mural production. Location, size, and who paints the murals are open.

11. Roadmap

  1. Name, research, brand foundation, and brand application. Complete.
  2. Icon libraries for every service area. Complete; photography and video direction next.
  3. Statistics system. Next.
  4. Canva templates, print first and then social media. Next.
  5. Social media marketing campaign, built with a marketing student. After the templates.
  6. Mural designs and concepts. Planned.
  7. Brand manual. Final deliverable, May 1, 2027.

Potential projects

GCC Cares’ plans for the Hub are still taking shape, and those decisions are theirs. If they move forward with any of these, the team can support them with design, as time and funding allow.

  • Farmers market signage. A branded banner, market and wayfinding signs, and table signage.
  • Painting the murals. With the team and scope decided as the Hub’s plans settle.
  • Web graphics and infographics. For the GCC Cares page on the college’s Drupal site, drawn from the sources-of-truth document as it grows.
  • Events calendar. Community partner visits and cooking and Career Services presentations, on the website, a screen in the Hub, or an app.
  • Screens and signage. A schedule and cooking-tips screen in the Hub, a pantry inventory board, QR resource cards, and GauchoTV templates.
