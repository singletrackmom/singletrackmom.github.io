GCC Cares
Owner
Michelle Blomberg
Runs
August 2026 to June 30, 2027

Mission

Give GCC Cares a brand that makes the Basic Needs Support Hub feel like community, not charity, so students walk in because they want to, and give the department a system it can run on its own after the design team moves on.

The deliverable. A brand manual handed off to GCC Cares by May 1, 2027, containing the identity, Canva templates that anyone on the GCC Cares team can update, a system for keeping statistics current so they flow into marketing and outreach materials, and a mural design for the Hub. Everything beyond that is a potential project, supported with design as time and funding allow.

The Hub

GCC Cares is consolidating its student support services into one destination in the former bookstore building. These are GCC Cares’ plans for the Hub, and they are still taking shape. GCC Cares decides what the Hub offers; our role is to design for whatever direction they choose.

AreaThe plan
Food accessA market-style pantry and Grab-N-Go pantry stocked through a St. Mary’s Food Bank partnership, with fresh and non-perishable food and donated spices so students can make real meals
Social servicesOffices for GCC’s social worker and AmeriCorps-funded social work interns, with a quiet waiting area
Community resourcesA shared space for partner organizations (ACCESS and SNAP enrollment, counseling referrals), a clothing boutique, and a conference room for Career Services and partner workshops
Kids CornerA play area so student parents can use services without childcare, painted with kid-friendly elements from the brand
Hangout spaceFree coffee, microwaves, and seating to eat, study, and connect
Cooking demonstrationsBudget meals and basic cooking skills in the nutrition area kitchens
Farmers marketA free monthly outdoor market with fresh produce, community vendors, and sometimes free haircuts

The refrigerators are waiting on an electrical upgrade, and the architects who will design the space have not yet been hired. Design proceeds in parallel; physical graphics for those zones wait until the space design is confirmed.

Audience

GCC is a federally designated Hispanic-Serving Institution, which shapes everything from signage language to food offerings to the bilingual needs of the space. Many students are also navigating housing, transportation, childcare, and financial instability. Design decisions should reflect this community’s dignity, resilience, and diversity.

  • 44% of GCC students are food insecure
  • More than half are first-generation college students
  • Average age 25, including single parents, veterans, unhoused students, students with disabilities, and students with mental health needs

Design Voice

Friendly, warm, energetic, and community-centered: hand-crafted and human rather than institutional, like a neighborhood market where people feel like regulars rather than recipients. The palette is calming and grounded but still warm. Language is welcoming and first-person (“Come on in,” “Help yourself,” “We’re here”), never procedural. The space should feel inviting, not clinical or charity-coded.

Brand Identity

Alongside the GCC Cares mark, the brand is built on a library of illustrations and icons keyed to each service area: healthy food and cooking, community connection, studying, self-care, families and children, and nature and calm. The library works from a social media avatar to a wall mural, and new illustrations can be added over time without redesigning the system. The Kids Corner gets its own kid-friendly subset. Talk with the social worker early about the imagery that resonates with the students who use the space; illustrations by a student who has used the pantry carry an authenticity no outside consultant can produce.

Murals

The mural designs are part of this project. Where they go, how large they are, and who paints them are not yet decided. Michelle has been part of both recent campus murals:

  • Pool mural. Designed by Michelle’s class. Michelle planned the project and managed the logistics, and a faculty member with a history of painting murals was hired to produce it with two students.
  • Math building mural. Created by the Art Club, led by its director, with Michelle as advisor, helping get the artwork onto the wall and painting it.

Roadmap

  1. Name the hub. Complete: the brand is GCC Cares, housed in the Basic Needs Support Hub.
  2. Research. Complete: space visits, student conversations, and a Pinterest inspiration board.
  3. Brand foundation. Complete: palette, the Spent VF type family, and the GCC Cares mark.
  4. Brand application. Complete: wayfinding signage, the brochure, and the open house towers.
  5. Asset development. Icon libraries complete for every service area; photography and video direction next.
  6. Statistics system. Next: GCC Cares keeps its sources-of-truth document updated, and those numbers feed the infographics so marketing and outreach stay current.
  7. Canva templates. Next: templates anyone on the GCC Cares team can update, with locked colors, fonts, and layouts, print first and then social media.
  8. Social media marketing campaign. After the templates, built with a marketing student.
  9. Mural designs and concepts. Planned, with placement chosen alongside the social worker.
  10. Brand manual. Final deliverable, May 1, 2027, with the templates and statistics system inside.

Potential projects

GCC Cares’ plans for the Hub are still taking shape, and those decisions are theirs. If they move forward with any of these, the team can support them with design, as time and funding allow.

  • Farmers market signage. A branded banner, market and wayfinding signs, and table signage.
  • Painting the murals. Producing the mural designs on the walls, with the team and scope decided as the Hub’s plans settle.
  • Web graphics and infographics. Graphics and content for the GCC Cares page on the college’s Drupal site, drawn from the sources-of-truth document as it grows.
  • Events calendar. When community partners will be on site, plus cooking and Career Services presentations, on the website, a screen in the Hub, or a GCC Cares app.
  • Screens and smart signage. The ideas below.

Future Exploration

None of this is a deliverable. These are ideas GCC Cares may or may not pursue; if they do, our part is the design that supports them. Any AI tool in this space must follow the same stigma-reduction principles as the physical space: no surveillance-like data collection, no login for basic information, anonymous where possible, and always a path to a real person.

Screens and signage

  • Screen in the Hub. The day’s schedule, which partners are in, healthy cooking tips, upcoming presentations, and current statistics.
  • Library interactive screen. Resource content, a pantry inventory board, or a CopaMigo access point, reaching students who don’t know the Hub exists yet.
  • Pantry inventory board. What’s available this week, updated by staff from a Google Sheet, so students know before they come.
  • QR resource cards. A small card in each zone linking to details like SNAP enrollment steps or the cooking schedule.
  • GauchoTV. Branded templates for event announcements, resource spotlights, and short promotional spots on campus screens.

AI ideas

  • Resource navigator. A stigma-aware assistant that asks “Here are things many GCC students use. Which sounds helpful?” instead of “Are you food insecure?” CopaMigo, in pilot, could live here as a kiosk or QR-linked web app.
  • Cook what you have. A recipe tool that suggests budget meals from what’s in a student’s pantry.
  • Benefits enrollment assistant. Plain-language, step-by-step help with SNAP, ACCESS, FAFSA, and emergency aid.
  • Content generation. A simple prompt system alongside the Canva templates so non-designers can produce on-brand posts quickly.

Status

The name, identity, mark, icon libraries, wayfinding signage, and open house materials are complete. The focus now is the brand manual: Canva templates anyone can update, the statistics system, a social media marketing campaign, and mural designs. The Open House tab shows the finished work.
