Basic Needs Support Hub

Experiential Learning · Case study

OverviewOpen HouseDesign brief

A year-long brand identity program for GCC Cares, the college’s basic needs department, art directed by a GCC alumna with faculty mentorship, as live client work.

Goal

Give GCC Cares a coherent visual identity that makes its new Basic Needs Support Hub feel like community rather than charity, and make that identity sustainable: a governed system of marks, icons, color, type, and templates that staff who are not designers can apply on their own, long after the student who built it has moved on. The project also delivers a mural design for the Hub. GCC Cares sets the direction for the Hub itself; the design work supports wherever that goes.

Audience

The identity has to speak to several audiences at once, each with a different question.

AudienceWhat the identity has to do
StudentsMake the Hub feel like a place students choose to walk into, for those facing food, housing, and financial insecurity at a Hispanic-Serving Institution
Current donorsShow corporate and community donors exactly what their money is funding
Prospective donorsMake the case for new gifts by pairing the need with proof of impact
Campus partnersGive staff and partner organizations a consistent identity they can apply on their own
Student designersTurn a real client, a real deadline, and a real building into the curriculum

Process

The project is structured as a cognitive apprenticeship (Collins, Brown, and Newman, 1989). The art director generates the concepts and owns the design decisions; my role is mentor and creative director, modeling the professional process, coaching through critique, and stepping in on production when the deadline requires it. The team shapes the content as well as the design: GCC Cares provides its material and statistics, we turn them into the message, and GCC Cares signs off. Each phase builds on the visual language the last one established.

Technology

  • Adobe Illustrator for the mark and the icon libraries.
  • AI as a brainstorming partner for the icon libraries, generating concepts for each service area and bringing icons hand-drawn by two designers into one consistent style.

Outcomes

The identity, the mark, the wayfinding signage, the brochure, and the open house panels are complete. Success for the full program is defined by three measures:

  • Adoption. GCC Cares staff produce their own flyers, posts, and signage from the templates, and the output stays on brand without a designer in the loop.
  • Consistency. The identity holds across every surface, from a social post to a six-foot tower.
  • Invitation. Students experience the Hub as a place they choose to walk into.

For the art director, the outcome is a complete brand system, from research to fabricated signage, in a professional portfolio.
