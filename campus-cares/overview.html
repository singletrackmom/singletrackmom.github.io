Basic Needs Support Hub

Design · Case study

OverviewOpen HouseDesign brief

A year-long brand system and creative operations program for GCC Cares, the college’s basic needs department: identity, environmental graphics, print production, and social content, art directed by a GCC alumna under my creative direction.

Goal

Give GCC Cares a coherent visual identity that makes its new Basic Needs Support Hub feel like community rather than charity, and make that identity sustainable: a governed brand system (mark, iconography, color and type palettes, and a template library) that non-designers can produce from on their own, so the brand scales without a designer in the loop, long after the art director has moved on. The project also delivers a mural design for the Hub. GCC Cares sets the direction for the Hub itself; the design work supports wherever that goes.

Audience

The identity has to speak to several audiences at once, each with a different question.

  • Students. Make the Hub feel like a place students choose to walk into, for those facing food, housing, and financial insecurity at a Hispanic-Serving Institution.
  • Current donors. Show corporate and community donors exactly what their money is funding.
  • Prospective donors. Make the case for new gifts by pairing the need with proof of impact.
  • Campus partners. Give staff and partner organizations a consistent identity they can apply on their own.
  • Student designers. Turn a real client, a real deadline, and a real building into the curriculum.

Team

  • Dean of Student Services. Oversees GCC Cares and its director.
  • Client. The director of GCC Cares, the campus social worker, who approved all content.
  • Creative director and faculty mentor. Me: I set the creative direction, direct and supervise the art director and the student designer, approve each phase before it goes to the client, built the content workflow, and built the social content strategy.
  • Art director. A GCC alumna and AmeriCorps member who develops the concepts and leads design execution, from the identity and towers to the Instagram posts and templates.
  • Designer. A Design Studio student I supervise, who designed the brochure and helped with the identity and infographics.
  • Work-study students and social work interns. Gather photos and video and keep up the Instagram after handoff.
  • Subject-matter experts. GCC’s 3D design faculty member and the STEAMworks director, for fabrication and laser cutting.

Process

The project is structured as a cognitive apprenticeship (Collins, Brown, and Newman, 1989). The art director develops the concepts and executes the design. I set the creative direction, review and approve the work at each phase, run critique, and step into production when the deadline requires it. I also built the content workflow: I gathered GCC Cares’ material and statistics, synthesized it with AI into the messaging, and routed it to GCC Cares for sign-off before any of it went to design. I secured the AmeriCorps funding for the art director role with the GCC Cares director and recruited the subject-matter experts for fabrication. Each phase builds on the visual language the last one established.

Potential projects

GCC Cares’ plans for the Hub are still taking shape, and those decisions are theirs. If they move forward, the team can support a farmers market banner and signage, painting the murals, website graphics, an online events calendar, and an interactive display in the Hub, among other ideas in the Design brief.

Technology

  • Adobe Illustrator for the mark and the icon libraries.
  • AI for concept generation on the icon libraries, unifying icons hand-drawn by two designers into one consistent style.
  • AI to synthesize research and draft messaging from the client’s own material, with every fact approved by the client before design.
  • Built for handoff to Canva, where the template library will live for non-designers.

Outcomes

Delivered: the identity, mark, wayfinding system, donor brochure, and a five-tower event installation produced in two weeks, plus the social content strategy and editorial calendar I built for the Instagram launch.

Social media plan

The working plan for the GCC Cares Instagram, built as a shared spreadsheet: audiences and post series, a posting calendar, a shooting guide for the work-study students, an audit of the department’s campus-wide emails, and a Canvas and syllabus statement faculty can paste.

Success for the full program is defined by three measures:

  • Adoption. Staff produce on-brand flyers, posts, and signage from the template library without a designer in the loop.
  • Consistency. The identity holds across every surface, from a social post to a six-foot tower.
  • Invitation. Students experience the Hub as a place they choose to walk into.

For the art director, the outcome is a complete brand system, from research to fabricated signage, in a professional portfolio.
