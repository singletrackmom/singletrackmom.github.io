Basic Needs Hub

Experiential Learning · Case study

OverviewDesign briefOpen House

A year-long brand identity program for GCC Cares, the college’s basic needs department, art directed by a GCC alumna serving as an AmeriCorps member, with faculty mentorship, as live client work.

Goal

Give GCC Cares a coherent visual identity that makes its new Basic Needs Hub feel like community rather than charity, and make that identity sustainable: a governed system of marks, icons, color, type, and templates that staff who are not designers can apply on their own, long after the student who built it has moved on.

Audience

The identity serves students facing food, housing, and financial insecurity at a Hispanic-Serving Institution, along with the donors and campus partners who fund the work. The project itself serves a second audience: the student designer, for whom a real client, a real deadline, and a real building constitute the curriculum.

Process

The project is structured as a cognitive apprenticeship (Collins, Brown, and Newman, 1989). The designer, a GCC alumna now studying at ASU and serving GCC Cares as an AmeriCorps member, joined as marketing designer, and her role has grown into art director: she generates the concepts and owns the design decisions. A second student from the GCC Design Studio joined to design the brochure. My role is mentor and creative director, modeling the professional process, coaching through critique, and stepping in on production where the deadline requires it. The team does not wait to receive copy from the client. We gather, write, and edit the content ourselves, then bring it to GCC Cares for sign-off, so the work spans content strategy as well as design. The work unfolds in phases, each building on the visual language the last one established.

Technology

  • Adobe Illustrator for the mark, icon libraries, and infographics; InDesign for the brochure and the tower panels.
  • AI to synthesize the department’s scattered web content, printed materials, and statistics into a single body of evidence for client review.
  • Laser cutting at the college’s STEAMworks makerspace for the display towers.
  • Canva, or another tool the department already uses, as the home for the template library.

Outcomes

The identity, the mark, the wayfinding signage, the brochure, and the open house panels are complete. Success for the full program is defined by three measures:

  • Adoption. GCC Cares staff produce their own flyers, posts, and signage from the templates, and the output stays on brand without a designer in the loop.
  • Consistency. The identity holds across every surface, from a social post to a six-foot tower.
  • Invitation. Students experience the Hub as a place they choose to walk into.

For the art director, the outcome is a complete brand system, from research to fabricated signage, in a professional portfolio.
