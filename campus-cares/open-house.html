Open House

Experiential Learning · Case study

OverviewOpen HouseDesign brief

How do you celebrate a space that does not exist yet? For the Hub’s open house, the answer was to let the evidence of need and impact define the room.

The Problem

The dean wanted an open house to celebrate the Hub’s large new space, but on opening day the space held only a kitchen. The design challenge was interpretive rather than decorative: show visitors what the room does now and what the next phase of the build-out will add, in a space with nothing in it to point at.

Audience

The open house was both a celebration and a fundraising event, so every panel had to speak to students and donors at once: here is the need, here is what your gifts already do, and here is what the next phase will add.

From Evidence to Message

The concept was ours: tell the Hub’s story through its impact. GCC Cares gave us what we needed to bring it to life, from its web content and printed materials to the statistics behind its work on campus. We used AI to synthesize that material, wrote and edited the messaging, and GCC Cares confirmed every number before it was designed. The art director and I worked out the content together, pairing impact infographics with student quotes so the installation would carry both the scale of the need and the human voice behind it. The art director designed the infographics, with help from the designer.

The Towers

Fire code ruled out signage hung from the ceiling, and the timeline was two weeks. Fabrication was tighter still: the cardboard arrived on a Wednesday, the printed posters on Thursday, and the open house opened the following Monday. The art director conceived the solution: five towers that define zones within the open room, each carrying the impact graphics that relate to what happens in that zone, now and in the next phase.

To build at that scale, we brought in two subject-matter experts: the college’s 3D design faculty member, who sourced the materials, built the template for the pieces and prepared the cardboard, and the STEAMworks director, who taught the art director and me to run the laser cutter.

Production

Laser finishing is what moved the towers from student project to professional exhibit: every panel shares the same tabs, corners, and edges, so the towers read as professional trade show signage.

Brochure

A brochure, designed by a second student from the GCC Design Studio, puts the same identity and impact story into visitors’ hands and outlasts the event.

Technology

  • AI to synthesize the department’s web content, printed materials, and statistics into one body of evidence, which became the content for the posters.
  • AI as a brainstorming partner for the infographics, testing different ways to visualize each statistic before the final versions were drawn.
  • Adobe Illustrator for the infographics; InDesign for the tower panels and the brochure.
  • The STEAMworks laser cutter for panel finishing, with spray mount on industrial cardboard.

Outcome

Five towers and 60 finished boards, designed in two weeks and mounted, laser-finished, and stacked in the four days between the posters arriving and the doors opening. Once assembled, the towers do double duty: they celebrate the space now and make the case to donors and our whole campus community for what comes next.

STEAMworks made a project of this scale possible: the tables, the materials, and the room to experiment and build.
