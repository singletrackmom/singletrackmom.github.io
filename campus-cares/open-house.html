Open House

Experiential Learning · Case study

OverviewDesign briefOpen House

How do you celebrate a space that does not exist yet? For the Hub’s open house, the answer was to let the evidence of need and impact define the room.

The Problem

The dean wanted an open house to celebrate the Hub’s large new space, but on opening day the space held only a kitchen. The design challenge was interpretive rather than decorative: show visitors what the room does now and what the next phase of the build-out will add, in a space with nothing in it to point at.

From Evidence to Message

Unlike a typical design engagement, the team did not receive finished content from the client. We gathered everything that existed about GCC Cares (its web content, its printed materials, and the statistics that document its impact on the campus community), used AI to synthesize it, then wrote and edited the messaging ourselves and brought it to GCC Cares for sign-off before any of it was designed. I proposed building the content around impact infographics and student quotes, so the installation would carry both the scale of the need and the human voice behind it. The art director then designed the infographics.

The Towers

Fire code ruled out signage hung from the ceiling, and the timeline was two weeks. Fabrication was tighter still: the cardboard arrived on a Wednesday, the printed posters on Thursday, and the open house opened the following Monday. The art director conceived the solution: five towers that define zones within the open room, each carrying the impact graphics that relate to what happens in that zone, now and in the next phase.

To build at that scale, we brought in two subject-matter experts: the college’s 3D design faculty member, on how to engineer and assemble large free-standing pieces, and the STEAMworks director, on running the laser cutter and setting up the files for it.

Production

Laser finishing is what moved the towers from student project to professional exhibit: every panel shares the same tabs, corners, and edges, so the towers read as professional trade show signage.

Brochure and Wayfinding

A brochure, designed by a second student from the GCC Design Studio, puts the same identity and impact story into visitors’ hands and outlasts the event. Wayfinding signs designed by the art director, first made to guide food pantry customers to the new building, are placed across campus to bring open house visitors to the Hub.

Technology

  • Adobe Illustrator for the infographics and icons; InDesign for the tower panels and the brochure.
  • AI to synthesize the department’s content and statistics into one body of evidence for client confirmation.
  • AI as a brainstorming partner for the infographics and icons, and to keep the hand-drawn icons consistent in style.
  • The STEAMworks laser cutter for panel finishing, with spray mount on industrial cardboard.

Outcome

Five towers and sixty finished panels, designed in two weeks and mounted, laser-finished, and stacked in the four days between the posters arriving and the doors opening. Once assembled, the towers do double duty: they celebrate the space now and make the case to donors for what comes next.
