India

About ten weeks, January to mid-March 2028. Family in Mumbai and Ahmedabad, the north while it’s cool, then the south.

The shape of it: land in Mumbai and stay with family, go to Ahmedabad for the kite festival on January 14, loop north through Rajasthan, Agra, Delhi, Rishikesh and Varanasi while it’s cool, then fly south for Goa, Hampi and Kerala. End back with family for Holi on March 11, then fly to Japan.

Stops

Tap a stop for the highlights and where to stay. Numbers match the map.

1. MumbaiEarly January · family

India’s biggest city: Bollywood, the sea, nonstop street food, and your cousins. An easy, soft landing.

What to do

  • Sunset walk on Marine Drive, then the Gateway of India.
  • Ferry to the Elephanta Caves, carved Hindu temples on an island.
  • Hike to the Kanheri Caves, old Buddhist caves inside Sanjay Gandhi National Park, right in the city.
  • Eat vada pav and pav bhaji; let your cousins pick.

Where to stay

  • With family.
  • If you need a night elsewhere: Zostel Mumbai or goSTOPS Mumbai.
2. AhmedabadAround January 14 · family

Your other family base, and the best place in India for Uttarayan, the kite festival. The whole city is on its rooftops flying kites, and the International Kite Festival happens along the Sabarmati Riverfront (January 10 to 14 in 2027).

What to do

  • Fly kites from the roof with your cousins; buy them at Patang Bazaar in the old city, which runs day and night that week.
  • Sabarmati Ashram, where Gandhi lived and planned the Salt March.
  • A morning heritage walk through the old city’s pols (gated lanes); the old city is a UNESCO World Heritage City.
  • Adalaj stepwell, a carved five-story well just outside the city.
  • Gujarat is a dry state: no alcohol sales without a permit.

Where to stay

  • With family. Ahmedabad doesn’t have much of a backpacker hostel scene.
3. UdaipurMid-January

The “city of lakes”: white palaces on the water, rooftop cafes and a slower pace. A good place to stay a week.

What to do

  • City Palace, then a boat on Lake Pichola.
  • Sunset from the Monsoon Palace on the hill above town.
  • Evening at Ambrai Ghat looking back at the palace.

Where to stay

  • Zostel Udaipur, goSTOPS Udaipur or Moustache Udaipur.
4. JaipurLate January

The Pink City, capital of Rajasthan: forts on the ridges, bazaars and bright old buildings.

What to do

  • Amber Fort in the morning, Nahargarh Fort for sunset over the city.
  • Hawa Mahal and Jantar Mantar, a 300-year-old observatory with giant stone instruments (an engineer’s stop).
  • Walk up to Galta Ji, the “monkey temple” in a gorge.

Where to stay

  • Zostel Jaipur, Madpackers Jaipur or Moustache Jaipur.
5. AgraLate January

The Taj Mahal. Two days is enough.

What to do

  • The Taj at sunrise, before the crowds. It’s closed on Fridays, and drones aren’t allowed.
  • Agra Fort, then sunset at Mehtab Bagh across the river.
  • Day trip to Fatehpur Sikri, an abandoned Mughal capital.

Where to stay

  • Zostel Agra, goSTOPS Agra or Zigzag Homestay.
6. DelhiEarly February

Chaotic, huge and full of history. Winter air quality is often bad, so keep it short.

What to do

  • Old Delhi: Chandni Chowk street food and Jama Masjid, India’s biggest mosque.
  • Humayun’s Tomb and Qutub Minar.
  • Lodhi Garden and the street murals of the Lodhi art district.

Where to stay

  • Zostel Delhi or goSTOPS Delhi.
7. RishikeshFebruary

Himalayan foothills on the Ganges: yoga, rafting, hikes and a big traveler scene. The town is vegetarian and alcohol-free.

What to do

  • White-water rafting on the Ganges.
  • Evening Ganga aarti (fire ceremony) at Triveni Ghat or Parmarth Niketan.
  • The Beatles Ashram, and hikes to waterfalls like Neer Garh.
  • A drop-in yoga class or a few days of a course.

Where to stay

  • Zostel Rishikesh or goSTOPS Rishikesh.
8. VaranasiMid-February

One of the oldest living cities in the world and Hinduism’s holiest. Intense, unforgettable, and the place most travelers say changed them.

What to do

  • A dawn boat ride along the ghats.
  • The evening Ganga aarti at Dashashwamedh Ghat.
  • The burning ghats, where cremations happen in the open. Be respectful and never take photos there.
  • Sarnath, where the Buddha first taught, as a half-day trip.

Where to stay

  • Zostel Varanasi, goSTOPS Varanasi or Roadhouse Varanasi.
9. GoaLate February

Beaches, old Portuguese towns and India’s most relaxed state. Fly here from Varanasi.

What to do

  • North beaches (Anjuna, Vagator) for nightlife; south (Palolem) to chill.
  • The Basilica of Bom Jesus in Old Goa, a UNESCO site.
  • Fontainhas, the colorful old Latin quarter in Panaji.
  • Rent a scooter only if you already ride; traffic is no joke.

Where to stay

  • Zostel Goa (Anjuna), Roadhouse Goa or Craft Hostels.
10. HampiEarly March

Ruins of a huge 1500s empire spread across a landscape of giant boulders. A climber’s paradise and a favorite of long-stay travelers.

What to do

  • World-class bouldering; rent a crash pad in town.
  • Virupaksha Temple, still active, and the stone chariot at Vittala Temple.
  • Sunrise from Matanga Hill and a round coracle boat ride on the river.
  • Go early in the day; it gets hot by March.

Where to stay

  • Small guesthouses in Hampi Bazaar or across the river in Virupapur Gaddi. Only a handful of hostels, so book a few days ahead.
11. BengaluruEarly March

India’s tech and aerospace city, home of its space agency. Younger and more modern than most of India, with a big brewery scene.

What to do

  • HAL Heritage Centre and Aerospace Museum, with real Indian aircraft and engines.
  • Visvesvaraya Industrial and Technological Museum.
  • Sunrise at Nandi Hills, and Cubbon Park.

Where to stay

  • Plenty of hostels; check Hostelworld for current ratings, since the scene here changes fast.
12. Kochi and KeralaMarch

Green, calm and tropical: backwaters, spice markets and tea hills. A good reset before heading back to family for Holi.

What to do

  • Fort Kochi’s Chinese fishing nets at sunset and a Kathakali dance show.
  • A day or overnight houseboat on the Alleppey backwaters.
  • Hike the tea hills in Munnar.

Where to stay

  • Zostel Kochi (Fort Kochi), Vedanta Wake Up Fort Kochi or The Maritime Hostel.
02

Where to stay

Hostels, homestays and Airbnb.

  • Yes, hostels are a big thing in India. Zostel is the largest chain, with hostels in more than 35 cities, and goSTOPS, Moustache, Madpackers and Roadhouse are common too. Dorm beds usually run about ₹500 to ₹2,000 a night ($6 to $25). Most have lockers, hot showers, Wi-Fi, a cafe, and group trips you can join.
  • Clean? It varies a lot. Hostelworld’s average rating for India is about 6 out of 10, so read recent reviews and stick to places rated 8 or higher. Paying a little more for a newer chain hostel is worth it.
  • Bedbugs: they can show up anywhere, including nice hotels. Search recent reviews for “bed bugs,” check the mattress seams and pillow with your phone light before you unpack, and keep your bag off the bed. Bites in a row of two or three are the giveaway; tell the front desk and change rooms.
  • Airbnb works in India, and so do family-run homestays and guesthouses, which are often the best deal for a private room. Travelers your age mostly stay in hostels to meet people, then splurge on a private room every week or so to sleep.
  • Book ahead around Holi (March 11, 2028) and in Goa. Hostels fill fastest around Diwali, Holi and Christmas to New Year.
  • Temple manners: take your shoes off, cover your shoulders and knees, ask before photos, and some temples only let Hindus inside.

Sources: Hostelworld India · Zostel locations

03

Weather

Why January to March.

  • November to March is cool and dry across most of India; the north is at its best now.
  • From April it gets very hot, especially Delhi, Rajasthan and Ahmedabad, and the monsoon arrives around June.
  • Delhi’s air is at its worst in winter, so don’t base yourself there.
