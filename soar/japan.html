Japan

About eight weeks, late March to mid-May 2028. Cherry blossoms, the Alps and the bullet train.

← SoarThe planIndiaJapan

The shape of it: land in late March as the cherry blossoms open, follow them north and west through April, and fly home by mid-May. Keep the whole stay under 90 days.

01

Why late March to May

  • Cherry blossoms in Tokyo usually open in late March to early April, with Kyoto and Osaka in early April. Farther north, in places like Hirosaki and Sapporo, they bloom late April to early May.
  • Late April to early May is Golden Week, Japan’s biggest holiday stretch. Trains and hostels fill up, so book that week early or stay put.
02

Getting in

Visa-free, up to 90 days

free

US citizens don’t need a visa for up to 90 days of tourism. It covers non-paid activities only, so no work, and that includes freelance work for clients back home. Japan plans an online travel authorization called JESTA around 2028; check before you fly whether it has started.

03

Stops

The numbers match the map.

1. Tokyo

Late March · arrive here

Blossoms along the Meguro River and in Shinjuku Gyoen, then neighborhoods like Shibuya and Akihabara. Stay a week or more.

2. Tsukuba

Day trip from Tokyo

JAXA’s Tsukuba Space Center has a visitor area with real rocket and satellite hardware. Check opening days before you go.

3. Hakone and Mt. Fuji

Early April

Hot springs, mountain trains and Fuji views on a clear day.

4. Matsumoto and the Japan Alps

April

An original castle, mountain towns and hiking. Blossoms here open mid-April.

5. Kanazawa

April

Kenrokuen garden and an old samurai district. Quieter than Kyoto.

6. Kyoto

Early April

Temples, shrines and the Philosopher’s Path in bloom. Very busy in blossom season, so book early.

7. Nara

Day trip from Kyoto or Osaka

Giant Buddha at Todai-ji and deer roaming the park.

8. Osaka

April

Street food, nightlife and a cheaper base for the Kyoto and Nara area.

9. Hiroshima and Miyajima

April

The Peace Memorial Park and museum, then the island shrine with the gate in the water.

10. Fukuoka

Late April

Easygoing city famous for its ramen stalls. A good last stop or a jump-off point.

11. Akita

Optional

Home of Akita International University, CU’s exchange partner. Worth a look if you might study in Japan later.

  • Rail Pass math. Overseas agents now charge more (¥53,000 for 7 days) than the official online site. A pass only pays off on days with long bullet-train rides, so for a slow eight weeks, regional passes or single tickets may cost less.
  • IC card. Load a Suica or similar card on your phone for city trains, buses and convenience stores.
  • Cash. Many small places still only take cash. 7-Eleven ATMs take US cards.
