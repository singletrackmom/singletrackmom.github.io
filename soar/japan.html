Japan

About eight weeks, mid-March to mid-May 2028 (or January and February for powder). Cherry blossoms, temples, the Alps and the bullet train.

← SoarThe planIndiaJapan

The shape of it (plan A): land in Tokyo in mid-March as the cherry blossoms open, ride spring snow in Hakuba, cross the Alps to Kanazawa, then Kyoto, Nara and Osaka in blossom season, and head west to Hiroshima and Fukuoka. Home by mid-May, under the 90-day limit. Plan B (Japan first, for powder) is laid out below the stops.

Stops

1. TokyoMid-March · arrive here

Shibuya Crossing. Photo: David Kernan, CC BY 4.0

The biggest city in the world, and somehow calm and spotless. Plan a week or more.

What to do

  • Shibuya Crossing, Meiji Shrine and Senso-ji, Tokyo’s oldest temple, in Asakusa.
  • Cherry blossoms in Shinjuku Gyoen and along the Meguro River (late March to early April).
  • Akihabara for electronics and games; teamLab for the digital art museums.
  • Hike Mt. Takao, about an hour west by train.

For you

  • Tokyo has more bouldering gyms than almost anywhere. B-Pump Akihabara is big, beginner-friendly and has English-speaking staff; the Climbing Tokyo meetup runs group sessions.
  • Ping pong is big in Japan: look for table tennis bars and drop-in clubs.
  • Japan has one of the strongest breaking scenes in the world, so look for open practice sessions and jams.
  • For DJing: Shibuya’s club scene and Tokyo’s record stores, some of the best in the world for vinyl.

For an engineer

  • Miraikan, Tokyo’s museum of emerging science and robotics.
  • Tokyo Skytree’s earthquake design borrows its central column from old wooden pagodas.

Where to stay

  • K’s House Tokyo or K’s House Tokyo Oasis, both in Asakusa.
  • UNPLAN Shinjuku.
2. TsukubaDay trip from Tokyo

Tsukuba Space Center. Photo: Tnk3a, CC BY-SA 3.0

Japan’s science city, home of JAXA’s Tsukuba Space Center. About 45 minutes from Tokyo on the Tsukuba Express.

What to do

  • JAXA Tsukuba Space Center’s visitor exhibits, with real rocket engines and a full-size space station module. Check open days first.
  • Hike or ride the ropeway up Mt. Tsukuba.

For an engineer

  • JAXA’s Tsukuba Space Center: rocket engines and a full-size module of Kibo, Japan’s lab on the International Space Station.

Where to stay

  • Stay in Tokyo.
3. Hakone and Mt. FujiLate March

Moto-Hakone and Lake Ashi. Photo: 663highland, CC BY-SA 4.0

Hot springs, volcanoes and the best views of Mt. Fuji. The mountain itself is only open to climbers in summer.

What to do

  • Soak in an onsen (hot spring bath; tattoos are sometimes not allowed, so check).
  • Owakudani volcanic valley, a pirate-ship boat on Lake Ashi, and the Hakone Open-Air Museum.
  • Lake Kawaguchiko and the Chureito Pagoda for the classic Fuji photo.

Where to stay

  • K’s House Hakone.
  • At Lake Kawaguchiko: K’s House Mt. Fuji or K’s House Fuji View.
4. HakubaEarly April (plan A) or January to February (plan B)

Happo-one in winter. Photo: Ski Mania, public domain

Host of the 1998 Winter Olympics, with 10 resorts in one valley and deep Japan Alps snow. In spring it’s sunny slush riding; in January and February it’s some of the best powder anywhere.

What to do

  • Snowboard Happo-one, which usually runs into early May.
  • Soak in an onsen after riding, and try the snow monkeys at Jigokudani if you have a day.
  • Spring is quiet and cheap; winter books out early.

For you

  • Snowboarding, obviously. In plan A, it’s spring riding; in plan B, it’s powder.

Where to stay

  • K’s House Hakuba Alps. Book months ahead for January and February.
5. MatsumotoApril

Matsumoto Castle. Photo: Lightning toothed whale, CC BY-SA 4.0

A mountain town with one of Japan’s few original castles, the black “crow castle,” and the gateway to the Japan Alps.

What to do

  • Matsumoto Castle, a National Treasure; climb the steep wooden stairs inside.
  • Daio Wasabi Farm by bike.
  • Kamikochi, an alpine valley with great hiking, usually opens in late April.

For you

  • Hiking in the Japan Alps from Kamikochi once it opens in late April.

Where to stay

  • Small guesthouses and business hotels; check Hostelworld for current options.
6. KanazawaApril

Kenrokuen garden. Photo: Japanexperterna.se, CC BY-SA 3.0

Kyoto’s quieter, less crowded cousin on the Sea of Japan.

What to do

  • Kenrokuen, one of Japan’s three great gardens.
  • Higashi Chaya, the old geisha district.
  • The 21st Century Museum of Contemporary Art, and seafood at Omicho Market.

Where to stay

  • K’s House Kanazawa.
7. KyotoEarly April

Fushimi Inari gates. Photo: Basile Morin, CC BY-SA 4.0

Japan’s old capital, with more than a thousand temples and shrines. Very busy in blossom season, so go early in the day.

What to do

  • Hike through the red gates of Fushimi Inari to the top (two to three hours round trip; start before 7am).
  • Kiyomizu-dera, the Arashiyama bamboo grove and Kinkaku-ji, the Golden Pavilion.
  • The Philosopher’s Path in bloom.

For you

  • Ride or raft the Hozu River gorge into Arashiyama.
  • Kyoto has plenty of bouldering gyms for a rainy day.

For an engineer

  • The Kyoto Railway Museum, with real Shinkansen cars and a working roundhouse.

Where to stay

  • K’s House Kyoto, near Kyoto Station. Book early for blossom season.
8. NaraDay trip or one night

Todai-ji. Photo: Wiiii, CC BY-SA 3.0

Japan’s first capital, with a giant Buddha and more than a thousand bowing deer roaming the park.

What to do

  • Todai-ji and its Great Buddha, in one of the biggest wooden buildings in the world.
  • Kasuga Taisha’s stone lantern paths.
  • Feed the deer (they bow, then they shove), and hike Mt. Wakakusa for the view.

For an engineer

  • Todai-ji’s hall is one of the largest wooden buildings in the world, rebuilt in the 1700s without modern tools.

Where to stay

  • Hilo Hostel or Yuzan Guesthouse, both near Nara Station.
9. OsakaApril

Dotonbori. Photo: Type specimen, CC BY-SA 3.0

Japan’s food capital: loud, friendly and cheaper than Tokyo. A good base for Kyoto, Nara and Kobe.

What to do

  • Street food in Dotonbori: takoyaki and okonomiyaki.
  • Osaka Castle and Kuromon Market.
  • Universal Studios Japan and Super Nintendo World.

For you

  • Osaka’s clubs and bars around Shinsaibashi and Amerikamura are livelier and cheaper than Tokyo’s.

Where to stay

  • Pax Hostel, Hostel Caranashi or &And Hostel Shinsaibashi East.
10. Hiroshima and MiyajimaLate April

Miyajima’s floating gate. Photo: redlegsfan21, CC BY-SA 2.0

A city rebuilt after the atomic bomb, now one of Japan’s friendliest, next to an island shrine with a gate in the sea.

What to do

  • The Peace Memorial Museum and Atomic Bomb Dome. Give it a full morning.
  • Ferry to Miyajima for the floating torii gate, and hike Mt. Misen.
  • Hiroshima-style okonomiyaki.

For you

  • Sea kayak from the mainland to Miyajima and paddle right up to the floating gate.

For an engineer

  • Mazda’s museum and factory tour (book ahead).

Where to stay

  • K’s House Hiroshima or The Evergreen Hostel.
11. FukuokaLate April to May

Fukuoka’s Seaside Momochi. Photo: Nryate, CC BY-SA 4.0

An easygoing city on Kyushu, famous for Hakata ramen and outdoor food stalls. Avoid long travel during Golden Week (late April to early May).

What to do

  • Dinner at the yatai food stalls along the river.
  • Day trip to Dazaifu Tenmangu shrine.
  • Fly home from here or take the bullet train back to Tokyo.

Where to stay

  • WeBase Hakata.
12. Niseko (plan B only)Mid-January to late February

Mt. Yotei from Hirafu. Photo: Oga, CC BY-SA 3.0

Japan’s most famous powder, on Hokkaido. The season runs late November to early May, but the deep snow is mid-January to late February.

What to do

  • Ride Grand Hirafu, Annupuri, Hanazono and Niseko Village on one pass.
  • Night riding most of the winter.
  • Onsen and ramen in Kutchan, the nearby town.

Where to stay

  • Niseko is expensive and books out 9 to 12 months ahead for peak weeks. Budget travelers stay in Kutchan or Sapporo and bus in.
13. AkitaOptional

Kakunodate samurai street. Photo: Hiroshi Sakamoto, CC BY-SA 3.0

Home of Akita International University, CU’s exchange partner. Worth a look if you might study in Japan later.

What to do

  • Day trip to Kakunodate’s samurai district, about an hour away and famous for cherry blossoms in late April.
  • Visit the AIU campus.

Where to stay

  • Business hotels near Akita Station.

03

Where to stay

Hostels, capsules and Airbnb.

  • Yes, and they’re some of the cleanest in the world. K’s House runs 13 well-reviewed hostels across Japan (Tokyo, Kyoto, Hakone, Mt. Fuji, Hakuba, Kanazawa, Hiroshima and more). Dorm beds run about $25 to $45; Japanese dorms often have curtained pod bunks for privacy.
  • Other cheap options: capsule hotels (a sleeping pod with shared baths, very Japan), business hotels like Toyoko Inn for a cheap private room, and guesthouses with tatami rooms.
  • Bedbugs are much rarer in Japan, but reports have been rising with more tourists, so use the same habits: read recent reviews, check the mattress, keep your bag off the bed.
  • Airbnb is legal but limited. Every listing has to be registered with the local government and can only be rented 180 days a year, and in 2026 the government let cities ban short-term rentals in residential neighborhoods. Expect fewer, pricier listings; hostels and business hotels are easier.
  • Book early for cherry blossom season in Kyoto and Tokyo, Golden Week (late April to early May), and any ski town in January and February.

Sources: B-Pump Akihabara · K’s House hostels · Japan vacation rental rules

04

Getting in

Visa-free, up to 90 days

free

US citizens don’t need a visa for up to 90 days of tourism. It covers non-paid activities only, so no work, including freelance work for clients back home. Japan plans an online travel authorization called JESTA around 2028; check before you fly whether it has started.
