Gap Semester

India and Japan in spring 2028: the calendar, the money, the visas and how to come back to CU on track.

The idea: take spring 2028 off from CU, spend about eleven weeks in India from early January while the weather is cool and dry, then about eight weeks in Japan for cherry blossom season, and be home by mid-May. Back at CU for fall 2028.

01

Staying on track at CU

Talk to your engineering advisor before anything gets booked.

  • You don’t have to reapply. CU lets undergraduates take up to two semesters off plus a summer after their last graded semester without reapplying. You submit a withdrawal form for the semester; a separate leave of absence form is optional and keeps access to some campus benefits. CU leave of absence
  • Check your graduation date. Engineering courses run in a set order, so a semester away can push some classes, and graduation, back. CU’s engineering abroad page says semester abroad programs aren’t available for aerospace students on a four-year plan. Ask your advisor how a spring off fits your degree audit. Engineers Abroad
  • Financial aid and scholarships. Ask the Office of Financial Aid what happens to your aid and any scholarships if you skip spring, and what you need to do to get them back for fall.
  • The other option: study abroad for credit. CU Education Abroad has semester programs in Japan, including an exchange at Akita International University (its spring term runs late March to early August, so it doesn’t match this calendar) and a program in Tokyo. You’d earn credit and use financial aid, but it’s school, not a gap semester. Japan programs at CU

03

Earning money for the trip

The honest version: earn it before you go.

Neither visa lets you work while you’re there. India’s e-Tourist visa is for recreation and sightseeing, and doing business on it counts as a violation. Japan’s visa-free entry covers non-paid activities only. Japan has a digital nomad visa, but it requires an income of at least ¥10 million a year. So plan the trip on money you’ve already saved, not on client work while you travel.
  • Drone video editing. You already do this. Build a short reel and take paid edits through 2027: local real estate agents, event venues, outdoor brands and other drone pilots who hate editing. Freelance sites like Upwork and Fiverr work for finding clients.
  • Spreadsheets and data cleanup. Small businesses pay for tidy Excel or Google Sheets work: inventory sheets, dashboards, formulas that save them time.
  • Engineering skills. CAD drawings, 3D models for printing and basic technical drafting are paid freelance work too. A summer 2027 internship is the biggest single boost to the travel fund.
  • Taxes. Freelance income counts. If you net $400 or more in a year from self-employment, you owe self-employment tax, and that applies whether you earn it at home or abroad. Keep about a quarter of each payment aside. IRS on self-employment tax
04

Your drone

Different rules in each country.

India: leave it home

India has largely banned importing drones since 2022, and foreign visitors need prior government permission to fly. A drone in your bag can be held at customs. Film India on your phone instead.

Japan: register it first

Any drone 100g or more has to be registered with Japan’s transport ministry, show its registration ID and broadcast Remote ID. An FAA registration doesn’t count, and registration can take over 10 days, so do it at least two weeks before you fly. Many places, including most cities, also need flight permission.

05

Passport, visas and safety

  • Passport valid at least six months past the day you come home, with blank pages.
  • India e-Visa: apply only on the government site, 4 to 120 days before you arrive. Copycat sites charge much more for the same form. Official India e-Visa portal
  • Japan: US citizens can visit for up to 90 days without a visa. Japan plans to start a JESTA online travel authorization around 2028, so check whether you need it before you fly. Japan visa exemption list
  • Travel medical insurance for the whole trip. US health plans usually don’t work abroad.
  • Enroll in STEP so the US embassy can reach you in an emergency. Smart Traveler Enrollment Program
  • Money: a debit and a credit card with no foreign transaction fees, plus a backup card kept separately.
06

Before you book anything

  • Meet your engineering advisor and check your degree audit.
  • Ask Financial Aid what a spring off does to your aid.
  • Pick dates, then price flights.
  • Set a savings goal and a monthly target for 2027.
  • Renew your passport if it expires before late 2028.
  • Decide on the drone: home for India, registered for Japan.
