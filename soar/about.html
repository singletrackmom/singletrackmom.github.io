Goal

Soar supports one student through the transition into an engineering program in two ways. First, it builds a sense of belonging in the field: current news, launches, the clubs worth walking into, and the feeds worth following, so the profession is part of his week without any pressure to network. Second, it takes the logistics of the first year off his plate: scholarships and summer internships surfaced early enough to act on, campus jobs near real hardware, a housing guide for the next lease decision, and a lunch plan for long class days. The standard advice to “network” asks a new student to reach out before there is anything concrete to reach for. Soar does the opposite and brings the field to him.

Audience

One first-year engineering student headed for aerospace. First-year persistence research ties staying in a STEM major closely to early belonging, through clubs, peers and contact with the profession, so the page leads with ways in rather than things to chase. It is a private, single-user build, which is why it can stay this simple.

Process

Soar is one living page rebuilt in place each morning by a scheduled agent, not a dated publication and not a job board. Each run curates the day’s headlines and launches, rechecks every deadline and link at the source, drops anything closed, and links straight to the company, program or funder. Dates and pay are labeled as guidance to confirm at the source.

Housing

Housing turns a lease decision into a side-by-side comparison. A cost table shows every option per school month, with food, utilities and summer rent on a 12-month lease included, so the true cost of moving off campus is visible before anything is signed. Each listing card shows the rent, the area, the lease dates and what is included, plus a bar for door-to-door time to each of the three campus locations he uses most, broken into walking and bus legs. A bar turns red past 25 minutes. Listings are grouped into spring sublets and next fall by area, followed by a short checklist of what to confirm before signing. Each check is dated, because listings move fast.

Lunch

Lunch answers a smaller daily question: how to get a real lunch on days classes run straight through, without paying extra or running out of swipes. Every place is numbered, marked for whether it takes a meal swipe or dining dollars, and mapped to the campus map. The rule it encodes: use a swipe when one is available, make your own the rest of the time, and buy a made sandwich only when neither works.

Privacy

Soar collects nothing. The pages are static, with no forms, logins or tracking, and they are noindexed and unlinked. No personal data is stored or transmitted.

Status

A personal build for family, in daily use. It is not a pilot and not headed for rollout. It is also the working model for the agents students build in Render: one reader, one living page, verified at the source.
