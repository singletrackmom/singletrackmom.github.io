Seven headlines worth knowing cold, pulled from NASA, Spaceflight Now, LASP and Colorado sources. Read one a day and you will sound like you have been in the field for years. Links verified 4 September 2026.

NASA’s next great observatory is up

Spaceflight Now

The big one on the calendar happened while you were moving in. The Nancy Grace Roman Space Telescope lifted off from Kennedy Space Center on a Falcon Heavy at 7:26 a.m. EDT on Sunday 30 August, a $4.3 billion mission built around a Hubble-class mirror originally made for a spy satellite. Its 300-megapixel instrument covers a patch of sky about a hundred times bigger per exposure than Hubble, so work that would take Hubble a hundred pointings, Roman does in one. It is now on a three-month cruise to L2 to join Webb.

Drone Practice & Gear

Not jobs. A plan to build real stick time before spending money, then the right first drones to buy. The Part 107 gets you legal, this gets you good. Links checked 4 September 2026.

The smart order: log hours in a simulator first (cheap, unlimited crashes), put hands on a sub-$100 trainer for muscle memory, rent a pro drone for any paid shoot, and only buy a camera drone once the gigs are real.

1. Fly for free at a local club field

Arvada Associated Modelers is an AMA Gold Leader club in Golden with a flying field, a Learn to Fly program and a collaboration with CU Boulder Aerospace Engineering. Their field is an FAA-recognized identification area (a FRIA), so it is a legal, friendly place to practice. Find other nearby fields through the AMA Club Finder. Joining a chartered club also starts the AMA membership clock the AMA Foundation scholarships require, which is 36 months out of the past 48, so the sooner the better.

2. Log hours in a flight simulator

Simulators build real stick skills with zero crash cost. Best value first:

DRL Simulator (about $19.99 on Steam): one-to-one physics modeled on real racing drones, with good structured training.

Liftoff: FPV Drone Racing (about $18.99, from liftoff-game.com): the most content for the money, strong all-rounder.

VelociDrone (velocidrone.com): the racer’s pick, most realistic racing physics, best for sharpening precision.

Uncrashed (on Steam): the cinematic and freestyle pick, beautiful environments for practicing smooth camera moves, which is the skill that sells event video.

3. Cheap trainer drone for real muscle memory

Ryze Tello (around $99, built with DJI tech, at the DJI Store or ryzerobotics.com): light, durable, programmable, the classic sub-$100 trainer for learning to fly indoors and out.

DJI Neo (around $169 without a controller, at the DJI Store, details at dji.com/neo): palm-sized at 135 g, shoots 4K, flies from a phone or a controller. A step up that already makes usable footage.

4. Rent before you buy for paid shoots

For an actual paying gig, rent a pro drone instead of owning one. BorrowLenses (LensRentals) ships DJI drones nationwide with no long-term commitment, so you can match the right aircraft to the job and pass the cost through to the client.

5. Recommended starter camera drone to buy

When the gigs are steady, the DJI Mini 5 Pro (from about $449 for the base kit, at the DJI USA store) is the smart first camera drone. Under 250 g so it is the lightest to fly legally, with a 1-inch sensor, obstacle sensing and long transmission range. Fly More combos with extra batteries and a controller run higher. Start with the base and add batteries when the work demands it.
