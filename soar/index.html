Drone Practice & Gear

Build stick time before you buy.

Simulator first, then a cheap trainer, rent for paid shoots, and buy a camera drone once the gigs are real.

1. Fly for free at a local club field

Arvada Associated Modelers in Golden has a legal flying field and a Learn to Fly program. Find others with the AMA Club Finder.

2. Log hours in a flight simulator

Real stick skills, zero crash cost:

DRL Simulator (about $19.99 on Steam): realistic racing physics and structured training.

Liftoff: FPV Drone Racing (about $18.99, from liftoff-game.com): the best all-rounder for the money.

VelociDrone (velocidrone.com): the racer’s pick for precision.

Uncrashed (on Steam): cinematic freestyle, good for smooth camera moves.

3. Cheap trainer drone for real muscle memory

Ryze Tello (around $99, ryzerobotics.com): light, tough, programmable, good for learning indoors.

DJI Neo ($139 without a controller at the DJI USA store, details at dji.com/neo): 135 g, shoots 4K, flies from your phone.

4. Rent before you buy for paid shoots

For a paid gig, rent a pro drone from Lensrentals and pass the cost to the client.

5. Recommended starter camera drone to buy

When the gigs are steady: the DJI Mini 5 Pro ($759 at the DJI USA store). Under 250 g, 1-inch sensor, obstacle sensing.
