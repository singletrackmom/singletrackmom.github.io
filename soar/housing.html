Housing

A quiet place of your own near engineering and East Campus. Spring sublets first, then fall by area.

← SoarSpringFall 2027Before you sign

The plan: first choice for spring is a single room on campus through your housing accommodation. If that doesn’t come through, a spring sublet, aim for $1,500 a month. Next fall, a 2-bedroom with a friend, about $1,100 each.

Off campus is not cheaper. Food and utilities add about $700 a month, and a 12-month lease means paying rent all summer while you’re home.

Every bus here is free with your Buff OneCard. The bars on each listing show door-to-door time to main campus, the Engineering Center and East Campus; red means more than 25 minutes. The Hill and the big student towers are left out on purpose, since they’re loud. Call before you visit; places go fast.

Before you sign

  • See it first. Never pay a deposit before a visit or a live video tour. Walk away from anyone who wants a wire, Zelle or gift cards, or who says the owner is out of state.
  • Expect to need a co-signer. With no rental history or full-time income, most landlords will ask for a parent to sign as a guarantor. Ask what they need from the co-signer before you apply.
  • Skip income-restricted buildings. Most affordable-housing buildings in Boulder don’t accept a household made up only of full-time students, so ask before applying anywhere that lists income limits.
  • For a sublet, get the landlord’s written OK. Ask who holds the deposit, whether you sign onto the main lease, and the exact end date.
  • Get a free lease review before you sign. CU’s Off-Campus Life office has an attorney who reads leases for enrolled students at no cost. Bring your student ID and the lease. Book a lease review.

Questions to ask every place

Write the answers down for each place so you can compare. If they won’t answer in writing, that’s a warning sign.

  1. What is the total I pay each month? Rent plus every monthly fee: trash, amenity, parking, pet, internet, “utility admin.” Get one number.
  2. Which utilities are included? Go down the list one at a time: electric, gas or heat, water and sewer, trash and recycling, internet or cable. Most apartments include water and trash, and many have no gas bill at all. You’ll usually pay electric and internet yourself. In a sublet, the current tenant may roll everything into one price, so ask.
  3. For anything not included, what does it usually cost? Ask them to estimate the average monthly bill, and ask whether bills run higher in winter.
  4. How much is the security deposit, and how do I get it back? It’s refunded at the end minus damage or cleaning. Ask what usually gets taken out, and take dated photos of every room the day you move in.
  5. What do I pay before I move in? Application fee, admin fee, deposit, first month, and sometimes last month. Add it up so there are no surprises.
  6. What are the exact lease dates? Start date, end date, and what happens if you need to leave early. Ask whether you can sublet over the summer.
  7. Is renters insurance required? Many leases require it. It’s usually a small monthly cost and covers your stuff.
  8. Who fixes things, and how fast? Who do you call for a repair or an emergency at night?
  9. Furnished or not? Bed, desk, couch, kitchen basics?
  10. Parking and laundry. Is there a spot and does it cost extra? Laundry in the unit, in the building, or none?
  11. How quiet is it? Ask who the neighbors are (students, grad students, families) and whether there are quiet hours. Visit on a Friday or Saturday night if you can.
  12. Where is the nearest bus stop? Check it against the times on each listing above.
