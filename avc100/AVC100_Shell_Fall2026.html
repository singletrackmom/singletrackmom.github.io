AVC 100 · Introduction to Digital Arts

Fall 2026 · 1 credit · half-semester (8 weeks) · fully online · Glendale Community College, Digital Media Arts

Syllabus

Course

AVC 100, Introduction to Digital Arts. A one-credit, half-semester foundation course for students across the college, including many who are new to college or to fully online classes. You will make three real pieces of digital art in three professional applications, and just as importantly you will learn a working process, the vocabulary of the field, and where to find support at GCC.

Term: Monday, August 24 through Saturday, October 17, 2026 (eight weeks). Format: fully online. Credit: 1 credit; plan for about 40 total hours of coursework across the eight weeks.

Instructor

Michelle Blomberg · Digital Media Arts · Glendale Community College
Email: your GCC email · Virtual office hours: day / time / link · Best way to reach me: Canvas Inbox / email

What you will learn (course outcomes)

By the end of AVC 100 you will be able to:

  • Operate a computer for digital art and design work.
  • Create digital art and design projects from concept to finished file.
  • Use the core tools inside Adobe Illustrator, Photoshop, and After Effects.
  • Use various input and output devices, from sketches and reference photos to print-ready and web-ready exports.
  • Prepare images for print and for presentation.

These are the five MCCCD course competencies for AVC 100. You demonstrate them through the projects and exercises below.

What you will make

  • Travel Poster (Adobe Illustrator): an 18 × 24 vector poster built from a color palette, simplified shapes, and clear typographic hierarchy.
  • Photographic Postcard (Adobe Photoshop): a composited 1200 × 1800 postcard that extends your poster’s palette and typeface, using selections and layers.
  • Social-Media Animation (Adobe After Effects): a short animated piece built from your postcard art, using keyframes and a storyboard, exported as an MP4.
  • Behance Portfolio: your three finished projects, published and sequenced strongest work first.

Every project runs the same three steps, Sketch, Build, Refine, so you learn the rhythm once: plan on paper and in Miro, build in the software, then revise using peer feedback.

Software and tools

Adobe Creative Cloud: Illustrator, Photoshop, After Effects, plus Adobe Color. We also use Miro (planning boards), Discord (critique and feedback), and Behance (your portfolio). Screen-recorded lessons are provided for every tool. Privacy policies for each tool: add links to Adobe, Miro, Discord, Behance, YouTube privacy pages.

Course rhythm and policies

  • Due dates: Wednesdays and Saturdays at 11:59 PM. Nothing is due on the first day of class.
  • Critique workflow: post your Miro board to Discord at least two days early, give feedback to classmates, screenshot the feedback, add it to your board, then submit the board to Canvas.
  • Workload: this is a one-credit course, so it is sized to about 40 total hours. If something is taking far longer, tell me, that is useful information, not a failing.
  • Late work: your late policy. Academic integrity / AI use: your policy. Accessibility & DRS: GCC DRS statement.

Support and belonging

This course is built to help you stay enrolled and get unstuck. Throughout the eight weeks you will meet the people and services at GCC who can help, advising, tutoring, GCC Cares, and the Digital Media programs, so that by the end you know where to turn. If you are struggling with anything, in the course or outside it, reach out early.

Welcome video script

About two minutes. Record it however is easiest (webcam or screen). Say it in your own words, this is just the shape of it.

Open: “Hi, welcome to AVC 100, Introduction to Digital Arts. I’m Michelle Blomberg, and I’m really glad you’re here. If you’ve never opened Photoshop or Illustrator in your life, you are exactly who this course is built for.”

What this is: “This is a short, one-credit course, eight weeks. In that time you’re going to make three real pieces of digital art: a travel poster in Illustrator, a photo postcard in Photoshop, and a short animation in After Effects. Then you’ll put all three in an online portfolio you can actually show people.”

How it works: “Every project runs the same three steps, sketch, build, refine. You’ll plan on paper first, then build in the software, then improve it using feedback from your classmates. There’s a short lesson video for every tool, so you’re never dropped into a program cold.”

The reassurance: “These programs look intimidating. That’s normal, and it’s okay. I’ve sized every project so you can reach a real result without the software becoming the reason you quit. This is one credit, so the workload is meant to fit that. If something’s taking way too long, that’s useful for me to know, tell me.”

The rhythm: “Things are due Wednesdays and Saturdays at 11:59. Nothing’s due today. Your first task is just to say hi and get set up.”

Close: “One more thing: this course is also here to connect you to the college, advising, tutoring, help when you need it. If you ever get stuck, on the work or anything else, reach out early. Let’s make some good stuff. I’ll see you in the first module.”
