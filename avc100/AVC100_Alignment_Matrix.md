# AVC 100 · Alignment Matrix

**Goal:** Establish which board-approved competencies the course actually evidences, and which it only claims.

**Audience:** Me, the faculty who will copy this course, and any future program review.

**Process:** Map the five MCCCD Official Course Competencies against all 26 graded items in the real Summer 2026 export. Assign each graded item to the competency it demonstrates, or to none. Report the gaps honestly, including the ones that are inconvenient.

### ✅ Competency 5 · Prepare images for print and presentation (~55 pts)

The rubrics evidence this more than the assignment titles suggest. **Read the criteria, not the names.**

| Assignment | Points | Rubric criterion that evidences it | Evidences |
|---|---|---|---|
| Graphic Design Poster, Step 2: Develop | 10 | "Final Miro board submitted with **PDF** and feedback" | print output |
| Graphic Design Poster, Step 3: Finalize | 10 | "Final Miro board submitted with **final PDF**"; "Poster finalized and polished (composition, readability)" | print output |
| Photographic Postcard, Step 2: Build | 15 | "**Page created at the correct size**"; "submitted with **JPG**" | print setup + output |
| Behance Portfolio | 10 | published portfolio | presentation |
| Social Media Animation, Step 2 | 15 | submitted as URL/upload | presentation |

**Competency 5 is the best-evidenced competency after 2.** A PDF is a print-ready output format; "correct size" is document setup for output; the portfolio and the animation are both presentation.

**The one honest gap:** no rubric assesses *print preparation* in the technical sense · **resolution, color mode (CMYK vs RGB), bleed, output profile.** Students export a PDF. Nothing checks that the PDF would actually print. That is a one-criterion fix, and it is worth making, because it is the difference between "produced a file" and "prepared an image for print."

### ✅ Competency 4 · Use various input and output devices (10 pts, assessed in the critique)

**The capture is now graded, and it is graded where it belongs: in the critique.**

Students photograph physical thumbnail sketches and bring them into Miro, then post the board to Discord for peer critique. **That is input-device use, and it is central to the pedagogy rather than bolted on.** The rubric now assesses it.

**Assessing it inside the critique is the right call, and not only for convenience.** Legibility is not the instructor's opinion; it is a fact about whether a classmate can read the work. **Peer critique is the only place where that can be verified rather than asserted.** If four classmates could critique the sketches, the capture succeeded. If they could not, it failed, and the student learns that from the people who could not see it.

### The criteria

| Assignment | New criterion | Pts | Evidences |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Poster, Step 1** | **Sketches captured for critique.** Thumbnails photographed or scanned square to the page, evenly lit, in focus, and at a resolution where the linework reads at full size in Miro. *If a classmate cannot see it, they cannot critique it.* | **2** | input · camera |
| **Postcard, Step 1** | **Sketches and reference photos captured for critique.** Same standard. Reference photographs are the student's own, and are sharp enough to work from. | **2** | input · camera |
| **Animation, Step 1** | **Storyboard captured for critique.** Same standard. Panels legible in sequence. | **2** | input · camera |
| **Postcard, Step 2** | **File prepared for output.** Correct resolution for the output size, correct color mode, bleed where required. The file would print. | **2** | output |
| **Poster, Step 3** | **Final PDF prepared for print.** Correct resolution, color mode, and page size. The PDF would print at the intended size. | **2** | output |

**Competency 4: 10 points. Roughly 4.6% of the grade, across five graded items.**

### What this changes for the student

**Nothing.** They already photograph their sketches. They already export a PDF. **The work is identical; the rubric now looks at it.**

### What it changes for the course

- **A board-approved competency moves from asserted to evidenced.** In a program review, there is now something to point at.
- **The critique gets better.** Illegible thumbnails are the single biggest reason peer critique fails in this course. Now they cost points, and the cost is visible to the student in the form of classmates who could not respond to their work.
- **The most AI-resistant criterion in the course is now graded.** A model cannot photograph twenty sheets of paper a student drew. **The capture criterion is, quietly, the strongest proof of authorship in AVC100.**
- **Competency 5's one gap closes at the same time.** The two output criteria (postcard file, poster PDF) assess print *preparation*, not just print *format*, which is what Competency 5 asks for and what nothing previously graded.

## Unaligned graded work · 58 pts, 28% of the grade

**Not a defect. A question with three answers.**

### Co-curricular, and deliberate (20 pts)

| Assignment | Points |
|---|---|
| Student Success: Online Learning Tips | 3 |
| Student Success: Where to get help | 3 |
| Student Success: GCC Cares | 3 |
| Student Success: Advising | 3 |
| Student Success: Reputable Tutorial/Learning Sources | 3 |
| Student Success: Getting Help with Coursework or Technology | 5 |

These serve an **institutional** outcome (persistence), not a course competency. That is legitimate and they should stay graded: the persistence literature (Tinto; Astin) is consistent that optional support resources go unused by the students who most need them, so low-stakes credit is the mechanism that makes the intervention land.

**Action: declare them.** Group them into a named `Student Success` assignment group and state the purpose in the module overview. A declared exception reads as a design decision. An undeclared one reads as an accident, and a reviewer cannot tell the difference from the outside.

*(Note: "Getting Help with Coursework or Technology" is a duplicate of "Where to get help," is orphaned from every module, and is worth 5 instead of 3. Delete it.)*

**All five competencies are now evidenced. One (Operate a computer) rests on a single item, which is acceptable for a competency this dated.** Twenty-eight percent of the grade serves no board competency, and most of that is defensible once it is declared.

**The change that closed the gap: five rubric criteria, 10 points, for work students already do.**

Capture is assessed **in the critique**, where legibility can be verified by the people who need to read the work rather than asserted by the instructor. **No new assignments, no new student effort** — the course always photographed sketches and always exported print files. **The rubrics simply never looked.**

**Remaining work:** declare the co-curricular items, and decide what to do with Industry Inspiration and the Course Reflection.
