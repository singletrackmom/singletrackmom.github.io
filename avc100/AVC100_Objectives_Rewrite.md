# AVC 100 · Objectives Rewrite

**Status: PROPOSAL.** Rewritten from the repo file. **Re-verify against the sandbox `.imscc`** once it lands, in case the sandbox objectives differ.

**Goal:** Make every objective observable, so it can actually be assessed, and so the principles-of-design scaffold stops being passive.

**Audience:** Me, and the faculty copying this course.

**Process:** Revised Bloom's (Anderson & Krathwohl, 2001). Ban the unmeasurable verbs. Target level for a 100-level studio: **Apply, Analyze, Create.**

---

## The rule

> **If you cannot observe it, you cannot assess it.**

"Understand," "learn about," and "be introduced to" describe what happens inside a student's head. Nobody can see inside a student's head, so nobody can grade it.

**The trap:** *Understand* is a valid Bloom's **level** and an invalid objective **verb**. The level names the cognition. The verb has to name the **observable evidence** of it.

---

## 🔴 The pattern problem

**"You will also be introduced to a few of the principles of design."**

This line appears **three times**, once per unit. It is passive and unmeasurable. *Being introduced to* something is not an outcome, it is something that happens to the student.

**And this is the most important content in the course.** You told me terminology, critique, and design principles are what AVC100 is actually for. That content is currently carried by the weakest objective in the file.

**The fix:** students do not get *introduced to* the principles. They **identify and name them in each other's work.** That is observable, gradeable, and it is exactly what the critique component needs.

| Unit | Principles introduced | New measurable objective |
|---|---|---|
| Illustrator | Contrast, Hierarchy, Alignment | "**Identify** contrast, hierarchy, and alignment in a classmate's poster, and **name** the element each one is acting on." |
| Photoshop | Proximity, Repetition, White space | "**Locate** proximity, repetition, and white space in a classmate's postcard, and **describe** the effect each has on your eye." |
| After Effects | Balance, Emphasis, Movement | "**Analyze** how balance and emphasis change over time in a classmate's animation." |

## Every objective, before and after

### Exercise: Build a color palette
**Before:** "**Learn about** color theory and color-palette creation, and make appropriate color-palette choices for a set of fictitious clients."
🔴 *Learn about* is unmeasurable. "Appropriate" has no criterion.

**After:** "Using Adobe Color, **construct** a five-color palette for each of three fictitious clients, and **justify** each palette against the client's stated audience and mood."
*Create · Evaluate*

---

### Exercise: Draw with the pen tool  ·  Illustrator
**Before:** "**Learn** some of the terminology for Adobe Illustrator software. You will create a document and **demonstrate an understanding of** how to use the Pen tool for drawing. You will also **be introduced to** a few of the principles of design."
🔴 Three unmeasurable phrases in one objective.

**After:** "**Define** the core Illustrator terms (artboard, path, anchor point, stroke, fill). **Trace** a reference shape with the Pen tool, **producing** closed paths with clean anchor points. **Identify** contrast, hierarchy, and alignment in a finished design."
*Remember · Apply · Analyze*

---

### Poster, Step 1: Plan
**Before:** "**Apply** an appropriate color palette, the principles of design, typography, and image selection, plus your new Illustrator skills, to plan a vector travel poster."
🟡 *Apply* is good. But this is Part 1 (sketching), so the verb overpromises: nothing is applied yet.

**After:** "**Sketch** four thumbnail compositions for a vector travel poster. **Select** one and **justify** the choice by naming the hierarchy and contrast decisions that drove it."
*Create · Evaluate*

---

### Poster, Step 2: Develop
**Before:** "**Use the feedback** from Step 1 to start building your poster in Illustrator, turning your iconic elements into simplified vector art..."
🟡 Mostly a description of the activity, not an outcome.

**After:** "**Construct** an 18 × 24 vector travel poster in Illustrator, **applying** a defined palette, limited typography, and a clear focal hierarchy. **Simplify** source imagery into clean vector shapes using the Pen tool."
*Create · Apply*

---

### Poster, Step 3: Finalize
**Before:** "Use the feedback from Step 2 to finalize and polish your poster, then export the final PDF."
🟡 "Finalize and polish" is not observable. What counts as polished?

**After:** "**Revise** the poster in response to at least two specific peer critiques. **Articulate** what changed and why, naming the design principle involved. **Export** a print-ready PDF and a web JPG."
*Evaluate · Create · Apply*

---

### Exercise: Select objects  ·  Photoshop
**Before:** "**Learn** the Photoshop selection tools by cutting three subjects out of their backgrounds... You will also **be introduced to** a few more of the principles of design."
🔴 *Learn* and *be introduced to*.

**After:** "**Isolate** three subjects from their backgrounds using Select Subject and the Object Selection tool, and **refine** the edges. **Locate** proximity, repetition, and white space in a finished composition."
*Apply · Analyze*

---

### Postcard, Step 1: Plan
**Before:** "**Plan** a travel postcard for the same destination as your poster, carried by the same typeface and color palette..."
🟡 *Plan* is weak as a verb. What does a plan look like?

**After:** "**Sketch** a 1200 × 1800 travel postcard that **extends** the poster's typeface and palette to a new format. **Explain** which selection technique each element will require."
*Create · Understand*

---

### Postcard, Step 2: Build
**Before:** "**Build** the postcard you planned, using your selection skills from the exercise..."
🟡 *Build* is fine but there is no criterion.

**After:** "**Composite** a destination photograph, an isolated subject, and typography into a layered 1200 × 1800 PSD, **maintaining** each element on its own editable layer."
*Create*

> **The "editable layer" criterion is not cosmetic.** The After Effects project depends on importing this PSD as a Composition with layers separated. If students flatten it, the animation project breaks. **This objective is load-bearing.**

---

### Exercise: Animate with keyframes  ·  After Effects
**Before:** "**Learn** how to use a template in After Effects by dropping your own picture into one that already has the motion built in... You will also **be introduced to** the last few principles of design."
🔴 *Learn*, *be introduced to*. **Also references the template, which was cut.** Renamed from "Make It Move."

**After:** "**Animate** a single layer into frame using two Position keyframes. **Explain** in your own words what a keyframe does. **Analyze** how balance and emphasis change over time in a moving composition."
*Apply · Understand · Analyze*

---

### Animation, Step 1: Storyboard
**Before:** "**Plan** the motion of your animation before you build it, reusing the same destination, animal, and type from your postcard."

**After:** "**Sketch** four to six storyboard frames with direction arrows, **sequencing** the order and timing of each element's entrance."
*Create · Apply*

---

### Animation, Step 2: Build
**Before:** "Build your social media post... **from the provided template** by dropping in your own postcard assets. You are not animating from scratch..."
🔴 **Stale. The template was cut.**

**After:** "**Import** a layered PSD into After Effects as a Composition. **Animate** two layers into frame using paired Position keyframes, **matching** the storyboard's timing. **Export** an H.264 MP4."
*Apply · Create*

---

### Behance Portfolio
**Before:** "**Pull** your three projects... into one published online portfolio on Behance, after a final round of revisions."
🟡 *Pull* is not a Bloom's verb, and the objective misses what makes a portfolio good.

**After:** "**Curate** three finished projects into a single published Behance project, **sequencing** them strongest work first. **Compose** a project description naming the tools used and one design decision you would defend."
*Create · Evaluate*

## Summary of what changed

| Problem | Count | Fix |
|---|---|---|
| "Be introduced to" (passive, unmeasurable) | 3 | Replaced with **identify / locate / analyze** in a classmate's work |
| "Learn" / "learn about" | 4 | Replaced with observable verbs |
| "Demonstrate an understanding of" | 1 | Replaced with **trace / produce** |
| No criterion (how would you know?) | 6 | Added an observable criterion |
| References the cut template | 2 | Rewritten for the no-template build |

**Banned throughout:** understand, know, learn, learn about, appreciate, be aware of, be familiar with, be introduced to, grasp, comprehend.

## Exercise names, renamed

Each exercise now announces **the one concept** it teaches. The name *is* the learning objective.

| Was | Now | The one concept |
|---|---|---|
| Color Palette Exercise | **Exercise: Build a color palette** | Building and defending a palette |
| Illustrator and the Pen Tool Exercise | **Exercise: Draw with the pen tool** | Paths and anchor points |
| Photoshop Selection Practice (Cut It Out) | **Exercise: Select objects** | Isolating a subject |
| Make It Move (After Effects Exercise) | **Exercise: Animate with keyframes** | Two points, and the software fills in between |

**Verb-first, all four.** The name of the exercise *is* the thing the student will do, which is the same rule the objectives now follow. A noun names a topic. A verb names an action you can observe.

"Cut It Out" and "Make It Move" were cute, but they hid the skill. A student scanning the module list now knows exactly what they are about to learn, and so does a faculty member copying this course.
