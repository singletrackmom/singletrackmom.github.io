# AVC 100 · End-of-Course Survey

**Goal:** Find out whether the student-success supports built into this course actually helped, from the students who used them. Close the loop on the redesign theory: that embedded support makes students feel connected, tells them where to go for help, and helps them stay.

**Audience:** AVC 100 students, final week. Also me, when I redesign this for fall. Also the FEP and any case study I write.

**Process:** Retrospective pre-post on confidence, a rating of each support I actually built, then honest questions about difficulty and belonging. Fifteen items, under six minutes. Anonymous.

---

## Before you build it, three things

**1. Anonymous, and say so at the top.** If they think you can identify them, the negatives disappear and you get the ceiling effect that made your AVC 181 data unusable.

**2. Tell them why.** One line at the top. Students give better data when they know it changes something.

**3. This survey cannot measure persistence.** The students who left are not here to answer it. Say that plainly in any writeup. What it *can* tell you is whether the people who stayed found the supports useful, and whether any of them nearly left.

---

# THE SURVEY

## Intro text (paste at the top of the form)

> This survey is **anonymous**. I cannot see who submitted it, and it does not affect your grade in any way.
>
> I rebuilt this course to try to make it feel less lonely and to make it obvious where to get help. I need to know whether that actually worked, so I can fix what did not. Please be honest, including about what fell flat. About 5 minutes.

---

## SECTION 1 · What changed for you

*Retrospective pre-post. Both asked now. This measures growth without needing a day-one baseline.*

**1. Thinking back to your FIRST WEEK in this class, how confident were you with the software (Illustrator, Photoshop, After Effects)?**
Scale 1 to 5: `Not at all confident` → `Very confident`

**2. And how confident are you with that software RIGHT NOW?**
Scale 1 to 5: `Not at all confident` → `Very confident`

**3. Thinking back to your first week, how confident were you that you knew where to go for help at GCC?**
Scale 1 to 5

**4. And how confident are you about that right now?**
Scale 1 to 5

> **This is your core measurement.** The gap between 1 and 2 is skill growth. The gap between 3 and 4 is the whole point of the student-success modules.

---

## SECTION 2 · Did the supports actually help?

**5. Rate how helpful each of these was to you.**

*Grid. Rows = each support. Columns:*
`Did not use it` · `Not helpful` · `A little helpful` · `Helpful` · `Very helpful`

Rows:
- The student-services videos built into the modules
- The Discord community
- The way the course was broken into modules
- Video demos of the software
- Instructor feedback on your projects
- Critique from classmates
- The course schedule and due-date structure
- Knowing where to find tech support
- The three-phase postcard project (one project across three programs)

> **The `Did not use it` column is not filler.** If half the class never opened the Discord, that is your finding, and it is a design finding, not a student failing.

**6. Which ONE of those helped you the most?**
*(Single choice, same list as above)*

**7. Which ONE do you think you would have been fine without?**
*(Single choice, same list, plus "None, I used all of them")*

> Question 7 is the one nobody asks and it is where you will learn the most. Be prepared to have something you love get named here.

---

## SECTION 3 · Support and belonging

*Likert, 1 to 5: Strongly disagree → Strongly agree*

**8. I felt connected to other people in this class.**

**9. I knew where to go if I needed help, academic or personal.**

**10. If I was struggling, I believed someone at this college would help me.**

**11. At some point in this class, I seriously considered dropping it.**
`Yes` / `No`

**12. If you answered Yes: what made you stay? Or what almost made you leave?**
*(Open text, optional)*

> **Questions 11 and 12 are the persistence data.** Not grades. Not completion. This is the only place a student who almost left can tell you why they did not.

---

## SECTION 4 · Difficulty, workload, and pride

*These are three different things. Keep them separate.*

**13. The level of difficulty in this course was:**
`Much too easy` · `A little too easy` · `About right` · `A little too hard` · `Much too hard`

**14. The amount of work in this course was:**
`Much too light` · `A little too light` · `About right` · `A little too heavy` · `Much too heavy`

> **Difficulty and workload are not the same.** "About right" difficulty with "much too heavy" workload means the course is not too hard, it is too *much*, and that is a seat-time problem you can actually fix. Dial Your Course exists for exactly this.

**15. How often did you feel overwhelmed in this course?**
`Never` · `Rarely` · `Sometimes` · `Often` · `Almost always`

**16. I am proud of the work I made in this class.**
Likert 1 to 5

**17. I learned things in this class I will actually use.**
Likert 1 to 5

---

## SECTION 5 · The instructor

*One question. This survey is about the course, not about me.*

**18. My instructor gave me clear instructions and useful feedback, and responded when I needed help.**
Likert 1 to 5

---

## SECTION 6 · In your own words

*This is the data you actually want. Three questions, all optional, no character limits.*

**19. What is one thing in this course that helped you the most, and why?**

**20. What is one thing that got in your way, confused you, or wasted your time?**

**21. Anything else you want to tell me?**

# HOW TO READ THE DATA

You said the data part is the part you are not good at. Here is exactly what to do, and none of it needs statistics.

## Step 1 · The confidence gains (Q1 to Q4)

For each student, subtract. Then average the differences.

- **Software gain** = Q2 minus Q1
- **Knowing-where-to-go gain** = Q4 minus Q3

Report it as: *"On average, students rated their software confidence 1.8 points higher at the end than they recalled feeling at the start, on a 5-point scale."*

**Do not** average Q1 separately and Q2 separately and compare those. Subtract within each student first, then average. That is the whole method.

**A gain under 0.5 is noise. Over 1.0 is real. Over 1.5 is a strong result.**

## Step 2 · The supports table (Q5)

For each support, count two numbers:

- **Reach:** what percent did NOT choose "Did not use it"? *(Did they even encounter it?)*
- **Impact:** of the people who used it, what percent said "Helpful" or "Very helpful"?

**These are different problems with different fixes.**

- **Low reach, high impact** → students who found it loved it, but most never found it. **This is a visibility problem. Move it, link it, require it.** Discord is the likeliest candidate here.
- **High reach, low impact** → everyone hit it, nobody valued it. **This is a quality problem, or it is filler. Cut it or rebuild it.**
- **High reach, high impact** → keep, and do more of it.
- **Low reach, low impact** → cut it. It is costing you time and giving nothing back.

Put this in a simple 2x2. It will tell you what to build next in about ten seconds.

## Step 3 · Persistence (Q11 and Q12)

Report the raw number: *"Of N respondents, X seriously considered dropping. Their reasons were…"*

Then read Q12 answers one by one and group them by theme. Do not count. **Read them.** Five students telling you the same thing in their own words is stronger evidence than a Likert average, and it will tell you what to fix.

## Step 4 · Difficulty versus workload (Q13, Q14, Q15)

Cross-tabulate difficulty against workload. You are looking for one specific pattern:

**"About right" difficulty + "too heavy" workload** = the course is correctly pitched but oversized. That is a seat-time finding, it is fixable, and it connects directly to the accreditor's concern about credit hours in online courses.

If overwhelm (Q15) is `Often` or `Almost always` for more than about a third of respondents, the course is not too hard, it is too dense. Different fix.

## Step 5 · The open text (Q19 to Q21)

Read all of them before you count anything. Pull out the exact sentences that repeat. **Quote them verbatim in your writeup.** One student sentence is worth more than a bar chart to any reader, and it is the only thing that will land in an interview.

# CAVEATS TO STATE OUT LOUD

Any honest writeup of this data has to say these things. Saying them makes the rest of it more credible, not less.

1. **Survivor bias.** Only students who completed the course answered. The people this redesign most needed to help may not be represented at all.
2. **Retrospective self-report.** Students are recalling how they felt in week one, not reporting it in week one. That is a known, accepted method, and it is still self-report.
3. **Small n.** A summer session of AVC 100 is not a study. It is a signal.
4. **Summer sessions have low persistence regardless of design.** Do not attribute the whole thing to the course.
5. **No control group.** I cannot claim the redesign caused anything. I can claim students who took the redesigned course reported X.

**Nothing here proves the redesign worked. It tells me whether it is worth testing properly in the fall, at full length, with a comparison.** That is what this is for.
