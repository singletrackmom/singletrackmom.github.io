The Tools We Use, and What They Know About You

This course asks you to create accounts on four commercial platforms. That is a real thing to ask of you, and you should know what you are agreeing to before you do it.

None of these companies are part of the college. They are outside vendors, and they collect data about you. Below is what each one does, why we use it, and where to read the fine print.

Discord · where critique happens

What it is for: posting your work in progress and giving feedback to classmates.

What to know: Discord stores your messages indefinitely, and they can be handed over under a subpoena. Do not post anything there you would not be comfortable seeing in public. You may use a display name that is not your legal name; just tell me what it is so I can give you credit.

Discord Privacy Policy

Miro · where your process board lives

What it is for: assembling your sketches, references, and feedback into one board per project.

What to know: anything you upload is stored on Miro's servers. Boards you share by link can be opened by anyone who has the link, so share deliberately.

Miro Privacy Policy

Adobe Creative Cloud · the software

What it is for: Illustrator, Photoshop, and After Effects. The college licenses this for you.

What to know: Adobe collects usage data and, if you store files in the Creative Cloud, your files. You can work locally instead of syncing to the cloud if you would rather Adobe not hold your work.

Adobe Privacy Policy

Behance · your published portfolio

What it is for: publishing your three finished projects where an employer could find them. Behance is owned by Adobe.

What to know: this one is public on purpose. That is the point of a portfolio. You control what you post and you can unpublish a project at any time. What you cannot control is who saw it while it was up.

Adobe / Behance Privacy Policy

YouTube · embedded tutorials

What it is for: some of the tutorials and inspiration videos in this course are hosted on YouTube.

What to know: you do not need an account to watch them. If you are signed into Google while watching, Google logs it.

Google Privacy Policy

These tools are required, and I want to be straight with you about that.

Adobe Creative Cloud is the software this course teaches. Discord is where critique happens, and critique is a large part of your grade. There is not a version of this course without them, and it would not be honest of me to pretend otherwise.

That is exactly why this page exists. You cannot opt out, so you should at least know what you are signing up for. That is true of this course and it will be true of every job you ever take in this field.

What you can control: your Discord display name does not have to be your legal name (tell me what it is so I can give you credit). You can work locally in Adobe instead of syncing your files to the cloud. And you decide what goes on Behance and what does not.

If a required tool is a barrier for you — for accessibility, cost, safety, or any reason you do not have to explain to me — email me in the first week. I cannot remove the tool from the course, but I can work with you and with Disability Resource Services to find a way through. Tell me early. Week seven is when I can no longer help.

Why this page exists

You are going to work in this industry, and every studio, agency, and client you ever work for will ask you to sign up for something. Reading what you are agreeing to, and knowing you can ask for an alternative, is a professional skill.
