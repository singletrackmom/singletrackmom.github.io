**Goal:** Replace AVC248's two portfolio *presentations* with narrated **walkthrough videos that live inside the portfolio, attached to each project.** A one-time three-minute talk about three projects is a school artifact that dies the day the semester ends. A short case-study video attached to each project is what employers actually watch, and it keeps working after graduation.

**Audience:** AVC248 Design Self Promotion students, graduating DMA designers building a job-ready portfolio.

**Process:** Midterm becomes a **rough cut** (one walkthrough video, in progress, critiqued). Final becomes the **full set** (a walkthrough per project, embedded in the portfolio). Competency 7 gets re-anchored, because the presentation was quietly carrying it.

#### The change

| | Now | Proposed |
|---|---|---|
| **Midterm (M4)** | Midterm In-Process Portfolio Presentation · ~3 hrs recorded · one talk covering ~3 projects | **Rough Cut Walkthrough** · ONE project, one video, deliberately unfinished. Posted for critique. |
| **Final (M8)** | Final Portfolio Presentation · ~2.5 hrs recorded · one talk covering the portfolio | **Portfolio Walkthroughs** · a short narrated video per project, embedded on that project's page in the portfolio. |

Everything else in M4 and M8 (Portfolio / Reel Plan, Final Resume, Identity Package, Leave-Behind, Updated Portfolio, Updated LinkedIn) stays as is.

#### Why the midterm stays a rough cut, and does not just disappear

The midterm is where students find out their video is too long, that they mumble, that they narrate *what they did* instead of *why they decided it*. If the first walkthrough they ever make is also the final one, they get no rep and no critique. Making the midterm an explicitly rough, single-project draft keeps the in-process critique loop that the presentation used to provide, at a fraction of the production anxiety.

#### ⚠️ The competency problem (read this before touching Canvas)

`competencies.html` currently maps the **Midterm Portfolio Presentation** under **TWO** competencies:

- **Competency 8** (portfolio, online and printed) · fine, walkthroughs serve this better than a presentation did.
- **Competency 7** (interview skills / professional etiquette) · **this is the problem.** It is listed alongside the Mock Interview, Big Interview, and the IP1 to IP12 interview-practice through-line.

That mapping exists for a reason: standing up and talking about your work under a little pressure **is** interview rehearsal. A walkthrough video is not. It is asynchronous, re-recordable, and about the work rather than about you performing.

**If both presentations become walkthrough videos, Competency 7 loses an anchor and nobody notices until an assessment review.**

Two honest fixes, pick one:

1. **Re-anchor Competency 7** on the Mock Interview + Big Interview + the IP1 to IP12 through-line, and remove the Midterm Portfolio Presentation from that row. Defensible: the interview through-line is already the substance of that competency, and the presentation was arguably a stretch fit.
2. **Add a "talk about your work" beat to the interview through-line.** One IP week becomes "walk me through a project," which is a real interview question and puts the skill back where it belongs, inside interview practice, where it is assessed properly.

Option 2 is stronger. It does not weaken the competency, it moves the skill to a better home and makes the mapping more honest than it was before.

#### What a walkthrough video actually is (spec sketch, needs your voice)

Short. Two to three minutes per project, not a presentation.

Structure, and this is the part worth teaching hard, because it is what separates a portfolio from a folder of pictures:

1. **The problem.** What was the brief, the constraint, the audience?
2. **The decision.** Show one real fork in the road and say why you went the way you went. Not "then I picked blue." Why blue.
3. **The result.** What the finished thing does.

The failure mode to warn them about: narrating a tour of their own screen ("then I opened Illustrator and made a shape"). Nobody hires that. Employers watch a walkthrough to hear a designer *think*.

#### New idea: a Custom Domain page

Optional for students, not required, but real. Verified July 11, 2026:

| Platform | Custom domain? | Real cost to a student |
|---|---|---|
| **Behance** | **NO.** Never has. Always `behance.net/name` | Free |
| **Adobe Portfolio** | Yes | **Free while enrolled** (comes with Maricopa Creative Cloud) · **DIES at graduation** |
| **GitHub Pages** | Yes | **Free forever.** Only cost is the domain itself |
| **Wix** | Only on a paid Premium plan | Free tier is `wixsite.com/name` only |
| **Squarespace** | Yes | **No free tier at all.** Paid only |
| **ArtStation** (the games/3D one) | **Pro only** | Paid. Domains from about $20/yr |

**The one idea the page teaches: your domain is portable, your host is not.**

Buy `yourname.com` once (about $12/yr). That is the only unavoidable cost and you own it forever. Point it at whatever is free today. Re-point it when that changes.

**The trap that makes this page necessary:** a student who points a new domain at Adobe Portfolio gets a beautiful free site that evaporates the semester they lose Creative Cloud. The domain survives. The host does not. If we do not say this out loud, we graduate people whose portfolio 404s exactly when they need it. This connects directly to the "About your Behance after graduation" note already written into AVC100.

Also: **Behance and a portfolio site are not competitors.** Behance is discovery, the place recruiters browse. The domain is the thing on the résumé. Students should have both, and the walkthrough videos should live on both.

Michelle can teach this credibly because she did exactly this in July 2026: bought `michelleblomberg.com`, pointed it at free GitHub Pages.
