## My self-built syllabus

Five modules, roughly two weeks each. Every module has skills to own, vocabulary to own, real
resources, and one applied task that becomes a portfolio or reel piece.

### Module 1: Animation Fundamentals (the principles, not the buttons)

**Skills to own:** timing, spacing, easing, anticipation, follow through, the difference between
linear and eased motion, why good motion feels physical.

**Vocabulary to own:** keyframe, ease in / ease out, the graph editor, spacing vs timing,
squash and stretch, overshoot, the twelve principles of animation.

**Real resources (free / low cost):**
- *The Illusion of Life: 12 Principles of Animation* overview by AlanBeckerTutorials on YouTube (free): https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=uDqjIdI4bF4
- *Animation Bootcamp* free lessons and the curriculum overview from School of Motion: https://www.schoolofmotion.com/blog
- Easing reference and cheat sheet: https://easings.net/

**Applied task:** Animate a single bouncing ball, then a logo reveal, paying attention only to
timing and easing. Post both side by side and write one paragraph on what changed when I fixed the
spacing. This is the first thing that goes in my "learning in public" thread.

---

### Module 2: After Effects, for Real This Time

**Skills to own:** shape layers, text animators, parenting, masks, track mattes, pre-comps, simple
expressions (wiggle, loopOut, time), rendering clean exports.

**Vocabulary to own:** composition, pre-comp, anchor point, parenting, track matte, alpha vs luma,
expression, null object, render queue vs Media Encoder.

**Real resources (free / low cost):**
- Adobe's official *After Effects User Guide* and Get Started tutorials (free): https://helpx.adobe.com/after-effects/tutorials.html
- *Explainer Camp* and free tutorials from School of Motion blog and YouTube
- Motion Design School and Ben Marriott free YouTube tutorials on shape and text animation: https://www.youtube.com/@BenMarriott

**Applied task:** Build one 10 to 15 second animated typography piece set to a quote I like, using
text animators and at least one simple expression. Export it clean at 1080p. This is reel piece one.

---

### Module 3: Editing and Timing in Premiere Pro

**Skills to own:** importing and organizing footage, cutting on the beat, basic color and audio
levels, exporting for web and social, the rhythm of a good cut.

**Vocabulary to own:** sequence, J-cut and L-cut, ripple delete, transitions vs cuts, lower third,
loudness (LUFS), proxy, export preset.

**Real resources (free / low cost):**
- Adobe's official *Premiere Pro* Get Started tutorials (free): https://helpx.adobe.com/premiere-pro/tutorials.html
- *Premiere Gal* free editing tutorials on YouTube: https://www.youtube.com/@PremiereGal
- Free music for practice edits: https://freemusicarchive.org/

**Applied task:** Cut a 30 second highlight edit from free stock or my own phone footage, timed to a
music track, with clean audio levels. Add one animated title made in After Effects. This becomes
reel piece two and proves I can move between the two apps.

---

### Module 4: Designing Motion for Social

**Skills to own:** designing for 9:16 and 1:1, safe zones, motion that reads with sound off,
captions and kinetic text, keeping brand and type consistent across frames.

**Vocabulary to own:** aspect ratio, safe zone, sound-off design, kinetic typography, looping
content, frame rate for social, accessibility captions.

**Real resources (free / low cost):**
- Meta's *Creative Best Practices* for Reels and social video (free): https://www.facebook.com/business/help
- School of Motion blog articles on designing for social and vertical formats
- Free caption and subtitle tooling overview and accessibility basics: https://www.w3.org/WAI/media/av/captions/

**Applied task:** Make one 15 second vertical (9:16) social piece for a real or invented local
business, designed to read with the sound off, with kinetic captions. This is reel piece three and
the most "hireable" looking item, because it matches what this studio actually makes.

---

### Module 5: Build the Reel and Get Feedback

**Skills to own:** sequencing a reel for impact, writing shot-by-shot context, exporting and hosting,
asking for and using feedback, presenting work like a professional.

**Vocabulary to own:** reel structure (hook, range, close), case study, call sheet of pieces, Vimeo
vs YouTube hosting, feedback loop, iteration.

**Real resources (free / low cost):**
- School of Motion *How to Make a Motion Design Reel* guidance (free blog content)
- Motionographer and Behance Motion galleries to study strong junior reels: https://www.behance.net/galleries/motion
- Free hosting: Vimeo basic and YouTube
- Feedback: post in the GCC Digital Media Discord and one motion design subreddit or Discord, and
  ask one contact from my networking tracker for a 10 minute review.

**Applied task:** Assemble reel pieces one through three plus one more short piece into a 45 to 60
second reel. Write a one line context note for each piece. Post it, ask three people for feedback,
and do one revision pass. This is the deliverable that makes me a real candidate for the job.

## How I run and improve this agent

Each AI chat starts fresh and remembers nothing, so this file is the part that carries my context.
When I sit down to work I paste this file into Claude (or another AI tool) and say what I want help
with this week. For example:

> Here is my career agent file. I just finished the Module 2 applied task and exported my
> typography piece. Look at where I am, suggest the most useful next step, and give me a focused
> plan for my 8 hours this week.

Then I update this file: check off what I finished, add links to the work I made, adjust the gaps
as they shrink, and add new ones as the job market shifts. The agent is never "done." It grows up
with me, the same way my portfolio does.
