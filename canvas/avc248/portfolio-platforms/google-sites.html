Google Sites

The easiest free website you can make. It will not look as good as the others, and you should know that before you choose it.

← Back to all platforms
Cost: $0 foreverCode needed: NoneCustom domain: Yes, freeSetup: under 1 hour

Goal

Get something live, free, this week, with the least possible friction.

Audience

Anyone who needs a site up now, or who wants a placeholder while they build something better.

Process

Sign in with the Google account you already have, pick a layout, add your projects, publish.

The honest pitch, and the honest problem

Google Sites is free forever, supports a custom domain for free, and you can have it live in under an hour. That combination genuinely does not exist anywhere else on this list. Free hosting and a free custom domain is rare.

And it will not look as good as the others. I am not going to soften that.

The templates are plain, the typography is limited, and there is very little you can do about either. You are a designer. A hiring manager who opens your portfolio and finds a generic site is going to notice, and it is fair for them to notice, because the site is a work sample whether you meant it that way or not.

It also reads as a school project, because schools are what use it.

So when is it the right call?

When something live beats nothing live. A plain site that exists today beats a beautiful site you are still planning in October. If you are applying for jobs now and have no portfolio now put this up and improve it later. That is a completely reasonable decision.

It is also fine as a temporary home while you learn to build a real one. Buy your domain, point it here, and re-point it later. Nobody clicking your résumé link ever has to know it moved.

What it looks like: clean, plain, and unmistakably Google. Fine spacing, safe fonts, nothing offensive and nothing memorable. It looks like a well-organized document that learned to be a website. For an engineer, that is fine. For a designer, it is a missed opportunity.

Set it up

  1. Go to sites.google.com and sign in with any Google account.
  2. Click Blank or pick a template.
  3. Name your site and give it a clear headline: your name and what you do.
  4. Add a page for each project. Use the Insert panel to add images, text, and embedded video.
  5. Embed your walkthrough videos. Google Sites embeds YouTube natively, and it is the one thing it does really well.
  6. Add an About page and a way to contact you.
  7. Preview on mobile using the preview button. It handles this automatically, which is nice.
  8. Click Publish. You get a free sites.google.com/view/yourname address.
  9. Optional: connect a custom domain under Settings, Custom URLs. It is free to connect. You just have to own the domain.

Pros and cons

Pros
  • Free forever, and free custom domain. That pairing is genuinely rare.
  • You already have a Google account.
  • Easiest builder on this page, by a wide margin.
  • Nothing to cancel, so nothing to lapse.
  • Excellent at embedding video.
  • Good, automatic mobile layouts.
Cons
  • It will not look as good as the others. This is the whole con and it is a big one for a designer.
  • Almost no layout control.
  • Limited typography. You cannot really bring your own type system.
  • It looks like a school project.
  • No real way to make it feel like you.
