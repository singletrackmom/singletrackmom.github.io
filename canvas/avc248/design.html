Courses and Programs · Case study

Course design

The capstone career course. Graduating students leave with a resume, a portfolio, interview practice on record, and career agents they built themselves and keep using after the course ends.

Goal

Send graduating designers into the job market with professional materials they can defend and the skill to keep their job search running on their own. The agents are not a tool students borrow; building them is part of the course.

Audience

Graduating Digital Media Arts, Animation, and Photography students in their final semester, fully online. Most have strong work and no experience presenting it, pricing it, or interviewing for it.

Process

Two through-lines run all term: six interview practice exercises that grow from a classified question list to recorded answers to two full mock interviews, and resume drafts spaced for instructor feedback. Inside the Canvas modules, students build their career agents one at a time from the same parts: a goal, where to look, rules for checking that every result is real, and what to report. The agent workflow grew out of Render, the career tool designed for this course; the course would not look the way it does without it. Render stays a separate tool students can own, and the thinking behind it now lives in the course itself. The last step is moving each agent’s written instructions into the student’s own account so it outlasts their school login.

The career agent is the last module on purpose. By then students have a resume, a portfolio and a target market, which are exactly what an agent needs. Each student first chooses what the agent is for (a job search, career exploration, freelance work, professional development, or starting a business), plans it on paper, builds and tests it in Gemini against its own rules, and puts it on a schedule as a daily digest or a web page. A fully worked example agent shows every step, from choosing the top twenty target employers to the format of each result.

Outcomes

Every module objective is written to the revised Bloom’s taxonomy (Anderson and Krathwohl, 2001), at or below the level of the competency it supports, and every graded assessment has an AI-exposure check using Oregon State University Ecampus’s Bloom’s Taxonomy Revisited, so the graded evidence is the student’s own thinking, recordings and design work. The alignment map lists every objective, its level and the assignment that measures it.

Before launch, the course was checked against the SUNY OSCQR online course quality rubric and walked through by eight synthetic student agents, built from the Student Journey Barriers Study personas, in two rounds of testing.

How success is measured

Every graded piece is the student’s own career material, built to a real job posting they chose. Points sit on revision across drafts and on the student’s ability to present and defend the work, ending in a recorded mock interview and a final portfolio presentation.

Pacing and interaction

Two deadlines a week. Everything is due at noon, Arizona time, never midnight; late work is accepted for 24 hours at 10% off, and nothing after noon the day after. Projects and project steps are posted to the class Discord by noon the day before they are due, with three critique comments by the due time; exercises, quizzes, readings and reflections have no Discord step.

Access

Designed for learner variability with Universal Design for Learning and built to WCAG 2.1 AA: captioned video, accessible color in every palette, and free alternatives for any tool a student cannot run at home. Every piece of student work is accessible too: captions on video (automatic captions, then corrected by hand), corrected transcripts for audio, alt text on images, and tagged documents, all graded at about 10 percent of each media rubric. Every instructor video is captioned and corrected the same way. Every AI step has a non-AI path, and no grade depends on access to one specific AI feature.

Technology

  • Career agents in Gemini. Students write the instructions for each agent and run it in Gemini: a job search agent that confirms every posting is still open, a panel that reads their application the way a hiring committee would, a mock interview panel, and an AI mentor that pushes them to find a human one.
  • AI that writes, not designs. Gemini helps research markets, draft and check resume language against a posting, and rehearse interviews. Students design their identity, leave-behind, and portfolio themselves.
  • Portfolio and presence. Adobe Creative Cloud and Adobe Portfolio or Behance, Google Sites for a personal site, LinkedIn, and Gemini Live for spoken interview practice.

Status

Taught every spring and fall. The agent-building thread moves fully into the Canvas modules when students have Gemini.
