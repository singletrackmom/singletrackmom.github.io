Courses and Programs · Case study

DesignCourse

The capstone career course. Graduating students leave with a resume, a portfolio, interview practice on record, and career agents they built themselves and keep using after the course ends.

Goal

Send graduating designers into the job market with professional materials they can defend, and with a set of career agents they built themselves that keep working after the course ends. Each student chooses one target job early, a real posting that anchors every assignment and every agent, and leaves with a living professional development plan rather than a finished portfolio alone.

Audience

Graduating Digital Media Arts, Animation, Photography, Graphic Design and Fine Arts students in their final semester, fully online. Most have strong work and little experience presenting it, pricing it or interviewing for it.

Process

Two through-lines run all term: six interview practice exercises that build from classifying question types to recorded answers to two full mock interviews, and resume drafts spaced so each returns with instructor feedback within five days. Assessment is authentic throughout: every graded piece is the student’s own career material, built against the target posting, and the course closes with the student designing, testing and scheduling a career agent.

Pacing and interaction

Ten modules over fifteen weeks and finals, each week 6.5 to 9.25 hours. Two noon deadlines a week, with project steps posted to Discord the day before for peer critique. Optional check-ins are recorded and captioned.

AI in This Course

The course is organized around a career agent system. Every agent works from the student’s target job, and each produces an instructions document the student owns. Students use Gemini only with their Maricopa (Google Workspace for Education) account, under which their data is not reviewed by people, not used to train models and not used for ads. Gemini writes and gives feedback; it never designs, and every mark, layout, color and type decision is the student’s.

How students build each agent. Either from a fill-in form taken from Render, the career tool designed for this course (its Career Counselor, Hiring Panel, Interview Panel and Job Search Agent forms are taken apart so students see what each field does), or from a short series of prompts that writes the agent’s instructions. Either way the output is an instructions document the student owns, saved in Gemini as a Gem or skill.

Taking the agents with them. Before graduation, each agent moves from the Maricopa Gemini account to the student’s personal Gemini, so nothing is lost when the school account closes. Each agent’s instructions and knowledge files live in one Google Drive folder the student copies to a personal account and recreates there. The course teaches the privacy difference: the education account’s data is not used to train models, while a personal account’s may be, depending on its settings.

How AI Supports Learning Here

  • Specification as learning. Writing an agent’s rules file (goal, must-haves, deal-breakers, a rating scale, twenty target organizations in three tiers, a checking rule and a report format) requires the student to articulate a career strategy precisely enough for a machine to follow.
  • Test, revise, retest. Students run the agent three times, open every result by hand, and change one rule for each miss, the same iterative evaluation used in professional AI work.
  • AI as rehearsal partner. A spoken mock interview with Gemini adds low-stakes, repeatable practice to the human-scored interview series.
  • Critical comparison. The tailoring exercise makes students identify where AI editing misrepresents them, building the judgment to use it on their own materials.
  • Self-regulated learning beyond the course. The Career Counselor and the hiring panel’s gap list turn the target job into a continuing development plan.
  • Verify and revise. The AI Primer in Module 0 covers prompting with role, task, context and format, two revisions, three fact-checks against sources, and a position statement weighing AI’s costs, including energy use (data centers drew about 1.5% of global electricity in 2024, per the International Energy Agency). Every AI use is recorded in an AI-use log row; “No AI used” is a complete entry.

Guardrails

  • Account and data. Maricopa account only during the course, under the district’s Gemini for Education terms. Phone numbers, email and street addresses are removed before anything is pasted into Gemini, and agent instructions contain nothing a student would not put on a public resume.
  • Authorship. The first resume draft and the goals statement are the student’s own; later AI feedback is tracked and logged. Every fact Gemini supplies is checked against a source the student opens.
  • Never AI-made. The identity, leave-behind and portfolio are designed by the student.
  • Students under 18 and 16. No grade depends on a restricted feature: typed answers replace Gemini Live, the student searches job boards and library databases in place of Deep Research, and Google Drive replaces YouTube where uploads are blocked. Students under 16 build a professional profile page on Google Sites in place of LinkedIn.

How to Adapt This

  1. Anchor the course on one real target. A single posting chosen early gives every assignment and every agent a shared, authentic reference point.
  2. Grade the rules file and the test log. The agent’s output matters less than the specification, the hand-checked results and the documented rule changes.
  3. Teach portability and privacy together. Have students keep agent instructions in a Drive folder they can carry to a personal account, and explain how the two accounts differ.

How the Course Was Tested

Before launch, the course went through synthetic student walk-throughs, a quality-rubric review, and an alignment and AI-exposure audit. No real students were involved in testing. Every course in the program goes through the same build and review method.

Synthetic Student Walk-Throughs

The testers are synthetic students: AI agents, each built from one persona in the Student Journey Barriers Study. Each agent worked through the course module by module as that student and logged every point where it got stuck or could not finish. A fixer pass applied the findings, and a second round re-ran all eight agents against the revised course.

Quality and Alignment Reviews

The course was revised to a SUNY OSCQR review, and every objective was audited against the revised Bloom’s taxonomy (Anderson and Krathwohl, 2001) so that each sits at or below its parent competency and is measured by a graded item.

Every Assessment Checked for AI Resistance

Every graded assessment was audited for AI exposure with Oregon State University Ecampus’s Bloom’s Taxonomy Revisited, asking whether a student could hand the task to an AI and pass. The design answers are work tied to the student’s own target job, recorded explanation, tracked drafts, peer critique on Discord, the AI-use log, and the rule that Gemini never designs.

Accessibility

Designed with Universal Design for Learning to WCAG 2.1 AA, with captioned instructor video, recorded check-ins, and a non-AI path for every AI step. Student work carries hand-corrected captions, corrected transcripts, alt text and tagged PDFs, worth about 10 percent of each media rubric, and portfolio and agent pages are checked with WAVE.

Status

Designed and fully built: ten modules, two through-lines of resume and interview practice, a final career package, the career agent system, and a Canvas import package. Taught every spring and fall.
