Design history, taught by students

In the classroom

A team project in Digital Graphic Arts 2 where students become the class expert on a design-history era and teach it back, in the visual language of the period.

What it is

A two-person team project in AVC 183, Digital Graphic Arts 2. Each pair takes a slice of design history between 1895 and 1989, researches it deeply, and becomes the class expert on that period. The deliverable is a presentation built in the visual language of the era itself, designed so a viewer can name the decade before reading a word, then presented to and taught to the class.

Who it’s for

Design students who learn a lecture-heavy subject better by researching, making, and teaching it than by memorizing it.

The goal

Turn design history into active, student-led learning: real research, a designed artifact, and the experience of teaching peers, so the period sticks because they built it and taught it.

How it works

Each team researches its period using academic databases, the library, and AI tools, then works a full design process from brainstorm to sketch to build. AI is allowed freely for research and content, but the artwork must come from real, primary sources, museum and archive collections, not AI-generated imagery. Students design the interface and presentation themselves and record their talk for the class. An optional extra-credit version rebuilds the project as an AI vibe-coding challenge, so students can make something interactive with no coding required.
