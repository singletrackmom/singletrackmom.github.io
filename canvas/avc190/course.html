Overview

In this course you design, build, test and publish a multi-page website for a fictitious client. Gemini plays your client: it creates the business, answers your interview questions in character, and drafts website copy for you to edit. You do all of the visual design. The course serves Graphic Design, Digital Media Arts, Animation and Web Design certificate students, and it assumes you have never built a website. No coding is required. Every student builds and publishes in Google Sites; students who want to write code may add the code option in Week 9.

This course is fully online and asynchronous. Plan on about eight hours of work each week, 120 hours across the 15 weeks, with two noon deadlines each week (Arizona time). You finish with a live website and a case study for your portfolio.

AI in This Course

Objectives

  • State when AI use in this course is assigned and allowed, and when it is not.
  • Explain how your work is protected when you use Gemini with your Maricopa account.

Overview

This course uses generative AI as part of the assignments. Our industry advisory board encourages us to teach current industry practice, and employers now expect graduates to work with AI. In Microsoft and LinkedIn’s 2024 Work Trend Index, 66% of business leaders said they would not hire someone without AI skills.

Using AI here is not cheating. Cheating is turning in work you did not do as if you did. In this course, AI use is assigned, it is disclosed in an AI-use log with every assignment, and you are graded on your own thinking: your research, your decisions and your design work. Other courses may set different rules, and those rules apply in those courses.

Your work is not used to train AI. You will use Gemini with your Maricopa account. Under the district’s Google for Education agreement, what you enter is not reviewed by people, not used to train AI models, and not used for ads. Always sign in with your Maricopa account, never a personal one. Your instructor will not put your work into any public AI tool.

Gemini writes; it never designs. Gemini helps with research, writing, feedback, planning and code. It never designs: your logo or wordmark, type, color, layouts, imagery and interface are your own, and you will show your process. No AI-generated images, logos, icons, mockups or other visuals are accepted in any assignment.

You will learn to question it. AI makes confident mistakes and reflects bias in its training data. This course teaches you to check every answer and to think critically about what AI gets wrong and what it costs.

Which AI Tools

Use Gemini with your Maricopa account, and no other AI tool, for this course: the Gemini app (including Gemini Canvas for code), Gemini in Google Docs and Sheets, and Gemini Notebook to keep your client brief, interview and sources together.

The AI-Use Log

Every submission includes an AI-use log with one row per use: the tool, what you asked it to do, what you kept, what you changed, and why. “None” is a valid entry. The log shows that you checked every AI output for accuracy (invented facts, wrong prices, broken logic) and accessibility (alt text quality, labels, keyboard use, reading level).

Rules

  1. Gemini may not write your critique comments, reflections, design rationales or defense answers, and may never stand in for test participants or invent data.
  2. Code built with Gemini Canvas counts as your work when the design and decisions are yours, the log shows your prompts and revisions, and you can explain how it works in your defense.
  3. Fictitious means fictitious: do not ask Gemini to imitate a real business, a real person or a living designer’s identifiable style.
  4. Never paste a test participant’s identifying information or a classmate’s work into any AI tool.
  5. Undisclosed AI use is handled under the college academic misconduct policy (see Course Policies and Support). When the instructor has a question about authorship, the first step is a conversation.

Discord Critique Guide

Objectives

  • Apply the course critique protocol to classmates’ work.
  • Follow the course rules for posting in Discord.

Overview

Discord is the course critique space. Join the Digital Media Arts Discord server and find the AVC190 course channel. Process Work and Projects are posted there by noon the day before they are due, and you leave three critique comments by the due time. Everything graded is also submitted in Canvas. A student who cannot use Discord posts the same work and comments in the Canvas discussion Critique Without Discord, with no penalty.

Posting Rules

  • Questions go in the course channel, not direct messages, so everyone benefits from the answer.
  • Set your server nickname to the name you want to be called and add a profile photo or avatar. Screen names are allowed; tell your instructor your screen name in Technology Setup so your critique is credited.
  • Add alt text to every image you post and a caption or transcript to every audio or video post.

Critique Protocol

Every critique comment has three parts and names at least one principle.

  1. I notice: describe what you saw or did, in neutral terms. (“On the phone layout, the Order button sits below three paragraphs of text.”)
  2. Because: name the principle or evidence. (“Visual hierarchy: the primary action should be the most visible element on a page whose goal is ordering.”)
  3. What if: offer one specific, testable suggestion. (“What if the button moved above the paragraphs and used the accent color from your style tile?”)

Critique the work, not the person. A comment such as “looks good” does not count unless it has all three parts. Comment on a classmate whose client type differs from yours at least once each week, and disagree with evidence.

Usability Testing Ethics and Consent

Objectives

  • Apply the course rules for card sorts and usability tests with real people.

Overview

The course includes a card sort (Week 4) and two rounds of usability testing (Weeks 10 and 13) with real people. These are classroom learning activities. Every session follows these rules, and E4 Testing Ethics and Consent Check confirms you know them before you run a test.

Rules

  1. Adult participants only. Participants are 18 or older. Do not recruit minors, including younger siblings or classmates under 18.
  2. Mostly people outside the course. At most one participant per round may be a classmate in this course.
  3. Informed consent every time. Read the consent script below, word for word, before each session. Consent is written or recorded verbally.
  4. Minimal data. No health, financial, immigration or other sensitive information. No names or identifying details in notes; participants are P1, P2 and P3, and card sorters are S1 and S2.
  5. Recordings. Only with explicit consent, stored only in your Maricopa Google account, never posted to Discord or Canvas, and deleted at the end of the course.
  6. No disclosure of disability. Never ask a participant to disclose a disability or diagnosis. If you want to test with someone who uses assistive technology, discuss the plan with your instructor first.
  7. AI and participant data. De-identified notes may be organized in Gemini Notebook with your Maricopa account. Identifiable participant data never goes into any AI tool, and AI never generates participants, quotes or results.
  8. Honesty. Reporting a session that did not happen, or inventing data, is academic misconduct.

Students Under 18

If you are under 18, you may run card sorts and usability tests with adults, and a parent or guardian may be present during any session. Tell your instructor in Week 1 so the instructor can answer any question your parent or guardian has.

Consent Script

Read this aloud at the start of every session:

“Thank you for helping with my class project for AVC190 at Glendale Community College. I am testing a website design, not testing you, so there are no wrong answers. I will ask you to try a few tasks and to think aloud as you work. The session takes about 15 minutes. I will take notes, and I will not write down your name or anything that identifies you; you will be listed only as a participant number. With your permission, I will record the screen and your voice. The recording stays in my college Google account, is not shared, and is deleted at the end of the course. You may skip any task or stop at any time without giving a reason. Do you agree to take part? Do you agree to be recorded?”

Record the answers to both questions in your session notes.

Glossary

Objectives

Overview

These terms appear in assignments from Week 2 on.

Glossary
TermMeaning
Card sortA method in which people group content cards and name each group, showing how they expect a site to be organized.
Copy deckA document that holds every word on the site, page by page, in the order it appears.
HeuristicA rule of thumb for usable interfaces; this course uses the ten usability heuristics from Nielsen Norman Group.
MockupA full-color, static design of a page at a specific screen width.
Mood boardA collection of type, color, imagery and texture that shows one possible visual direction.
PrototypeA clickable version of the design that lets a person try tasks before the site is built.
Proto-personaA description of a typical visitor based on the brief and public sources, with its assumptions labeled for testing.
ResponsiveA single page that rearranges itself for phone, tablet and desktop screens.
Severity ratingA score from 0 (not a problem) to 4 (prevents the task) given to each usability problem.
SitemapA planning diagram of every page and how the pages connect. It is not the same as a “site map” page published for visitors.
Style tileA single page that shows a brand’s web type, colors, buttons and image style before full pages are designed.
User flowA diagram of the screens, decisions and actions one visitor takes to finish one task.
WCAG 2.1 AAThe Web Content Accessibility Guidelines, the accessibility standard most colleges and governments follow.
WireframeA simple grayscale layout that shows where content and controls go, without final color, type or images.

Scope

  • Five to seven pages. Home, About, a services or products page, the interactive element page, Visit or Contact, and one or two more that the sitemap justifies.
  • One interactive element beyond navigation, with a scope that a first-time visitor can use in under two minutes.
  • No real transactions or personal data. No payments, logins or accounts. A contact or order form may appear as a demonstration only; it must say it is a class project and must not collect real personal information.
  • Accessibility statement on every site (a page or a footer section) that names the standard (WCAG 2.1 AA), what was tested, and known limits.
  • Design freeze at the end of Week 13. Week 14 is for fixes and the case study.
  • Motion is optional and must respect reduced-motion settings or offer a pause; nothing flashes more than three times a second.
