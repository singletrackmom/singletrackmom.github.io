Story forms and tools

Case study · Learning Design · AI Tools & Strategy

Course designStory forms and tools

The competencies are locked. The medium is not. Every student follows the same method, and the finished story can take whatever form fits the story and the student’s major.

One Method, Any Medium

Whatever the form, every student chooses a story, gathers an archive, builds the Teller, writes a treatment and script, storyboards, produces, and presents. Every finished piece pairs designed picture with sound: narration, the narrator’s real voice, and sound design. All forms are graded on the same rubrics, which describe what each criterion looks like in each form.

Story Forms

  • A talking character. An original animated character, a narrator or guide the student designs, who speaks the story in the student’s recorded voice. Built as a puppet in Adobe Character Animator or rigged in After Effects. It is never a likeness of the real narrator.
  • Type as the storyteller. Kinetic type or a graphic interface carries the story: words that move, build, and break to match the narration. Designed in Illustrator or InDesign and animated in After Effects.
  • A photographic story. The student’s own photography with archival images, sequenced as a photo essay with camera moves on stills. Lightroom and Photoshop, assembled in Premiere Pro.
  • Documentary or live-action video. Footage of the place, the objects, and the hands, cut with archival material. Shot on a phone or camera and edited in Premiere Pro or DaVinci Resolve.
  • Animation or motion comic. Drawn, painted, cut-out, or 3D frames with limited animation and parallax. Fresco, Photoshop, Krita, or Blender, finished in After Effects.
  • An interactive or vibe-coded story. A small web page or branching piece the viewer moves through, built with AI coding help in Gemini Canvas, or in Twine, and hosted as a link. A natural fit for Gaming students, as long as the experience still delivers a complete story with sound.
  • A form the student proposes. Any other medium, approved at the pitch, as long as it meets the same rubrics.

Rules That Apply to Every Form

  • Most of the screen time is authored. The archive and the student’s own drawing, photography, footage, or type carry the story; generated images are studies and labeled extras.
  • Consent travels with the material. Nothing from an interview goes into any AI tool until the narrator signs a release that allows it.
  • The Teller is never public. It is shown only by screen recording.
  • Everything is disclosed. Every milestone carries the AI-use log, and the final carries a credit line naming what was made by hand and what was assisted.
