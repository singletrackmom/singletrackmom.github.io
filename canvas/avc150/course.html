Multimedia Storytelling

AVC 150

OfferedEvery fall; first offering Fall 2027. 15 weeks plus finals week; about 120 hours.
What students makeThe Living Archive: a storyteller, the Teller, built in Gemini Notebook from a narrator’s consented archive, and a 3 to 5 minute recorded, edited interview with it. The recording is the finished story and the archive of the story. It ships with an error audit, a curator’s statement, key art, the archive package and a case-study page.

Accessibility of student work

Everything students make must work for someone who cannot see it or cannot hear it. Media rubrics give about 10% of the points to this.

  • Video: captions generated automatically (YouTube Studio, Premiere Pro Speech to Text, Google Vids or Meet), then every line corrected by hand against the audio.
  • Audio: a transcript, auto-transcribed and then corrected by hand.
  • Images: alt text on every meaningful image, one or two sentences on what matters; decorative images marked decorative.
  • Documents and pages: real headings, readable contrast, alt text, descriptive link text, tagged PDFs; web pages checked with WAVE.
  • Discord: alt text on every image; captions or a transcript with every audio or video post.

AI in this course

Class schedule listing:

This course uses generative AI tools (Google Gemini) in assignments, as recommended by our industry advisors. Each student builds an AI storyteller in Gemini Notebook from a consented story archive, then records and edits an interview with it to tell the story. Students use AI for research, writing and feedback, and create every visual themselves. Every interviewee signs a Consent Affirmation, records a video consent, and signs an agreement naming AI processing before their material is uploaded, and may withdraw at any time. All AI use is disclosed.
ToolUsed for
Gemini Notebook (formerly NotebookLM)The Teller: the demo Teller in Week 1, the Mini Teller in Week 2, the real Teller from Week 6. Also transcript drafts, the paper edit and the fact check. Its audio features may voice the Teller’s answers, labeled as AI.
Gemini app (with Gemini Live)Story starters, loglines, interview questions and rehearsal, feedback on the voice guide, email drafts, Deep Research leads
Gemini in Docs, Sheets and GmailDraft metadata, alt text, audit-log rows and emails, every word checked
Premiere Pro, Audition, Adobe PodcastSpeech-to-text, text-based editing, auto captions (always corrected), Enhance Speech
PhotoshopContent-Aware Fill and the Spot Healing Brush to repair archive photographs, each change logged as restoring or inventing
Content CredentialsReading the provenance of a labeled AI image (D3)
  • Using AI here is not cheating: it is assigned, disclosed in the AI-use log, and students are graded on their own thinking, writing, design and edit decisions.
  • The Teller is built only in Gemini Notebook. It answers only from the sources the student loads, and every Teller opens by stating that it is an AI.
  • The Teller speaks as a narrator who knows the archive, or in the first person as the person, like the grandfather bot in Artificial. First person is allowed only when the narrator agrees to that mode in the Signed Agreement.
  • Gemini writes and gives feedback. It never designs and never generates images or video.
  • A synthetic AI voice may read the Teller’s answers when it is labeled as AI on screen and in the credits. No one clones a real person’s voice or likeness, and no one changes a word presented as a narrator’s quotation or as the Teller’s answer.
  • Students make every visual (the Teller’s portrait, archive cards, journal pages, the conversation map, key art) from archive material and their own drawing and photography.
  • Every process step carries an AI-use log row: tool; what I used it for; what I kept, changed or rejected. “No AI used” is a complete entry. Project 1 and the Final add a full AI-use statement and a credit line.
  • Students use Gemini only with their Maricopa account. Under the district’s Google for Education agreement, what they enter is not reviewed by people, not used to train AI models and not used for ads (Gemini for Education; education FAQ).
  • AI makes confident mistakes and reflects bias. The error audit in Week 8 is where students find and document them; grades go to what the student authored, tested and fixed, never to how fluent the Teller sounds.

WhenConsent step

Week 13Narrator reviews the rough cut of the recorded interview and may still ask for changes.
  • Nothing of a narrator’s goes into any AI tool until the Signed Agreement is signed with item 4 (AI processing) marked yes. The consent video never goes into any AI tool.
  • The narrator mode needs item 5. First person needs item 6 (living narrator) or Part D (a family member or estate holder, for someone who has died). A synthetic voice for the Teller needs item 13, and it is never made to sound like the narrator.
  • A narrator who says no to AI still takes part: the student builds the Teller only from material the student owns, or switches to a self-interview. Neither path is graded lower.
  • Students keep their archive and Teller by default. If a narrator withdraws, the student tells them: “If you change your mind, I’ll delete your material within seven days and confirm in writing.” A student whose narrator withdraws finishes with the Practice Archive or a self-interview at no penalty.
  • Private material lives in Canvas only, never Discord. The Teller is never public; it is shown live only inside the class and otherwise by recording.

Overview

In this course you build a storyteller, called a Teller: an AI in Gemini Notebook that knows one person’s story because you built its archive. You then interview it, and the recorded, edited interview is your finished story. The model is Amy Kurzweil’s graphic memoir Artificial: A Love Story (Catapult, 2023), in which her father builds a chatbot of her grandfather from his archive of letters and writing, and she works through the family story by interviewing it.

This week you set up your accounts and recorder, learn how a large language model differs from a grounded tool (a model predicts likely words and does not know facts; Gemini Notebook answers from the sources you give it and cites them, but can still invent where the archive has a gap), and interview the instructor’s demo Teller, built from one life history recorded by the Federal Writers’ Project in the 1930s and now in the public domain. You see on the first day what you will build.

Competencies

  1. Present an overview of storytelling, including elements of the story, the power of the story and a variety of ways to present a story.
  2. Explain the impact of voice, place and culture in storytelling.
  3. Describe the importance of effectively blending language, image and power in the creation and production of a personal story.
  4. Identify, describe and apply the basic principles, methods and techniques used to craft a personal story.
  5. Identify, develop and present the elements of the personal story for peer review.
  6. Edit and finalize the story in preparation for the creation of a digital story.
  7. Use basic design principles to lay out and produce a storyboard for the personal story.
  8. Use computer hardware and software, tools, commands and procedures to manipulate graphics, video and sound in producing and editing the digital story.
  9. Edit and produce a short digital personal story.
  10. Archive the story on relevant technology.
  11. Present the digital story for peer review.
