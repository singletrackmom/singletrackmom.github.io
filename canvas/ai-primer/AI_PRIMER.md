# AI Primer

A one-hour, student-facing AI lesson and lab for every course I teach, built to pitch to other digital media classes. Students complete it once, earn a badge, and carry it into their next class instead of repeating it.

Status: planning. Nothing built yet. Searched the repo 13 Nov 2026; no earlier primer exists.

## Purpose

Student buy-in for AI. Students leave knowing why digital media students need AI skills, what is protected and what they control, how AI can help them learn, and why using it in this class is not cheating.

## Core message

- Industry expects this skill. Bring one real job posting or number, current at the time of recording.
- You paid good money for this class. You are here to think critically, and AI is a tool for that thinking, not a replacement for it.
- In this class we collect process work. Evidence of learning counts as much as the final product.
- AI can help you learn: make a notebook, flashcards, a practice quiz, a tutor that explains a concept a second way.
- Here is what is protected and what you control (not “it’s safe”).

## Objections to answer head on

Research shows these are the reasons students push back. The primer names each one instead of dodging it.

1. **Training data and consent.** “AI isn’t learning from your artwork” only covers what students upload. The bigger objection in art and design is that models were trained on other artists’ work without consent. Name it and state my position.
2. **Privacy.** Only claim what is verified: with a school account, Copilot Chat does not train on prompts. Confirm the Maricopa setup with district IT before recording. Personal accounts and image tools follow different rules.
3. **Fear of being accused.** Students avoid AI because they are afraid of being flagged. Answer with a clear class policy: what is allowed, how to disclose it, and how process work shows it is their own.
4. **Accuracy.** The top reason students limit AI use. Teach verification: confirm every claim in the library databases (the design history rule).
5. **“It makes us lazy.”** Meet it in their words. AI that does the thinking for you is the problem; AI that makes you think harder is the point.
6. **Water and climate.** Expect it. Give an honest, brief answer.
7. **Jobs.** “Industry wants this” can backfire with design students who fear AI will take design jobs. Frame AI literacy as protection for their job prospects.
8. **Opting out.** Decide whether there is a no-AI path before pitching to other faculty. They will ask.

## Structure, about an hour

| Part | Time | What happens |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Why | 5 min | My own short video: industry, critical thinking, why this class uses AI. |
| What’s protected | 10 min | Training data, privacy with the school login, accuracy, environment, jobs, opting out. |
| Lab | 30 min | Sign in to Copilot with the Maricopa account. Turn a reading or class notes into flashcards, ask Copilot to tutor and quiz you, verify one claim in the library databases, critique one weak answer. |
| Not cheating | 5 min | The policy: what’s allowed, how to disclose it, why process work counts. |
| Check and reflect | 10 min | Short knowledge check plus a reflection submitted as process evidence. Earns the badge. |

## Two layers

- **Core, any class:** the learning tools, verification, privacy, policy, “show your thinking.” Works in classes where students don’t make things.
- **Creative add-on, digital media classes:** training data and consent, AI in the design process, process work as evidence, jobs in creative fields.

## The badge

- A badge that carries from class to class has to live outside any one course. Copying the module into every course means every instructor tracks completion separately.
- Better model: one self-enroll Canvas course (WashU’s module works this way) that issues the badge; instructors check it instead of reassigning the lesson.
- Open question: does Maricopa have Canvas Credentials (badges) turned on? If not, what is the fallback?
- Expiration: AI tools and policies change fast. Plan a yearly refresh instead of a badge that lasts forever.

## Existing resources to adapt, not rebuild

Verify every license before reusing.

- **AI Fluency for Students** (Anthropic with Prof. Rick Dakan, Ringling College of Art and Design, and Prof. Joseph Feller, University College Cork). The lesson on AI as a learning partner. Art-school co-author fits my students best.
- **Phoenix College GenAI 101** (sister Maricopa college). Part 5, AI for Students. Review before pitching district-wide. https://sites.google.com/phoenixcollege.edu/pcgenerativeai/generative-ai101
- **Columbia College**, short video on AI as a learning partner. https://excellence.columbia.ca/?p=3749
- **Roger Williams University**, AI Literacy for Students, including a short video on building study guides and flashcards with AI. https://www.rwu.edu/node/208266
- **WashU**, 30-minute self-enroll Canvas module that unlocks a secure AI tool on completion. https://ai.washu.edu/?p=762
- **Northwestern**, Canvas Commons module on how generative AI works and the ethics of using it in academia. https://www.it.northwestern.edu/departments/it-services-support/teaching/teach-tech/2025/canvas-module-for-enhancing-student-ai-literacy-now-available.html
- **MSU Denver, Penn State, Occidental**, importable Canvas modules. Several are CC BY-NC-SA.
- **Collin College**, session on building Copilot tutoring agents through Microsoft 365. https://digitalcommons.collin.edu/staffpublications/195

What I found is generic and runs 30 to 45 minutes. Nothing I found is short and built for design students, or takes on the training-data objection directly. That is the gap this primer fills.
