# GCC Institutional Learning Outcomes · Complete Reference

**Goal:** One place to look up every GCC ILO, its exact Canvas name, and where it fits in a Digital Media Arts course.

**Audience:** Me, and the DMA faculty I am going to talk into using these.

**Process:** Extracted from the seven official GCC ILO rubric documents. Canvas names are verbatim, so they can be matched against **Outcomes → Find → [domain folder]** in any Canvas course.

---

## How ILOs actually work · read this first

**They are not course points.** From the official scoring note:

> *"ILO results are used to strengthen the overall student learning experience and are **not used for the evaluation of individual students or faculty**."*

An ILO score rides alongside the grade. A student can earn an A and score **Emerging** on an ILO, and nothing is wrong.

### The scoring rule almost everyone gets backwards

> *"Each ILO begins with the words, 'By the end of their **college experience**...' ... **Student work that may be considered proficient in the context of a course may be developing in the context of an ILO.**"*

**You are scoring against a graduating student, not the one in front of you.**

So a first-semester AVC100 student *should* mostly score **Emerging (2)**. **That is not a failure. That is the signal.** It is how the institution sees growth from first semester to last.

> **If every 100-level course reports Proficient, the data is worthless and nobody can prove students grew. Score honestly and score low.**

### The five-point scale (identical across all seven domains)

| | |
|---|---|
| **Advanced** | 4 · Demonstrates in-depth understanding of the process or concept |
| **Proficient** | 3 · Demonstrates understanding. Minor errors do not impede understanding |
| **Emerging** | 2 · Demonstrates beginning understanding |
| **Attempted** | 1 · Attempted, additional work needed |
| **No Submission** | 0 |

**Mastery = 3. Calculation = Highest Score.**

### Which means: one attachment per ILO is enough

Canvas takes the **highest** score across every place an outcome is attached. Attaching an ILO to nine rubrics does not make it more rigorous · **it gives more chances at the same number**, adds grading, and clutters the rubric.

> **One ILO, one criterion, at the single place the evidence is strongest. Late in the course, where the student is at their best.**

---

## How to attach one

1. Open the assignment → its **Rubric** → the **pencil** to edit
2. **Find Outcome** → the domain folder → the ILO → **Import**
3. It becomes a criterion. Score it on the 0–4 scale, **against a graduating student's standard.**

*(If **Outcomes** is not in your course navigation: **Settings → Navigation → drag Outcomes up → Save**.)*

# The seven domains

## 1 · Communication Visual · `ILO-VC`

**The DMA home domain. This is ours.**

| Canvas name | What it assesses |
|---|---|
| **ILO-VC1 Purpose: Responsive & Focused Visuals** | Context, audience, purpose; focuses all elements of the work |
| **ILO-VC2 Design: Unity & Arrangement** | Deliberately arranges visual elements; structures work to direct a viewer's interaction |
| **ILO-VC3 Execution: Technical Skills** | Technically executes an idea in a medium; **selects appropriate equipment, software, peripherals, and workflow** |
| **ILO-VC4 Delivery: Visual Production** | Compelling choices about delivery; **physical or digital environment** |
| **ILO-VC5 Analysis: Critique & Interpretation** | **Analyzes, critiques, and interprets** work from a visual, historical, or conceptual framework |

**Note what VC3 and VC4 say.** *"Equipment, peripherals"* and *"physical or digital environment"* are **MCCCD Competencies 4 and 5** in better words. The ILO authors understood the discipline; the 1998 competency writers did not.

**And VC5 is the one that matters most for us: critique.** In AVC100 critique is ~20% of every rubric and maps to **no** district competency. VC5 names it exactly.

## 2 · Communication Oral · `ILO-OC`

**We are already doing this and not claiming it.**

| Canvas name | What it assesses |
|---|---|
| **ILO-OC1 Organization: Cohesive Presentation** | Intro, sequenced body, transitions, conclusion |
| **ILO-OC2 Language: Effective & Appropriate to Audience** | Imaginative, memorable, audience-appropriate language |
| **ILO-OC3 Delivery: Compelling Presentation** | Posture, gesture, eye contact, vocal expressiveness |
| **ILO-OC4 Support: Credible Material & Presenter** | Explanations, examples, evidence establishing authority |
| **ILO-OC5 Message: Compelling Central Message** | Precisely stated, repeated, memorable, supported |

> **The AVC248 walkthrough videos are an oral communication assessment and nobody has said so.** A student presenting their own project and defending a decision on camera hits **all five** of these. That is an entire ILO domain the DMA program is already delivering and has never claimed.

## 4 · Critical & Creative Thinking · `ILO-TI` (critical) and `ILO-TE` (creative)

**The creative half is written for studio art and almost nobody in our department knows it exists.**

### Critical Thinking

| Canvas name | What it assesses |
|---|---|
| **ILO-TI1 Issues: Comprehensive Problem Statement** | States the problem clearly and completely |
| **ILO-TI2 Evidence: Source Evaluation** | Interprets and evaluates sources; questions experts |
| **ILO-TI3 Context: Analyzes Assumptions** | Analyzes own and others' assumptions |
| **ILO-TI4 Position: Acknowledges Complexities** | Imaginative position; limits acknowledged; other views synthesized |
| **ILO-TI5 Conclusions: Logical Outcomes** | Logical conclusions, informed evaluation, prioritized evidence |

### Creative Thinking · **read these carefully**

| Canvas name | What it assesses |
|---|---|
| **ILO-TE1 Acquiring: Evaluates Creative Process** | **Evaluates creative process and product using domain-appropriate criteria** |
| **ILO-TE2 Risks: Seeks Untested Approaches** | **Actively seeks out risky, untested directions**; personal risk, risk of failure, going beyond the parameters |
| **ILO-TE3 Solutions: Planning and Rationale** | Logical plan, recognizes consequences, **articulates the reason for the solution chosen** |
| **ILO-TE4 Contradictions: Integrates Divergent Perspectives** | Integrates alternate or contradictory ideas |
| **ILO-TE5 Innovation: Extends Knowledge** | Extends a novel idea to create new knowledge |
| **ILO-TE6 Transformation: Creates New Forms** | Connects, synthesizes, transforms ideas into new forms |

> **TE1 is critique. TE3 is the design rationale. TE2 is the thing we say we want and never grade.**
>
> **ILO-TE2 · "Taking Risks"** rewards a student for attempting something that might not work. **Our rubrics currently punish that.** A student who tries an ambitious composition and lands it at 80% scores lower than one who plays it safe and lands at 95%. **TE2 is the institutional permission slip to fix that**, and it is free.

## 5 · Information Literacy · `ILO-I`

| Canvas name | What it assesses |
|---|---|
| **ILO-I1 Recognize: Understands Scope** | Grasps the complexity of the question |
| **ILO-I2 Recognize: Explains Key Concepts** | Narrows and explains key concepts |
| **ILO-I3 Recognize: Resources Address Subtopics** | Finds resources for subtopics |
| **ILO-I4 Locate: Understands Differences in Resources** | Databases vs. open web vs. print |
| **ILO-I5 Evaluate: Identifies Search Results** | Results that answer each part of the question |
| **ILO-I6 Evaluate: Selects Current or Seminal Research** | Current and/or foundational sources |
| **ILO-I7 Evaluate: Verifies Authority, Bias, Credibility** | Credentials, bias, credibility |
| **ILO-I8 Applies: Interprets Research** | Contributes rather than restates |
| **ILO-I9 Ethical Use: Source Attribution** | Consistent citation, in-text matched to references |

**Where it lands for us:**

- **AVC100 "Reputable Tutorial/Learning Sources"** → **ILO-I7**. That Student Success item is already a source-credibility exercise and it is currently worth 3 points and no outcome.
- **Design history research** → **I4, I6, I7, I9**. This is the natural home.
- **Image licensing (Pexels, Unsplash, copyright)** → **ILO-I9**. We already teach it and never claim it.

## 7 · Personal & Community Well-Being · `ILO-WB`

**The Student Success module. This is the justification we have been missing.**

| Canvas name | What it assesses |
|---|---|
| **ILO-WB1 Institution: Awareness of Resources** | **Demonstrates how to access** Career Services, Counseling, Financial Aid, Center for Learning, Advising, DRS, Library, Food Pantry, Veteran Services |
| **ILO-WB2 Well-Being: Enhances Physical Health** | Physical well-being behaviours |
| **ILO-WB3 Well-Being: Enhances Mental Health** | Mental, emotional, intellectual, spiritual well-being |
| **ILO-WB4 Relationships: Fosters Healthy Relationships** | Healthy relationships; **turn-taking with team members** |
| **ILO-WB5 Community: Addresses Community Challenge** | Institutional, local, or global challenge |
| **ILO-WB6 Reflection: Reviews Prior Learning** | **Reviews prior learning to reveal changed perspectives** (AAC&U Lifelong Learning) |

### Two things worth stopping on

**ILO-WB3's own example, verbatim from the rubric:**

> *"Student describes how their **art (visual, digital, music, dance, etc.) adds meaning to their life**."*

**Someone put a Digital Media example inside the mental-health ILO and nobody in our department noticed.** It is a standing invitation.

**And the WB1 trap:** the criterion says *"**demonstrates how to access**."* Reading a page about Advising is **Emerging (2)**. **Making the appointment and reporting what happened is Proficient (3).** Most Student Success items across the college are awareness, not demonstration, and score a 2 forever. **One sentence fixes it.**

| **ILO-Q2 Application: Successful Calculations** | Calculations sufficient to solve |

## The department argument, in one paragraph

**GCC tracks every student's ILO attainment toward graduation, and Digital Media Arts is contributing almost nothing to that record.** Not because our students are not demonstrating these skills · **they demonstrate them constantly** · but because our rubrics never say so, and several of our faculty are not in Canvas at all.

**The ILOs are already written for us.** VC3 describes selecting equipment and workflow. VC5 describes critique. TE2 describes taking creative risks. WB3's own example is a student describing how their art gives their life meaning. **W4 describes a portfolio without using the word.**

**One outcome, attached to one criterion, on one assignment, in the back half of a course.** That is the entire ask. **It changes nothing about how we teach and it makes our students visible in the institution's record of what they can do.**
