## Goal, Audience, Process

**Goal.** Move students from pushing type around to working inside a system and then authoring one, and send them out with a portfolio they can show and defend.

**Audience.** AVC 183 students in Digital Media Arts, fully online. Most are in their first or second semester of design and have never worked to a written spec.

**Process.** Nine weeks of foundation with four graded critiques, then a five-week identity capstone, then a portfolio week. Every project starts with hand sketches and ends with a critique. Concept first, tool second.

## Weeks, with real dates

Fall 2026 runs Aug 22 to Dec 18. Instruction is 16 weeks, Aug 24 to Dec 13. Finals week is Dec 14 to 18 and carries no new work in this course.

**Due-date convention.** Sunday and Wednesday, with two exceptions noted below.

### Week 1 · Aug 24 to 30 · Design is the organization of information
Course intro, Canvas tour, Adobe and Figma logins, Discord, OBS setup. Sketchbook expectations and critique norms stated up front.

The through-line, stated day one: graphic design is the organization of information.

**Activity:** type walk. Photograph ten pieces of type in the wild, post to the discussion with one sentence each on what the type is doing.
**Competencies:** 1

### Week 2 · Aug 31 to Sep 6 · Letter
Letterform anatomy, type classification, the vocabulary. Shape Type and Kern Type as low-stakes homework.

**Word project opens.** Twenty hand thumbnails before anyone opens Illustrator.
**Reading:** *Graphic Design and Print Production Fundamentals*, type chapter, CC BY 4.0. Lupton, &ldquo;Letter.&rdquo;
**Competencies:** 2

### Week 3 · Sep 7 to 13 · Word project build
Sketch gate closes. Build the strongest three in Illustrator. Type pairing introduced through Type Connection, framed as a decision with a reason behind it.

*Labor Day Mon Sep 7, campus closed. No due date lands on it.*
**Tool:** Illustrator
**Competencies:** 1, 2, 5

### Week 4 · Sep 14 to 20 · Word project critique, then asset prep
**Critique 1.** Recorded walkthrough, posted, peers respond.

Then the unit that was missing entirely: sourcing and reformatting outside assets. Raster versus vector, resolution and effective ppi, RGB versus CMYK, when to use which file type, and what happens when you get it wrong.
**Competencies:** 3, 6

### Week 5 · Sep 21 to 27 · Typographic composition
The *Mac is Not a Typewriter* content, updated and expanded. This is the week that fixes the things that make student work look amateur at a glance.

Covered: **line breaks and rag.** Why a heading that drops two words onto a third line reads as broken, why it is worse when centered, and why left aligned and ragged right is the default for almost everything. Then one space after a period, curly quotes versus primes and dumb quotes, en and em dash usage, ordered versus unordered lists, hanging indents and why a wrapped bullet must not slide back under the marker, tracking versus kerning versus leading, and when small caps are appropriate.

**Reading:** Butterick&rsquo;s *Practical Typography*, free, all rights reserved, assign by link only.
**Tool:** InDesign. Hanging indents and leader tabs are an InDesign job. Figma&rsquo;s list support is weak, do not fight it.
**Competencies:** 2

### Week 6 · Sep 28 to Oct 4 · Hierarchy three ways
> Use a modular grid to arrange a text in as many ways as you can. By employing just one size of type and flush left alignment only, construct a typographic hierarchy exclusively by means of spatial arrangement. Then add variables such as weight, size, and alignment.

Two passes. Pass one is constrained: one size, flush left, spatial arrangement only. Pass two opens up weight, size, and alignment. The constraint is what teaches hierarchy, so the constrained pass is graded on its own.

**Sketch gate:** the constrained pass is done on paper first.
**Competencies:** 2, 5

### Week 7 · Oct 5 to 11 · Hierarchy critique, grid introduced
**Critique 2**, judged against one question: can a stranger tell what to read first, second, third, without being told?

Then grid introduced.
**Competencies:** 3

### Week 8 · Oct 12 to 18 · Grid
1. Graphic design equals the organization of information. Grid represents structure, organizes content, provides consistency.
2. &ldquo;Sounds constricting.&rdquo; Choose the right grid. Make rules. Break the rules for impact.
3. Case studies, each shown twice, clean and with the grid overlaid: Vignelli Canon spreads, NPS Unigrid, the 1988 Otsuka poster, Herbert Matter&rsquo;s polio poster, Paul Rand&rsquo;s *Forum*, Ben Faydherbe&rsquo;s *Haagse Zomer*, Ed Fella&rsquo;s *Artemisia*.
4. Eames: &ldquo;Design is a plan for arranging elements in such a way as to best accomplish a particular purpose.&rdquo;
5. Applying it in InDesign: ruler guides, page margins, column guides, baseline grid.

The Fella poster is the break-the-rules example and it only works after the Vignelli ones. Keep the order.

**Walk the actual NPS Unigrid Design Specifications PDF.** Public domain, real point sizes, picas, module dimensions, house typeface. It makes &ldquo;work inside a system&rdquo; concrete instead of abstract.
**Competencies:** 2

### Week 9 · Oct 19 to 25 · Poster, three grids
Same content, three structures: a column grid, a modular grid, and a deliberately broken one. Sketch gate first.
**Tool:** InDesign
**Competencies:** 2, 5

### Week 10 · Oct 26 to Nov 1 · Poster critique, color, and output
**Critique 3.**

Then color: harmonies, contrast, and accessibility. Adobe Color plus the color chapters of the BCcampus textbook.

Then the output unit, which satisfies four competencies and had no home in the old draft: bleed and slug, crop and registration marks, packaging an InDesign file, export presets, and preflight.

**The print proof.** Output one piece at home, at work, or at a print shop. Photograph it flat next to a ruler and submit the photo with the press-ready PDF and a short reflection on what changed between screen and paper. Cost is a few dollars. This is the only physical deliverable in the course and it is what competencies 9 and 10 require.
**Competencies:** 3, 8, 9, 10

### Week 11 · Nov 2 to 8 · Capstone opens

> 🚫 **DO NOT USE THE RESTAURANT BRIEF.** It was invented by an assistant session on 21 Aug 2026 and is not a real project. **The real branding project already exists in AVC 181**, where students select from an existing group of clients. There is no menu deliverable. Pull the actual client list and project requirements from Canvas before this week is built. Nothing below the client line is verified.

Branding capstone. Students select a client from the existing AVC 181 client group.

Students write their **own** client-discovery questions, by hand, not with AI. Then AI plays the fictitious client answering those questions, and the client&rsquo;s answers steer their branding decisions the way a real brief would. The questions and the thinking stay theirs.

Each student also picks one real brand manual from a short vetted list to study and report on. Reading an unfamiliar system and explaining what it governs is the skill. Reading only, no compliance project.

Also this week: copyright and Creative Commons literacy, with Openverse as the practical companion.
**Competencies:** 2

### Week 12 · Nov 9 to 15 · Logo development and a Figma primer
Logo thumbnails on paper. Concept development, no computer until the sketch gate closes.

**Figma primer**, one guided throwaway exercise rebuilding an existing screen with named styles. Deliberately placed here so week 15 is not first contact with Figma on the highest-stakes deliverable of the semester.

*Veterans Day Wed Nov 11, campus closed. This week&rsquo;s midweek due date moves to Thu Nov 12.*
**Competencies:** 1, 5

Logo refinement, palette, type system. Mini brand manual starts.
**Tools:** Illustrator, InDesign
**Competencies:** 5

### Week 14 · Nov 23 to 29 · Application, light by design
Production only, no critique, no new instruction. Menu layout, which is the best possible excuse for leader tabs and tab alignment, and the three-panel Instagram set.

Three-screen site in Figma using shared styles, not hand-typed hex.

Mini brand manual finished: logo usage, clear space, minimum size, palette with hex and CMYK, type system, and at least one &ldquo;don&rsquo;t&rdquo; page.

## Threads that run all semester

- **Hand sketching gates every computer phase.** No exceptions, on every project. Sketches are submitted as photos: flat, full page, even light, legible. State the standard in week 1 or you will receive a semester of blurry phone shots.
- **Typographic composition** from week 5 forward is a critique criterion on every project, not a one-week topic. Quotes, dashes, rag, spacing, and list formatting get checked every time.
- **Accessibility** is a critique criterion on every project, not a standalone unit.
- **Copyright and Creative Commons** is enforced from week 1 through image sourcing and formally taught in week 11.
- **Critique** is competency 3 and competency 7, the most weighted outcome in the course. Four graded critiques, weeks 4, 7, 10, and 16.

## Online delivery notes

- **Critique workflow** reuses the AVC100 pattern: OBS to YouTube unlisted to Discord to Canvas. Do not invent a second workflow for this course.
- **Software access.** Adobe CC, Figma education, and Discord all confirmed in week 1 before any project depends on them.
- **The print proof in week 10 is the only thing that requires students to leave their computer.** Give three routes (home printer, workplace, print shop) and a cost expectation so nobody is surprised.
- **6.0 periods for 3 credits** is heavy contact time. In an async course that translates to a substantial weekly workload. Run the schedule through Dial Your Course to check time on task before the syllabus posts.

## Resources

- **NPS Unigrid Design Specifications.** [npshistory.com/brochures/unigrid.pdf](https://npshistory.com/brochures/unigrid.pdf). **U.S. government work, public domain,** fully remixable. Real grid spec with module dimensions, point sizes, leading, margins, house typeface, and a 24-color standard palette. Note that it specifies justified columns, which collides with the left-aligned default taught in week 5. Teach the collision rather than hiding it.
