## Goal, Audience, Process

**Goal.** Move students from pushing type around to authoring a brand system, and send them out with a Behance portfolio they can show and defend.

**Audience.** AVC 183 students in Digital Media Arts, fully online. Most are in their first or second semester of design and have never worked to a written spec.

**Process.** Four studio projects in sequence, each built in numbered steps with a Discord critique between every step. Hand sketching gates every computer phase. A design-history research thread runs in the background. The semester ends in a portfolio.

## Weeks

Fall 2026 runs Aug 22 to Dec 18. Instruction is 16 weeks, Aug 24 to Dec 13. Finals Dec 14 to 18 carries no new work.

**Due dates:** Sunday and Wednesday, except week 12 midweek moves to Thu Nov 12 (Veterans Day), and week 14 gets one due date, Wed Nov 25, with nothing over Thanksgiving.

**Critique workflow, every step:** post to the Discord channel, receive feedback, comment on at least 4 classmates, screenshot the Discord post, submit the screenshot to Canvas for the process grade. This is 181&rsquo;s existing pattern and it is why process work is worth 40 percent.

### Week 1 · Aug 24 to 30 · Start here
Syllabus acknowledgement, course overview, Meet Your Instructor. Technology Login Challenge Part 1 and Part 2 (OBS). Helvetica film with a 100-word reflection.
**Competencies:** 1

### Week 2 · Aug 31 to Sep 6 · Reading, then Word/Meaning Part 1
Reading: *Thinking with Type*, introduction, the type-anatomy diagram on p. 32, and pp. 40 to 94. Written reflection.

**Word/Meaning Part 1.** Brainstorm 50 descriptive verbs, narrow to 5. Post all 50 with the 5 favorites to Discord. Comment on 4 classmates. Screenshot to Canvas.
**Competencies:** 2

### Week 3 · Sep 7 to 13 · Word/Meaning Part 2
At least 20 square thumbnail sketches per word, five words, by hand or on a drawing tablet. Lowercase sans serif. No color, no distortion, no extraneous elements, no broken letterforms.

*Labor Day Mon Sep 7, campus closed. No due date lands on it.*
**Competencies:** 2

### Week 4 · Sep 14 to 20 · Word/Meaning Part 3
Illustrator or InDesign, vector only, no Photoshop. Five designs, each 8x8 inches. All five on one page. Post to Discord, take critique, rework.
**Competencies:** 1, 5

### Week 5 · Sep 21 to 27 · Word/Meaning Part 4, final
Refine from Part 3 feedback. Black and white, lowercase, 8x8 with the page outline visible. Final critique in Discord, PDF of each design to Canvas.
**Competencies:** 3, 5

### Week 6 · Sep 28 to Oct 4 · Reading, then Recipe Step 1
Reading: *Thinking with Type*, pp. 103 to 171. Kerning, tracking, line spacing. Written answer: explain 3 ways to create hierarchy with type.

**Recipe Step 1.** Find a recipe with typographically playable language. Edit to about 50 words with a title, ingredients, and method. Post to Discord.
**Competencies:** 2

### Week 7 · Oct 5 to 11 · Recipe Step 2
InDesign, 8x8, **12-column grid.** Helvetica 12 point. Two designs. Hierarchy by placement, leading, and kerning only. No bold, no italic, no scale change.
**Competencies:** 2, 5

### Week 8 · Oct 12 to 18 · Recipe Step 3
Two more designs, same grid and size. Bold, italic, and all caps now allowed. Type size stays at 12 point. Must look nothing like the first two.

**Design History thread opens.** Group sign-up by time period, 1895 to 2015. See the thread section below.
**Competencies:** 2, 5

### Week 9 · Oct 19 to 25 · Recipe Steps 4 and 5
Step 4: two designs, scale now allowed. Step 5: two designs with an image in the negative space, not covering the page.

Step 5 is where **sourcing and reformatting outside assets** is taught: resolution and effective ppi, raster versus vector, RGB versus CMYK, file types, and placing rather than pasting.
**Competencies:** 5, 6

### Week 10 · Oct 26 to Nov 1 · Recipe Step 6, final, and output
Revise all eight designs. One PDF, eight 8x8 pages.

Then the output unit: bleed and slug, crop and registration marks, packaging an InDesign file, export presets, resolution and effective ppi, CMYK conversion, and preflight.

**Deliverable is a press-ready PDF built correctly for print.** No paper. This is an online course and students do not turn in physical work.
**Competencies:** 3, 8, 9, 10

### Week 11 · Nov 2 to 8 · Logo Steps 1 and 2
Read *Pencil to PDF* from *Logo Design Love*.

**Step 1.** Choose one client from the five and invent the business name using the word-association process.
**Step 2.** At least 50 thumbnail sketches by hand in an unlined sketchbook, 9 to 12 per page.

The five clients (verbatim from 181): city zoo · multicultural and gender non-specific makeup company · technology service for people 50 to 100 · kids&rsquo; snack company · car repair company employing a population that has difficulty finding work. Full briefs in the appendix.
**Competencies:** 2

### Week 12 · Nov 9 to 15 · Logo Step 3
Top 6 sketches refined. At least 5 versions of each. One typeface and a palette of no more than 3 colors, chosen against the client&rsquo;s stated audience and goals. Never stretch or distort type.

*Veterans Day Wed Nov 11, campus closed. Midweek due date moves to Thu Nov 12.*
**Competencies:** 2, 5

### Week 13 · Nov 16 to 22 · Logo Steps 4 and 5
Step 4: narrow to the best 1 or 2, refine on the computer. Must work in black and white as well as in the 3 colors. No screens, no gradients.
Step 5: apply to a stitched one-color t-shirt, a business card, signage, and one other relevant use. Show the palette and typeface. Write the audience and goals in detail.
**Competencies:** 5, 8

### Week 14 · Nov 23 to 29 · Logo Step 6 and Advertisement Parts 1 and 2
Light by design. Step 6: final logo changes from critique. Advertisement Part 1: write the three-part message. Part 2: at least 20 sketches by hand, same grid across all three panels.

Reading: pp. 205 to 249 on scaffolding and consistent grid structure.

*Thanksgiving Nov 26 to 29, campus closed. One due date, Wed Nov 25.*
**Competencies:** 2, 3

### Week 15 · Nov 30 to Dec 6 · Advertisement Parts 3, 4, and 5
Build, refine from feedback, submit final. Three panels sharing one grid structure, each carrying one message, an image, and the logo. Consistent rhetorical elements pulled from the logo.

**Student chooses the format:** three posters, a three-panel Instagram campaign, or **a three-screen website built in Figma.** Same brief, same grid discipline, different medium.
**Competencies:** 1, 5

### Week 16 · Dec 7 to 13 · Behance portfolio and course evaluation
Build or update Behance: name, headshot, header image, a short professional description, and at least one project from this course with process work and a written description. Submit a working URL.

Design History presentations posted and reviewed. Course evaluations.

Last day of classes Sun Dec 13.
**Competencies:** 3, 4, 11

### Draft module idea: Brand guidelines as design tokens, applied across media (2 Aug 2026)

The insight worth teaching is not a specific tool. It is the **design-system / design-token mindset**: define the brand once (palette, type scale, spacing, logo usage), then apply those same tokens consistently everywhere. That concept is core graphic design, it spans print and web, and it is tool-durable. Tools churn; the systematic-brand-application concept does not. This is a strong "outlast the semester" lesson.

**Goal:** Students learn to express a set of brand guidelines as reusable tokens (color, type, spacing, logo rules) and apply them consistently across print, screen design, and web, understanding that each medium implements the same tokens its own way.

**Audience:** AVC 183 digital graphic design students (Digital Media Arts). Many will move between print and interactive work, so the through-line matters.

**Process / how it works:** Start from a brand guide the students build, turn it into a token set, then implement the same tokens in each medium and compare. Teach the concept first, the tools second.

#### The same tokens, per medium

- **Print** applies brand tokens through Illustrator and InDesign: paragraph styles, character styles, object styles, and swatches.
- **Adobe CC Libraries** is the piece to flag for this course. It shares the same brand colors, type, and logos across every Adobe file, print and web. For graphic design students this is arguably the most directly relevant "brand tokens everywhere" tool, and they are already in the Adobe suite.
- **Figma** applies them through variables and shared styles.
- **Web** applies them through CSS custom properties (variables) or a Tailwind config.

#### Where Tailwind fits (honest placement)

- Tailwind CSS is a **utility-first CSS framework**: a large library of ready-made classes (`p-4`, `text-lg`, `bg-white`) applied directly in the HTML, plus a config file that holds the brand tokens (colors, type, spacing) so the web build matches the brand guide.
- It is **one example on the web side** of the tokens lesson, not the headline. It is a front-end implementation tool, not a UX-design tool.
- **Free and open source** (MIT license). There is a paid add-on (Tailwind Plus) with pre-designed components, never required.
- **Pedagogy caution:** teach CSS fundamentals first (box model, specificity, flexbox, grid). Handing beginners Tailwind before they understand CSS trains them to paste utility classes without understanding styling. Introduce Tailwind as an intermediate piece, framed as the **design-to-development handoff**: how a design gets built by, and handed to, developers.

#### Figma and Tailwind

- They do not connect natively. The bridge is the shared **design-token** idea: Figma variables (brand colors, type, spacing defined once) map to the same tokens in a Tailwind config.
- Plugins exist that try to export Figma tokens or frames into Tailwind classes, but the auto-generated code is rough. The durable link is the token concept, not a plugin.

#### Open item / next step

- Run this module concept past the UX-practitioner advisory panel (same industry-validation loop used for the AVC 2xx UX course). If the panel and the AVC 183 competencies do not call for front-end frameworks, keep Tailwind out of the design course and teach the tokens concept with Adobe (styles, swatches, CC Libraries) and CSS variables instead. Do not add Tailwind just because it is popular.
- Decide whether this lives as one module or threads through the whole brand unit.

### Updated course objectives (draft, 2 Aug 2026)

The inherited objectives are artifact-based and dated (they literally say "design a letterhead" and "design a business card"). A modern beginning graphic design course should be **skill and competency based**, not tied to specific deliverables. Draft rewrite, students will be able to:

1. Apply the core design principles (hierarchy, contrast, alignment, proximity, repetition, balance, and white space) to build clear, intentional compositions.
2. Design with a grid, using grid systems to structure layout and create visual order.
3. Demonstrate typography fundamentals: letterform anatomy, type classification, type pairing, typographic hierarchy, and spacing (kerning, tracking, leading) for readable, expressive type.
4. Apply color theory (harmonies, contrast, and accessibility) to design work.
5. **Apply an existing brand system** from a real brand manual and design consistently within its rules (the "apply before you build" step).
6. **Build a simple brand identity** of their own (logo, palette, type system, and a short set of guidelines) and document it as a mini brand manual.
7. Source and use imagery legally, with copyright and Creative Commons literacy and correct attribution.
8. Use industry tools (Illustrator, InDesign, Photoshop) and prepare files correctly for screen and print.
9. Work through a real design process: brief, research, iteration, critique, and presentation.
10. Make accessibility-aware decisions (contrast, legibility) in their design work.

**Signature progression Michelle wants:** apply an existing brand first (hand students a finished brand manual, they design within it), then build their own brand. This teaches consistency and rationale before creative ownership. Good brand systems to apply are in the OER list below (NASA, USWDS, Mailchimp).

**The Canvas pattern, confirmed 29 Aug 2026 by reading the imscc:**

- **Section headings are a solid color bar**, white text: `<h3 style="background:#COLOR;color:#fff;padding:8px 13px;">Heading</h3>`
  - **AVC 183 uses plum `#7a5080`.** Used 138 times in that package.
  - **AVC 248 uses navy.** Confirm the exact hex from a current Canvas page before building.
- **Callout box**, for deadlines and warnings: `<div style="background:#f7f0e6;border:1px solid #ddc9ae;border-left:5px solid #8a5a2b;padding:14px 16px;margin:0 0 18px;">` with a bold colored lead line in `#8a5a2b`.
- **Tables** use `border="1" cellpadding="6" cellspacing="0" style="border-collapse:collapse;width:100%;"` with header cells in the same bar color, white text.
- **Links** are plain `<a href="..." target="_blank">`. No color set. No `rel` needed for Canvas.
- **Everything else is plain semantic HTML.** No classes, no stylesheet, no `<style>` block. Canvas strips those.

**Why this note exists.** On 29 Aug 2026 four wrong versions of a Canvas page were produced in a row, because the format was guessed from the website CSS instead of read from the package. The answer was in the `.imscc` the whole time. **Do not guess the Canvas format. Unzip and look.**
