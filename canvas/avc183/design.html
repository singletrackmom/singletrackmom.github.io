Graphic Design

Courses and Programs · Case study

Course designDesign HistoryExample

The first graphic design course. Students move from pushing type around to working inside a real brand and then authoring their own, through studio projects with critique between every step, a design history thread they teach to each other, and a portfolio they can show and defend.

Goal

Move beginning designers from arranging type to authoring a brand system, and send them out with a portfolio they can show and defend.

Audience

Design students in their first or second semester, most of whom have never worked to a written specification. The course is a foundation for Graphic Design, Digital Media Arts, and Animation students.

Process

Each project is built in numbered steps, with a Discord critique between every step. Hand sketching gates every computer phase, so ideas are worked out before the software opens.

ProjectWhat students learn
Word and MeaningMaking a word look like what it means, using only type, scale, and placement, from twenty hand thumbnails per word to final vector designs
Recipe (grids)A 12-column grid, leading, and type hierarchy, unlocking bold, italic, caps, scale, and finally an image in negative space
Someone Else’s BrandWorking inside a real city’s published brand guidelines to design a welcome kit: a poster built to print spec, an Instagram ad, and kit mockups
Your Own BrandNaming a business for a client, fifty hand thumbnails, a logo that works in black and white, a press-ready business card, apparel or signage, and a three-panel Instagram campaign
Figma websiteThe student’s own brand pulled into Figma as styles and carried across a three-page website
Design HistoryTeams research a period of design history and teach it to the class, in a presentation designed in that era’s visual language
PortfolioA Behance portfolio with process work and a written rationale

Outcomes

The course is built to the district’s revised competencies. A student can:

  1. Describe the graphic design process, including history, research, concept development, and production.
  2. Apply elements and principles of design, including composition, grids, hierarchy, typography, and color.
  3. Use digital design and page-layout software for document setup, text formatting, image placement, and layout.
  4. Develop design concepts through research, sketches, and preliminary layouts.
  5. Produce a multipage layout incorporating typography, imagery, and appropriate layout techniques.
  6. Evaluate graphic design projects according to design principles, accessibility, technical quality, and communication effectiveness.

Pacing and interaction

Two noon deadlines a week, with work posted to the class Discord the day before for critique and a short late window after.

Technology

  • Adobe Creative Cloud. Illustrator for marks and vector work, InDesign for layout, Photoshop for images, and Figma for the website.
  • AI that writes, not designs. Students use Gemini for research and writing, and direct AI to build the Design History presentation from a requirements document they write first. Every mark, layout, and poster is designed by the student.
  • Critique and presentation. Discord for step-by-step critique, OBS for recorded presentations, and Behance for the portfolio.

Status

Taught every spring and fall.
