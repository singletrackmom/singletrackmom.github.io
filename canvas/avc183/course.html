How this course works

AVC 183 is fully online and 3 credits. There are no live class meetings. Plan on about 8 hours a week for 15 weeks.

  1. Watch the two videos in Module 0: Get to Know Your Instructor and Getting Started in the Course.
  2. Read this Start Here section and the Start Here page.
  3. Finish the Module 0 work in Week 1, starting with AI in This Course and the AI Primer.
  4. Check the full calendar on the course schedule.

Read AI in This Course in Module 0 during Week 1. It explains how AI is used here and how your work is protected.

Every module has the same parts: Objectives, Overview, Watch, Read, Practice, Assessment and Critique. Each graded item names the objective it measures.

Two rules hold on every project: sketch by hand before you use the computer, and never stretch or distort type.

Deadlines

  • Work is due at noon Arizona time on Thursday or Sunday. Canvas shows each due date.
  • Process Work and Projects: post to #avc183-graphic-design-1 by noon the day before. Then make three critique comments by the due time. Exercises have no Discord step.
  • Work turned in up to 24 hours late loses 10%. After that hard close, Canvas accepts nothing. No deadline falls on a day the college is closed.

Your instructor and getting help

Your instructor is a working graphic designer and Digital Media Arts faculty member. Discord office hours are in the welcome announcement.

  • Discord: ask software and project questions in #avc183-graphic-design-1 on the Digital Media Arts Discord, so everyone sees the answer. If you cannot use Discord, use a Canvas discussion.
  • Canvas Inbox: grades, accommodations and anything private (how to use the Inbox).
  • Replies come within one business day. Process Work is graded within three days. Projects get written feedback within one week.
  • Software problems: if software will not open or sign in, tell your instructor that day. Adobe sign-in problems usually take about a day to fix.

Critique

Critique means giving and getting feedback on design work. In each comment:

  • Name one design principle (in Module 3, one brand rule) and point to where it shows in the work.
  • Say what works first. Then suggest a change, not only a problem.
  • Be respectful. Talk about the work, never the person. Screen names are fine.
  • Put your Discord post and your three comments on one Miro board (an online whiteboard). Submit a screenshot of it to Canvas with the step.

Accessibility of your work

Everything you post must work for people who cannot see it or hear it. Alt text is a short written description of an image for people who use screen readers.

  • Images: add alt text to every image that matters, including on Discord. Post images as PNG or JPG, never PDF.
  • Video and audio: turn on automatic captions, then fix every line by hand. Add a corrected transcript to audio.
  • Documents and pages: use real headings, readable contrast and tagged PDFs. Check web pages with WAVE and colors with the WebAIM Contrast Checker.
  • Outside tools: privacy and accessibility for Discord (privacy, accessibility), Adobe (privacy), Miro (privacy, accessibility), Google (privacy, accessibility) and Canvas (privacy, accessibility). If a tool does not work with your assistive technology, contact your instructor and Disability Resources and Services. If OBS does not work for you, use the recorder you already use.

Course competencies

  1. Describe the graphic design process, including history, research, concept development, and production. (Modules 0, 3, 6)

  5. Produce a multipage layout incorporating typography, imagery, and appropriate layout techniques. (Modules 2, 5)

AI in This Course

This course uses generative AI (Google Gemini) in assignments. Our industry advisors asked us to teach it, and employers now expect it. In Microsoft and LinkedIn’s 2024 Work Trend Index, 66% of business leaders said they would not hire someone without AI skills.

  • Using AI here is not cheating. Cheating is turning in work you did not do as if you did. Here, AI use is assigned and recorded in your AI-use log, and you are graded on your own thinking, research, choices and design work. Other courses may have different rules.
  • Your work is not used to train AI. Use Gemini only with your Maricopa account, never a personal one. Under the district’s Google for Education agreement, people do not review what you enter, and it is not used to train AI or for ads (Gemini for Education; education FAQ). Your instructor never puts your work into a public AI tool. Never enter personal information, like your student ID or phone number, into any AI tool.
  • AI does not make your work. Gemini helps with research, writing, feedback and planning. It never designs. Every mark, layout, color and type choice is yours, and you show your process.
  • The Design History exception: this is the one project where AI may design. Gemini builds the presentation from requirements you write. You check every fact in library databases. Historical artwork is always real and sourced, never AI-made. No AI-made images go in any other design work.
  • You will learn to question it. AI makes confident mistakes and repeats bias from its training data. It is trained on artists’ work, often without permission, and it uses a lot of energy (International Energy Agency, Energy and AI). You learn to check every answer against a source you open yourself.
  • Tools: Gemini for research, writing, feedback and planning; Gemini Notebook (formerly NotebookLM), which answers only from sources you add; and Gemini Canvas, in Design History only.
  • The AI-use log: one Google Sheet you keep all term, with four columns: date, tool, what you asked, and what you kept or changed. If you used no AI, write “No AI used.” Submit the row, or a screenshot of it, with each assignment.
  • Students under 18: your account blocks some Gemini features, such as Deep Research and image editing. No assignment needs a blocked feature. If Gemini refuses a step, ask on Discord or in Canvas Inbox for another way to meet the same objective.

Instructor notes

Video: Word/Meaning walkthrough (about 4 minutes)

  • The constraints: type only, black and white, lowercase sans serif, square, no distortion.
  • Show a strong thumbnail sheet: range of size and placement, positive and negative space.
  • Common mistakes: nouns instead of verbs, stretched letters, added images.

Objectives

  • 1.1 Classify the anatomy of letterforms in lettering you photograph, using the vocabulary of the reading (Course Competency 2)
  • 1.2 Generate 50 descriptive verbs and at least 20 hand thumbnails for each of five chosen words within fixed constraints (Course Competency 4)
  • 1.3 Construct five vector designs that express meaning through placement, scale, and positive and negative space (Course Competency 4)
  • 1.4 Revise your designs in response to peer critique and justify each final design in one sentence that names the principle it uses (Course Competency 6)

Overview

Make five words look like what they mean, using only type: no color, no pictures. Working with type alone shows how placement and scale carry meaning. You move from 50 handwritten verbs, to 100 thumbnails (small, quick sketches by hand), to vector designs, to a final. Vector art is built from points and lines, so it stays sharp at any size.

Word/Meaning Part 2: Thumbnails (10 points, Process Work)

This assignment measures Module Objective 1.2, which supports Course Competency 4.

Do not submit this part until you have feedback on Part 1.

  1. Create at least 20 thumbnail sketches of each word, 100 in all, on paper or a drawing tablet. Do not use computer type.
  2. Make each thumbnail about 2 by 2 inches, several on a page. The final is square, so sketch square.
  3. Draw black lowercase sans serif letters (letters without small feet) on white. Change size and placement. Use both the letters (positive space) and the empty space around them (negative space).

You may repeat a letter, use a capital if needed, and let letters overlap or run off the page. You may not use color, stretch or distort type, add backgrounds or images, or break letters apart.

AI: No AI use. Log “No AI used.”

Rubric (10): at least 20 thumbnails per word, 100 total 4 (Objective 1.2); by hand, lowercase sans serif, square 2 (Objective 1.2); constraints held: no color, no distortion, no extra elements 2 (Objective 1.2); Discord post with alt text, three principle-based comments, compiled in Miro 2 (Objective 1.2).
