Welcome to Digital Graphic Arts I

Read this first. Then read the two pages after it, then do the four assignments.

Everything in this module is due Thursday, Aug 27, at noon.

Estimated time: about 5 hours this week, most of it software setup.

This course is fully online. There are no live meetings. Everything runs on a rhythm you can plan your life around.

The deadline rule, and it never changes

Work is due at noon. Critique on that work is due at noon the next day.

Nothing in this course is ever due at midnight. You post your work by noon, you get a full day to look at what everyone else made, and your feedback is due at noon the following day. You should never be up at 11:30 at night trying to comment on eight people’s posts.

Deadline days are Monday and Thursday, starting in week 2. This first week has one deadline, Thursday at noon, so you have time to get your software working.

The full sixteen weeks are on the course schedule. Bookmark it.

What you will make

Four projects, in this order:

  1. The word project. Make five words look like what they mean, using nothing but type. No color, no pictures, no tricks.
  2. The recipe project. One recipe, eight designs, a twelve-column grid, and one typeface at one size. Constraints unlock one at a time. This is where you learn grids.
  3. Someone else’s brand. You get a real professional design system, the one the federal government uses, and you design inside their rules. You also meet Figma here, step by step.
  4. Your own brand. Pick a client, name the business, build the logo, then take it out into print, apparel or signage, a social campaign, and finally a three-page website in Figma.

You finish by putting all of it into a Behance portfolio you keep long after this class ends.

How you are graded

CategoryWeight
Process work on projects40%
Projects35%
Exercises, quizzes, reflections25%

Process outweighs the finished piece on purpose. Sketches, drafts, and critique are worth more than the final file. You cannot skip to the finish. Designers who only show final work cannot explain their decisions, and explaining decisions is the job.

Two rules that hold all semester

  • You sketch by hand before you touch the computer. Every project. There is a page in this module on how to photograph sketches so they count.
  • You never stretch or distort type. It is scored on every project. It is the fastest way to make work look amateur.

If something is broken, tell me today

Adobe licensing fails for somebody every single semester, and it takes about a day to fix. If Illustrator or InDesign will not open or will not sign in, email me the day you find out, not Wednesday night. I can help on Monday. I cannot help at 11:45 on Wednesday.
