This course is fully online. There are no live meetings. Everything runs on a rhythm you can plan your life around.

The deadline rule, and it never changes

Everything is due at noon, Arizona time. Nothing is ever due at midnight.

Projects and project steps go up on the class Discord for critique by noon the day before they are due, and you leave three critique comments on classmates’ work by the due time. Exercises, readings, quizzes, discussions and reflections have no Discord step; they are due at noon. Late work is accepted for 24 hours after a deadline at 10% off. Nothing is accepted after noon the day after the due date.

Deadline days are Thursday and Sunday, starting in week 2. This first week has one deadline, Thursday at noon, so you have time to get your software working.

The full sixteen weeks are on the course schedule. Bookmark it.

What you will make

Four projects, in this order:

  1. The word project. Make five words look like what they mean, using nothing but type. No color, no pictures, no tricks.
  2. The recipe project. One recipe, eight designs, a twelve-column grid, and one typeface at one size. Constraints unlock one at a time. This is where you learn grids.
  3. Someone else’s brand. You get a real professional design system, the one the federal government uses, and you design inside their rules. You also meet Figma here, step by step.
  4. Your own brand. Pick a client, name the business, build the logo, then take it out into print, apparel or signage, a social campaign, and finally a three-page website in Figma.

You finish by putting all of it into a Behance portfolio you keep long after this class ends.

How you are graded

Process outweighs the finished piece on purpose. Sketches, drafts, and critique are worth more than the final file. You cannot skip to the finish. Designers who only show final work cannot explain their decisions, and explaining decisions is the job.

Two rules that hold all semester

  • You sketch by hand before you touch the computer. Every project. There is a page in this module on how to photograph sketches so they count.
  • You never stretch or distort type. It is scored on every project. It is the fastest way to make work look amateur.

Make your work accessible

Every piece of work you submit or post has to be usable by someone who cannot see or hear it. Video gets accurate captions: start with automatic captions (YouTube Studio, Premiere Pro Speech to Text, Google Vids or Meet), then read every line and fix names, terms and timing by hand. Audio gets a corrected transcript. Every meaningful image, including images you post in Discord, gets alt text: one or two sentences on what matters, not “image of.” Documents and slides use real headings, readable contrast and alt text, and PDFs are exported with tags. Portfolio and Google Sites pages get alt text, captions on embedded video, headings in order and a check with the WAVE accessibility checker. On assignments with a rubric, captions, transcripts and alt text count for about 10 percent of the points.

If something is broken, tell me today

Adobe licensing fails for somebody every single semester, and it takes about a day to fix. If Illustrator or InDesign will not open or will not sign in, email me the day you find out, not Wednesday night. I can help on Monday. I cannot help at 11:45 the night before a deadline.
