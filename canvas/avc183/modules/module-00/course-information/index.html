AVC 183 Graphic Design. 3 credits. Fully online and asynchronous, 15 weeks, January 16 to May 9, 2027. Prerequisite or corequisite: a grade of C or better in AVC100, or permission of the instructor.

Course description

Introduction to graphic design principles and digital design techniques. Emphasis on composition, typography, color theory, visual hierarchy, and the application of design principles using industry standard tools.

Course competencies

  1. Describe the graphic design process, including history, research, concept development, and production. (Understand)
  2. Apply elements and principles of design, including composition, grids, hierarchy, typography, and color. (Apply)
  3. Use digital design and page-layout software for document setup, text formatting, image placement, and layout. (Apply)
  4. Develop design concepts through research, sketches, and preliminary layouts. (Create)
  5. Produce a multipage layout incorporating typography, imagery, and appropriate layout techniques. (Create)
  6. Evaluate graphic design projects according to design principles, accessibility, technical quality, and communication effectiveness. (Evaluate)

Course objectives

Every objective is written with the revised Bloom’s taxonomy (Anderson and Krathwohl, 2001), sits at or below the level of its course competency, and is measured by a graded assignment. Each assessment was also checked for AI exposure using Oregon State University Ecampus, Bloom’s Taxonomy Revisited, so graded evidence is work only you can produce: hand sketches, your own photographs, construction files, recordings and verification logs.

Process outweighs the finished piece on purpose. Designers who show only final work cannot explain their decisions, and explaining decisions is the job.

Deadlines and late work

Everything is due at noon, Arizona time. Nothing is ever due at midnight.

There are two deadlines a week, Thursday and Sunday; Week 1 has one, on Thursday. Projects and project steps (Process Work and Projects) are posted to the class Discord by noon the day before they are due, and you leave three critique comments on classmates’ work by the due time. Exercises, readings, quizzes and reflections have no Discord step. Late work is accepted for 24 hours after a deadline with a 10 percent deduction. Nothing is accepted after noon the day after the due date. When a holiday falls on a deadline or its hard close, the deadline moves a day earlier, so nothing is due when campus is closed.

Comments on classmates who post during the 24-hour window are encouraged and ungraded. They are good community citizenship, not extra credit.

Process Work is graded within three days of the due date. Projects are graded with written feedback within one week.

AI in this course

The AI rule in this course. Use Gemini with your Maricopa account only, never a personal account. When you need a notebook built from your own sources, use Gemini Notebook, not Gems. Gemini writes, it never designs: it helps with research, writing, feedback and planning, and every mark, layout and visual decision is yours. The one exception is the Design History project, where Gemini also builds the presentation from requirements you write, and you verify every fact in library databases. Every use goes in your AI-use log.

If you are under 18, your Maricopa Google account blocks some Gemini features (for example Deep Research and image editing). No assignment requires a blocked feature. If Gemini refuses a step, post in Discord or send a Canvas Inbox message and you will receive an alternate step that meets the same objective.

The full statement, with sources, is on AI in This Course.

Computers and software

The district provides Adobe Creative Cloud and Gemini at no cost. Figma and Miro have free education plans. Students without a computer or reliable internet can borrow a laptop or hotspot through GCC’s Office of Information Technology (OIT) Student Technology Request.

Accommodations

Students with a disability who need an accommodation contact Disability Resources and Services as early as possible. Recording tasks always have an equivalent: if OBS does not work with your assistive technology, use the recorder you already use, and tell your instructor so an equivalent is approved.
