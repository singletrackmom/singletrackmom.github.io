Graphic Design 2

Courses and Programs · Case study

Course design

The second graphic design course. Students build a complete visual identity system for a client, document it in an accessible brand guidelines book, and present the whole process as a portfolio case study.

Goal

Take students from a single logo to a governed brand system, and from a single layout to a long, styled, accessible publication: the two skills a working designer needs most.

Audience

Graphic Design students who have completed Graphic Design and are building toward a professional portfolio.

Process

One client carries the whole term, in three phases. Every step is posted for Discord critique before it is due, and each phase ends in a presentation.

PhaseWhat students build
IdentityResearch and a creative brief, sixty thumbnails, three logo directions, a refined mark, an accessible palette and type system, stationery and collateral, and a client presentation
PublicationA brand guidelines book in InDesign: type specimen, grids and parent pages, paragraph and character styles, images and anchored objects, interactive features, and accessibility tagging
PortfolioA portfolio case study and recorded presentation, followed by a final revision

Outcomes

A student can:

  1. Develop visual identity systems through logo design and brand applications.
  2. Apply advanced typography, composition, and color techniques to graphic design projects.
  3. Use advanced digital design and publishing tools for complex layouts, styles, and document organization.
  4. Produce professional graphic design materials for print and digital applications.
  5. Create multi-page publications incorporating advanced layout techniques, technical document features, and interactive elements.
  6. Evaluate graphic design projects for visual effectiveness, accessibility, technical quality, and portfolio presentation.

Pacing and interaction

Two noon deadlines a week, with work posted to the class Discord the day before for critique and a short late window after.

Technology

  • Adobe Creative Cloud. Illustrator for the mark and identity, InDesign for the guidelines book, Photoshop for imagery, and Acrobat for accessibility tagging.
  • AI as a studio partner. Gemini Notebook holds the client research, Gemini critiques a mark at small sizes, drafts brand voice options, explains GREP patterns, and drafts alt text the student checks. Every use goes in a short AI-use log.
  • Marks are drawn by hand. Every logo is student-drawn vector work with its construction file as evidence, because AI-generated marks cannot be owned or defended by a client.

Where the course is going

The next step is a brand generator. Students build the brand, write its rules into a generator (Gemini, a Figma component library, or a small web tool), let it produce a batch of posters or social posts, and critique the batch against their own rules. The designer’s job moves toward making the brand and judging what a machine produces.

Status

Offered every spring.
