## Description

Advanced graphic design for print and digital media. Development of visual identity systems, advanced typography, and complex multi-page layouts using industry-standard tools. Evaluation of design work for effectiveness, accessibility, and portfolio presentation.

## Competencies

1. Develop visual identity systems through logo design and brand applications. (I)
2. Apply advanced typography, composition, and color techniques to graphic design projects. (II)
3. Use advanced digital design and publishing tools for complex layouts, styles, and document organization. (II, III)
4. Produce professional graphic design materials for print and digital applications. (III)
5. Create multi-page publications incorporating advanced layout techniques, technical document features, and interactive elements. (III)
6. Evaluate graphic design projects for visual effectiveness, accessibility, technical quality, and portfolio presentation. (IV)

## Outline

- **I. Visual Identity and Brand Applications:** logo development and refinement; visual identity systems; business correspondence and brand collateral; brand consistency across applications.
- **II. Advanced Design Principles and Techniques:** advanced typography and type styling; composition, grids, and visual hierarchy; advanced color applications and effects; illustration and image integration.
- **III. Advanced Digital Publishing:** paragraph, character, and nested styles; parent pages and complex page layouts; text wrapping, anchored objects, and image treatments; advanced document organization and production techniques.
- **IV. Portfolio Development and Evaluation:** design critique and refinement; professional production standards; portfolio selection and organization; presentation of completed design projects.

**Policies built into the gradebook**

- Late window: work submitted after the deadline and before the hard close loses 10 percent. Nothing is accepted after the hard close.
- One resubmission per milestone (MS1 to MS5), due within seven days of receiving feedback. The resubmitted score replaces the original. Resubmissions are not available for practice, discussions, Project 1 or the Final.
- Feedback turnaround: milestones within 2 to 3 days of the deadline; Project 1 and Final Part A within 5 days; practice and discussions within one week.
- Every graded item that uses AI includes a short AI-use log (Section 5.4). “No AI used” is an acceptable entry.

## 5. AI integration plan

### 5.1 Principles

1. AI is a studio tool here, used often and on purpose, and always paired with something the student authors.
2. The district pays for **Google Gemini** (student accounts from January 2027) and **Adobe Creative Cloud**. The course uses those first. Gems are being replaced by Gemini skills (saved instructions with files); the brand generator uses skills as agents, with a saved-instructions chat as the fallback. Otherwise the course uses Gemini chat, **Gemini Notebook**, and Gemini in Google Docs and Slides. Gemini writes; it never designs: no AI images, moodboards, mockups or book imagery.
3. **No generative imagery.** Adobe generative features are not used for course visuals; students make every image themselves or use credited, openly licensed images.
4. **Logos and final identity marks are student-drawn vector work.** AI is used for research, word association and moodboards only. (Reason in 5.3.)
5. Every use is logged in a short AI-use log.

### 5.6 The brand generator (Modules 12 to 14, Weeks 13 to 15)

Students build a brand generator: a system that applies their brand to new content. It runs as an open design challenge, kept modest because the brand book stays the center of the course.

1. **Challenge brief and agent plan (S16).** The student picks a target (a 3 to 5 page brand website, a scheduled social campaign of a few weeks to three months, or an approved idea of the same size) and writes an agent plan: at least a client content agent (Gemini plays the client or its marketing team and writes the copy from the client packet, brief and voice pages) and a brand checker; others optional (calendar planner, code helper, image scout). Agents are Gemini skills, or a saved-instructions chat for students without skills.
2. **Rules and content (S17).** Rules version 1 for the look and the words; content for a test batch; both agents built and tested; images from the student’s own bank or a free stock site (AI may select real photos; it never makes them; every image logged with source, photographer and license).
3. **Prototypes and direction (S18).** At least two approaches (Figma templates and a sheet, Figma Buzz, a generator vibe-coded with Gemini, a scheduled social calendar, InDesign Data Merge, or the student’s own) on the same 3 to 5 piece test batch; one direction chosen with a rationale citing the tests.
4. **Build, run, revise (S19 and Project 2).** Full first batch, checker review plus the student’s own scoring and usable rate; rules version 2; second run; final set (site published, or calendar scheduled or marked ready to schedule); a 2 to 3 minute captioned demo video; a process log of every test.

Grading weights the process (prototypes, tests, rationale, iteration) over polish. Unverified items to check before the term: whether Gemini skills are on for student Maricopa accounts and for students under 18; that Gemini Canvas Export to Google Sites and the Sites Embed code option work on student accounts; Figma education verification for students under 18.

## Brand generator decision, 25 Oct 2026 (Michelle)

  1. a poster,
