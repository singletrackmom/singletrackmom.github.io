How Critique Works

Critique format. Every comment names one specific strength and why it works, one specific issue and where it is (for example, “the tagline in the stacked lockup”), and one suggestion tied to a design principle (hierarchy, contrast, alignment, proximity, legibility, consistency or accessibility), in two to four sentences. Comment on three different classmates, starting with posts that have the fewest comments. Critique the work, not the person.

If you cannot use Discord, use the matching Canvas discussion for every post and comment; tell the instructor through Canvas Inbox.

Accessibility of Your Work

Everything you make must work for someone who cannot see it or cannot hear it. About 10 percent of the points on every media assignment go to this.

  • Video: start with automatic captions (YouTube Studio, Premiere Pro Speech to Text or Google Meet), then read every line and fix names, terms and timing by hand.
  • Images: alt text on every meaningful image, one or two sentences on what matters; decorative images marked decorative.
  • Documents and PDFs: real headings, readable contrast, alt text, and PDFs exported with tags.
  • Web pages: headings in order, descriptive link text, alt text, captions on embedded video, checked with WAVE.
  • Discord: alt text on every image; captions or a transcript with every audio or video post.

AI in This Course

This course uses generative AI tools (Google Gemini) in assignments, as recommended by our industry advisors. Students use AI for research, writing and feedback, and create their own design work.

This course uses generative AI as part of the assignments. Our industry advisory board encourages us to teach current industry practice, and employers now expect graduates to work with AI. In Microsoft and LinkedIn’s 2024 Work Trend Index, 66% of business leaders said they would not hire someone without AI skills.

Using AI here is not cheating. Cheating is turning in work you did not do as if you did. In this course, AI use is assigned, it is disclosed in an AI-use log with every assignment, and you are graded on your own thinking: your research, your decisions and your design work. Other courses may set different rules, and those rules apply in those courses.

Your work is not used to train AI. You will use Gemini with your Maricopa account. Under the district’s Google for Education agreement, what you enter is not reviewed by people, not used to train AI models, and not used for ads. Always sign in with your Maricopa account, never a personal one. Your instructor will not put your work into any public AI tool. Gemini, Gemini Notebook and Google Sites are the only AI tools this course uses.

AI does not make your work. Gemini writes; it never designs. Gemini helps with research, writing, critique questions and checklists. It never makes images, moodboard images, mockups, book imagery or any other visual. Every mark, layout, image and visual decision is yours, and you show your process. Every logo is drawn by you in vector, and you submit the construction file.

You will learn to question it. AI makes confident mistakes and reflects bias in its training data. This course teaches you to check every answer and to think critically about what AI gets wrong and what it costs.

If a Gemini feature is blocked on your account. Some Gemini features are limited for students under 18. If a feature an assignment names is blocked, use the equivalent listed on that assignment (for example, research in a Google Doc with GCC Library databases in place of Gemini Notebook), and note it in your AI-use log. No grade depends on access to a specific AI feature. If an assignment lists no equivalent, send a Canvas Inbox message and one will be set up.

Overview

You are here because you want a career in graphic design. The people who will hire you have already decided AI skills matter: in Microsoft and LinkedIn’s 2024 survey, 66% of business leaders said they would not hire someone without AI skills, and 71% said they would rather hire a less experienced candidate who has them. Three out of four knowledge workers already use AI on the job. Our industry advisors asked us to teach it. Leaving you without these skills would put you behind the people you will compete with.

Many of you have serious doubts about AI, and some of those doubts are right. AI is trained on artists’ work, often without permission. It makes confident mistakes. It reflects the biases in its training data. Data centers used about 1.5% of the world’s electricity in 2024, and the International Energy Agency expects that to more than double by 2030. This course does not hide those problems.

Here is the case for learning it anyway. Refusing AI does not slow it down; it only removes your voice from how it gets used. The designers who decide how AI is used in their studios are the ones who understand it well enough to be trusted. Employers also say creativity, communication and critical thinking matter more than ever, because AI cannot do those for you. This course keeps the creative work in your hands, teaches you to use AI for what it does well, and to catch it when it is wrong. You can leave as an informed critic; you cannot be one without knowing how it works.

Is this cheating? No. Cheating is turning in work you did not do as if you did. Here, AI use is assigned, it is disclosed in an AI-use log with every assignment, and you are graded on your own thinking: your research, your decisions and your design work.

How your work is protected. Use Gemini only with your Maricopa account. Your instructor never puts your work into a public AI tool. Read the exact wording: Google for Education, Gemini for Education and the Workspace with Gemini FAQ for Education.

Gemini writes; it never designs. In this course Gemini helps with research, writing, critique questions and checklists. Every mark, layout, image and visual decision is yours, and you show your process.

Overview

Clients approve what they understand. This module turns the identity work into a client presentation and explains why the mark itself must be human-made. A logo is usually protected in two ways: trademark law protects it as a sign that identifies the client’s goods or services, and copyright law protects the artwork. The U.S. Copyright Office has stated that material generated by AI without sufficient human creative control is not protected by copyright, so a client could not stop others from copying an AI-generated mark as artwork. AI image tools can also produce results that resemble marks already in use. A designer who hands a client a logo is promising that the client can own and defend it, so every mark in this course is drawn by you in vector, with the construction file as evidence.

From Gemini to a Web Page

You can turn something you make in Gemini into a real web page. You do not need to write any code. Your Final Project Part B case study is published on Google Sites the same way, so this short setup step makes publishing familiar.

Part 1: Make the App

  1. Open your web browser (Chrome works best).
  2. Go to gemini.google.com.
  3. Look at the top right corner. Make sure it shows your Maricopa account (your MEID@maricopa.edu). If it shows a personal account, click the picture and switch to your Maricopa account.
  4. Click in the box at the bottom that says “Ask Gemini.”
  5. Type this, then press Enter: “Make an interactive quiz with 5 questions about brand identity design. Make it a working app I can click.”
  6. Wait. Gemini builds the app in a panel on the right side of the screen.
  7. Click through your quiz to make sure it works.
  8. If something is wrong, type what to fix in the box (for example, “Make the buttons bigger”) and press Enter.

Part 2: Send It to Google Sites

  1. Look at the top of the app panel for the Export button. Click Export.
  2. Click Google Sites.
  3. A new tab opens with a Google Sites draft. Your app is already on the page. This draft is private; only you can see it.
  4. Click the title at the top of the page and type a name for your page.
  5. Click below the app and type one sentence about what your app does.

Part 3: Publish and Share

  1. Click the blue Publish button in the top right corner.
  2. Type a short web address for your site (for example, your-name-quiz). Click Publish.
  3. Click the arrow next to Publish and click Copy published site link.
  4. Go to the AVC 283 Discord channel. Paste the link and press Enter.

If you get stuck: take a screenshot and post it in the AVC 283 Discord channel with the step number you were on. Screen-reader users: the Export menu and the Sites Publish button are labeled buttons; if a step does not read clearly, post in Discord or send a Canvas Inbox message and an equivalent will be set up.

Good to know: your site follows the district’s sharing rules, so people outside Maricopa may not be able to open it. Gemini may build how the app works, but every design choice on your page (words, colors, images) is yours. Source: Google Workspace Updates, Publish interactive apps from the Gemini app to Google Sites.
