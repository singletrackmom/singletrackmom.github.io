How This Course Works

AVC 283 is fully online and asynchronous: there are no live class meetings, and you work on your own time each week. You build one client’s brand identity all semester. You should already know the basics of Illustrator, Photoshop and InDesign from AVC 183 Graphic Design.

Plan on about 8 hours a week, or 120 hours in all. A new module opens each Monday. Every module has the same parts in the same order: Objectives, Overview, Watch, Read, Practice, Assessment and Critique. Each graded item has its own page with objectives, instructions and a rubric (the scoring guide). Every video has captions and a transcript. Read AI in This Course in Module 0 during Week 1.

Deadlines

Work is due at noon Arizona time every Thursday and Sunday; Week 1 has one deadline, on Thursday. For process steps and projects, post in the AVC 283 Discord channel by noon the day before. Work up to 24 hours late loses 10 percent. After that cutoff, called the hard close, nothing is accepted.

No work is due when the college is closed, and Canvas shows any moved deadline. If you can, comment on classmates who post late. It earns no points, but it helps the class.

Critique

Every critique comment has three parts and is two to four sentences long:

  1. Strength: one specific thing that works, and why.
  2. Issue: one specific problem and where it is (for example, “the tagline in the stacked lockup”).
  3. Suggestion: one fix tied to a design principle: hierarchy, contrast, alignment, proximity, legibility, consistency or accessibility.

Comment on three different classmates. Start with posts that have the fewest comments. Critique the work, not the person.

If you cannot use Discord, post and comment in the matching Canvas discussion instead. Tell the instructor through Canvas Inbox.

Accessibility of Your Work

Everything you make must work for people who cannot see it or cannot hear it. About 10 percent of the points on each media assignment go to accessibility. These rules apply to every assignment:

  • Video: add captions. Start with automatic captions (YouTube Studio, Premiere Pro Speech to Text or Google Meet). Then read every line and fix names, terms and timing by hand.
  • Images: add alt text to every image that carries meaning. Alt text is a short written description that a screen reader reads aloud. Write one or two sentences on what matters. Mark decorative images as decorative.
  • Documents and PDFs: use real headings, readable color contrast and alt text. Export PDFs with tags, the hidden labels that tell a screen reader what each part is.
  • Web pages: headings in order, link text that says where the link goes, alt text, and captions on video. Check the page with WAVE.
  • Discord: alt text on every image; captions or a transcript with every audio or video post.

AI in This Course

This course uses Google Gemini, a generative AI tool, in some assignments. You use it for research, writing and feedback. You make all of the design work yourself. Our industry advisory board asked us to teach AI because employers now expect it. In the 2024 Microsoft and LinkedIn Work Trend Index, 66% of business leaders said they would not hire someone without AI skills.

Using AI here is not cheating. Cheating is turning in work you did not do as if you did. Here, AI use is assigned, and you record it in an AI-use log with every assignment. You are graded on your own thinking: your research, your decisions and your design work. Other courses may have different AI rules. Follow those rules in those courses.

Your work is not used to train AI. Always sign in to Gemini with your Maricopa account, never a personal one. Under the district’s agreement with Google, what you type is not read by people, not used to train AI, and not used for ads. Your instructor will not put your work into any public AI tool. The only AI tools in this course are Gemini and Gemini Notebook.

Gemini writes; it never designs. Gemini helps with research, writing, critique questions and checklists. It never makes images, mockups or any other visual. Every logo, layout, image and visual choice is yours, and you show your process. You draw every logo yourself in vector (shapes built from points and paths, so they stay sharp at any size), and you submit the construction file.

You will learn to question it. AI makes confident mistakes and repeats bias from the data it learned from. You will learn to check every answer and to think about what AI gets wrong and what it costs.

AI has real costs. AI is trained on artists’ work, often without permission. Data centers used about 1.5% of the world’s electricity in 2024, and the International Energy Agency expects that to more than double by 2030.

AI Primer

You are here to build a career in graphic design, and employers already expect AI skills. In a 2024 Microsoft and LinkedIn survey, 66% of business leaders said they would not hire someone without AI skills. And 71% said they would rather hire a less experienced person who has them. Three out of four knowledge workers (people who work with information) already use AI at work. Without these skills, you would fall behind the people you compete with for jobs.

So why learn it? Refusing AI does not slow it down. It only takes away your voice in how it is used. The designers who shape how a studio uses AI are the ones who understand it. Employers also say creativity, communication and critical thinking matter more than ever, because AI cannot do those for you. In this course, the creative work stays in your hands. You learn to use AI for what it does well and to catch it when it is wrong. You can leave as an informed critic, but only if you know how it works.

From Gemini to a Web Page

You can turn something you make in Gemini into a real web page, with no code. You publish your Final Project Part B case study on Google Sites the same way.

Part 1: Make the App

  1. Open your web browser (Chrome works best).
  2. Go to gemini.google.com.
  3. Look at the top right corner. Make sure it shows your Maricopa account (your MEID@maricopa.edu). If it shows a personal account, click the picture and switch to your Maricopa account.
  4. Click in the box at the bottom that says “Ask Gemini.”
  5. Type this, then press Enter: “Make an interactive quiz with 5 questions about brand identity design. Make it a working app I can click.”
  6. Wait. Gemini builds the app in a panel on the right side of the screen.
  7. Click through your quiz to make sure it works.
  8. If something is wrong, type what to fix in the box (for example, “Make the buttons bigger”) and press Enter.

Part 2: Send It to Google Sites

  1. Look at the top of the app panel for the Export button. Click Export.
  2. Click Google Sites.
  3. A new tab opens with a Google Sites draft. Your app is already on the page. This draft is private; only you can see it.
  4. Click the title at the top of the page and type a name for your page.
  5. Click below the app and type one sentence about what your app does.

Part 3: Publish and Share

  1. Click the blue Publish button in the top right corner.
  2. Type a short web address for your site (for example, your-name-quiz). Click Publish.
  3. Click the arrow next to Publish and click Copy published site link.
  4. Go to the AVC 283 Discord channel. Paste the link and press Enter.

If you get stuck: post a screenshot in the AVC 283 Discord channel with the step number. Screen-reader users: the Export menu and the Publish button are labeled buttons. If a step does not read clearly, post in Discord or send a Canvas Inbox message, and another way will be set up.

Good to know: your site follows the district’s sharing rules, so people outside Maricopa may not be able to open it. Gemini may build how the app works, but every design choice on your page is yours: words, colors and images.
