How This Course Works

The course is fully online, with no set class times. Log in to Canvas and Discord at least three times a week, and plan on about 8 hours of work a week. The hours shown on each module are estimates, so some weeks run a little lighter or heavier.

Every module has the same parts in the same order: Objectives, Overview, Watch, Read, Practice, Assessment and Critique. Each graded item names the module objective it measures and the course competency (a skill the college requires this course to teach) it supports.

Deadlines

Work is due at noon, Arizona time, every Thursday and Sunday. Projects and project steps go on Discord by noon the day before, and your three critique comments are due with the work. Work up to 24 hours late loses 10%; after that it is not accepted. Week 1 has only the Thursday deadline.

Critique Guidelines

Critique happens on the Digital Media Discord, in the {{code}} channel. Comment on the work, not the person. Start each comment with I noticed, I wondered, or What if, and name a specific part of the work. Questions go in the course channel, not in direct messages. Set your server nickname to the name you want to be called; screen names are allowed. If you cannot use Discord, post in the matching Canvas discussion instead.

Accessibility of Your Work

Everything you make must work for someone who cannot see it or hear it. About 10% of the points on media rubrics are for this.

  • Video: make automatic captions (YouTube Studio, Premiere Pro Speech to Text, Google Vids or Meet), then fix every line by hand.
  • Audio: a transcript, made automatically and then corrected by hand.
  • Images: alt text (one or two sentences on what matters) on every meaningful image. Mark decorative images as decorative.
  • Documents and pages: real headings, readable contrast, alt text, link text that says where it goes, and tagged PDFs. Check web pages with WAVE.
  • Discord: alt text on every image; captions or a transcript with every audio or video post.

AI in This Course

This course uses generative AI (Google Gemini) in assignments, as recommended by our industry advisors.

Your work stays yours. You use Gemini through your Maricopa account. That is Gemini for Education, the educational version of Gemini. Google states that what you enter there is not reviewed by people and is not used to train AI models (Gemini for Education; Google’s education FAQ). Your artwork and writing are not used to teach the AI. A personal Google account is different: depending on its settings, what you enter there may be used to improve Google’s AI. That is why every assignment uses your Maricopa account.

Industry expects it. In the 2024 Microsoft and LinkedIn Work Trend Index, 66% of business leaders said they would not hire someone without AI skills, and 71% said they would rather hire a less experienced candidate with AI skills than a more experienced candidate without them (Work Trend Index). Our industry advisory board recommends that we teach these tools.

  • Using AI here is not cheating. Cheating is turning in work you did not do. Here, AI use is assigned, you list it in an AI-use log, and you are graded on your own thinking, writing, design and editing. Other courses may have different rules.
  • Privacy. Your instructor never puts your work into a public AI tool.
  • AI does not make your work. Gemini helps with research, writing, feedback and planning. It never designs: your marks, layouts and visual decisions are your own, and you show your process.
  • You will learn to question it. AI makes confident mistakes and reflects bias in its training data. This course teaches you to check its answers and to think critically about what it gets wrong and what it costs.
  • It has real costs. AI is trained on people’s creative work, often without permission. Data centers use a growing share of the world’s electricity (International Energy Agency).
  • AI-use log. Every assignment has a row in your AI-use log: the tool, what you asked, and what you kept, changed or rejected. “No AI used” is a complete entry.
  • Under 18? Some Gemini features may be blocked on your account. If one is, message your instructor in Week 1. An equal option keeps the same grade.

AI Primer (10 points, Process Work)

What this is. Your first time using Gemini in this course, and it takes about an hour. No experience with AI is needed. Complete Technology Setup first.

What Gemini is. Gemini is an AI chat tool from Google. You type a request, called a prompt, and it writes an answer. It learned from an enormous amount of text written by people, so it can sound knowledgeable about almost anything. It does not know you, and it has never been where you have been.

Why this assignment. Some assignments in this course use Gemini for research and feedback. Before you use it for that, you will see for yourself what it does well, where it falls short, and what you bring that it cannot.

Your work stays yours. You use Gemini through your Maricopa account, so what you enter is not reviewed by people and is not used to train AI models. See AI in This Course for Google’s exact wording.

This assignment measures Module Objective 0.2, which supports Course Competency {{ai_comp}}.

Part 1: Sign in

  1. Open Chrome and go to gemini.google.com.
  2. If you see Sign in in the top right corner, click it. Sign in with your Maricopa account: your MEID followed by @maricopa.edu, then your MEID password on the Maricopa sign-in page.
  3. Check the top right corner. It should show your Maricopa account. If it shows a personal Gmail account, click the picture and switch to your Maricopa account.

If Gemini does not open or says it is not available for your account (this can happen for students under 18), message your instructor in Canvas Inbox. You will receive an equal option with the same grade.

Part 2: Ask the question

  1. Click the box at the bottom that says Ask Gemini.
  2. Type Do I need to learn AI to get a good job in the digital media industry? and press Enter.
  3. Copy your prompt and Gemini’s answer into a Google Doc.

Part 3: Read it like a critic

  1. In your Doc, mark:
    • one point you agree with;
    • one point that sounds generic, overstated or like a sales pitch;
    • one question you would rather ask a working professional in the field.
  2. Gemini is made by Google, a company that sells AI. Write one sentence on how that might shape its answer.

Part 4: Give it better directions

A stronger prompt tells Gemini four things: a role (who it should act as), a task (what to do), context (who it is for and why) and a format (how the answer should look). Example: You are a hiring manager at a small design studio in Phoenix. List the skills you look for in a new junior designer, and say honestly where AI skills rank among them. I am a first-year community college student. Use a short ranked list.

  1. Rewrite your prompt using all four parts, and add one detail about yourself: the kind of work you want to do. Press Enter.
  2. Copy the new prompt and answer into your Doc. Write one sentence on how the answer changed.

Part 5: Reflect

  1. In four to six sentences: Did either answer change how you think about AI and your career? Where might a tool like this help you in your own work, and where would you not want to use it?

Part 6: Start your AI-use log

  1. At the bottom of your Doc, add a table with three columns: Tool, What I asked, and What I kept, changed or rejected. Add one row for this assignment. Every assignment in this course has a row like this, so your instructor can see how you used AI and what was your own.

Submit the link to your Google Doc.

Grading (10 points): critic’s mark-up 3; revised prompt with your own detail 3; reflection 2; AI-use log row 2.
