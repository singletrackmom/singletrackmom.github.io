# AI in every course: Module 0 additions and Module 1 AI Primer (standard)

Student-facing rule: describe the workflow as simply how the course works. Never say something is new, now, changed, moved, revised or replaced; students never saw an earlier version.

Set 24 Oct 2026. Every course uses this. State facts; do not tell students to drop or to come talk about it.

## Course notes (class schedule listing)
This course uses generative AI tools (Google Gemini) in assignments, as recommended by our industry advisors. Students use AI for research, writing and feedback, and create their own design work.

## Syllabus section "AI in This Course" (also a page in Module 0)
This course uses generative AI as part of the assignments. Our industry advisory board encourages us to teach current industry practice, and employers now expect graduates to work with AI. In Microsoft and LinkedIn's 2024 Work Trend Index, 66% of business leaders said they would not hire someone without AI skills.

**Using AI here is not cheating.** Cheating is turning in work you did not do as if you did. In this course, AI use is assigned, it is disclosed in an AI-use log with every assignment, and you are graded on your own thinking: your research, your decisions and your design work. Other courses may set different rules, and those rules apply in those courses.

**Your work is not used to train AI.** You will use Gemini with your Maricopa account. Under the district's Google for Education agreement, what you enter is not reviewed by people, not used to train AI models, and not used for ads. Always sign in with your Maricopa account, never a personal one. Your instructor will not put your work into any public AI tool.

**AI does not make your work.** Gemini helps with research, writing, feedback and planning. It never designs: your marks, layouts and visual decisions are your own, and you will show your process.

**You will learn to question it.** AI makes confident mistakes and reflects bias in its training data. This course teaches you to check every answer and to think critically about what AI gets wrong and what it costs.

## Module 1 · AI Primer (about 6 hours, weeks 1 to 2; renumber the course's later modules)
Instructor notes: expect pushback and welcome it; grade the reasoning, not the opinion; model checking an AI answer live in the first video.

Objectives:
- 1.1 Explain why employers expect AI skills, and the real costs of AI.
- 1.2 Explain in plain terms how a large language model produces an answer, and why it can be wrong.
- 1.3 Write, test and revise a prompt, and verify the result.
- 1.4 State a personal position on using AI in creative work, backed by evidence.

Overview / Why we use AI (student-facing):
You are here because you want a career doing this work. The people who will hire you have already decided AI skills matter: in Microsoft and LinkedIn's 2024 survey, 66% of business leaders said they would not hire someone without AI skills, and 71% said they would rather hire a less experienced candidate who has them. Three out of four knowledge workers already use AI on the job. Our industry advisors asked us to teach it. Leaving you without these skills would put you behind the people you will compete with.

Many of you have serious doubts about AI, and some of those doubts are right. AI is trained on artists' work, often without permission. It makes confident mistakes. It reflects the biases in its training data. Data centers used about 1.5% of the world's electricity in 2024, and the International Energy Agency expects that to more than double by 2030. This course does not hide those problems.

Here is the case for learning it anyway. Refusing AI does not slow it down; it only removes your voice from how it gets used. The designers who decide how AI is used in their studios are the ones who understand it well enough to be trusted. Employers also say creativity, communication and critical thinking matter more than ever, because AI cannot do those for you. This course keeps the creative work in your hands, teaches you to use AI for what it does well, and to catch it when it is wrong. You can leave as an informed critic; you cannot be one without knowing how it works.

Is this cheating? Same three points as the syllabus: assigned, disclosed, your thinking is graded.

From Gemini to a web page. PLACEMENT: not in the AI Primer. Put this lesson in the first module where students publish or upload work to a site in that course (just in time), as a short setup step before that assignment. Write the steps exactly like this, one small action per step, plain words, with a screenshot for each step:

You can turn something you make in Gemini into a real web page. You do not need to write any code.

Part 1: Make the app
1. Open your web browser (Chrome works best).
2. Go to gemini.google.com.
3. Look at the top right corner. Make sure it shows your Maricopa account (your MEID@maricopa.edu). If it shows a personal account, click the picture and switch to your Maricopa account.
4. Click in the box at the bottom that says "Ask Gemini."
5. Type this, then press Enter: "Make an interactive quiz with 5 questions about [your field]. Make it a working app I can click."
6. Wait. Gemini builds the app in a panel on the right side of the screen.
7. Click through your quiz to make sure it works.
8. If something is wrong, type what to fix in the box (for example, "Make the buttons bigger") and press Enter.

Part 2: Send it to Google Sites
9. Look at the top of the app panel for the Export button. Click Export.
10. Click Google Sites.
11. A new tab opens with a Google Sites draft. Your app is already on the page. This draft is private; only you can see it.
12. Click the title at the top of the page and type a name for your page.
13. Click below the app and type one sentence about what your app does.

Part 3: Publish and share
14. Click the blue Publish button in the top right corner.
15. Type a short web address for your site (for example, your-name-quiz). Click Publish.
16. Click the arrow next to Publish and click Copy published site link.
17. Go to the class Discord channel. Paste the link and press Enter.

If you get stuck: take a screenshot and post it in the class Discord channel with the step number you were on. Screen-reader users: the Export menu and the Sites Publish button are labeled buttons; if a step does not read clearly, post in Discord or contact the instructor and an equivalent will be set up.

Good to know: your site follows the district's sharing rules, so people outside Maricopa may not be able to open it. Gemini may build how the app works, but every design choice on your page (words, colors, images) is yours. Source: Google Workspace Updates, "Publish interactive apps from the Gemini app to Google Sites" (https://workspaceupdates.googleblog.com/2026/10/publish-interactive-apps-from-gemini-app-to-Google-Sites.html).

Practice: (1) Write a prompt for something in your field, then revise it twice using role, task, context and format. (2) Fact-check three claims in Gemini's answer.

Assessment: AI Primer, 10 points, Process Work. Submit: (1) your first prompt, the two revisions and one sentence on what changed; (2) the three claims you checked, with the source that confirmed or corrected each; (3) your first AI-use log entry (one row: tool, what you asked, what you kept or changed). Rubric: prompt and revisions 4, fact-check with sources 4, AI-use log 2. This assignment measures Module Objectives 1.2 and 1.3.

Critique (Discord), counts as participation only: post two or three sentences on where you stand on AI in your field and why; reply respectfully to three classmates who see it differently. This covers Objectives 1.1 and 1.4.

Due: the standard two-noon-deadline pattern.
