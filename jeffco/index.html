4. Behavioral questions + answers

"Tell me about yourself."

Innovator who led campus technology for years, then brought that into teaching digital media, now builds with AI every day and runs a 10-college study turning research into a governed AI roadmap. Close with why this role and that you live in Jeffco.

"Why this position, and why would you be successful?"

You love finding promising tech, running pilots, and helping good ideas take hold. 20+ years doing exactly that inside one of the country's largest public systems. You build with AI hands-on and make complex tech understandable. And you want to serve the community you live in.

"Describe a time you implemented a new process."

Train-the-Trainer: problem (speed + fear), action (peer leaders), outcome (independent in weeks), lesson (adoption is emotional).

"A time you evaluated a technology and helped implement it for business users."

Platform evaluation / RFP. Decided by starting from user workflows and needs; weighed cost, integration, support. Impact: adoption plus training to sustain it. You were the bridge between users and IT.

"A time you built support for a new idea people were skeptical about."

District study or an AI demo to leadership. Listen first, find the shared goal under competing priorities, present a concrete use case, start small with a pilot so people can see it before committing.

"Leading a pilot from concept to recommendation."

Render or the study. Defined the problem, set measurable outcomes up front, ran it, and were willing to say scale, modify, or stop based on evidence.

"How do you handle ambiguity / being a self-starter?"

You build things nobody asked you to build yet because you saw the need (Render, CopaMigo, the AI Community of Practice you started and run). You define the work, not just execute a handed-down plan.

"Mentoring interns or junior staff."

~20 interns a year plus a standing student team; you recruit, direct, coach, and hand off real ownership. AmeriCorps so they are paid. The highlight of your career.

"A failure, or a technology you decided NOT to adopt."

Have one ready. Good angle: a promising tool you recommended against because it collected data it should not, or did not fit the real workflow. Shows judgment and the scale/modify/stop discipline.

"How do you explain complex technology to a non-technical audience?"

Decades of teaching in person and online, presenting at conferences, demoing AI to senior leaders and frontline staff. You turn how a tool works into language each audience can act on. That is what translation really means.

5. Technical questions + answers

"How would you evaluate an emerging technology to decide if it's worth a pilot?"

Walk the framework (section 6): scan and find real case studies, run discovery with the department, assess feasibility, risk, cost, and governance, and only pilot if there is a clear problem and a way to measure success. Evidence before commitment.

"How do you design and run a pilot? How do you measure success?"

Define the problem and success metrics up front, keep it small and structured, involve the people who will use it, set a time box, then measure against the metrics. End with a clear recommendation: scale, modify, or stop, plus who owns it next.

"Risk and governance with AI, especially in government?"

Screen every option against data privacy and security first (your hard no-student-data line), keep a human in the loop, be transparent about what the tool does and its limits, and plan for responsible, sustainable ownership.

"A department is excited about an AI tool that isn't ready. What do you do?"

Honor the enthusiasm, then bring evidence. Propose a small pilot with clear success criteria so the decision is based on real results, and be honest if it should wait. Protect the relationship and the county at the same time.

"What emerging technologies are you watching right now?"

2–3 specific: AI agents / agentic workflows (you build them), retrieval and no-PII data patterns, automation of routine service requests so staff focus on people. Tie each to a plausible county use (permits, resident service requests, internal knowledge search).

6. The framework to memorize

The spine of the whole job. It maps one-to-one to their five duty areas.

  • 1. Scan & spot track trends, collect case studies, notice a real pain point.
  • 2. Discovery talk to the department; understand the actual workflow and constraints first.
  • 3. Feasibility & risk value, cost, integration, security/privacy, governance, effort.
  • 4. Buy-in present a concrete use case, secure approval and sponsorship for a pilot.
  • 5. Pilot design define success metrics and a time box; keep it small.
  • 6. Run & measure execute cross-functionally; collect evidence against the metrics.
  • 7. Evaluate & recommend scale, modify, or stop, based on results.
  • 8. Handoff ownership plan, change management, training, governance for what scales.
Your worked example: the AI-committee proposal you wrote for GCC is this framework applied to one organization. Be ready to say "I have already written the governance and rollout case for standing up AI in an institution; here is how I would adapt it for a county."

7. Public-sector & responsible-AI angle

  • Governance & risk first. Public trust is the currency: data privacy, security, transparency, human-in-the-loop.
  • Change-management vocabulary. Know Prosci / ADKAR basics (Awareness, Desire, Knowledge, Ability, Reinforcement); your Train-the-Trainer story is change management in action.
  • Automation frees people, it does not replace them. Take routine load off staff so they spend time on residents and judgment.
  • Equity and access. Emerging tech should make services easier to reach for everyone, screened for bias.

The AI-strategy course you asked about

  • Best-name option: AI For Business (Wharton). Deployment strategy, governance, people. Credible to cite.
  • Most "blueprint" option: AI Leadership & Strategic Implementation (Starweaver). You end with a personal AI strategy blueprint and use cases.
  • Fastest single course: Successful AI Strategies: A CEO's Perspective (Fractal).
  • If governance is the gap: Strategic AI Governance.
Remember: you are not a "PM-wannabe." You are the person who has already scanned, piloted, evaluated, and driven adoption of technology inside a huge public system for two decades, and who builds with AI every day. This posting was practically written for you. Walk in like it.
