Light and Lasers: a hybrid workforce course

Course build · a continuation of Synthetic SMEs

Overview The course ← The Synthetic SMEs project

A continuation of the Synthetic SMEs project: the same AI-agent build method, applied this time to a hybrid, in-person-lab course for workforce learners. The demonstration is MNT130 Introduction to Light and Lasers, a 4-credit, 12-week hybrid photonics course at Rio Salado College on the Photonics Technician Operator pathway.

Draft build. A build-method demonstration, not a course in production. The physics content and the LASER-TEC module links require the faculty subject-matter expert’s review before any student sees it. The six in-person lab dates and locations are set by the instructor and the department.

Goal

Show that the Synthetic SMEs build method is not tied to one modality or one audience. The first course was a fully online graduate course; this one is a hybrid workforce course with six required in-person labs. Same orchestrated-agent method, held to the standards that apply to each part: online-quality standards for the online lessons, and laser and lab-safety standards for the hands-on work.

Audience

Rio Salado workforce learners on the Photonics Technician Operator pathway, most of them working adults in a two-year program, not graduate students. And, for this portfolio, hiring teams who want to see whether the method holds up when the modality and the learner change.

Process

The same panel, recast for the context. The synthetic subject-matter expert is an industry photonics practitioner rather than a research-university PhD; the synthetic student is modeled on a Maricopa two-year workforce learner rather than an incoming graduate; the instructional-designer agent is unchanged. The build skill gained a hybrid section, so it now scopes online-quality standards (OSCQR) to the online lessons, assesses each in-person lab with a safety-gated skills-demonstration rubric anchored to ANSI Z136.1, right-sizes Regular and Substantive Interaction because the labs carry face-to-face contact, and splits seat time across online and lab hours. Seat time is the credit-hour budget the Higher Learning Commission requires, so it is planned and itemized in every lesson; what is assessed, though, is demonstrated skill: the labs are scored by a signed-off skills-demonstration rubric, and success is measured by mastery and the certification pass rate. A named human subject-matter expert reviews before delivery. The activities are designed against an active-learning framework (ICAP).

Status

Draft. The six-lesson course is authored with point-valued rubrics (900 points total), itemized seat time, and safety-gated labs, and it is ready for the faculty subject-matter expert to verify the technical content and confirm the LASER-TEC module links. It is a rigorous starting point, not a finished course to drop on students.
