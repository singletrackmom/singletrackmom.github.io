Current research

Every project I own, run as a real product backlog, so priority, progress, and what is actually blocked live in one place instead of in my calendar and my head.

Open the dashboard →

Goal

Stop keeping the portfolio in my head. Every project gets a product goal, a release plan, a sprint backlog, its impediments, a definition of done, and a product backlog, so I can see at a glance what is genuinely in flight and what is quietly stuck.

The rule the whole board enforces: a project is not done when it is built. Design, build, launch or pilot, then measure. Nothing here is finished until there is evidence it worked, and every card shows how far along that arc it really is. Everything on it is a prototype or in testing. Nothing is in production, and the board says so.

Audience

Me first. It is also the honest answer to “how do you run your projects,” so I am willing to put it in front of a hiring team or a committee.

Process

I am preparing for the Certified Scrum Product Owner exam, and rather than practice the vocabulary on invented case studies I built the board against my own work. Every project is a product, I am the product owner, and the terms have to be used correctly on things I actually own. Learning a language by having to speak it about something real.

Ten products, ranked. Cards open one at a time. The Scrum vocabulary is defined on the dashboard itself, at the top, so the terms sit next to the work they describe. The header counts are computed from the data rather than typed, because a dashboard that lies about its own numbers is worse than no dashboard.
