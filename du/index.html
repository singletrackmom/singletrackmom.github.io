What this job is, in plain words

You help DU faculty use educational technology well, through programs (courses, webinars, faculty learning communities, self-paced workshops) and one-on-one consultations. You are also first-level support for Canvas and the other tools, and you work with IT on Canvas changes and integrations. The posting names emerging technologies twice, which is the opening for AI and immersive work.

Why this jobPrompts only.

The original job descriptionVerbatim, captured 31 October 2026

Located within the Vice Provost of Faculty Success within the Office of the Provost, the current mission of the Office of Teaching and Learning is to support faculty in the advancement of transformative, inclusive, and impactful teaching and learning across all disciplines and modalities to actualize DU’s educational promise to all students The OTL staff supports faculty by advancing skills in course design, pedagogy, inclusive teaching, Scholarship of Teaching and Learning and academic assessment. We seek a highly organized, self-motivated, tech-savvy, solution-oriented, and customer-focused individual to serve as Faculty Developer.

Position Summary

The Faculty Developer reports to the Assistant Vice Provost/Director of the Office of Teaching and Learning (OTL) and works collaboratively with OTL staff members and university faculty. The primary function of the Faculty Developer is to support the adoption and effective use of educational technology through faculty development offerings and consultations. Additionally, the Faculty Developer provides the first level of support for troubleshooting educational technology issues with faculty and staff. This is a full-time staff position.

Faculty Development Programs and Initiatives

  • Design, develop, implement, and assess research-informed faculty development programs, surrounding online learning pedagogy, educational technology, and emerging technologies.
  • Support faculty and staff in the adoption and use of learning technologies in pedagogically effective ways. This includes but is not limited to faculty development courses, webinars, faculty learning communities, self-paced workshops, and other specialized offerings as needs arise.
  • Work in collaboration with IT to support the administration of the Learning Management System (Canvas), including changes with the LMS, integrations, policies and guidelines, technical challenges, developing and delivering training, and best practices.

Consultations

  • Conduct confidential and problem-solving consultations with faculty members and academic departments about teaching and learning issues, particularly as it pertains to online learning, educational technology, and emergent teaching trends.
  • Provide technical support and troubleshooting for the learning management system and other instructional technologies supported by the university.
  • Provide support for individuals and departments as they explore solutions for evolving teaching needs through the adoption of new educational technologies.
  • Conduct confidential and problem-solving consultations with faculty members and academic departments about teaching and learning issues, particularly as it pertains to online learning and the LMS.
  • Provide technical support and troubleshooting for the learning management system and other instructional technologies supported by the university.

Collaboration within and beyond the OTL

  • Participate as a collaborative team member and provide thought leadership in areas of expertise through support and implementation of OTL activities and programs
  • Collaborate with OTL staff to support initiatives related to new faculty onboarding, ongoing academic year programming, and others as needed.
  • Represent the OTL and provide expertise to campus-wide initiatives and participate in campus-wide committees that intersect with the role.
  • Provides backup technical support to OTL-supported educational technology systems as needed.
  • Other duties as assigned by supervisor

National Trends and Practice

  • Maintain currency in the field of faculty development, educational and emergent technology, and online education
  • Contribute to national dialogue and scholarship by participating in national and/or local conferences and engaging in research necessary to share DU’s achievements with the broader scholarly community.
  • Seek out professional development, and actively participate in relevant professional organizations (as funding allows).

Knowledge, Skills, and Abilities

  • Knowledge of emerging trends in higher education related to educational technology and online learning
  • Proficiency with Learning Management System administration
  • Knowledge of trends and effective faculty development practices
  • Ability to create inclusive learning environments
  • Knowledge of teaching trends in adult learning
  • Ability to work collaboratively with faculty, staff, and students
  • Ability to work independently and with minimal supervision
  • Ability to manage competing priorities and deadlines
  • Public speaking and presentations
  • Effective written and oral communication

Required Qualifications

  • Masters degree.

Preferred Qualifications

  • Experience supporting the administration of an academic LMS platform
  • Experience working in a higher education setting
  • Expertise in instructional design.

Working Environment and Schedule

Standard office environment. Unexpected interruptions occur often and stress level is moderate to high. Noise level is quiet to moderate. Ability to work in front of a computer for extended periods of time. Occasionally required to move about the office/campus.

Monday – Friday, 8:00 a.m. – 4:30 p.m. In accordance with the University’s flexible work policy, this position is eligible to be considered for partial remote work. Further details regarding this plan will be determined with the hiring manager and are dependent on the division and team specific needs.

Questions to ask themThe first three matter most

  • Latitude. “How much room does this role have to propose and launch its own programs, for example in AI or immersive technology, alongside the OTL’s existing calendar?” Listen for whether ideas go through the director, an advisory board or a planning cycle.
  • Partial remote. “What does partial remote look like for this team in practice? Which days are people on campus, and how is that set?” DU policy: the unit decides and it becomes a Flexible Work Agreement, which the unit can change.
  • Outside employment. “I teach one online graduate course part time. How does DU handle outside teaching for staff?” Timing: ask HR, or at the offer stage, not in a first panel. The Conflict of Interest policy asks for disclosure to the supervisor; it does not address teaching elsewhere directly.
  • The staff page lists a vacant Faculty Developer of Online Learning. Is this that role, and what happened to the integrative and experiential learning one?
  • How is Canvas administration split between the OTL and IT? Who handles integration and LTI requests?
  • With Copilot Chat available to everyone and the new Generative AI Guidelines, what are faculty asking the OTL for most?
  • What would a successful first year in this role look like, and how does the OTL assess its programs?
  • What is this role’s part in the Zoom to Teams transition?
  • With the new colleges forming, which faculty groups do you expect to need the most support next year?
  • Is there funding for conferences and professional organizations, such as POD Network, OLC or EDUCAUSE?
