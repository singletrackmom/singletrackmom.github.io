Why it matters here: UDL is written into the OTL mission. DU runs a Neurodiversity Institute for faculty built on UDL, and the OTL has run a UDL champions group. Expect it.

What it is

  • A course design framework from CAST, a nonprofit. Plan for learner variability up front instead of fixing barriers one student at a time.
  • Current version: UDL Guidelines 3.0, July 2024. DU’s Neurodiversity Institute still cites 2.2. 3.0 renames “checkpoints” to “considerations,” adds identity and removing biased barriers, and aims at learner agency.
  • UDL is not accessibility. Accessibility: can a student reach the content (captions, alt text, headings, contrast, WCAG 2.1 AA). UDL: does the course give real choices in how students engage, take in content and show learning.

Evergreen tickets and the fix

  • “My students can’t see it.” Unpublished module or item, availability dates, the “Assign to” settings, or module prerequisites. First move: Student View.
  • “The grade total is wrong.” Assignment group weights, grades not posted under the posting policy, missing versus excused, and blank cells counting as zero when final grades sync. Fix: grade or excuse every cell before final grades.
  • “I copied my course and it’s a mess.” Duplicate content from importing twice, dates not shifted, quizzes converting, broken links. Fix: date adjustment during import, then the Link Validator under Settings.
  • “A student needs more time or an extension.” “Assign to” for that student, or New Quizzes accommodations for every quiz at once.
  • “A student needs access after the term ended.” Extend that section or the course end date so the student can finish an incomplete.
  • “Too many notifications” or “students say they never got it.” Notification preferences are per user; announcements versus Inbox; check the student’s settings.

What DU actually has

What changed in 2026

  • Copilot left Word, Excel, PowerPoint and OneNote for free users. Microsoft announced it in March for April 15, then moved it to mid-May 2026. Help with email and calendar in Outlook stayed. Faculty who used the Copilot button in Word last year may think it broke. It didn’t; it moved to the Copilot app.
  • Teach. An education hub inside the Copilot app, on by default for faculty with Copilot Chat since January 2026: lesson plans aligned to standards, adjusting reading level or difficulty, drafting quizzes and rubrics from learning objectives, and making class resources.
  • Usage limits. On November 3, 2026, Microsoft announced fair-use caps on more Copilot Chat features for unlicensed accounts. The Researcher and Analyst agents stay paid-only.
  • Coming: Microsoft is working on putting Copilot inside learning management systems and LMS assignments, including a Copilot starter prompt in Teams and LMS assignments (in development as of October 2026).

What it’s good at

  • Drafting and rewriting: emails, announcements, assignment directions, feedback banks, a plainer version of a dense syllabus paragraph.
  • Working from a file you upload: summarize a reading, pull key terms, turn notes into a study guide, compare two documents.
  • Web answers with links: it searches the web and cites sources, so you can check them.
  • First drafts of course materials: quiz questions, rubric rows, discussion prompts, case scenarios, through Teach or plain chat.
  • Safe for DU data under DU’s own guidelines, which is the main reason faculty should use it over a personal ChatGPT account.

Where it falls short

  • The free version doesn’t know your stuff. It answers from the web and whatever you upload. It can’t search your inbox or OneDrive unless you have the paid license.
  • No more one-click help inside Word or PowerPoint without a license.
  • Generic pedagogy. Teach lesson plans lean toward lecture-style, with students as passive listeners, and its standards alignment is built for K-12, not college courses.
  • Accuracy. It makes mistakes, especially with math and facts. It shows a “check for mistakes” note for a reason.
  • Caps on heavy use for free accounts.

Questions to ask themThe first three matter most

  • Latitude. “How much room does this role have to propose and launch its own programs, for example in AI or immersive technology, alongside the OTL’s existing calendar?” Listen for whether ideas go through the director, an advisory board or a planning cycle.
  • Partial remote. “What does partial remote look like for this team in practice? Which days are people on campus, and how is that set?” DU policy: the unit decides and it becomes a Flexible Work Agreement, which the unit can change.
  • Outside employment. “I teach one online graduate course part time. How does DU handle outside teaching for staff?” Timing: ask HR, or at the offer stage, not in a first panel. The Conflict of Interest policy asks for disclosure to the supervisor; it does not address teaching elsewhere directly.
  • The staff page lists a vacant Faculty Developer of Online Learning. Is this that role, and what happened to the integrative and experiential learning one?
  • How does this role work with the Neurodiversity Institute and the UDL work? Is there a UDL piece in the Canvas course reviews?
  • How is Canvas administration split between the OTL and IT? Who handles integration and LTI requests?
  • With Copilot Chat available to everyone and the new Generative AI Guidelines, what are faculty asking the OTL for most?
  • Are faculty and the OTL happy with Copilot, or is DU looking at other models, such as Gemini, ChatGPT Edu (which CU adopted) or Claude? Who decides, the AI Steering Committee or IT?
  • Do new adjuncts get a peer mentor, or is mentoring mainly for appointed faculty? Does the OTL still run the Peer Teaching Mentor program?
  • Who builds online courses now? The OTL staff page lists no instructional designers. Does this role take on design consultations, and how does it work with University College’s design team?
  • What would a successful first year in this role look like, and how does the OTL assess its programs?
  • What is this role’s part in the Zoom to Teams transition?
  • With the new colleges forming, which faculty groups do you expect to need the most support next year?
  • Is there funding for conferences and professional organizations, such as POD Network, OLC or EDUCAUSE?
