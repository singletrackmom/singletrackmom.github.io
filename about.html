About Michelle Blomberg

I’m a learning experience designer and strategist working at the intersection of AI, design, and higher education, focused on what learning looks like next. I have always been an early adopter, at the forefront of whatever medium comes next, and right now that means AI. My biggest effort is a district-wide student-journey study across all 10 Maricopa colleges and their roughly 140,000 students, my League for Innovation Fellows project, mapping where students hit barriers and where AI can remove friction without losing the human connection that keeps them enrolled. Alongside it I build the tools that test those ideas: Render, a personal learning environment I took from a blank page to a working pilot, and increasingly agentic systems, assistants that watch, decide, and act rather than wait at a chat prompt.

Two decades of teaching design, a field where the tools never stop changing, taught me that the durable skill is the thinking underneath, not the software of the moment, and that is the lens I bring to AI. Marshall McLuhan’s idea that every new medium reshapes how we see feels truer than ever: preparing people for a future that doesn’t exist yet means designing for the ways of learning that are coming, not just the ones we have. The judgment I care about most is where AI accelerates learning and where human connection is what keeps a student moving, measured not by features shipped but by learning, retention, and engagement. I stay close to that edge, constantly researching and building toward what higher education becomes as AI reshapes how we learn, assess, and connect.

Background

My background spans design, education, and educational technology: from web and graphic design to UX design, to software product management at an EdTech startup, to instructional technology leadership at Glendale Community College, to my current faculty role in Digital Media Arts, where I teach graphic design and UX and served as Program Director for over a decade. I led the campus LMS team during the Blackboard era and contributed to the RFP that brought Canvas. I lead a campus AI community of practice and deliver faculty workshops on using AI in teaching. The curriculum I design is workforce-aligned, shaped with an industry advisory committee, and rooted in experiential learning and the personal learning environment model from my graduate research.
