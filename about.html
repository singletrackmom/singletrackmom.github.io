About

Welcome. I’m a college faculty member with 20+ years in higher education.

I hold a master’s in Educational Technology and served as my program’s director for over a decade. My path ran through graphic and web design, product management at an EdTech startup, and a role as Director of Instructional Technology for my campus’s Innovation Center, before I came to teaching. I’m a League for Innovation in the Community Colleges Fellow.

I teach design, a field where the tools never stop changing, so the real skill is the thinking underneath: design principles, visual communication, how people process information. Marshall McLuhan’s “The Medium Is the Massage” is a book I used to have my students read; every new medium reshapes how we see, and what prepares students for a future that doesn’t yet exist is learning to carry that thinking into whatever tool comes next.

AI is the biggest version of that shift yet, and the way I learn best is to build with it, to expand what is possible rather than replace the creative work. So alongside teaching I make tools for student success and for fun. Render is a learner platform I took from a blank page to a working pilot, mapping the student journey and designing the loops that bring learners back. More and more the work is agentic: I design and run my own AI agents, scheduled assistants that watch, decide, and act on their own, working across my whole computer rather than a single chat window. I give each one reusable Markdown instructions so I’m not starting from scratch every time, which is how a whole suite of personal agents became part of my daily practice rather than a stack of one-offs.

The judgment call I make over and over is where AI accelerates learning and where human connection is what keeps a student motivated and moving, measured not by features shipped but by learning, retention, and engagement. My district study maps the UX barriers students hit across all ten colleges, then works to fill those gaps with targeted, human-in-the-loop AI pilots; it is my Fellows project and my biggest effort. I also lead the Student Support and Success domain of the district’s AI Resource Center, the group coordinating AI across all ten colleges and roughly 140,000 students. On my own campus I lead an AI community of practice and facilitate faculty development, so colleagues can adopt these tools with confidence. The rest of the work here is experiential learning, students doing real work for real audiences: campus murals and client projects my students take on, the internship program I oversee, the Campus Cares brand system, and The Traveler.
