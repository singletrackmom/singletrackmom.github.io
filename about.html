About

I’m an AI strategist and learning experience designer with more than 20 years in higher education.

I hold a master’s in Educational Technology with an adult online learning emphasis, which I earned online as a working adult myself, and served as a program director for over a decade. My path ran through UX and design, product management at an EdTech startup, and instructional-technology leadership at my campus’s Innovation Center. I have taught for decades, moving into full-time faculty after leading instructional technology. I’m a League for Innovation in the Community Colleges Fellow.

What I actually do is design learning. I start from measurable outcomes and work backward, align activities and assessment to those outcomes, and build the result in the LMS, held to WCAG accessibility from the first draft. I led my campus’s LMS team and co-chaired the district’s eLearning Advisory Group. Running pilots is a throughline for me: I take something new, test it for real, and scale what works, from learning platforms to today’s AI tools. I design for competency over seat time, ground my courses in adult learning theory and backward design, and assess through authentic, portfolio-based work rather than tests. The learners I design for are mostly working adults balancing jobs, family, and school, and they are the audience I know best.

AI is the biggest version of that shift yet, and I learn it by building. The learning science is the part most AI experimentation skips, and it is what I bring: I design tools and agents that remove friction for students, always with a human in the loop, judged not by features shipped but by whether they improve learning, retention, and engagement, and tested for efficacy before anything scales. The projects here show that work.

My biggest effort is a student-journey study across my district’s ten colleges and roughly 140,000 students, my League for Innovation Fellows project: I research where students hit barriers to pinpoint where human-in-the-loop AI could actually help. Alongside it, I co-chair a Student Support and Success domain on the district’s AI Resource Committee and lead a campus faculty AI community of practice.

Experiential learning ties it all together, and it is where assessment is going: competency shown through authentic, real-world work and AI-powered simulation, not tests and discussion boards. My students do real work for real audiences: campus murals, client projects, the internship program I oversee, the Campus Cares brand system, and The Traveler.

My home discipline is design, now spanning graphic and UX design, and the class I love most is the career-launch capstone, where students prepare for real work. Teaching design taught me what carries across every tool: the thinking underneath. As McLuhan put it, every new medium reshapes how we see, so what lasts is the judgment about when a tool helps and when human connection matters more.
