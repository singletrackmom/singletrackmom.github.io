The harder ones
22An assessment AI can't just complete.
  • The move: shift the evidence from the finished artifact to the record of making it (process, iteration, response to critique)
  • Anchor it to something only this student had, their own work or goals, and have them defend a decision out loud
  • Individualized by default: across our programs students pick their own subject and source their own files, so almost no two projects are alike (exercises sometimes supply files, projects almost never), which makes generic AI output useless
  • AVC 248: spoken project walkthroughs, their performance, not a file a model could generate
  • AVC 100: applied identification tied to the student's own piece, not a generic quiz
  • Simulations: put the student in a live role or scenario where they make and defend decisions in real time (the data-science capstone’s stakeholder defense), which a model can’t sit through for them
  • My online courses: students record themselves presenting their work, scaffolded; every project runs ~4-5 process steps, all through critique and reflection
  • Grade the AI use itself, they disclose how they used it
  • Line: "the artifact never protects the assessment; the record of making it does"
23A time you failed / changed course.
